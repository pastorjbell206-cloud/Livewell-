# Grief Flow — First Drafts

These five essays are AI-generated first drafts in the voice of James Bell, written for the LiveWell grief flow (track: doubt, audience: individuals). They are the most pastoral of the content tracks. The prophetic edge is held lightly here; weight and lament carry the writing, and no essay resolves the pain cheaply or offers easy theodicy.

**Status: `draft`. None of these are publish-ready.** Every file requires James's voice pass before it goes anywhere near the site.

## Before publishing — required

1. **Fill every `[PERSONAL STORY]` placeholder with true material.** In a grief flow especially, a fabricated personal loss is harmful. These placeholders mark spots where a real, specific account from James — witnessed at a graveside or carried himself — would land hardest. Do not invent one. Supply a true one, or cut the placeholder and let the surrounding prose carry the weight.
   - `the-weight-that-stays.md` contains 1 placeholder.
   - The other four use composite/anonymized pastoral observation ("I have stood in those rooms") rather than a specific invented loss, and need a truthfulness check on those lines.

2. **Voice pass by James.** Confirm cadence, theology, and that nothing reads as borrowed. Check the scholars cited (Wolterstorff's *Lament for a Son*, Lewis's *A Grief Observed*, Brueggemann on the lament psalms, von Balthasar on Holy Saturday) against James's own reading and convictions.

3. **Forbidden-language check.** Drafts were written against the CLAUDE.md forbidden list (no *delve, leverage, navigate, journey*-as-metaphor, etc.; no exclamation marks; no signposting; no reader-directives; no announced honesty). Re-run the publish voice check before status moves off `draft`.

4. **Endings.** Each essay is built to leave weight, not comfort that erases the grief. Preserve that on edit. Resist the urge to add a "may we" or a resolving line.
