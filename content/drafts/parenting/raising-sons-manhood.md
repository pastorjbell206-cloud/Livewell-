Your son is twelve and the algorithm has already found him. It is whispering one of two scripts into his earbuds, and he is going to internalize one of them unless you give him something truer first. The first script says strength is dominance — get yours, dominate the room, never show the wound, treat softness as a tax the weak pay to the strong. The second script says masculinity itself is the problem — apologize for the shape of your body, distrust your own desire to protect and build, become as inoffensive as possible and call it growth. I want to talk to the father, or the mother raising a boy alone, who can feel both scripts pulling at a son and does not know what to put in their place.

The culture is handing your son two counterfeits and demanding he choose. One inflates him. One erases him. Both are lies.

Not the strongman. Not the apology. The cross.

## The crisis is real and it is old

I was raised without a father, so I learned early what the absence does. It does not produce nothing. It produces a boy who builds a model of manhood out of whatever is lying around — television, the angriest man on the block, the loudest voice on the screen, the silence where a father should have answered the questions. The boy fills the vacuum, because a boy cannot tolerate a vacuum where his identity is supposed to be. He fills it with whatever is available. Right now what is available is monetized rage.

The data is not subtle. A large share of American boys are growing up with fathers absent or present-but-unavailable, and the under-fathered boy is the one most exposed to the counterfeits, because he has no living model against which to measure them. Robert Bellah, in *Habits of the Heart*, traced how American individualism hollowed out the inherited institutions that once formed people into a shared self. The boy used to be apprenticed into manhood by a community of men. That apprenticeship has largely collapsed. What replaced it is a feed.

## Why both counterfeits are theological failures

The strongman script fails because it builds a self that cannot be saved. A man who has organized his entire identity around never being weak, never being wounded, never needing anyone, has built a fortress the gospel cannot enter. The gospel comes to the weak. It comes to the one who knows he needs rescue. The strongman has made himself, by definition, un-rescuable. He has armored the exact place the grace was supposed to get in. He will succeed at dominance and starve to death inside it.

The apology script fails for the opposite reason and lands in the same ditch. It tells the boy that the strength itself — the drive to protect, to provide, to build, to fight for something larger than himself — is a defect to be managed. But Scripture does not ask men to surrender their strength. It asks them to crucify it and receive it back aimed at something worth dying for. The problem was never that men are strong. The problem is what the strength serves.

Here is the third thing. "Husbands, love your wives, as Christ loved the church and gave himself up for her." Read what that verse actually demands. It does not tell the man to dominate and it does not tell him to disappear. It tells him to spend his strength to the point of death on behalf of someone else. That is not weakness and it is not domination. It is power laid down on purpose. The model is a man with the strength to call legions of angels who chose instead to be nailed to a board for people who were mocking him. That is the manhood. It is harder than both counterfeits, which is exactly why the counterfeits sell.

## What a son actually learns from

He does not learn this from a lecture. He learns it from a body in front of him doing it. Boys learn manhood the way they have always learned it — by watching a man and copying him. This is the part no algorithm can replace and no sermon can shortcut. Your son is learning what a man is from how you handle being wrong, how you treat his mother when you are tired, whether you keep your word when it costs you, what you do with your anger, whether you can apologize without it killing you.

If he never sees a man lay his strength down for someone weaker, he will not believe it is possible, and he will default to the script that at least makes him feel like something. The cross has to be embodied before it can be taught. Bonhoeffer said the cost of discipleship is the call to come and die. A boy needs to watch a man die a hundred small deaths — to his ego, his comfort, his need to win the argument at the dinner table — before the word *sacrifice* means anything but a sermon noise.

## The protection he needs from you now

You cannot keep the feed away from him entirely; that war is mostly lost on the level of devices. But you can do the thing the feed cannot. You can know him. The boy who is deeply known by a present father or a fierce mother is far harder for the counterfeits to capture, because he is not starving. The counterfeits feed on the vacuum. Fill the vacuum with presence and the whispers lose most of their power. Not all. Most.

And give him men. If you are a single mother, this is not a failure to admit; it is a need to meet. Find him faithful men — an uncle, a coach, a pastor, the kind of man you would want him to become — and put them in his life on purpose. The community apprenticeship has collapsed at the cultural level. Rebuild a small piece of it around your specific son. I was raised by a mother without a father in the house, and I can tell you the men who stepped into that gap did more for me than they ever knew. A boy can be substantially fathered by men who are not his father. It takes intention, and it takes a parent humble enough to ask. Ask.

## Why he needs the wound permitted

There is one more thing the strongman script gets exactly backwards, and your son is absorbing it right now. The script says a man does not feel, does not break, does not weep, does not need. Then it wonders why men are dying — by their own hand at rates that should stop the country cold, isolated, unable to name what is killing them because naming it would breach the one rule they were given. A boy taught that the wound is shameful does not stop being wounded. He just learns to bleed where no one can see, until the bleeding becomes something worse.

The man your son needs to watch is a man who can be wounded out loud. The shortest verse in Scripture is two words. *Jesus wept.* He wept at a grave, in public, in front of everyone, and the text does not treat it as a lapse in his masculinity. It treats it as the love of God made visible in a man's face. The Lord of the universe stood at his friend's tomb and broke down, and then walked into the tomb and undid death. The strength and the tears are in the same man, in the same scene, ten verses apart. That is the integration the counterfeits cannot offer. The strongman has the strength and no tears. The apology script has the tears and no strength. Your son needs to see both, in one man, and the most available candidate to be that man is you.

## The verdict

The world will offer your son a throne and a cage and tell him to pick. Hand him a cross instead, and let him watch you carry one first. Strength that protects. Power that serves. A man who can be wounded, can be wrong, can lay it all down, and is not diminished by any of it. That is the only manhood that can be both strong and saved.

He is going to become a man either way. The only question is which one he is copying. Be the one worth copying. Then get out of the way and watch what God does with a boy who was actually fathered.
