# Who Is God? — the attributes, for a newcomer

A five-essay cluster introducing classical theism to someone who only knows a
vague or cartoon God. The goal is not to defend doctrine to insiders but to
make the historic attributes of God land as fresh, weighty, and worth wanting
for a person standing at the edge — a skeptic, a returner, or a Christian who
inherited a flattened picture and never got the real one.

**Track:** theology
**Audience:** individuals
**Register:** modern and accessible (Comer-adjacent), Keller architecture —
open in the real question, state a bold thesis early and bold it, then build.
Warm and inviting, never insider-smug. Self-implication is welcome and earned:
James came to faith from atheism, so the cartoon God being dismantled is one he
once rejected himself.

Technical terms (impassibility, *deus absconditus*, aseity, *tota simul*,
*sui generis*) live in the **body only** — never in the titles. Titles are
plain questions a newcomer actually asks.

## The essays

1. **`is-god-angry.md`** — "Is God Angry?"
   Wrath rightly understood: not divine temper but settled opposition to evil;
   why a God who never got angry could not be good.
   Scholars: D. A. Carson; Miroslav Volf (*Exclusion and Embrace*, 1996);
   Abraham Heschel (*The Prophets*, 1962).
   Scripture: Exodus 34:6–7; Romans 1:18; Nahum 1; John 3:36.

2. **`the-holiness-of-god.md`** — "What Does It Mean That God Is Holy?"
   Otherness, the numinous, Isaiah's terror; holiness as category, not moral
   grade; the bad news before the good news.
   Scholars: R. C. Sproul (*The Holiness of God*, 1985); Rudolf Otto
   (*The Idea of the Holy*, 1917).
   Scripture: Isaiah 6; Leviticus 11:44; Habakkuk 1:13; Revelation 4.

3. **`does-god-change-his-mind.md`** — "Does God Change His Mind?"
   Immutability vs. the God who "relents"; classical theism explained gently;
   why an unchanging God is the only one you can lean your weight on.
   Scholars: Thomas Aquinas; Boethius (*The Consolation of Philosophy*, c. 524).
   Scripture: Malachi 3:6; James 1:17; Jonah 3:10; Exodus 32:14.

4. **`the-god-who-hides.md`** — "Why Does God Hide?"
   Divine hiddenness and the felt absence; the silence as a province of faith,
   not exile from it; the hidden God revealed in the cross.
   Scholars: Martin Luther (the hidden God / theology of the cross);
   John of the Cross (the dark night of the soul).
   Scripture: Isaiah 45:15; Psalm 13; Job 23; Romans 11:33.

5. **`is-god-in-control.md`** — "If God Is in Control, Why Doesn't It Feel
   Like It?"
   Providence and sovereignty without glibness; primary and secondary causes;
   the doctrine does not call the wreckage good.
   Scholars: John Calvin (*Institutes*, 1559); Boethius; Thomas Aquinas.
   Scripture: Romans 8:28; Genesis 50:20; Proverbs 16:9; Ephesians 1:11.
