# The Church Calendar / The Liturgical Year

A cluster of long-form essays introducing the historic Christian year to a
curious newcomer or post-Christian reader. The goal is to present the
liturgical seasons as something fresh, formative, and counter-cultural —
never insider-smug, never assuming the reader already belongs. Classical
terms (Advent, Lent, Eastertide, Ordinary Time, Pentecost) are introduced
and explained in the body; they never appear in the titles, which are kept
newcomer-legible per the LiveWell title register.

**Track:** theology
**Audience:** individuals
**Format:** essay
**Status:** draft

## Body register

Modern and accessible in the mode of John Mark Comer, built on Keller
architecture: open in the real question a newcomer is actually asking, state
the thesis early and in bold, then build the argument with load-bearing dated
scholars and four to six Scripture references, and end on weight rather than a
clean application turn. One `> [PERSONAL STORY — James to supply]` placeholder
sits near the top of each essay. Warm and inviting throughout. Self-implication
welcome. Openings are varied across the five.

## Title register (LiveWell-approved)

Clear, direct, a real question a newcomer would ask or a plainly-named
phenomenon; lightly edged; newcomer-legible. No Latin, no insider allusions,
no colon-subtitles, no "Not X. Y." Classical terms go in the body, not the
title.

## Voice gate (from CLAUDE.md — non-negotiable)

No forbidden words (delve, leverage, unlock, transformative, navigate,
tapestry, foster, unpack, landscape, nuanced, multifaceted, authentic,
holistic, journey as metaphor, blessed as adjective). No forbidden phrases.
No therapy-speak. No exclamation marks. No signposting or reader-directives.
No announced honesty. No invented private family scenes — public facts or
placeholder only.

## The essays

1. [why-the-church-has-a-calendar.md](why-the-church-has-a-calendar.md) —
   "Why Does the Church Have Its Own Calendar?" Time as formation; the year
   that tells a different story than the consumer calendar.
   Scholars: James K.A. Smith (*You Are What You Love*, 2016), Alexander
   Schmemann, Robert Webber. Scripture: Genesis 1, Leviticus 23, Galatians 4:4.

2. [what-is-advent.md](what-is-advent.md) — "What Is Advent, and Why Wait for
   Something That Already Happened?" Waiting, longing, the two comings.
   Scholars: Fleming Rutledge (*Advent*, 2018), Bobby Gross. Scripture:
   Isaiah 9:2, Luke 1–2, Revelation 22:20, Romans 8:22–25.

3. [why-lent.md](why-lent.md) — "Why Would Anyone Give Something Up for Lent?"
   Fasting, repentance, the wilderness. Scholars: Alexander Schmemann (*Great
   Lent*, 1969), Richard Foster. Scripture: Matthew 4:1–11, Joel 2:12–13,
   Psalm 51, James 4:8–10.

4. [easter-is-not-a-day.md](easter-is-not-a-day.md) — "Easter Was Never Meant
   to Be One Sunday." Eastertide, the fifty days, resurrection as the center.
   Scholars: N.T. Wright (*Surprised by Hope*, 2008), Alexander Schmemann.
   Scripture: 1 Corinthians 15, Acts 2, John 20–21, Colossians 3:1–4.

5. [ordinary-time.md](ordinary-time.md) — "What the Boring Middle of the Year
   Teaches You." Ordinary Time; faithfulness in the unspectacular Tuesday.
   Scholars: Eugene Peterson (*A Long Obedience in the Same Direction*, 1980),
   Tish Harrison Warren (*Liturgy of the Ordinary*, 2016). Scripture: Luke 2:52,
   1 Thessalonians 4:11, Colossians 3:17, Zechariah 4:10.
