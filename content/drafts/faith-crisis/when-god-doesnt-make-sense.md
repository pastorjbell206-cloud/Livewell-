You did everything you were told and the math still did not work.

You prayed and the marriage ended anyway. You tithed and the diagnosis came anyway. You raised the kids in the faith and one of them walked away anyway, and somewhere in the wreckage you ran the equation again, the one you were handed in your first year of believing. Faithfulness in, blessing out. It was supposed to balance. It did not balance. And the not-balancing produced a quieter terror than open atheism ever did, because the God you doubted was not a God you had rejected. He was a God who had stopped adding up.

I came to faith from atheism, so let me be honest about what I expected on the way in. I expected the universe to make more sense once God was in it. I expected the equation to resolve. What I found instead was a God who would not be solved, and it took me years to understand that the unsolvability was not the problem with the faith. It was the faith.

We were sold a calculation. Somewhere in American Christianity the gospel got reduced to a transaction, and the transaction promised coherence. Tim Keller spent a career taking that apart, showing that the older, sturdier faith never promised that the books would balance in this life. The prosperity preachers promised it. The self-help-with-a-cross crowd promised it. But the actual scriptures hand you something stranger and far less marketable. They hand you Job.

Job is the book the equation cannot survive.

Here is a man the text itself calls blameless, and the calamity falls anyway, all of it, in an afternoon. His friends arrive and they are the theology we were raised in. They run the math. Suffering means sin, they say, so confess the sin and the suffering will lift, because that is how the equation works. They are not cruel men. They are orthodox men. They are defending the coherence of God, and they spend thirty-some chapters defending it, and at the end God turns and says they have not spoken rightly of him. Read that slowly. The men who defended God's logic are the men God rebukes. The man who shouted at heaven and demanded an answer is the man God commends. The book is an assault on the equation, written into the canon, left there on purpose.

And when God finally answers Job, he does not answer the question.

He does not explain the suffering. He never mentions the wager in the prologue, never tells Job why, never hands over the chart that would make it add up. He asks instead about the foundations of the earth, the storehouses of the snow, the wild donkey who scorns the city. It looks like evasion. It is not evasion. It is God refusing to be reduced to a function Job can compute, and Job's response is not new information. It is relationship. *I had heard of you with the hearing of the ear, but now my eye sees you.* He did not get the answer. He got the Answerer. Those are not the same thing, and the difference is the whole book.

This is harder for us than it was for Job, and Charles Taylor told us why.

In *A Secular Age* he describes the buffered self, the modern person sealed inside his own mind, for whom meaning is something we manufacture rather than something we receive. We are the first people in history trained from birth to expect the cosmos to be transparent to reason. We assume that if a thing is real it can be explained, and if it cannot be explained it is probably not real. So when God does not make sense, we hear it as evidence against him. Job did not have that reflex. He had the reflex to stay in the room with a God he could not parse. We have lost the reflex, and the losing of it is why our doubt cuts deeper and heals slower than his.

I have stood at enough hospital beds to know the equation does not comfort anyone. It only condemns. When you tell a grieving mother that God has a reason, you have made yourself one of Job's friends, and you have not noticed that the book you are quoting from is the book that silences you. I have done it. Early on I did it from a kind of fear, the fear that if I could not explain God I would lose him, and so I explained him to people who needed his presence and got my chart instead. The chart is cold. The chart is always cold. It is the thing we reach for when we cannot bear the silence.

But there is a difference between a God who does not make sense and a God who is senseless, and everything hangs on it.

A senseless God is chaos with a name. The God of Job is not chaos. He is coherent in himself and only opaque to us, the way the foundations of the earth are coherent and only opaque to a man who was not there when they were laid. The Psalms live in this exact gap. *How long, O Lord? Will you forget me forever?* That is Psalm 13, and it is not a crisis of faith. It is faith doing what only faith can do, which is to address the silence rather than walk away from it. The atheist does not write Psalm 13. The atheist has no one to shout at. Lament is the native tongue of people who still believe and cannot make it add up, and scripture is full of it because God is not embarrassed by it. He canonized it.

So what do you do when God does not make sense.

You stop demanding that he do. Not because the demand is wrong, Job demanded and was praised, but because the demand was never the point and resolution was never the promise. You learn the difference between an explanation and a presence, and you learn it the hard way, usually at a graveside. You pray the angry Psalms instead of swallowing them. And you wait in the silence the way Job waited, not because the silence is comfortable but because the alternative is to walk out of the room where God actually is, into a room where everything makes sense and no one is home.

The cross is the place this all converges. The most God-forsaken cry in scripture comes from the mouth of God, *my God, my God, why have you forsaken me,* and it is a question, and it is not answered from the sky, and three days later the answer turns out to be a person and not a sentence. That is the pattern. It has always been the pattern. The God of the Bible does not hand you the equation. He climbs into the part of it that does not balance.

You wanted a God who would make sense. You were given a God who makes scars.

Stop solving him. Start following him into the dark, where the seeing finally happens.
