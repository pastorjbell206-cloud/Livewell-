# The Creeds — What Christians Have Always Believed

A five-essay cluster introducing the ancient, orthodox core of the Christian
faith to a curious newcomer or post-Christian who does not yet know historic
Christianity. The aim is to make the oldest things feel fresh and weighty —
never insider-smug, never assuming the reader already shares the vocabulary or
the assumptions.

**Track:** theology
**Audience:** individuals
**Format:** essay (long-form, ~2,400–3,000 words each)
**Status:** draft

## The register

Modern and accessible (in the line of John Mark Comer), built on Keller-style
architecture: open in the real question a newcomer is actually asking, state a
bold thesis early and in bold, then build to an earned conclusion. Warm and
inviting to someone standing outside the faith. Self-implication is natural and
welcome here — James came to belief *from* atheism, so the convert's-eye view
is a feature of the cluster, not a pose. Each essay carries load-bearing dated
scholars, four to six Scripture references woven into the prose (not listed),
and an ending that leaves weight rather than resolving it.

Classical and technical terms (homoousios, the Cappadocians, Chalcedon,
ubique semper ab omnibus) live in the *body*, never in the title. Titles follow
the newly approved LiveWell style: a real question a newcomer would ask, or a
plainly named phenomenon — clear, direct, lightly edged, newcomer-legible. No
Latin, no insider allusions, no colon-subtitles, no "Not X. Y." formula in the
title line.

## Voice gate

All five essays were written against the LiveWell forbidden-language list (see
CLAUDE.md): no *delve, leverage, unlock, transformative, navigate, tapestry,
foster, unpack, landscape, nuanced, multifaceted, authentic, holistic*, no
*journey* as metaphor, no banned phrases or therapy-speak, no exclamation
marks, no signposting, no reader-directives, no announced honesty.

## Anti-fabrication

No invented private family scenes. Only public facts about James are used
(came to faith from atheism, raised without a father, five sons, working
pastor). Each essay carries exactly one `> [PERSONAL STORY — James to supply]`
placeholder near the top, marking where a real first-person memory would land.
