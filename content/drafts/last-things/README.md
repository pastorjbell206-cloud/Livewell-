# The Last Things — Heaven, Hell, Judgment, and Hope

A five-essay cluster introducing Christian eschatology — what comes after death,
judgment, and the grounds of hope — to a curious newcomer or skeptic. The aim is
to handle the hardest and most caricatured doctrines in the faith honestly: never
lurid, never fear-mongering, never flinching past the real objection. Each essay
takes the strongest version of the alternative seriously before making its case.

**Track:** theology
**Audience:** individuals
**Format:** essay (long-form, ~2,400–3,000 words each)
**Status:** draft

## Scope note

This cluster is *eschatology* — what comes after death, the nature of heaven and
hell, final judgment, and the ground of hope. It is deliberately distinct from
the existing pastoral grief cluster and from the standalone "problem of suffering"
apologetics essay (`full-17-the-problem-of-suffering-honestly.md`). It does not
re-argue the problem of evil; it asks the further questions about what comes after.

## The register

Modern and accessible (in the line of John Mark Comer), built on Keller-style
architecture: open in the real question a newcomer is actually asking, vary the
opening each time, state a bold thesis early and in bold, then build to an earned
conclusion. Warm but unflinching — honest about the hard parts, never lurid or
manipulative. Self-implication is natural here: James came to belief *from*
atheism, so the convert's-eye view and the honest naming of his former
objections are features of the cluster, not a pose. Each essay carries
load-bearing dated scholars, four to six Scripture references woven into the
prose (not listed), and an ending that leaves weight rather than resolving it.

On contested matters — above all the nature of hell — the main historic views are
represented fairly rather than reduced to a single party line.

Titles follow the approved LiveWell style: a real question a newcomer would ask,
or a plainly named phenomenon — clear, direct, newcomer-legible. No Latin, no
insider allusions, no colon-subtitles, no "Not X. Y." formula in the title line.

## Voice gate

All five essays were written against the LiveWell forbidden-language list (see
CLAUDE.md): no *delve, leverage, unlock, transformative, navigate, tapestry,
foster, unpack, landscape, nuanced, multifaceted, authentic, holistic*, no
*journey* as metaphor, no banned phrases or therapy-speak, no exclamation marks,
no signposting, no reader-directives, no announced honesty.

## Anti-fabrication

No invented private family scenes. Only public facts about James are used (came
to faith from atheism, raised without a father, five sons, working pastor). Each
essay carries exactly one `> [PERSONAL STORY — James to supply]` placeholder near
the top, marking where a real first-person memory would land.
