# Who Is Jesus? — Christology for a Newcomer

A five-essay cluster for the person who has only a vague cultural impression of
Jesus — the good-teacher, be-kind-to-outsiders figure assembled out of greeting
cards — and who is willing to look at the historical and doctrinal figure
underneath. The aim is to make the person of Jesus vivid and weighty: his
claims, his two natures, his miracles, his death, and his resurrection.

The register is doctrinal and devotional, not primarily apologetic. It
**complements** the apologetics essay *"Was Jesus Really Just a Good Teacher?"*
(in `content/drafts/why-believe/`), which carries the trilemma-style argument
for taking his claims seriously. This cluster assumes a reader willing to hear
what the claims *mean* and asks what follows if they are true — it does not
re-run the case that they are.

## Audience

Individuals. Primarily the skeptic and the questioning newcomer; written warm
and inviting, never insider-smug. James writes from inside the question, having
come to faith from atheism.

## The five essays

1. **Who Did Jesus Claim to Be?** — `who-did-jesus-claim-to-be.md`
   The startling claims read in their Jewish monotheistic context. Why "Son of
   Man" is the highest title, not a humble one; why the claims got him killed.
   Anchors: N. T. Wright, *Jesus and the Victory of God* (1996); Larry Hurtado,
   *Lord Jesus Christ* (2003). Scripture: John 8:58, Mark 14:61–62, John 10:30,
   Matthew 16:13–16.

2. **How Can Jesus Be Both God and Human?** — `fully-god-fully-human.md`
   Chalcedon (451) for a newcomer: the doctrine as the church's refusal to
   amputate either half. Why the divinity and the humanity are each
   load-bearing. Anchors: Athanasius, *On the Incarnation*; Cyril of
   Alexandria. Scripture: John 1:14, Colossians 2:9, Hebrews 4:15,
   Philippians 2:5–8.

3. **What the Miracles Were Actually For** — `what-the-miracles-mean.md`
   Miracles as signs of the kingdom — previews of a healed world — rather than
   proof or magic. Anchors: N. T. Wright (kingdom); C. S. Lewis, *Miracles*
   (1947). Scripture: John 2:1–11, Mark 2:1–12, John 11, Luke 7:18–23.

4. **Why Did Jesus Have to Die?** — `why-did-jesus-have-to-die.md`
   The atonement, the major views explained fairly (Christus Victor,
   substitution, demonstration) and shown to need one another. Anchors: John
   Stott, *The Cross of Christ* (1986); Gustaf Aulén, *Christus Victor* (1931);
   Fleming Rutledge, *The Crucifixion* (2015). Scripture: Isaiah 53, Mark 10:45,
   Romans 3:21–26, 1 Peter 2:24.

5. **What Actually Changes If Jesus Rose** — `what-the-resurrection-changes.md`
   The implications of the resurrection (not the historical case, covered
   elsewhere): death defeated, the body and world not disposable, new life now.
   Anchors: N. T. Wright, *Surprised by Hope*; Richard Bauckham. Scripture:
   1 Corinthians 15:17–20, Romans 6:5–11, 1 Peter 1:3, Colossians 3:1.

## Voice and constraints

- Track: `theology` · Audience: `individuals` · Format: `essay`
- ~2,400–3,000 words each. Body register: modern and accessible, Keller-style
  architecture (open in the real question, bold thesis early, build to a
  weight-bearing ending).
- One `> [PERSONAL STORY — James to supply]` placeholder near the top of each.
- Titles are plain newcomer questions or plainly-named phenomena. Technical
  terms (hypostatic union, Chalcedon, Christus Victor, firstfruits) live in the
  body only, always explained in plain language.
- Forbidden-language gate (see `CLAUDE.md`) observed throughout. No invented
  private family scenes — public facts and placeholders only.
