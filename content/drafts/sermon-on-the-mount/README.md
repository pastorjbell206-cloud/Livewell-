# The Sermon on the Mount

A five-essay cluster walking Jesus's most famous teaching for a newcomer — read
as the charter of an upside-down kingdom, not as detached spiritual advice. The
register is modern and accessible (Comer-adjacent), built on Keller-style
architecture: open in the real situation, state a bold thesis early and in bold,
then build the case. Warm and inviting throughout, anchored by load-bearing
dated scholars, ending on weight rather than tidy resolution.

These are full drafts, not outlines. Each carries publish-md frontmatter and is
ready for James's editorial pass — beginning with the single
`[PERSONAL STORY — James to supply]` placeholder near the top of each piece.

## The cluster

1. **What Jesus Said the Good Life Actually Is**
   `what-jesus-said-the-good-life-is.md`
   The Beatitudes (Matthew 5:1–12) — blessing pronounced on the wrong people,
   and the recovery of what *blessed* (makarios) actually means.
   Scholars: Jonathan Pennington (*The Sermon on the Mount and Human
   Flourishing*, 2017), Dallas Willard (*The Divine Conspiracy*, 1998).
   Scripture: Matthew 5:1–12, Luke 6:20–26, Isaiah 61.

2. **Jesus Cared About More Than Your Behavior**
   `its-not-just-what-you-do.md`
   The heart behind the act — anger, contempt, lust (Matthew 5:21–30). Jesus
   moves the line off the hands and onto the heart, and why that is mercy.
   Scholars: Dallas Willard, Dietrich Bonhoeffer (*The Cost of Discipleship*,
   1937).
   Scripture: Matthew 5:21–30, 1 John 3:15, Proverbs 4:23.

3. **The Hardest Thing Jesus Ever Asked**
   `the-hardest-thing-jesus-asked.md`
   Enemies, retaliation, turning the cheek (Matthew 5:38–48). What it does and
   does not mean, and why it is the clearest window onto the heart of God.
   Scholars: Dietrich Bonhoeffer, Martin Luther King Jr., Miroslav Volf.
   Scripture: Matthew 5:38–48, Romans 12:19–21, Leviticus 19:18.

4. **What Jesus Said About Worry and Money**
   `what-jesus-said-about-worry-and-money.md`
   Treasures, anxiety, two masters (Matthew 6:19–34) — one cure for both,
   because both are a question of where security comes from.
   Scholars: Dallas Willard, Craig Blomberg.
   Scripture: Matthew 6:19–34, Luke 12:22–34, Philippians 4:6–7.

5. **Why Just Agreeing With Jesus Isn't Enough**
   `why-hearing-isnt-enough.md`
   Judging, the narrow gate, the two foundations (Matthew 7) — the gap between
   hearing and doing, and why admiration is the trap.
   Scholars: Dallas Willard, Dietrich Bonhoeffer.
   Scripture: Matthew 7:1–5, Matthew 7:13–14, Matthew 7:24–27, James 1:22.

## Voice rules (from CLAUDE.md — non-negotiable)

Titles are clear and direct, newcomer-legible, with no Latin, no insider
allusions, no colon-subtitles, and no "Not X. Y." constructions. Bodies avoid
the full forbidden-language list (delve, leverage, unlock, transformative,
navigate, tapestry, foster, unpack, landscape, nuanced, multifaceted,
authentic, holistic, journey-as-metaphor, and the forbidden phrases and
therapy-speak), use no exclamation marks outside direct Scripture quotation,
avoid signposting and reader-directives, and end on weight rather than a clean
application turn. No invented private family scenes — public facts only, with a
single supplied-story placeholder per essay.
