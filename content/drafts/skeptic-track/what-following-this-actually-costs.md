We need to talk about the price before you do anything with the rest of it. Not because I want to close a sale. Because I would be lying to you if I let you walk out of these seven essays thinking the thing I have been describing is available cheap.

I came to all this at twenty-four, out of atheism, and the version of me that did not believe assumed the whole enterprise was a comfort product. Church as emotional support. God as the friend who agrees with you. I thought Christians believed because believing made them feel better, and I could not respect a thing whose only claim on a person was that it soothed them. If that is what this were, you should not want it. Neither should I.

So here is what it actually costs, said plainly, by a man who is still bad at paying it.

It costs your money. Not in the sense that there is a fee, a subscription, a tithe you calculate and resent. It costs the assumption underneath the money, the quiet certainty that what you earn is simply yours. That the number in the account is a fact about you, a measure, a defense against the future. Christianity does not ask you to give more to charity than the decent atheist already does. It asks something worse. It says the money was never yours to begin with, that you are a steward of what passed through your hands and not an owner of what landed in them, and that on the day you die the question will not be how much you accumulated but what you did with what you were lent. I have sat with that and flinched. I have five sons and a mortgage and the same animal instinct you have to build a wall of resources between my family and the dark. The faith keeps dismantling the wall. It tells me the security I am building is a smaller god than the one I claim to serve, and it is right, and I rebuild the wall anyway, and I am telling you this so you understand I am not describing a hill I have climbed. I am describing one I am still on.

It costs your marriage, and it costs your sex life, and I will not soften either one. The culture I live in, the one you live in, treats the body as private property and desire as its own justification. What you want, if it is strong enough and hurts no one who did not consent, is treated as a fact you are obligated to honor. Christianity walks into that room and says the body is not yours in that way. It says sex is a covenant language, that it means something whether or not you intend it to, that it makes a promise with the flesh that the mouth may be trying to withhold. This is not a rule about behavior. It is an account of what a person is. And it costs, it costs the freedom to treat another human being as an experience, it costs the fantasy that you can give your body without giving anything else, and inside marriage it costs the exit. Covenant means you stay when leaving would be easier and defensible and blessed by everyone you know. I have been married long enough to have wanted, on specific afternoons, the door the culture holds open. The faith closed it. Not with a threat. With a promise I had already made and a God who takes such promises more seriously than I do.

It costs your politics, and this is the one that will embarrass you, whoever you are. I watch people arrive at church having already decided which half of the country is the problem, and they expect the faith to bless the arithmetic. It will not. The cross does not belong to your party. It will not be recruited. If you are on the right, it will convict you where you have made an idol of the nation, where you have wrapped the flag around the crucifix until you cannot tell which one you are kneeling to, where you have treated the vulnerable as a threat to be managed rather than a neighbor to be loved. If you are on the left, it will convict you where you have decided the ancient moral vision of the church is simply bigotry with a longer footnote, where you have subtracted every hard word of Jesus that your tribe finds distasteful and kept only the ones that flatter you. I have felt the blade turn on my own certainties more than once, and the temptation every time is to conclude the blade is malfunctioning. It is not. A faith that only ever indicts the people you already dislike is not a faith. It is your politics wearing a robe. The real thing costs you the comfort of a clean enemy.

And it costs your image. This is the deepest one, and the last, because it sits underneath the other three. The reason we hoard money and guard the body and enlist God in our tribe is that we are each, quietly, the center of our own account of the world. The main character. The point. Christianity's most offensive claim is not about sex or money or the afterlife. It is that you are not the point. That the story is not finally about you, your growth, your happiness, your legacy, your being seen. It is about God, and you are a creature in it, loved past all reason but not central. Everything in me resists this. I built a public life. I have my name on books and on the front of a building's worth of expectation. And the faith keeps telling me the applause is not the reward, that the man who needs to be seen has already received the only wage that kind of hunger pays, and that the invisible thing done for no audience is worth more than the visible thing done for the crowd. I know this. I preach it. I still check to see who noticed. I am inside this indictment and I am not writing my way out of it.

Someone will say I am overselling the difficulty to make the thing seem important. I understand the suspicion; I would have had it. So let me put the whole argument in one sentence. A faith that asks for everything is at least not trying to sell you something cheap. The religion you are right to despise is the one that costs nothing and promises everything, the one that hands you a better life at no charge and calls it grace. There is an old and hard idea, associated most with a German pastor the Nazis killed, that grace which asks nothing of you is not grace at all, only a comfortable counterfeit. I think that idea is true, and I think the truth of it is the best evidence I can offer you that the real thing is not a scam. Con artists lower the price. They tell you it is easy. What I am describing raises the price to the top of the market and refuses to discount, and it does that because it is not selling you an enhancement to the life you already have. It is asking for that life, whole, to hand you back a different one.

This is not a better version of your existing life. It is a different one.

Jesus said the quiet part out loud, which is more than most of his followers manage. Before he asked anyone to follow, he told them to do the math. "For which of you, desiring to build a tower, does not first sit down and count the cost, whether he has enough to complete it?" (Luke 14:28). He was not recruiting. He was warning. And elsewhere, with no marketing softness at all: "If anyone would come after me, let him deny himself and take up his cross daily and follow me" (Luke 9:23). The cross was an instrument of execution. He was not using it as a metaphor for inconvenience. He meant a death, the death of the self that insists on being the point.

I am not going to ask you to decide anything today. That is the one thing I refuse to do, because the manufactured urgency, the hand raised while every head is bowed, the pressure to close before you have counted, all of it belongs to the cheap version, and the cheap version is the thing you were right to walk away from. The door does not slam. No one is standing behind it with a clipboard timing your hesitation.

I only wanted you to know the number before you priced it in your head as something small. It is not small. It is everything you have, and it does not come with a receipt you can wave at the difficulty later. I have paid pieces of it and clutched other pieces and lied to myself about the difference, and I am a pastor, which means I am telling you the cost from inside the failure to pay it, not from above it.

So I will leave it where it belongs, which is in your hands and not mine. The claim is that a man rose from the dead and that this changes what a life is for. If it is false, none of the price is worth a cent. If it is true, no price is too high, and the ones still holding on to their money and their bodies and their tribe and their name, I am one of them, are the ones who have not yet understood what we are being offered in exchange.

Count it slowly. No one worth trusting will hurry you.
