"God Bless America" is not a prayer. It is a flag. We sing it at ball games and we sing it after disasters and we sing it the way other nations sing about their kings — as if the singing made it true.

The God of the Bible does not bless America.

He blesses the nations. He saves a remnant from every tribe. He raises empires and he ruins them, and the prophet who tells you which is happening to your own empire usually dies of natural causes if he is lucky and the rest of the time he does not. There is no covenant between God and the United States. There never was. The story we have been telling ourselves for two hundred and fifty years is not a Christian story. It is a civic religion wearing the costume of Christianity, and the costume is starting to come off.

## What Christendom was

Christendom was the long settlement in which the church and the political order assumed each other. It begins, by most reckonings, with Constantine in 312, takes its mature shape by the eighth century under Charlemagne, weathers the Reformation in altered form, and ends — depending on which century you read by — somewhere between 1648 and last week.

It was always going to end.

The fifth-century version of it ended for Rome with Augustine writing *City of God* to a church that did not yet know its empire was dying. The seventeenth-century version of it ended for Europe through three slow centuries beginning at the Peace of Westphalia, when nations agreed to stop killing each other over which Christianity was the right one and noticed, eventually, that the noticing made them less Christian and more national. The American version of it is ending now, in our generation, and the panic in our institutions is the panic of a church that mistook the costume for the body.

I am not speaking against any nation. The nations are God's, and what he wills for the nations is for them to flourish under his ordering. I am speaking against a confusion, and the confusion is ours. We mistook the building for the inhabitants. We thought the prayer at the inauguration was the church. We thought the Christian on the Supreme Court was the church. We thought the rally with the flag and the cross side by side was the church. The church is the body of Christ — a community of the willing, scattered through every nation, never identical with any.

## What political power has always cost

When the church receives political power, it pays for it spiritually. This is not a partisan observation. It is the pattern of two thousand years.

Constantine made Christianity safe and then Christianity became boring; the desert fathers had to flee the cities to find what the cities had lost. The medieval church accumulated land and influence and became the institution Luther had to crack open to find Christ again. The American settler-evangelicals built schools and hospitals and built also the theological infrastructure that made slavery defensible and segregation respectable and a particular kind of capitalism the Christian one and a particular kind of nationalism the Christian one. We did not build well. We built a house large enough to hold our preferences and called the preferences theology.

Tim Keller used to say that the church is most itself when it is willing to lose. He meant that as architecture, not slogan. He meant that the church's posture toward worldly power has to be the posture of the suffering servant — present in the city, working for the city's good, prepared to be hated for it. The American church, on the whole, has been prepared to be hated. It has not been prepared to be wrong. It has not been prepared to lose. And so the slow extraction of cultural privilege from white American Christianity has felt, to many of us, like persecution. It is not persecution. It is the long correction of a confusion.

## What persecution actually looks like

I am writing this in 2026, in a country where Christians are not jailed for prayer, are not denied jobs for crossing themselves, are not shot for owning a Bible. In Nigeria, the Plateau State villages are. In China, Pastor Wang Yi has been in prison since 2018. In Pakistan, Christians live under blasphemy laws that can take a life on a rumor. The Coptic communities in Egypt, the underground house churches in North Korea, the dwindling Aramaic-speaking villages in Iraq and Syria — these are the brothers and sisters whose persecution we should not name with the same word we use for the discomfort of having lost a culture-war argument.

When white evangelicalism in America calls itself persecuted, it does not lie deliberately. It lies because it does not know what persecution is. It has not visited those who do. It has not been arrested with them. It has not buried their children. The vocabulary of persecution is a vocabulary the American church has borrowed without paying. The borrowing is itself a sign of how much we have lost — not lost to the culture, but lost in ourselves.

## I sang the songs

I came to faith from atheism at twenty-four. The faith I came to was real. The America I came to celebrate alongside it was not — or it was real, but it was not Christian, and I had not yet learned to tell the difference.

I sang the songs. I prayed the prayers at high-school football games. I voted the line. I told the lie, and I told it from a pulpit, more recently than I want to admit. My grandfather's generation did not have the tools to see this clearly; mine does, and ran the bit anyway. The first essay in this series is the essay I would write to my younger self, if my younger self would have heard it. He would not have heard it. He had to find this out by being wrong for a long time.

The verdict has to be said plainly because most of us have spent most of our lives outside it.

There is no Christian America. There has never been a Christian America. There can never be a Christian America — not because of any failure on our part, but because of how God works. The Kingdom of God is not a nation-state. It is not a voting bloc. It is not a Supreme Court majority. It is not what the church has when it has political power and it is not what the church loses when the political power goes. The Kingdom is a community drawn from every nation, scattered through every nation, citizens here of a country whose government is not yet finished forming itself.

This is not bad news. It is the only news that has ever been good. The Kingdom that does not depend on Caesar cannot be taken away by Caesar.

## What the prayer should be

"God Bless America" is not wrong because it asks God for too much. It is wrong because it asks God for too little. The God who blesses America in the way our liturgies imagine is small — a tribal deity who picks sides, a strongman with a Bible, an outcome dispenser, a useful instrument of national feeling. The God of Abraham, Isaac, and Jacob is bigger than that, and harder, and far less convenient. The God who walks across the water does not ride in motorcades.

He blesses the meek. He blesses the merciful. He blesses those who hunger and thirst for righteousness, those who are persecuted for it, those who are reviled and falsely accused. He has not yet, in two thousand years, blessed an empire. We should not be the first generation to expect it.

I do not know what the right prayer for an American church is. I know this is not it. I know the church that learns to pray it differently will be a smaller church, and a quieter church, and probably a better one. The carving has already started. The question is whether we will recognize the hand doing it.
