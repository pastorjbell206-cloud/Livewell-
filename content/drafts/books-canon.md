# The Canon — Seven Books That Define the Brand

Per the elite strategy: 50 manuscripts is too many. 7 books is the canon
that defines you to a reader who has never heard of you. Below: each book
with a one-paragraph positioning, a chapter list, and the opening of
chapter 1 in voice. The first three are the load-bearing ones.

---

## Book 1 — *When God Bless America Replaces Thy Kingdom Come*

**Slug:** `when-god-bless-america-replaces-thy-kingdom-come`
**Subtitle:** *On the End of Cultural Christianity and What Comes Next*
**Track:** `after-christendom` · **Audience:** `individuals` · **Length target:** 80,000 words / 12 chapters
**Status:** Manuscript exists. Needs editorial pass + real cover + audiobook recording.

**Positioning:** The flagship. The brand book. The diagnosis half of the After Christendom argument. The book that gets you cited and quoted. Ship first, as the real edition: serious cover, real Amazon listing, audiobook recorded by you, foreword from someone in the Russell Moore / Tim Alberta / Andy Crouch tier. This is the book a reader gives as a gift to skeptical, theologically curious, or politically exhausted friends.

**Argument arc:**
1. The Disease Beneath the Symptoms
2. Why Seduction Is Deadlier Than Persecution
3. What Happened to Us — A Brief History of American Christendom
4. The Theological Root: What Actually Preserves the Church
5. Christian Nationalism: A Theological Failure
6. The Strongman Cannot Save Us
7. What the Early Church Knew About Living in Babylon
8. The Black Church as the Faithful Remnant
9. The Cost of Confessing
10. The Disciplines That Form Resistance — Sabbath, Hospitality, Catechesis
11. What Faithful Church Looks Like Now
12. The Kingdom Whose Government Is Not Yet Finished

**Opening of chapter 1 — in voice:**
"I begin this book on a Sunday afternoon in a year I will not name because the year does not matter. The American flag was on the platform of the church I had just preached at. It is on the platform of most of the churches I will preach at this month. I have said nothing about it from any of those platforms in a long time. The silence was prudence, and the prudence was complicity, and I am writing this book because I have run out of prudence. The disease beneath all our visible symptoms is a confusion the American church has held for two hundred and fifty years. We have confused the Kingdom with the country. We have written the confusion into our liturgies and our hymns and the prayers we offer at high-school football games. The confusion is killing us, and the killing is slow, and most of us have not yet noticed because most of us are still inside the costume. This book is about getting the costume off."

**Sources for the bibliography:** The After Christendom essay sources listed in `essays-after-christendom.md`. Plus James Davison Hunter (*Democracy and Solidarity*), Tim Alberta (*The Kingdom, the Power, and the Glory*), Robert Bellah (*Habits of the Heart*), Stanley Hauerwas, Lesslie Newbigin, Mark Noll, Tom Holland.

## Book 2 — *After Christendom*

**Slug:** `after-christendom`
**Subtitle:** *How to Be the Church When the Empire Forgets You*
**Track:** `after-christendom` · **Audience:** `individuals` · **Length target:** 70,000 words / 10 chapters
**Status:** Manuscript exists in seed form. Substantial new writing needed for the constructive sections.

**Positioning:** The constructive sequel to Book 1. Diagnosis book is *America*; cure book is *After*. This is the book pastors hand to elder boards, the book churches study in adult Sunday school, the book that becomes the operations manual for the small faithful church in the post-Christendom moment. Ship 6 months after Book 1.

**Argument arc:**
1. The Moment We Are Actually In
2. The Practices That Survived the Last Christendom Collapse
3. Sabbath as Resistance
4. Hospitality as Witness
5. Catechesis Without the Culture's Help
6. Liturgical Formation in a Distracted Age
7. The Household as the Smallest Church
8. The Parish as Mission
9. The Institutions We Have to Build (Schools, Mutual Aid, Funerals)
10. The Joy of the Smaller Church

**Opening of chapter 1 — in voice:**
"The first book ended with a verdict. Christendom is over, and the over-ness is mercy. This book takes up the harder question: what do we do now. I have written most of my career on the question, and I am only now beginning to think I know how to answer it, and the answer is going to disappoint anyone hoping for a program. There is no program. There are practices. The practices are older than the empire we are leaving. The practices have survived every empire's collapse so far. The practices are how the church has always lived in the cracks of regimes that did not pretend to be Christian. They will work for us, too, if we will recover them. Most of the book is about the recovering."

**Sources:** Lesslie Newbigin, James K.A. Smith (*You Are What You Love*; *Imagining the Kingdom*), Tish Harrison Warren (*Liturgy of the Ordinary*), Andy Crouch, Stanley Hauerwas + Willimon (*Resident Aliens*), Walter Brueggemann (*Sabbath as Resistance*), Andrew Walls, Lamin Sanneh.

## Book 3 — *The Christian and the Skeptic*

**Slug:** `the-christian-and-the-skeptic`
**Subtitle:** *Honest Apologetics from a Former Atheist*
**Track:** `doubt` · **Audience:** `individuals` · **Length target:** 65,000 words / 9 chapters
**Status:** Full manuscript in OneDrive. Needs editorial pass + cover + framing as memoir-apologetics rather than apologetics-against-atheism.

**Positioning:** Your unique credibility book. No other writer in the popular Christian space has the "I was an atheist at twenty-four" position you have. The book has to be framed as *from one former skeptic to another* — not as a refutation of atheism. The reader is a deconstructing evangelical, a thoughtful agnostic, a former Christian, a serious atheist. The Skeptic Track on the site funnels here.

**Argument arc:**
1. The Atheism I Believed and Why I Left It
2. The Questions That Actually Matter
3. What Secular Explanations Still Have to Explain
4. The Problem of Suffering, Honestly
5. The Historical Jesus, Without the Shortcuts
6. The Bible Without the Marketing
7. Morality Without God — and With Him
8. What Following This Actually Costs
9. The Faith I Have Now and What It Cost Me

**Opening of chapter 1 — in voice:**
"I was an atheist at twenty. I was a thoughtful one. I had read enough — Russell, Dawkins, Hitchens — to feel I was not missing anything important. The Christians I knew confirmed the impression; their arguments were thinner than mine and they had not engaged the strongest version of mine in any way I could see. I assumed I had won the question and could move on. I had not won the question. I had not yet asked the right one. The right one took me four years to find and another two to act on, and the path was longer and stranger than any of my reading at twenty had prepared me for. This book is the path. I am writing it to the version of me that was twenty, and to anyone now standing where he was. I am not going to argue you into faith. I am going to tell you what I found when I was honest enough to look."

**Sources:** Charles Taylor (*A Secular Age*); Alvin Plantinga (*Warranted Christian Belief*); Tim Keller (*The Reason for God*; *Making Sense of God*); David Bentley Hart (*The Experience of God*); C.S. Lewis (*Surprised by Joy*); G.K. Chesterton (*Orthodoxy*); N.T. Wright; Iris Murdoch (for the moral-realism argument from a non-believer).

## Book 4 — *The Loneliness of the Pastor*

**Slug:** `the-loneliness-of-the-pastor`
**Subtitle:** *Why Pastors Quit, and What Would Let Them Stay*
**Track:** `pastoral-ministry` · **Audience:** `pastors` · **Length target:** 60,000 words / 10 chapters
**Status:** New manuscript. Built on the existing PCN materials. Could be written in 90 days from existing PCN content.

**Positioning:** The PCN-aligned book. The book pastors will buy by the dozen for their elder boards and their own libraries. The book PCN promotes at every gathering. Bulk pricing for elder teams. This is the book that makes the PCN distribution moat real.

**Argument arc:**
1. Why Pastors Are Quitting
2. The Loneliness Beneath the Burnout
3. The Theological Loneliness — Questions You Cannot Ask Out Loud
4. The Pastoral Confidentiality That Isolates
5. When the Board Becomes the Adversary
6. The Marriage That Cannot Be a Ministry Partnership
7. The Spiritual Director the Pastor Cannot Live Without
8. The Pastor's Group That Actually Pastors the Pastor
9. The Structures That Let the Pastor Stay (Sabbatical, Pay, Housing, Insurance)
10. The Joy That Returns When the Structures Are Right

**Opening of chapter 1 — in voice:**
"I have spent five years inside the Pastors Connection Network, watching pastors quit. Some of them quit because they should have — the calling had ended for them, or had never really begun. Most of them quit because no one had built them a way to stay. The leadership writing I read in seminary did not name what is actually breaking them. The books on burnout treat the burnout as a personal problem. It is a personal problem. It is also a structural one, and the structure is what the books mostly will not say out loud. This book says it out loud. I am writing for the pastor reading it at one in the morning, considering whether he can do this another year. I want him to know what is breaking him. I want him to know what would let him stay. I want him to know he does not have to do it alone."

**Sources:** Eugene Peterson (*The Pastor*); Paul Tripp (*Dangerous Calling*); Barna's *State of Pastors* reports; Glenn Packiam (*The Resilient Pastor*); Bonhoeffer (*Life Together*); the PCN's own accumulated case material.

## Book 5 — *The Honest Marriage*

**Slug:** `the-honest-marriage`
**Subtitle:** *Deep Theology for the Actual Tuesday Afternoon*
**Track:** `marriage` · **Audience:** `couples` · **Length target:** 60,000 words / 10 chapters
**Status:** Strong manuscript exists. Needs sharpening and a unifying voice; the chapters drift in places.

**Positioning:** The marriage book. Genre-defining title is *The Meaning of Marriage* (Keller). This is the book that sits next to it. Position: the book for couples who do not want platitudes — covenant theology applied to the actual Tuesday afternoon of being married. The voice is the After Christendom voice applied to marriage.

**Argument arc:**
1. Why the Marriages Around You Are Failing
2. What Marriage Actually Is — Covenant, Not Contract
3. Your Marriage Will Get Worse Before It Gets Better
4. Most of What You Fight About Is Not What You Are Fighting About
5. The Marriage That Survives Your Parents
6. Sex in Marriage Is Not What You Were Taught
7. Money, Honestly
8. When One of You Is in Crisis
9. When Both of You Are in Crisis
10. The Marriage at Thirty Years

**Opening of chapter 1 — in voice:**
"The marriages in your life are mostly failing. They are not failing in the way the advice industry tells you they are failing, and they are not failing for the reasons the advice industry says. The advice industry tells couples that marriage is hard but worth it. The advice industry lies. Marriage is not hard the way running a marathon is hard. Marriage is hard the way being slowly carved by another person is hard. The marriages that are failing in your life are failing because both spouses have flinched from the carving. This book is for couples who are willing to stop flinching. Some of you will not want to. Some of you will. The book is for the second group."

**Sources:** Tim and Kathy Keller (*The Meaning of Marriage*); John Gottman; Sue Johnson (*Hold Me Tight*); Edwin Friedman; Bonhoeffer; Eugene Peterson; the marriage essays in `essays-marriage.md`.

## Book 6 — *Politics and the Cross*

**Slug:** `politics-and-the-cross`
**Subtitle:** *The Third Option*
**Track:** `politics` · **Audience:** `individuals` · **Length target:** 70,000 words / 10 chapters
**Status:** New manuscript. Built from the politics essays plus extensive new writing.

**Positioning:** The politics book that does not pick a team. Most controversial of the seven. Likely to either build the platform massively or pull half the audience away. Worth writing anyway. The Third Option position is rare and load-bearing.

**Argument arc:**
1. The Cross Is Not a Flag
2. What Left and Right Both Get Right
3. What Left and Right Both Get Wrong
4. The Third Option Is Not Centrism
5. Voting Is Not Discipleship
6. When Pastors Become Pundits
7. Jesus Was Not a Capitalist
8. Jesus Was Not a Socialist
9. The Sermon on the Mount as Politics
10. Citizenship in the Country That Is Not Yet

**Opening of chapter 1 — in voice:**
"I have lost friends in both directions because of this book. I have lost them before the book was written. I expect to lose more after it is. The losing is part of the cost of writing it. I want to be clear about the position from the first page so that the readers who will not want to keep going can put the book down now. I do not vote for one team. I do not vote for the other. I have voted in every election for a quarter century, and the voting has gotten harder, not easier, as I have grown into the theology this book is trying to describe. The third option is not centrism. The third option is the gospel applied to politics, and the gospel applied to politics is unlivable for anyone who has committed first to a team. I am writing for the readers willing to make a different commitment."

**Sources:** Stanley Hauerwas (*Resident Aliens*); Oliver O'Donovan (*The Desire of the Nations*); James K.A. Smith (*Awaiting the King*); John Howard Yoder (*The Politics of Jesus*); William Cavanaugh (*Migrations of the Holy*); David French; Tim Alberta; David Bentley Hart's recent essays.

## Book 7 — *Deconstruction of Faith*

**Slug:** `deconstruction-of-faith`
**Subtitle:** *For the Ones Who Cannot Stay and Cannot Quite Leave*
**Track:** `doubt` · **Audience:** `individuals` · **Length target:** 60,000 words / 9 chapters
**Status:** Full manuscript in OneDrive. Needs reframing — currently positioned against deconstruction, should be positioned alongside the deconstructor.

**Positioning:** For the deconstruction crowd. Framed *with* them, not against them. The argument: deconstruction is the first step; the second step is rebuilding on what survives. Reach: enormous. Audience: hostile to typical apologetics. Position: empathetic, intellectually honest, costly. This is the book that builds a bridge to the people leaving evangelicalism without telling them they're wrong for leaving.

**Argument arc:**
1. Why Deconstruction Is Happening Now
2. What You Are Actually Tearing Down
3. The Difference Between Inheritance and Faith
4. The Specific Evangelicalisms That Deserve to Die
5. What Survives the Demolition
6. Reading the Bible Without the Marketing
7. Prayer When You Cannot Anymore
8. The Church You Might Be Able to Stay In
9. The Faith on the Other Side

**Opening of chapter 1 — in voice:**
"I want to be honest with you in a way most books on deconstruction have not been. I do not think you are in rebellion. I do not think you have a sin problem. I do not think you need to recover the conviction you had at seventeen. Most of what you are tearing down deserves to come down, and the tearing down is, on the whole, the first responsible thing many of you have done in your faith life. This book is for the readers who have torn down most of an inheritance they were given as children and cannot quite figure out what is left. I am writing as a former atheist, current pastor, and ongoing skeptic. I am not going to ask you to rebuild what you tore down. I am going to ask you to consider what survives, and whether the surviving thing is enough to live on. For me it has been. I want to tell you what I found."

**Sources:** Pete Enns (*The Bible Tells Me So*; *How the Bible Actually Works*); Sarah Bessey; Rachel Held Evans (cite respectfully); A.J. Swoboda (*After Doubt*); Beth Allison Barr; Lecrae's recent work; David Brooks (for the secular case for religious return); Charles Taylor.
