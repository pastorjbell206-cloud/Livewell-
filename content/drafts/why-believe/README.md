# Why Believe — Apologetics for the Post-Christian

A cluster of long-form essays written for the skeptic and the curious outsider.
James Bell came to faith from atheism, and these essays are written from inside
that memory — honoring the doubt, steel-manning the objection, never cheap-shotting
unbelief. The posture is Keller's architecture (state the doubt at full strength,
make a bold but honest thesis early, build to an earned conclusion) in a modern,
accessible register. Confident, never triumphalist. The case is offered as the
best available explanation, never as a proof, and the limits of each argument are
named on the page.

**Track:** doubt
**Audience:** individuals
**Status:** draft (each essay carries one `[PERSONAL STORY — James to supply]`
placeholder near the top, before the thesis)

## The essays

1. **Did the Resurrection Actually Happen?** — `did-the-resurrection-happen.md`
   The historical case handled honestly: the minimal facts, the early creed in
   1 Corinthians 15, the rivals taken seriously.
   Scholars: N.T. Wright (*The Resurrection of the Son of God*, 2003),
   Gary Habermas (minimal-facts approach), Tom Holland (*Dominion*, 2019).

2. **Why Would Anyone Trust the Bible?** — `why-trust-the-bible.md`
   Canon, transmission, the telephone-game myth, the eyewitness question, the
   contradictions taken at face value.
   Scholars: F.F. Bruce (*The New Testament Documents*, 1943),
   Richard Bauckham (*Jesus and the Eyewitnesses*, 2006), N.T. Wright.

3. **Do You Have to Choose Between Faith and Science?** — `faith-and-science.md`
   The conflict thesis and its nineteenth-century origins; science born among
   believers; where science genuinely stops.
   Scholars: Francis Collins (*The Language of God*, 2006), John Polkinghorne,
   Alister McGrath.

4. **If God Is Good, Why Is There So Much Suffering?** — `if-god-is-good-why-suffering.md`
   The intellectual problem of evil at full strength, refusing glib answers.
   (Distinct from the pastoral grief essay — this is the argument.)
   Scholars: C.S. Lewis (*The Problem of Pain*, 1940), Alvin Plantinga
   (free-will defense), Tim Keller (*Walking with God through Pain and
   Suffering*, 2013), Dostoevsky (Ivan's challenge, *The Brothers Karamazov*).

5. **Was Jesus Really Just a Good Teacher?** — `was-jesus-just-a-good-teacher.md`
   The claims of Jesus; Lewis's trilemma examined and pressure-tested against
   the legend objection.
   Scholars: C.S. Lewis (*Mere Christianity*, 1952), N.T. Wright,
   Larry Hurtado (*Lord Jesus Christ*, 2003).

## Voice rules (from CLAUDE.md — non-negotiable)

Forbidden words and phrases per CLAUDE.md apply. No signposting, no
reader-directives, no announced honesty, no exclamation marks. Structural moves:
short-long-short rhythm, self-implication (the former atheist is always inside the
question), verdicts not summaries, endings that leave weight.

## Fact-check notes for editors

- The early dating of the 1 Cor 15 creed (within a few years of the crucifixion)
  is widely held across the scholarly spectrum but the precise window is debated;
  the essays say "within a few years / months" without pinning a single date.
- The Draper (1874) and White (1896) titles and dates are verifiable; the claim
  that they "manufactured" the conflict thesis reflects the consensus of modern
  historians of science but is a historiographical interpretation, not a neutral
  fact — flagged for an editor comfortable with the framing.
- "Three generations of imbeciles are enough" is NOT used here (it belongs to the
  politics cluster); no such contested quote appears in these drafts.
- Hurtado's "early high Christology" thesis is mainstream but contested by some
  scholars (e.g., evolutionary-development models); the essay states it as his
  argument, not as settled fact.
- All Scripture references should be verified against a standard translation
  before publishing.
