# The People Who Came Before

Church history for newcomers. This cluster exists to make the Christian past
vivid and human for someone who knows none of it — not a survey, not a
timeline, but the stories of real people whose decisions made the faith you can
walk into today possible.

These are written in the warmer, narrative register: modern and accessible
(closer to John Mark Comer than to a scholarly monograph), Keller-style
architecture under the warmth, vivid welcome rather than indictment. The reader
is not assumed to believe anything yet. The door is held open, not slammed.

## Voice rules (from CLAUDE.md — non-negotiable)

Forbidden words: delve, leverage, unlock, transformative, navigate, tapestry,
foster, unpack, landscape, nuanced, multifaceted, authentic, holistic, journey
(as metaphor), blessed (as adjective). Forbidden phrases: "in today's world,"
"now more than ever," "here's the thing," "I want to be real with you," "God's
got this," "gospel-centered" (without earning it), "authentic community," "hold
space," "your truth," "do the work," "your feelings are valid," "lean into,"
"showing up." No exclamation marks. No announced honesty. For this newcomer
cluster the approved title register is clear and plainly-named — no Latin, no
insider allusions, no colon-subtitles, no "Not X. Y." titles.

## Anti-fabrication

No invented private family scenes. For historical figures, stay with
well-attested tradition; anything legendary or contested is flagged as such in
the text (e.g. the *Life of Antony*'s visions and demons; Tyndale's reported
last words; the Ethiopian tradition). Only public facts about James; each essay
carries one `> [PERSONAL STORY — James to supply]` placeholder near the top for
him to fill in his own voice.
