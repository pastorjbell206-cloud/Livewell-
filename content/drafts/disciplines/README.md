# The Classical Spiritual Disciplines

A cluster of long-form essays presenting the ancient Christian practices as a
counter-formation to a frantic, distracted age. Written for the curious
newcomer and the burned-out modern person, not for insiders. The premise across
all five: the old practices are not relics to admire but medicine to take, and
the disorders they treat — hurry, distraction, exhaustion, disordered desire —
are precisely the disorders of the present moment.

## Register

- **Track:** theology
- **Audience:** individuals
- **Format:** essay
- **Body register:** modern and accessible (Comer), with Keller-style
  architecture — open in the real ache, state the thesis early and in bold,
  then build the case. Warm and inviting, never insider-smug. Load-bearing
  dated scholars. Four to six Scripture references each. A weight-bearing
  ending that leaves the reader carrying something. One
  `> [PERSONAL STORY — James to supply]` placeholder near the top of each
  essay.
- **Title register:** clear, direct, newcomer-legible, lightly edged. No Latin,
  no insider allusions, no colon-subtitles, no "Not X. Y." in the title.
  Technical terms (the daily office, lectio divina) live in the body, not the
  title.

## The essays

1. **`praying-at-fixed-hours.md`** — "Praying at the Same Times Every Day, Like
   Christians Always Did." The daily office / fixed-hour prayer. Praying on a
   schedule rather than by mood.
   *Scholars:* Phyllis Tickle (*The Divine Hours*, 2000), Benedict's *Rule*
   (c. 530), Eugene Peterson.
   *Scripture:* Psalm 119:164, Psalm 55:17, Daniel 6:10, Acts 3:1.

2. **`why-christians-fast.md`** — "Why Christians Fast (and Why You Probably
   Stopped)." Fasting as voluntary hunger that re-orders desire.
   *Scholars:* Richard Foster (*Celebration of Discipline*, 1978), Dallas
   Willard, the desert fathers.
   *Scripture:* Matthew 6:16–18, Matthew 4:1–4, Joel 2:12, Isaiah 58.

3. **`silence-and-solitude.md`** — "What the Desert Fathers Knew About
   Silence." Silence and solitude against an engineered noise.
   *Scholars:* Henri Nouwen (*The Way of the Heart*, 1981), Dallas Willard, the
   desert fathers (Abba Moses).
   *Scripture:* 1 Kings 19:11–13, Psalm 46:10, Mark 1:35, Luke 5:16.

4. **`a-day-of-doing-nothing.md`** — "Why a Day of Doing Nothing Is an Act of
   Defiance." Sabbath as resistance to a productivity culture.
   *Scholars:* Walter Brueggemann (*Sabbath as Resistance*, 2014), Abraham
   Joshua Heschel (*The Sabbath*, 1951), John Mark Comer.
   *Scripture:* Exodus 20:8–11, Deuteronomy 5:15, Mark 2:27, Hebrews 4:9–10.

5. **`reading-the-bible-slowly.md`** — "How to Read the Bible Slowly, the Old
   Way." Lectio divina / meditative reading against information-grazing.
   *Scholars:* Eugene Peterson (*Eat This Book*, 2006), Guigo II (the ladder of
   monks, c. 1150), Richard Foster.
   *Scripture:* Psalm 1:2, Joshua 1:8, Jeremiah 15:16, Luke 2:19.

## Voice rules (from CLAUDE.md — non-negotiable)

**Forbidden words:** delve, leverage, unlock, transformative, navigate,
tapestry, foster, unpack, landscape, nuanced, multifaceted, authentic,
holistic, journey (as metaphor), blessed (as adjective).

**Forbidden phrases:** "in today's world," "now more than ever," "here's the
thing," "I want to be real with you," "God's got this," "gospel-centered"
(without earning it), "authentic community," "hold space," "your truth," "do
the work," "your feelings are valid," "lean into," "showing up."

No exclamation marks. No signposting. No reader-directives that announce
themselves. No announced honesty.
