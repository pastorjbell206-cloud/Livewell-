# The Sacraments / The Means of Grace

A cluster of long-form essays written for a curious newcomer or post-Christian
reader — someone outside the church, or recently outside it, asking why historic
Christianity insists on physical things: bread, water, wine, the spoken word of
confession. The aim is strange-then-beautiful, never insider-smug. Each essay is
irenic about the genuine differences between Catholic, Orthodox, and Protestant
views without flattening them into a false agreement.

Track: `theology`. Audience: `individuals`. Format: `essay`. Status: `draft`.

## Register

Modern and accessible (Comer-adjacent), built on Keller-style architecture: open
in the real question a newcomer actually asks, state a bold thesis early (bolded
in the text), then build the case. Warm and inviting throughout. Load-bearing
named scholars with dates. Four to six Scripture references per essay. A
weight-bearing ending that does not over-resolve. One `> [PERSONAL STORY — James
to supply]` placeholder near the top of each essay.

Titles follow the approved LiveWell newcomer-legible style: a plain question or a
plainly-named phenomenon, lightly edged, no Latin, no colon-subtitles, no
insider allusions. Classical terms (Eucharist, transubstantiation, real
presence, absolution, sacrament, Incarnation) appear in the body only.

## Notes for editing before publish

- Each essay carries one `[PERSONAL STORY — James to supply]` placeholder near
  the top. These are deliberately specific in their bracketed prompts but contain
  no invented family scenes — fill with real material before publishing.
- Denominational claims are stated to be defensible and charitable to each
  tradition. If a particular church's practice is referenced specifically,
  verify it still matches that body's current discipline before publishing.
- These are markdown drafts. Publishing follows the same flow as the rest of
