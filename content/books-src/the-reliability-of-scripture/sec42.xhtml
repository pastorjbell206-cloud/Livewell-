A PASTORAL NOTE ON BIBLICAL LITERACY IN THE CONGREGATION

The surest investment in a congregation's lasting faith is the biblical literacy it was never given.

For Pastors, Leaders, and Parents

This note is for pastors, small group leaders, and parents who have worked through this book and are now wondering what to do with the knowledge it represents. The single most important investment you can make in the long-term faith of your congregation, your small group, or your family is biblical literacy: not merely the ability to read the Bible but the capacity to understand what is being read, to ask the right questions of the text, to know the context of the passages you encounter, to recognize the genre you are standing in.

The equipment is available. It was not delivered.

Why the Equipment Was Never Delivered

The nineteen-year-old in the introduction to this book did not leave the faith because the faith was intellectually indefensible. She left because the community she was part of had never equipped her to defend it. The equipment is available. It was not delivered.

So invest in your congregation's theological education, not at the expense of the devotional life, because the two must go together, but theological education that enables people to engage their culture's questions with confidence rather than anxiety. The person who knows why the manuscript tradition of the New Testament is extraordinary can hear a friend quote Misquoting Jesus without panic; the person who has walked through the Dead Sea Scrolls evidence can meet the claim that the Bible has been corrupted in transmission with actual information rather than with the deflection that looks, to the skeptic, like avoidance.

Preach Through Books, Not Only Topics

Preach through books of the Bible rather than through topics. Topical preaching has its place, but it produces a congregation that knows your themes rather than knowing the text. The congregation that has been preached through the whole Bible (all of it, the comfortable and the difficult, the clear and the complex) is the congregation that has some handle on what the text actually says, and it is harder to destabilize by critics who quote one verse without context, because it already knows the context.

Make Room for the Hard Questions

Make room for questions. Create spaces in your congregation where the hard questions can be asked without being treated as evidence of spiritual deficiency. The person who asks hard questions in church is the person who has not yet given up on the church. Honor their engagement. Give them honest engagement in return.

The Congregation Worth Building

That is the congregation worth building. That is the family worth raising. That is the life worth living. The ground is firmer than we were told. Let us build on it.
