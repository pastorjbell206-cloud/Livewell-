APPENDIX A

Recommended Reading

A guided reading list for Christian apologetics — the best books on God, the Bible, the resurrection, suffering, and doubt.

This appendix recommends books for readers who want to go deeper, organized by topic and, where appropriate, marked introductory (Intro), intermediate (Int), or advanced (Adv). The list deliberately includes the best defenders of views other than the author's own, because a faith that can be tested is a faith worth holding.

General Christian Apologetics: Best Books

Keller, Timothy. The Reason for God. (Intro) The single most accessible and intellectually respectable introduction to Christian apologetics for the thoughtful skeptic. Starts with the objections, works through the evidence, ends with the gospel.

Keller, Timothy. Making Sense of God. (Intro) A prequel to The Reason for God, written for post-Christian readers who have not yet found Christian claims worth considering.

Lewis, C. S. Mere Christianity. (Intro) The classic. Worth rereading every five years. Lewis's moral argument, his trilemma, and his argument from desire are all here.

McDowell, Josh. Evidence That Demands a Verdict. (Int) A massive compendium of historical, textual, and manuscript evidence. Reference work more than continuous read.

Strobel, Lee. The Case for Christ. (Intro) Strobel interviewing leading scholars on the historical case for Jesus. Accessible and engaging.

Turek, Frank, and Norman Geisler. I Don't Have Enough Faith to Be an Atheist. (Intro) Straightforward presentation of the evidentialist case.

Philosophy of Religion

Craig, William Lane. Reasonable Faith. (Adv) The leading defense of the Kalam cosmological argument. Dense but rewarding.

Plantinga, Alvin. God, Freedom, and Evil. (Int) The book that retired the logical problem of evil.

Plantinga, Alvin. Warranted Christian Belief. (Adv) A philosophical defense of the rationality of Christian belief without needing to prove it from neutral premises.

Swinburne, Richard. The Existence of God. (Adv) A Bayesian cumulative-case argument.

The Historical Case for the Resurrection: Best Books

Habermas, Gary, and Michael Licona. The Case for the Resurrection of Jesus. (Int) The minimal facts approach in book form.

Wright, N. T. The Resurrection of the Son of God. (Adv) The definitive scholarly defense of the historicity of the resurrection. Seven hundred and thirty-eight pages.

Licona, Michael. The Resurrection of Jesus: A New Historiographical Approach. (Adv) Methodology and evidence, treated as a professional historian would.

Bible Reliability: Best Books

Wallace, Daniel. Revisiting the Corruption of the New Testament. (Adv) Responses to Bart Ehrman on textual criticism.

Bruce, F. F. The New Testament Documents: Are They Reliable? (Int) A classic short introduction.

Blomberg, Craig. The Historical Reliability of the Gospels. (Int) A detailed examination of the Gospels as historical sources.

Bauckham, Richard. Jesus and the Eyewitnesses. (Adv) A landmark argument that the Gospels are the testimony of eyewitnesses, not the product of anonymous community traditions.

The Problem of Evil and Suffering: Best Books

Lewis, C. S. The Problem of Pain. (Intro)Lewis, C. S. A Grief Observed. (Intro) Lewis's own journal after his wife's death.Moltmann, Jürgen. The Crucified God. (Adv) A theology of the cross as God's answer to suffering.Wright, N. T. Evil and the Justice of God. (Int)Yancey, Philip. Where Is God When It Hurts? (Intro)

Science and Christianity

Collins, Francis. The Language of God. (Intro) The former director of the Human Genome Project on why he became a Christian.

Lennox, John. God's Undertaker: Has Science Buried God? (Int) Oxford mathematician on the science/faith intersection.

McGrath, Alister. The Foundations of Dialogue in Science and Religion. (Int)

Numbers, Ronald. Galileo Goes to Jail and Other Myths about Science and Religion. (Int) The warfare thesis dismantled by a leading historian of science.

Polkinghorne, John. Belief in God in an Age of Science. (Int) A former Cambridge physicist who became an Anglican priest.

Other Religions and Pluralism

Netland, Harold. Encountering Religious Pluralism. (Int)Newbigin, Lesslie. The Gospel in a Pluralist Society. (Int) Essential.Stackhouse, John. Humble Apologetics. (Int)Zacharias, Ravi. Beyond Opinion. (Int)

The New Atheism (and Responses)

Dawkins, Richard. The God Delusion. (Intro, opposing view) Read this if you want to know what the new atheists argue.

McGrath, Alister. The Dawkins Delusion. (Int) The most thorough scholarly response.

Keller, Timothy. The Reason for God. Chapters 1–7 address the common objections from this school directly.

Sire, James. The Universe Next Door. (Int) A map of worldviews, including the various flavors of atheism.

Christian Classics (Primary Sources)

Augustine. Confessions. (Int) Augustine's own conversion narrative and theological autobiography.

Lewis, C. S. The Great Divorce. (Intro) A literary meditation on hell, heaven, and the human will.

Bonhoeffer, Dietrich. The Cost of Discipleship. (Int) What following Jesus actually costs.

Pascal, Blaise. Pensées. (Int) Aphoristic brilliance from one of the greatest minds of the seventeenth century, written toward a book he never finished.

Chesterton, G. K. Orthodoxy. (Int) Chesterton's own account of arriving at Christian faith through a process of elimination.

Best Books for the Doubting Christian

Alcorn, Randy. If God Is Good. (Int)Gutenson, Charles. Reclaiming the Spiritual in Art. (Int)McKnight, Scot. The Jesus Creed. (Intro)Peterson, Eugene. Run with the Horses. (Intro)Tripp, Paul. Suffering. (Int)

One More

If you read only one book from this list, read The Reason for God by Keller. If you read two, add Mere Christianity. If you read three, add The Resurrection of the Son of God. These three cover more ground more rigorously than any three other Christian books currently in print.
