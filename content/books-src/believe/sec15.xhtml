APPENDIX B

Greek & Hebrew Glossary

A glossary of the key Greek and Hebrew words behind the argument — pistis, hypostasis, theopneustos, tetelestai, and more.

This appendix gathers the Greek and Hebrew words studied throughout Believe. Each entry gives the word in transliteration, the original language, a brief definition, and the chapter where it appears.

Key Hebrew Words

aman — to be firm, to support, to lean on; the root of our English "amen." Genesis 15:6 uses the verbal form for Abraham's faith. (Ch. 12)

bara — to create with purpose and order; used in the Hebrew Bible only of God. Genesis 1. (Ch. 7)

chata — to sin, literally "to miss the mark." An archery metaphor. (Ch. 9)

cherem — that which is devoted for destruction, particularly in the conquest narratives. (Ch. 11)

damah — to be like, to resemble, to imagine. Isaiah 40:25. (Ch. 11)

darash — to seek earnestly, to search, to inquire. Deuteronomy 4:29. (Ch. 8)

eved — servant, slave, bond-laborer; a broad term covering multiple socioeconomic categories. (Ch. 11)

ge-hinnom — Valley of Hinnom, a real valley south of Jerusalem; the source of the word gehenna. (Ch. 6)

hevel — vapor, breath, vanity; used thirty-eight times in Ecclesiastes. (Ch. 10)

natsach — perpetuity, forever; related terms are used in the context of God's word enduring. (Ch. 2)

olam — eternity, long duration. Ecclesiastes 3:11. (Ch. 10)

qoheleth — the Preacher or Teacher, the narrator of Ecclesiastes. (Ch. 10)

tachlit — purpose, end, completion. (Ch. 10)

yakach — to reason together, to argue a case, to present evidence. Isaiah 1:18. (Ch. 7)

Key Greek Words (Pistis, Hypostasis, Tetelestai)

agape — self-giving love that seeks the beloved's good without concern for reciprocation. John 3:16, 1 John 4:8. (Ch. 12)

aionios — eternal, age-long. Matthew 25:46. (Ch. 6)

akoloutheō — to follow, to walk behind. Verb used for discipleship. (Ch. 12)

apokatastasis — restoration, reestablishment. Acts 3:21. (Ch. 6)

aparneomai — to disown, to deny. Luke 9:23. (Ch. 12)

charis — grace, unearned favor, gift. Ephesians 2:8. (Ch. 9)

dikaioō — to declare righteous, to justify. Pauline usage throughout Romans. (Ch. 12)

dokimion — the process of testing metal by fire. James 1:3. (Ch. 5)

ego eimi — "I am." The Septuagint's translation of God's name from Exodus 3:14; used by Jesus in John 8:58. (Ch. 3)

ekenosen — emptied himself. Philippians 2:7. (Ch. 3)

ekzēteō — to earnestly seek, to search out. Hebrews 11:6. (Ch. 8)

elenchos — legal proof, evidence. Hebrews 11:1. (Ch. 11)

elthen eis heauton — "he came to himself," woke up. Luke 15:17. (Ch. 12)

en morphe theou — in the essential form/nature of God. Philippians 2:6. (Ch. 3)

gehenna — hell, derived from the Hebrew ge-hinnom. (Ch. 6)

graptos — written, inscribed. Romans 2:15. (Ch. 8)

hen — one, a unified reality. John 10:30. (Ch. 3)

hilasterion — propitiation, mercy seat. Romans 3:25. (Ch. 3)

hyperidōn — having overlooked, having tolerated. Acts 17:30. (Ch. 8)

hypostasis — substance, assurance. Hebrews 11:1. (Ch. 1)

kenosis — self-emptying (noun form of ekenosen). (Ch. 3)

logizomai — to reckon, to credit to an account. Romans 4. (Ch. 12)

martys — witness; source of the English "martyr." (Ch. 4)

mataioo — to render futile, to empty of meaning. (Ch. 10)

metamorphoō — to transform, to change form. Romans 12:2. (Ch. 12)

metanoeō — to change one's mind, to repent. (Ch. 12)

pistis — faith, trust based on evidence. (Ch. 1, 11)

proskuneo — to bow down, to offer the worship reserved for God. (Ch. 3)

sarx — flesh, the orientation of the self apart from God. Romans 7–8. (Ch. 12)

sympathesai — to suffer with, to feel the same suffering. Hebrews 4:15. (Ch. 5)

theopneustos — God-breathed, inspired. 2 Timothy 3:16. (Ch. 2)
