## Book Description (Amazon "Description" field)

The American church did not develop a race problem when Critical Race Theory arrived. The church already had one. CRT simply exposed it, and when people saw what was underneath, they panicked — some on the right, certain it was a Marxist assault on the faith; some on the left, certain it was the long-overdue reckoning. Both responses were panic. Neither was thinking.

James C. Bell refuses both. He sets out a fair summary of what CRT scholars actually claim before anyone agrees or disagrees, then tests it against Scripture. He shows where the Bible already names what CRT observes — corporate sin in Nehemiah and Daniel, the prophets' demand for justice, the goodness of ethnicity — and where CRT collides with the gospel's view of the human person, guilt, and reconciliation. Along the way he tells the church's own racial history honestly: the biblical defense of slavery, the segregation it chose, and the theology of the Black church that white Christianity ignored.

The gospel, Bell argues, demands more than any secular theory can offer and more than the church has been willing to give. This book is neither a defense nor a dismissal of CRT. It is an honest reckoning, and an invitation to a church that could actually lead.
