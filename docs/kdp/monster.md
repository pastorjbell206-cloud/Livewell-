## Book Description (Amazon "Description" field)

He kept a Bible on his desk, prayed before meals, and led his household in devotions. He also owned other human beings, and believed Scripture sanctioned it. He was not a monster. That is the point.

Every generation reads the Bible with total sincerity and gets parts of it badly wrong. The slaveholder, the German Christian who sang in church, the Pharisee who memorized the Law — each was certain he stood on the right side of God's Word. Each was wrong, and none could see it. The mechanism that fooled them is still running, and the most dangerous sentence a Christian can think is, "I would never have done that."

James Bell, a pastor who came to faith from atheism, writes for the reader brave enough to ask where that mechanism is operating in them. He examines how culture, fear, and tribe quietly rewrite Scripture; how both conservative and progressive churches build blind spots; and how the Bible itself supplies the prophets, the correction, and the humility we need. This is not a call to doubt your convictions. It is a call to hold them with both hands open. The face in the mirror is the one you have not yet examined.
