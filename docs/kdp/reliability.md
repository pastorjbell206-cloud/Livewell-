## Book Description (Amazon "Description" field)

A nineteen-year-old walked back into her church's college ministry and announced she was done. She laid out the arguments calmly — the manuscripts, the contradictions, the council that supposedly voted the Bible into existence — and nobody in the room could answer her. Not because her case was airtight. Because no one had ever been taught to think about the Bible rather than simply read it.

James Bell, who came to faith from atheism through the door of doubt, wrote this book for that student and for everyone who has stood where she stood. He takes the reader into the manuscript room and the archaeologist's trench: the thousands of New Testament copies, the Dead Sea Scrolls that closed a thousand-year gap, the Tel Dan inscription naming the house of David, the early creed in 1 Corinthians 15, and the historical case for the empty tomb. He answers the hard questions honestly — who decided the canon, whether scribes changed the text, what to do with apparent contradictions — without pretending the difficulties away.

The evidence does not force belief. It does something quieter and more durable: it clears away the casual dismissal and gives you warrant to build a life on this text. The questions deserve serious answers. This is the case.
