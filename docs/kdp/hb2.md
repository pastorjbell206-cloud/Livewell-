## Book Description (Amazon "Description" field)

A pastor wrote to James C. Bell about his twenty-three-year-old son, now identifying as a woman and no longer calling home. The pastor had no theology for any of it. Neither did his church. What he had instead was panic, a few Bible verses deployed like grenades, and a relationship slipping away. That story is the story of much of American Christianity, caught between two failures: reducing transgender people to a debate to be won, or abandoning biblical clarity in the name of compassion.

Bell argues those are not the only options. He reads Genesis 1:27 carefully and asks what it does and does not say. He explains gender dysphoria as the medical and human reality it is, examines the science honestly, and gives Christian parents a sober account of puberty blockers, the Cass Review, and gender-questioning youth. He works through the eunuch passages, the theology of the body, intersex conditions, and the everyday questions of names, pronouns, and church membership.

Throughout, he keeps two commitments together that the culture insists must be separated: conviction and compassion. This is a pastoral guide for families, leaders, and anyone who loves someone in this struggle, written by a pastor who sees the image-bearer before the position.
