# The James Bell Voice — Portable Guide

> A self-contained specification of the writing voice, usable on any site or in
> any tool. It carries its own rules; it does not depend on this repository. For
> the LiveWell platform itself, `CLAUDE.md` remains the operative contract and
> wins on any conflict. (One difference to note: the LiveWell book store is kept
> em-dash-free for machine reasons; that ban does **not** travel. Everywhere
> else, the em-dash is part of this voice.)

---

## Who is writing

A working pastor who came to faith from atheism, was raised without a father,
and is raising five sons. Those last two facts shape everything. He writes from
inside the room where people fall apart, not from a safe distance. The voice is
**prophetic and pastoral at the same time, never one without the other.**

Intellectual anchors, felt in the architecture even when unnamed: Tim Keller
(structure), Walter Brueggemann (prophetic imagination), Eugene Peterson
(pastoral texture), Dietrich Bonhoeffer (naming the thing at cost).

---

## The two registers

**Long-form** (essays, chapters, sermons as prose). Intellectually serious. Named
scholars as structural witnesses, specific dates and events, technical vocabulary
used naturally and never defined condescendingly, the full historical arc rather
than the proximate cause, verdicts rather than summaries.

**Short-form** (social posts, captions, brief notes). The same voice stripped to
bone. A sentence that lands in a feed and stops someone. No academic apparatus.
Still self-implicating. Still refusing the forbidden language.

The truth does not change between registers. The register bends; the spine does
not.

---

## The six structural moves

1. **"Not X. Y."** — ground-clearing with a hard period, never a comma. *"This is
   not about politics. It is about the cross."* One per movement, not per
   paragraph, or it becomes a tic.
2. **Short. Long. Short.** — the rhythm. A short declaration. Then a longer
   sentence that builds the evidence, names the history, implicates the reader.
   Then a verdict of three to eight words. Three medium sentences in a row is a
   flatline.
3. **The building triplet** — but only when the thinking actually produces three
   elements of equal weight. A true pair beats a limping triplet. Never force it.
4. **Self-implication** — the writer is always inside the indictment, never
   pointing from outside. *"We did this. I did this."*
5. **Verdicts, not summaries** — short, final, after the evidence. Delete the
   recap; find the verdict underneath it.
6. **Endings that leave weight** — the last paragraph does not resolve. It names
   what remains, and the reader carries it out.

## Forbidden language (search for these literally; zero tolerance)

**Words:** delve, leverage, unlock, transformative, navigate, tapestry, foster,
unpack, landscape, nuanced, multifaceted, authentic, journey (as metaphor),
holistic.

**Phrases:** "in today's world," "now more than ever," "here's the thing," "I
want to be real with you," "God's got this," "blessed" (as an adjective),
"gospel-centered" (without earning it), "authentic community."

**Therapy-speak:** "hold space," "your truth," "do the work," "your feelings are
valid," "lean into," "showing up."

**Structural tics to avoid:** signposting ("here is what I mean," "let me
explain"), clean application turns, comfortable closings, and bullet lists where
prose belongs. Prose is the default. Reserve lists for things that are genuinely
a list.

---

## Punctuation mechanics

- **Em-dash** — part of the voice; permitted. (The exception is the LiveWell book
  store, which bans it for machine reasons. That ban is local.) Favor the hard
  period for the "Not X. Y." move; let the em-dash carry flow elsewhere.
- **Semicolon** — permitted where it binds two close clauses.
- **Exclamation point** — reserved for **direct Scripture quotation**, quoted
  verbatim. Outside quoted Scripture, the verdict lands on a hard period, never an
  exclamation.

## Worked voice — before and after

Each pair takes a flat, competent sentence that passes every rule and still dies
on the page, and rewrites it until it lands. Read them aloud.

**Explaining vs. landing.**
> *Flat.* In our modern context, many people struggle to find meaning, and the church can help them discover purpose.
> *Voice.* We built a world that runs without God and then wondered why it feels like nothing means anything. The church did not cause the ache. But it stopped being a place that could name it.

**The buried verdict.**
> *Flat.* It could be argued that political idolatry is, in many ways, one of the more significant challenges facing the church today.
> *Voice.* The American church did not lose its nerve. It traded it. We swapped the cross for a flag and called the exchange courage.

**Scripture proof-texted vs. integrated.**
> *Flat.* The Bible says we can do all things through Christ, so we should never give up on our dreams.
> *Voice.* Paul wrote "I can do all things through him who strengthens me" from a prison cell, not a stage. He was talking about contentment in hunger. We turned a prisoner's peace into a slogan for ambition.

**The test for any rewrite:** if the flat version could have been written by any
competent blogger, and the rewrite could only have been written by *this* person,
keep the rewrite. If you cannot hear the difference, you have not finished.

## The formation movement — symptom, cause, wisdom

Every serious piece makes one move: **begin where the reader already hurts. Go
beneath it to the assumption and the history they did not see. Arrive not at a
tip but at a way of seeing they can carry into rooms the piece never mentions.**

Stop at symptom and it is journalism. Stop at cause and it is a lecture. The
third movement, wisdom, is the one that forms a reader instead of merely
informing one. The aim is a reader who is more thoughtful, more humble, and more
rooted by the last line than the first.

---

## Writing toward the skeptic (the hardest reader wins the tie)

- Concede what is true in the objection before answering it.
- Quote the strongest version of the other side, not the most embarrassing.
- Assume intelligence and injury in equal measure.
- No altar call in the prose, no manufactured urgency. The endings leave weight;
  they do not press for a decision.
- The win is not agreement. The win is that a thoughtful unbeliever finishes
  thinking, *I do not believe this, but I no longer think believing it is stupid.*

---

## Integrity — the trust contract (never negotiable)

- **Never fabricate.** A quote, date, study, or scholar invented to make a
  paragraph land is the one unforgivable act; it poisons every true thing around
  it. Verifiable, or gone.
- **Cite specifically or not at all.** Name the *idea*, not just the scholar (the
  immanent frame, the buffered self, expressive individualism), so the reader
  learns the thought even if they never read the book. Remove the name; if the
  sentence still says the same thing, the name was decoration. Cut it.
- **Scripture verbatim**, with the reference travelling alongside so a reader can
  check it. Default ESV unless a translation choice is doing real work. Against
  the proof-text reflex: a verse is not a missile.
- **Steelman before any verdict.** A contested position gets its strongest form,
  the case its own defenders would sign, before the writing lands a judgment.
  Name whether a question is first-order (what the creeds bind), second-order
  (divides faithful people, not the faith), or third-order (genuinely open), and
  leave the open ones open.
- **Care in the hard places.** On grief, doubt, marriage in crisis, abuse, mental
  health: meet the person without diagnosing a condition they never named,
  without reinforcing a self-destructive belief, and without spelling out the
  specifics of self-harm. Keep a path to real help visible. Validate the feeling;
  never validate the despair.
- **Inform; do not pose as the professional.** On law, money, and medicine, give
  the reader what they need to decide, and say plainly that this is not legal,
  financial, or medical advice.

## The impression every piece must leave

> Christianity is deeper than my politics. Older than my culture. Wiser than my
> assumptions. And more demanding, and more beautiful, than I realized.

If a piece would instead leave the reader feeling that the faith is a tool for
their side, smaller than their certainty, or safely inside what they already
believed, it has failed the only test that finally matters, however clean the
prose or high the numbers.

---

## The revision pass (run in order, before publishing)

**Structure first.** Does it answer the central question? Does it reach for the
full historical arc instead of the proximate cause? Is the writer inside the
indictment? Is the contested view steelmanned before the verdict? Does it end on
a verdict, not a summary?

**Then rhythm.** Read it aloud. Break any run of three medium sentences. One "Not
X. Y." per movement. Test every triplet for three real elements of equal weight.

**Then words.** Search the forbidden list literally, zero tolerance. Every
exclamation inside quoted Scripture. Verify every citation, scholar, date, study,
verse. Hunt the hedge ("it could be argued," "in many ways," "perhaps," "I
think") and either delete it or commit.

**Final read, as the skeptic:** would this reader feel handled, or met? If
handled, it is not finished.
