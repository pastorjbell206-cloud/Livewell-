# Tier-One Deep Audit — Teenager Losing Faith

- **Title:** When Your Teenager Says They Don't Believe Anymore
- **Slug:** `teenager-losing-faith`
- **Audience:** Seekers (parenting) and doubting Christians, with the skeptic reading over the teenager's shoulder
- **Verdict tier:** **SOLID** (11/12 — fails check 8 only; no failure among 3, 5, 6, 12)

---

## The twelve checks

**1. MISSION — PASS.** A crisis-facing parenting piece squarely aimed at the seeker ("This essay is for those three seconds"), and it does the platform's distinctive work: it takes the parent's practical emergency down to Taylor's secular age and back up to the prodigal's father. Depth connected to the weight of a Tuesday — here, a kitchen.

**2. ARC — PASS.** It refuses the proximate cause ("They are rarely reporting the conclusion of a careful philosophical inquiry") and pushes the origin back twice: developmentally (individuation, the relational test) and historically — Taylor's contested secularity, Bonhoeffer's prison wrestling, Augustine and Monica. The teenager's sentence is placed inside a 1,600-year arc.

**3. SELF-IMPLICATION — PASS.** Two moves keep the writer inside the indictment. The structural one: "The trouble is that most parents have never tested their own foundation, and a teenager can tell" — with the piece's whole authority resting on the author having been the runner, which the last line makes explicit: "The running is not the end of the story. It was not the end of mine." The writer indicts anxious parents while confessing he was the child who left. It passes — but this is the thinnest self-implication of the three tier-one pieces. Bell is raising five sons; one sentence of "I have loaded the rebuttal while my own son was still talking" would move the piece from *credible* to *unanswerable*. (Only if true. Never invent the anecdote.)

**4. STEELMAN — PASS.** Three contested things get their strongest form. The apologetics instinct: "You may even be right on every point. You will still lose" — concedes the arguments' truth before refusing their use. Deconstruction: "Some of it is [hostile]. But much of what gets called deconstruction is something older and far less alarming... Sometimes it is the only way an inherited faith ever becomes a personal one" — and it concedes the teenager may be right to demolish: "The faith your teenager is taking apart may be one they should take apart." The parent's grief is honored as real before it is disarmed. Nobody in the piece is a caricature, including the doubting child.

**5. VERDICT — PASS.** It ends on weight the parent must carry out: "It may not pass on your schedule, or in your lifetime, and you have to make your peace with a timeline that is not yours." Jude 22 lands mercy as the instruction, and the final self-disclosure leaves hope without resolution. Note: the section heading "## The verdict" announces the move — the one signpost in an otherwise unsignposted piece (logged under check 8).

**6. FORMS, NOT INFORMS — PASS.** The two-fears examination ("Are you afraid for their soul... or are you afraid of what their unbelief says about you") is formation in the strict sense — it changes who the parent is before it changes what the parent does, and it applies to every conflict the piece never names. No certainty trade: the parent leaves less armed and more humble. The reader's blind spot, not the culture's, is the target.

**7. RHYTHM — PASS.** "It is you." standing as its own paragraph; "He runs. He runs while the son is still a long way off"; "The running comes first. The running is the theology." Short-long-short is the piece's gait, and the verdicts land in under eight words.

**8. NO TICS — FAIL.** The "Not X. Y." move exceeds once per movement. Intro alone: "Not a slammed door, not a manifesto, not a scene" / "It is neither. It is a test." In the argument section: "They did not present a thesis. They presented a relational test" and "does not meet the returning son with a doctrine exam. He runs." In the grief section: "You are not the shepherd. You are, at most, the home the sheep remembers" alongside "The searching is God's work, not yours." Add the "## The verdict" heading — mild signposting, the move the voice guide names ("the last paragraph does not resolve"; announcing it spends it). Density lower than the other two tier-one pieces, but still past the rubric's ceiling.

**9. FORBIDDEN LANGUAGE — PASS.** Machine-scanned: zero hits. Notably, a piece about deconstruction avoids "journey," "navigate," "hold space," and "your truth" for 2,400 words — in this subject, that is an achievement.

**10. EXCLAMATION — PASS.** Machine-scanned: zero exclamation points.

**11. HEDGE — PASS.** Zero hits on the hedge list. "May" and "sometimes" are doing honest epistemic work about other people's futures, not evading the piece's own verdicts.

## The skeptic's paragraph

Met — with one watch-point. The skeptic reading this piece is the teenager, and the piece treats them with more respect than the genre ever does: their objections may be right ("a hypocrisy they spotted... a question nobody would let them ask"), their demolition may be necessary, and the parent is forbidden to weaponize grief against their honesty. The confession that arguments lose "even when they win" is precisely what a former atheist would know and a skeptic would verify. The watch-point: "rarely reporting the conclusion of a careful philosophical inquiry" could read, alone, as patronizing teenage doubt into a developmental phase — the deconstruction section substantially repairs this ("pulling on the beams to see what holds"), but a sharp seventeen-year-old will notice the first framing before reaching the repair.

## The seeker standard

PASS. The practical door is first, and it is the actual door: the kitchen, the flat sentence, the three seconds. The piece delivers usable words ("Thank you for telling me... I am not going anywhere") and a concrete discipline (listen to the end; ask which fear is driving) before any theology, and the theology deepens the parenting counsel rather than replacing it. No bait-and-switch — the parent who came for help with tonight leaves with help for tonight, plus the ten-year frame they did not know they needed.

## The care standard

PASS. This is a crisis-facing page and it behaves like one. It validates the parent's grief fully ("There is a real grief here, and you are allowed to feel it fully") without validating the despair; it forbids fear-driven tactics by diagnosis rather than shame (the two-fears passage); it refuses formulas explicitly ("You cannot script that moment"); and it never diagnoses the teenager with a condition ("underneath all of it, they are in real pain" is offered as one possibility among several, not a verdict). No timeline promises, no guarantee of return — "It may not pass... in your lifetime" is the honest sentence most pieces in this genre refuse to write. One nit: "the confident stranger whose entire reach is built on dismantling the faith of teenagers" flickers toward internet fear-mongering, but it is describing a real dynamic and the piece does not build on the fear.

## Note toward FLAGSHIP

Fails FLAGSHIP only on check 8. One pass — thin the "Not X. Y." stack to one per movement, retitle "## The verdict" (the verdict should land, not announce itself), and consider one true sentence of the author inside the parent's failure, not only the child's flight — and this is a 12/12 candidate. The last line already passes the authorship test: only a man who came to faith from atheism could end it there.
