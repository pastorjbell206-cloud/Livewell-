# Tier-One Deep Audit: Why Would Anyone Trust the Bible?

- **Title:** Why Would Anyone Trust the Bible?
- **Slug:** `why-trust-the-bible`
- **Audience:** The skeptic (audience #1) — squarely; the doubting Christian rides along.
- **Verdict tier:** **SOLID** (11/12; the single failure is check 8, a rhythm/tic failure, not a trust or formation failure)

This is the closest of the three to flagship. It fails 12/12 only on tics and
signposting — one editing pass on check 8 and a decision on the three citation
flags would put it in flagship contention.

## The twelve checks

1. **MISSION — PASS.** Written directly at the platform's hardest reader on the platform's central lane; the author's own atheism is the spine ("It was load-bearing for my unbelief").
2. **ARC — PASS.** Reaches past the meme-level objection to manuscript transmission, canon formation, and first-century onomastics — the full history, not this week's argument.
3. **SELF-IMPLICATION — PASS.** "I lived inside that story for years." / "That is the part I have to own. My unbelief had felt like the brave, clear-eyed option... It turned out to be the credulous option." The writer is the first defendant.
4. **STEELMAN — PASS.** "A perfectly transmitted false claim is still false." / "You can disagree with Bauckham — scholars do." / The "sincerely mistaken" position is granted as "coherent," and glib Christian harmonizations are called "genuinely bad." The objections get their strongest form before any verdict.
5. **VERDICT — PASS.** Ends on weight, not recap: "Most of the people most confident it cannot be trusted have done everything with it except that."
6. **FORMS, NOT INFORMS — PASS.** Teaches a transferable way of weighing testimony (branching transmission, periphery-vs-center divergence, the criterion of embarrassment) and refuses the certainty trade repeatedly ("None of this proves the resurrection, and I will keep saying that").
7. **RHYTHM — PASS.** Short verdicts land throughout: "It is a good plot. It is not history." / "A myth has no governor to name."
8. **NO TICS — FAIL.** Two infractions. Signposting, in bold: "**Here is what I will argue, and I will try to give the objections their full weight before I answer them**". And the "Not X. Y." move stacks four deep in the canon movement alone: "It is a good plot. It is not history." / "The canon was not decided by decree. It emerged by recognition, slowly, from below." / "They were not suppressed by a conspiracy. They lost because they arrived late" / "That is not the preface of a man inventing a religion. It is the preface of a man doing research and showing his work." Each is good alone; together in one movement the move becomes the tic the rubric names.
9. **FORBIDDEN LANGUAGE — PASS.** Machine-scanned: zero hits on words, phrases, therapy-speak.
10. **EXCLAMATION — PASS.** Machine-scanned: zero exclamation points.
11. **HEDGE — PASS.** No hedge does a verdict's job; qualifiers like "unlikely to get right" are epistemic precision, not flinch.
12. **CITATIONS — PASS**, with three flags (inventory below). Nothing fabricated; no quotation is attributed that cannot be sourced.

## The skeptic's paragraph

Mostly met. He concedes the things I came ready to make him concede — that perfect transmission proves nothing about truth, that Bauckham is contested, that "sincerely mistaken" is a position I'm allowed to hold at the end — and he calls the glib Christian answers bad before I can, which is the single most disarming thing a writer like this can do. The self-portrait of his own unbelief as secondhand folklore stung because it was aimed at himself first. Two places I felt handled: the Gallic Wars comparison reads like it walked in from an apologetics pamphlet, and citing 2 Peter as Peter's own eyewitness memo — in an essay whose whole method is "the scholarship doesn't support your story" — is exactly the move he spent five sections teaching me to catch. Fix those and I have nowhere comfortable to stand except the closing question, which I did not enjoy, because I have in fact been quoting the trailer.
