# Tier-One Deep Audit — The Slow Drift

- **Title:** The Slow Drift That Ends More Marriages Than Affairs Do
- **Slug:** `the-slow-drift-that-ends-marriages`
- **Audience:** Seekers (marriage), with the doubting Christian close behind
- **Verdict tier:** **CUT/REWRITE** — mechanically forced by a check-12 failure (a saying misattributed to Jesus). Everything else in the piece is SOLID-grade or better; this is one revision pass from being a corpus anchor. The rubric is explicit that a citation failure is automatic CUT-tier, and this one — putting Song of Solomon in Jesus' mouth — is exactly the error a Bible-literate skeptic would catch and never forgive.

---

## The twelve checks

**1. MISSION — PASS.** Squarely the fourth audience ("people seeking to live well — marriage"), and it serves the platform's refusal to separate depth from lived experience: the piece meets a Tuesday afternoon and finds covenant theology underneath it.

**2. ARC — PASS.** It refuses the proximate cause on principle — "You will not find one" — and pushes the origin back three times: to the mechanics of attention (Gottman's bids), to the Hebrew *shamar* in Genesis, and to Bellah's diagnosis of a culture that "treat[s] every bond as provisional." Symptom, then cause, then the covenant God who "kept turning toward a people who kept turning away." The full movement.

**3. SELF-IMPLICATION — PASS.** A whole section carries it: "I need to be honest about my part... There were only the moments where I chose the urgent over the present — the email over the eyes across the room... I did not betray anyone. I was simply not there, repeatedly, with a clear conscience." The writer is the drifter, not the diagnostician.

**4. STEELMAN — PASS (by default, and by temperament).** Nothing doctrinally contested. Where a reader's resistance is foreseeable — "we want repair to match the weight of the loss" — the piece states the desire fairly before refusing it, and it credits the drifter's self-account honestly ("The drifter feels like a hard worker") instead of caricaturing neglect as malice.

**5. VERDICT — PASS.** "The vineyard does not need a hero. It needs a keeper" is the verdict, and the final paragraph leaves the reader holding a live instruction rather than a resolution: "Pay attention to the small turning away. It is never small." The closing clause ("saved the same way, by a hand that finally refuses to let go") tilts toward comfort but is earned by the covenant argument and keeps the weight on the reader's own hand.

**6. FORMS, NOT INFORMS — PASS.** The reader leaves with a way of seeing — bids, keeping, the unwatered day — applicable to rooms the piece never names (friendship, prayer, a congregation). No certainty trade: the piece removes the villain the reader came looking for. No four-step box; the counsel is a posture, "the willingness to be present without applause."

**7. RHYTHM — PASS.** The opening pair is the register in miniature: "Most marriages do not die from a wound. They die from a draft." Long accumulating sentences land short throughout ("That is the drift." / "It worked.").

**8. NO TICS — FAIL.** The "Not X. Y." move stacks past once per movement. Worst in the *shamar* section, four in a few paragraphs: "To keep something is not to possess it. It is to stay awake over it." / "The opposite of keeping is not hatred. The opposite of keeping is neglect" / "We do not have an affair against our marriages. We simply stop keeping them." / "Not scandal. Not enemy." The intro also doubles it ("do not die from a wound... die from a draft"; "does not end because of a single Tuesday. It ends because of a thousand of them"). Each is strong alone; together the reader hears the machine.

**9. FORBIDDEN LANGUAGE — PASS.** Machine-scanned: zero hits (words, phrases, therapy-speak).

**10. EXCLAMATION — PASS.** Machine-scanned: zero exclamation points.

**11. HEDGE — PASS.** Zero hits on the hedge list. Qualifiers like "usually" are doing descriptive work, not evading a verdict.

**12. CITATIONS — FAIL.** One misattribution of Scripture to Jesus; full inventory below. Under the rubric this is the automatic CUT-tier failure: "every... Scripture reference is either verifiable or flagged." A false "Jesus said" is worse than an unverifiable one — it is fabricated authority at the highest possible level of authority.

## The skeptic's paragraph

Met. The theology arrives late, labeled, and earned — "There is a deeper reason the small turnings are the only cure, and it is theological rather than practical" — after the piece has already been true on purely observable ground. Gottman is taken at his strongest and neither baptized nor dismissed. A skeptical spouse could read the first four sections, disagree with the fifth, and still have received the whole practical argument. The one place the skeptic winces is the misattributed "Jesus said" — for the reader who knows the Song of Solomon, it poisons the surrounding trust exactly as the Content Integrity standard predicts.

## The seeker standard

PASS. The practical door is first and stays open: it opens inside the reader's search ("If you have been searching for why you and your spouse are growing apart in marriage"), and the counsel is concrete and unspiritualized — "You turn toward the sentence instead of the phone. You ask the question and stay for the whole answer." No bait-and-switch: the covenant theology deepens the practical answer rather than replacing it.

## The care standard

PASS. No fear-mongering and no formula. It validates without alarm — "fatigue does not feel like sin"; "They are not cruel people" — refuses a villain, refuses a grand-gesture fix, and explicitly dismantles the panic-purchase of repair ("we want repair to match the weight of the loss"). The one hard line — "The ones who frighten me are calm" — names real danger without diagnosing the reader, and the piece hands the calm couple a next step rather than a verdict.

## The three most important fixes

1. **Fix the misattribution (the decisive one).** Either re-source it — "The Song of Solomon knows the enemy of love is small: *the little foxes that spoil the vineyards* (Song of Solomon 2:15)" — or cut the foxes and let the swept house (Matthew 12:44), which *is* Jesus, carry the sentence alone. Also soften the adjacent overclaim "Drift is the spiritual condition Jesus named most often," which currently leans on the false citation for support.
2. **Thin the "Not X. Y." density.** One per movement. Keep "Most marriages do not die from a wound. They die from a draft" (the piece's spine) and "The vineyard does not need a hero. It needs a keeper" (the verdict); recast the mid-piece cluster in the *shamar* section into ordinary syntax.
3. **Tighten citation precision while the file is open.** Genesis 2:15 keeps the *garden*, not "the ground"; give Gottman his book (*The Relationship Cure*) so the strongest secular witness travels with its source; confirm the Bellah gloss stays clearly a gloss.

Re-tier after the pass: with check 12 repaired and check 8 thinned, this is a 12/12 candidate — the drift/keeping argument could only have been written by this author.
