# Tier-One Deep Audit — Preaching to a Divided Room

- **Title:** How to Preach to a Politically Divided Church
- **Slug:** `preaching-to-a-divided-room`
- **Audience:** Pastors (primary), with the doubting Christian overhearing
- **Verdict tier:** **SOLID** (11/12 — fails check 8 only; no failure among 3, 5, 6, 12)

---

## The twelve checks

**1. MISSION — PASS.** This is the platform's central territory — the church's political capture — written for its strategic audience. A pastor could hand this to another pastor; it survives both the personal read and the "can I use this" read.

**2. ARC — PASS.** It does not start at this week's outrage. It reaches to Acts 20 (the whole counsel), Amos, Nathan and David (2 Samuel 12), and Brueggemann's account of prophetic speech, and it names the deeper assumption: "the politicians and the broadcasters got there first and supplied the only frame the people have." The origin is pushed back to the frame, not the fight.

**3. SELF-IMPLICATION — PASS.** Explicit and early: "I have done both. I have preached the sermon that flattered my own instincts and called it courage, and I have ducked a text I should have preached and called it prudence." Renewed at the hinge: "Here is the discipline that holds the whole thing together, and it is the one I most often fail." And in the close: "every time I lend it mine, I dull the edge that was already there." The writer never leaves the indictment.

**Even-handedness count (decisive for this piece).** Indictments aimed at the right: the God who defends "the widow and the orphan and the immigrant"; Amos's hearers "trampling the poor in the dust while keeping their festivals scrupulously"; "the half that plays well with the donors"; "the indictment of injustice on the comfortable"; "the warning against the love of money on the rich man in the third row who funds the building"; the border-rigid man. Aimed at the left: "the God who will by no means clear the guilty"; "the texts that wound the left"; "the call to order on the rebellious"; the woman "gone rigid the other way." Tally roughly 6 right / 4 left — within range, and the two compassionate portraits (the frightened father, the wounded daughter) are exactly one per tribe, given identical grammar and identical mercy. The piece also states the symmetry test as its own discipline: "could a thoughtful member of either tribe walk out convinced I was secretly preaching for the other one." The instrument fires both directions. No failure here.

**4. STEELMAN — PASS.** Both failure modes are given their inside logic before the verdict: siding "feels, in the moment, like effectiveness"; silence is "called prudence"; "both feel like wisdom from the inside." The piece even steelmans its own method against abuse — the paragraph beginning "There is a detail worth pausing on" refuses to let Nathan become a technique for never landing the point: "The indirection bought him a hearing; the directness, once he had it, spent that hearing on the truth." That is the piece arguing against its own most likely misreading.

**5. VERDICT — PASS.** No recap, no application box. The final sentence names the cost and leaves it on the pastor's desk: "That is the only unity worth preaching toward, and it will cost you the easy applause every single Sunday you reach for it."

**6. FORMS, NOT INFORMS — PASS.** It refuses the certainty trade explicitly — the whole argument is that comforting one tribe "preach[es] him deeper into his capture." The year-long test ("does every section of your divided room get wounded, and does every section get comforted") is a way of seeing a pastor can apply to sermons the piece never names. No flattery loop: the pastor-reader is the one indicted.

**7. RHYTHM — PASS.** Short-long-short throughout. "Study what Nathan did, because it is everything." / "Not them. You." / "The sword is sharp enough." Long accumulating sentences land on short verdicts consistently.

**8. NO TICS — FAIL.** The "Not X. Y." move exceeds once per movement in at least two sections. In the whole-counsel movement: "Not the comfortable counsel. Not the half that plays well with the donors." / "It is not. It is editing God down to the size of a room" / "Those are not gentle words. They are the words of a man who expects his preaching to cut." In the Brueggemann movement, four in close succession: "did not win by stating the obvious more loudly. They won by reframing reality" / "The prophet does not win the room's argument. He changes what the room is able to see" / "He did not endorse a party. He pulled the roof off" / "He did not give them a position. He gave them a mirror." Each instance is good; the density turns the signature into a tic — the reader starts hearing the mechanism.

**9. FORBIDDEN LANGUAGE — PASS.** Machine-scanned: zero hits on words, phrases, and therapy-speak lists.

**10. EXCLAMATION — PASS.** Machine-scanned: zero exclamation points. (Note: ESV 2 Samuel 12:7 reads "You are the man!" — the essay drops the exclamation. Permissible restraint, but see citations note on verbatim.)

**11. HEDGE — PASS.** Zero hits on the hedge list. "You may have a pulpit" is earned humility at the end of a hard test, not a hedge doing a verdict's job.

**12. CITATIONS — PASS.** Full inventory below; nothing unverifiable, no invented quotation.

## The skeptic's paragraph

Met, not handled. The proof is the treatment of the two rigid congregants: "The man who has gone rigid about the border is often a father terrified for his children's future. The woman who has gone rigid the other way is often a daughter who has seen real cruelty and cannot bear to see it ignored." Intelligence and injury assumed in equal measure, on both sides, in parallel grammar. A skeptical reader — or a tribal one — finds their own position described from inside its fear rather than caricatured from outside its politics. The piece also never pretends the method is safe or that the author has mastered it, which is the honesty a skeptic checks first.

## Note toward FLAGSHIP

This piece fails FLAGSHIP only on check 8, and it passes the authorship test — the Nathan analysis and the "preach to the fear, not the position" turn could not have been written by a generic Christian blogger. One thinning pass on the "Not X. Y." density (keep the strongest instance per movement, recast the rest) would make it a 12/12 candidate.
