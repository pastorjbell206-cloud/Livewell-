# Tier-One Deep Audit: When the Church Is the Thing That Hurt You

- **Title:** When the Church Is the Thing That Hurt You
- **Slug:** `when-the-church-is-what-hurt-you`
- **Audience:** The doubting Christian who left over real harm (audience #2), crisis-adjacent; the skeptic reads over their shoulder.
- **Verdict tier:** **REVISE** (fails checks 8, 9, 11 — three failures, all fixable in one pass; plus a care-standard gap and quotation-precision flags that must be closed before distribution traffic)

The pastoral spine of this piece is the strongest of the three — the millstone
section and the forgiveness-is-not-silence section are the platform at full
strength. The failures are word-level and mechanical, but on a crisis-adjacent
page headed for traffic, word-level is not optional.

## The twelve checks

1. **MISSION — PASS.** Written straight at the wounded leaver with the skeptic's honesty standard; refuses the recruit-them-back move the genre defaults to.
2. **ARC — PASS.** Reaches past the reader's proximate wound to Ezekiel 34, Psalm 55, Matthew 18, and the mechanics of institutional power (Langberg, DeGroat) — the church has sat in this dark before.
3. **SELF-IMPLICATION — PASS**, thinly. "We have a sentimental Jesus in this country" and "something we have forgotten" put the writer inside the church's failure — but a pastor indicting the shepherd class never once stands inside it ("I have preached the forgiveness sermon too fast" would cost him something; nothing here does). Passes the rubric's letter; note for revision.
4. **STEELMAN — PASS.** The "not all churches" objection is named in its strongest suspicious form before the bride distinction is made ("I know how that can sound — like a dodge... the thing people say to get you back in the building"), and real forgiveness gets its full content before the weaponized version is condemned.
5. **VERDICT — PASS.** Ends on weight, asks nothing back: "he is, right now, more furious about what they did than you have ever let yourself be." No threshold, no timeline, no comfortable closing.
6. **FORMS, NOT INFORMS — PASS.** Hands the reader precision they can carry into rooms the piece never names: power inverted to protect the institution from the victim; forgiveness ≠ trust ≠ reconciliation ≠ silence. The flattery-loop risk (sanctifying the reader's anger) is checked by the bride distinction and "do not let them take Jesus too."
7. **RHYTHM — PASS.** Short verdicts land: "He picked up a millstone." / "That is the wound."
8. **NO TICS — FAIL.** Signposting: "What I mean is this." and "Here is what I will tell you." And the "Not X. Y." move runs more than once per movement — in the naming section alone: "this is not a relational misunderstanding. It is an abuse of power" and "Naming it accurately is not bitterness. It is the first honest thing"; in the forgiveness section: "That is not forgiveness. That is cover-up wearing forgiveness as a costume" and "forgiveness that is forced is not forgiveness at all. It is performance." Repeated at this density, the ground-clearing move becomes the tic.
9. **FORBIDDEN LANGUAGE — FAIL.** One machine-confirmed hit on the forbidden phrase list: "And here is the thing about timing: forgiveness that is forced is not forgiveness at all." Delete the frame; the sentence is stronger without it.
10. **EXCLAMATION — PASS.** Machine-scanned: zero exclamation points. (Note: the NIV's own exclamation in Ezekiel 34:2 was silently dropped from the quotation — that is a check-12 verbatim problem, logged below.)
11. **HEDGE — FAIL.** Hedges doing a verdict's job at the essay's two boldest moments: "The anger is, in many cases, the most spiritually accurate response in the room" and "In some real sense you were taking God's side against people who had stopped being on it" (with its twin, "She is, in a real sense, another of his victims"). Commit or qualify honestly; "in some real sense" is a flinch on the sentence the whole essay exists to say.
12. **CITATIONS — PASS**, with flags (inventory below). References are correct and every quotation is sourceable — but two quotations are not verbatim as presented, which on this platform is a trust matter, not a style matter.

## The skeptic's paragraph — and the care standard

I came braced to be recruited and I wasn't — no ask to walk back in, no timeline, no suggestion that my anger is the disease. The bride-versus-the-men-who-wore-her-clothes distinction is the move I would normally call a dodge, and he names that before I can say it, which bought him the right to make it. Where I feel handled: the essay leans its entire weight on "This is God speaking. This is not a disgruntled ex-member" — and then quotes God loosely, trimming a sentence and a punctuation mark without telling me. If the words are load-bearing, get them exact; I check.

**Care standard (crisis-adjacent):** Validates the feeling, never the despair — yes, and unusually well: the anger is legitimized, the leaving is dignified, and the piece still refuses to let the reader hand over Jesus with the building. Does not diagnose the reader with a condition they never named — the DeGroat section is careful ("the word is overused"). **But the path to real help is not visible.** The essay tells the reader they can "testify against him" and speak "loudly, so it is not done to the next person," yet never says where help lives: no mention of a trauma-informed counselor, of reporting abuse to civil authorities, of any concrete door besides the kitchen table and God. For a page that will meet actual abuse survivors under distribution traffic, that is a gap the house standard names explicitly ("Keep a path to real help visible").

## The three most important fixes

1. **Make every Scripture quotation verbatim or marked** (section "The shepherds who ate the sheep"; section "The friend, not the stranger"). Restore or ellipsis-mark the dropped NIV sentence in Ezekiel 34:4; keep or footnote the exclamation in 34:2; either quote Psalm 55:14 exactly or recast "*We used to walk to the house of God together*" as open paraphrase; name the translation once. The essay's authority rests on "This is God speaking" — the quotations must survive a reader checking them.
2. **Add one visible path to real help** (natural seam: the end of "What the abuse actually was — naming it precisely," beside Langberg and DeGroat). One or two sentences in Bell's voice: a trauma-informed counselor is not a detour from faith; abuse that was a crime belongs with the authorities, not only with God. This is the care-standard requirement, and it costs the prose nothing.
3. **One word-level pass on tics and hedges.** Delete "And here is the thing about timing" (forbidden phrase — check 9); cut the signposts "What I mean is this." and "Here is what I will tell you."; keep one "Not X. Y." per movement and convert the rest; commit on "In some real sense you were taking God's side" — either it is true and the hedge goes, or it is not and the sentence goes.
