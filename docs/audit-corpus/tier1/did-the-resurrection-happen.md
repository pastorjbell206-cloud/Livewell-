# Tier-1 Audit — Did the Resurrection Actually Happen?

- **Slug:** `did-the-resurrection-happen`
- **Audience:** the skeptic (lead)
- **Verdict tier:** **FLAGSHIP**

## The twelve checks

1. **MISSION — PASS.** The central question, answered for the hardest reader; the whole piece is built for the skeptic it names.
2. **ARC — PASS.** Reaches past "the Bible says" to the pre-gospel creed of 1 Corinthians 15 and the historians' minimal-facts method — the long evidential arc, not the proximate claim.
3. **SELF-IMPLICATION — PASS.** "I came to this evidence as an enemy of it." "I had thought my unbelief was the result of looking. It was the result of not looking, dressed up as looking."
4. **STEELMAN — PASS.** Hallucination, fraud, and wrong-tomb each stated in its strongest form ("The skeptic's explanations are not stupid, and treating them as if they were is how Christians lose the argument they think they are winning") before any answer.
5. **VERDICT — PASS.** Ends on weight, not recap: "The matter was supposed to be closed on a Friday. The strange thing — the thing that still has not stopped happening — is that it would not stay closed."
6. **FORMS, NOT INFORMS — PASS.** Grants the skeptic a respectable exit ("You may examine all of this and still conclude it did not happen") and indicts the lazy version of belief and unbelief alike — humility, not the certainty trade.
7. **RHYTHM — PASS.** Short declaration, accumulating middle, short verdict, sustained across sections.
8. **NO TICS — PASS.** The "Not X. Y." move is used sparingly, not per paragraph; no signposting.
9. **FORBIDDEN LANGUAGE — PASS.** Machine scan: zero hits.
10. **EXCLAMATION — PASS.** Machine scan: zero.
11. **HEDGE — PASS.** Concessions are deliberate ("Not a proof. Not a knockdown"), not hedges standing in for a verdict.
12. **CITATIONS — PASS.** All verifiable and accurately used.

## The skeptic's paragraph

I was met, not handled. He quoted Russell's own side of the ledger before he touched mine, conceded up front that this is "the best explanation" and not a proof, and told me plainly I could weigh it all and still walk away without being called a fool. The thing that lands is not the argument for the empty tomb — it is the sentence about my own posture: that I had decided the answer without doing the work and called the verdict reason. I can still disagree. What I cannot do is pretend he was selling me something.
