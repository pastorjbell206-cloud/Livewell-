# Tier-One Deep Audit: What If You're Done With Church but Not With Jesus?

- **Title:** What If You're Done With Church but Not With Jesus?
- **Slug:** `done-with-church-not-jesus`
- **Audience:** The dechurched believer — the doubting Christian first, the seeker close behind.
- **Verdict tier:** **REVISE** (fails checks 8 and 11 — the REVISE band — and two of its citation flags are load-bearing and must be closed before this page takes distribution traffic)

The architecture is excellent — symptom, cause, wisdom, exactly the formation
movement — and the coal image will outlive the essay. But its central statistic
is mis-attributed in a way a search engine exposes in one query, and its
emotional climax rests on a historical claim about Psalm 27 that cannot be
verified. Those are trust problems, and this audience is the one rebuilding
trust.

## The twelve checks

1. **MISSION — PASS.** Written at the largest real audience the platform has — the believing dechurched — on the exact seam of the platform's mission.
2. **ARC — PASS.** Refuses the proximate cause (bad churches, lazy Sundays) and pushes the origin back three centuries via Taylor: faith privatized, moved indoors, church reduced to accessory. "You are being a perfectly formed product of the last three hundred years."
3. **SELF-IMPLICATION — PASS.** "I would have stopped too." And the essay's best sentence of this kind: "I have been done with it too, and I am a pastor."
4. **STEELMAN — PASS.** The case for leaving gets its strongest form before any turn: "your faith... feels purer, more yours... and a lot of the time you are right, because the pew had become a performance and the walk is sincere." A leaver's own defender could sign that.
5. **VERDICT — PASS.** Ends on the ache and an open question, not an altar call: "whether the ache you keep lowering your voice to admit is, in fact, the oldest prayer in the book, asking to come home."
6. **FORMS, NOT INFORMS — PASS.** The reader leaves with transferable sight: the left/stayed-away distinction, the scaffolding insight, the coal physics — applicable to any solitary practice the piece never names. No certainty trade; the reader's faith is affirmed as real throughout.
7. **RHYTHM — PASS.** "That is the whole essay. Everything after this is me earning it." Sentence lengths vary; verdicts land short ("It went out the way fires go out when no one tends them").
8. **NO TICS — FAIL.** Signposting: "Now the gentle turn, and I mean gentle." and "Let me describe what I have watched happen." And the "Not X. Y." move stacks three deep in the closing movements: "That is not a contradiction. That is grief." / "that is not the residue of an old habit. That is Psalm 27 still running in your bones." / "That is not the fine print. That is the gift." Any one of them closes a movement; all three inside two sections is the tic the rubric caps at one per movement.
9. **FORBIDDEN LANGUAGE — PASS.** Machine-scanned: zero hits on words, phrases, therapy-speak.
10. **EXCLAMATION — PASS.** Machine-scanned: zero exclamation points.
11. **HEDGE — FAIL.** Three "I think"s carry the essay's central verdicts — the exact hedge the rubric names: "I think the dechurched but believing — and that is most of you reading this — are not running from God at all. I think a lot of you are running toward the God you found, and away from the people who handled him carelessly." And: "And I think the reason your faith feels lonelier than it used to is that Peter never said that line alone." Delete all three; the sentences are verdicts and should stand as verdicts.
12. **CITATIONS — PASS**, with four flags (inventory below) — two of them load-bearing. Nothing is fabricated; every quotation is sourceable; but two claims will not survive a checking reader as written.

## The skeptic's paragraph

The Taylor section met me — "you are a perfectly formed product of the last three hundred years" is the first time anyone has explained my position to me without insulting it, and the concession that the first year out really is better bought a lot of trust. Then I googled the study, because that is what I do, and the "two researchers" turned out to be two pastors, and "the largest study ever done" turned out to be the book's own jacket copy. That does not make the forty million wrong; it makes the essay careless in the one place it claimed the authority of research, and careless once is careless everywhere — his rule, not mine. The coal image is honest as description, though as argument it quietly assumes the church is the only fire. And the cave detail at the climax is invented certainty — the psalm does not say where it was written — which is exactly the kind of thing this platform keeps telling me it never does.

## The three most important fixes

1. **Fix the study's attribution** (paragraph two): Davis and Graham are pastors who commissioned the research (conducted by Ryan Burge and Paul Djupe); "largest study ever done" becomes their claim, not the essay's fact. This is the essay's central statistic and the first thing a checking reader verifies.
2. **Repair the Psalm 27 climax** (section "What you actually want"): "he wrote that line surrounded by the men who had refused to leave him in the cave" asserts a setting Scripture does not give Psalm 27. Either ground the companionship point in texts that carry it (Psalm 57 and 142 are cave psalms by superscription; 1 Samuel 22:1–2 gives David the men) or render the scene as open imagination. The point survives; the fabricated certainty must not.
3. **One word-level pass on hedges and tics:** delete the three "I think"s (check 11 — the sentences are verdicts); cut "Now the gentle turn, and I mean gentle." and "Let me describe what I have watched happen"; keep one "Not X. Y." in the closing movement and convert the other two; recast "*Do you want to leave too?*" so paraphrase is not dressed as quotation.
