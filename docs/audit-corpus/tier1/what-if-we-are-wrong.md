# Tier-One Audit — What If Christianity Is Wrong?

- **Title:** What If Christianity Is Wrong?
- **Slug:** `what-if-we-are-wrong`
- **Audience:** Doubting Christians first (the piece names its own search-bar reader: "the exact phrase a believer types into the search bar when the house is asleep"); skeptics read over the shoulder — and the piece addresses them directly in the wager section, which is where it gets into trouble.
- **Verdict tier:** **REVISE**

## The twelve checks

1. **MISSION — PASS.** The 2 a.m. question, asked all the way down for the doubting Christian — squarely the platform's central work.
2. **ARC — PASS.** Reaches past the night-doubt to Taylor's five-hundred-year account of the secular age and to the first-century tomb; it does not start or end at the surface.
3. **SELF-IMPLICATION — PASS.** "I have asked it… it is my own first language, and it never fully leaves"; "I lived in that house. I know how warm it stays."
4. **STEELMAN — FAIL.** The doubt itself is steelmanned beautifully (it "feels like clarity, like a curtain pulled back"), but secular morality is met only in its nihilist form, which its serious defenders would not sign. Failing sentences: "The secular story cannot ground a single one of these convictions, and its most honest voices know it." — secular moral realism is a live position held by serious philosophers, and it is never given its form before the verdict lands. And: "The atheist who weeps at injustice is a believer with cold feet." — a line the essay never earns against the strongest opponent, only against the borrowed-capital version of him.
5. **VERDICT — PASS.** "And what if, having asked it honestly, all the way to the floor, you find you cannot get the stone to stay in front of the tomb." Weight, not recap.
6. **FORMS, NOT INFORMS — FAIL** (certainty trade, final movement). The middle of the piece forms — hold the doubt out loud, let the sheltered faith die — but the close relieves the doubting reader's anxiety by making the other side dishonest by definition: "Both of you are betting your whole existence on something you cannot prove. Only one of you is honest about it." That is the certainty trade, and "The atheist who weeps at injustice is a believer with cold feet" is a line the tribe would cheer. It also contradicts the essay's own Taylor paragraph, which says the cross-pressure "only humbles everyone in the room."
7. **RHYTHM — PASS.** Sentence lengths vary; verdicts land short ("Let it." "He always works through the crack.").
8. **NO TICS — FAIL.** "Not X. Y." three times in the opening movement alone: "The question does not knock. It is already in the room." / "The question does not feel like temptation. It feels like clarity…" / "A faith that has never met the question has not been tested. It has only been sheltered…" — and twice more in the Thomas movement ("It does not banish the doubters. It builds on one." / "He did not wait for the man to resolve the contradiction. He worked through the crack."). Repeated at this density, the signature move becomes a tic.
9. **FORBIDDEN LANGUAGE — PASS** (with a note). A literal grep fires on "chose the powerless and called them blessed" — "blessed" as adjective, but functioning as a Beatitudes allusion, not the forbidden folk usage. Recommend converting it to actual quotation (Luke 6:20, verbatim ESV) in the revision pass, which removes the ambiguity and strengthens the sentence.
10. **EXCLAMATION — PASS.** Zero exclamation points. (See citations: the Mark 9:24 quotation actually *loses* its ESV exclamation point, which the rules permit inside verbatim Scripture — restore it or mark the line as paraphrase.)
11. **HEDGE — PASS.** No hedges doing a verdict's job; the verdicts commit.
12. **CITATIONS — PASS with 3 flags.** Nothing fabricated; three Scripture renderings are off the platform's ESV-verbatim-with-reference standard. Inventory below.

**Score: 9/12 (fails 4, 6, 8).** Per the rubric, any single failure among 3, 5, or 6 — and 2–4 total failures — lands REVISE. Fixable in one pass; the bones are strong.

## The skeptic's paragraph

For half this piece I was met better than I am met by most atheists writing about doubt — he grants that my position is reachable from his, quotes Taylor saying the wind humbles everyone in the room, and admits his own edifice looks like wishful thinking some mornings. Then the last movement takes it back. I'm told my ethics are a memory of his religion, that my weeping at injustice is cold feet, and finally that of the two of us "only one of you is honest about it" — meaning not me. I've read my Hume and my metaethics; I did the accounting he says the night conveniently hides, and I'd have signed his wager framing if he hadn't attached a dishonesty verdict to my side of it. He met me at the door and handled me at the exit. That's the part I'd screenshot, and not kindly.

## The three most important fixes

1. **Cut or rewrite the honesty verdict in "You cannot escape the wager."** The passage "Both of you are betting your whole existence on something you cannot prove. Only one of you is honest about it." indicts the skeptic wholesale, contradicts the essay's own Taylor paragraph ("the wind… only humbles everyone in the room"), and fails the platform's skeptic standard (the win is that they cannot accuse us of dishonesty — not that we accuse them). Keep the wager; drop the verdict on the other bettor. There are skeptics who state their side of the wager plainly — the essay's own opening grants them that honesty; let the ending keep granting it.
2. **Steelman secular moral grounding before the verdict in "The morality you cannot stop believing."** Before "The atheist who weeps at injustice is a believer with cold feet" and "The secular story cannot ground a single one of these convictions, and its most honest voices know it," give one paragraph to the strongest secular account of moral realism in the form its defenders would sign — then land Holland's claim as what it actually is, a genealogical argument about where the West's instincts came from, not a proof that no secular grounding can exist. The verdict survives the steelman; without the steelman it reads as caricature to the very reader the platform ranks first.
3. **One mechanics pass: thin the tic, fix the Scripture.** Reduce the opening movement's three "Not X. Y." constructions (quoted under check 8) to one. Normalize the three flagged Scripture quotations to verbatim ESV with references traveling — John 20:27, Psalm 13:1–2, and Mark 9:24 with its exclamation point restored inside the quotation — and consider making "called them blessed" an actual Luke 6:20 quotation while in the passage.
