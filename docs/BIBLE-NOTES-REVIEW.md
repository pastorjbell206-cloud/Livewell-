# The Study Bible's notes: review log

Every chapter note and book introduction is drafted to `docs/BIBLE-NOTES-SPEC.md`,
checked by `scripts/validate-bible-notes.mjs`, then read by a fact-checking
reviewer who corrects errors in place. This log records what each review
changed and the claims it left in place but flagged for James's team to
double-check against a primary source.

## Genesis introduction and chapters 1–14

**Corrected.** The introduction now says the classic Documentary Hypothesis
finds three of its four sources in Genesis, not two. Genesis 1: shemesh is
kin to the Mesopotamian sun god's name Shamash (not itself a god's name);
bara has only God as subject in its basic stem; the image and the ban on
carved images, and the "Us" of 1:26, are marked as interpretations. Genesis 3:
the chiasm no longer has God questioning the serpent. Genesis 5: Methusael
spelled as the BSB has it; the count of "and then he died" corrected; the
passing of a fallen nature marked as interpretation. Genesis 6: the section
begins at 5:1. Genesis 7: God speaks in verses 1–4, so shutting the door is
the only thing he is said to do beyond speaking. Genesis 11: Babel's meaning
attributed to the Babylonians; the ziggurat softened to "most interpreters".
Genesis 12: the oak of Moreh as an oracle tree softened. Genesis 14: Abram
adds the name LORD to Melchizedek's title; Salem "generally identified" with
Jerusalem.

**To double-check.**
- Genesis 4: Lamech's "seventy-sevenfold" and Matthew 18:22 share the same
  Greek number (Septuagint of Genesis 4:24).
- Genesis 10: "about seventy names" depends on how the table is counted.
- Genesis 12: the Beni Hasan tomb painting's date (about 1900 BC) and the 2014
  Aravah camel-bone study (Sapir-Hen and Ben-Yosef).
- Genesis 14: Psalm 110:1 as the Old Testament verse the New Testament quotes
  most often (tallies differ).
- Genesis 7–8: the flood's mirror structure (best known from Gordon Wenham);
  exact day counts vary between versions.

## Genesis 15–32

**Corrected.** Genesis 16: Hammurabi's law on a wife giving her slave woman
(§146) concerns a wife of a particular priestly class. Genesis 28: "Almighty"
for Shaddai comes mainly from the Latin Vulgate; the Greek uses it chiefly in
Job. Genesis 29: "the land of the people of the east" is the narrator's
phrase; Isaac's blindness, not darkness, made Jacob's deception possible.

**To double-check.**
- Genesis 25: the Nuzi tablet on selling an inheritance share (the Nuzi
  parallels' weight is debated).
- Genesis 31: the bride's father passing part of the marriage payment to his
  daughter rests on comparative evidence, not the text.
- Genesis 24: the camel dating (Sapir-Hen and Ben-Yosef, 2014) is recent and
  debated; it is presented as an open question.
