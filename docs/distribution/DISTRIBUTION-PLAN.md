# The Distribution Plan — Quarter One

> Output of the Distribution Engine board (`docs/DISTRIBUTION-ENGINE-PROMPT.md`),
> run against the actual corpus (313 published pieces in the content data, 23
> books). The three ready-to-post packs for the first three essays live beside
> this file. Everything here is held to the short-form register in `CLAUDE.md`:
> the same voice stripped to its bones, self-implicating, no forbidden language,
> no manufactured urgency.

---

## 1. The weekly cadence

One busy pastor. Four touches a week, all drawn from one essay, so the week's
work is one good decision, not seven.

| Day | Channel | What goes out |
|---|---|---|
| **Tuesday morning** | Substack | The issue: one essay, framed, linked. (The site already promises "new essays Tuesday morning" — the cadence keeps the promise.) |
| **Wednesday noon** | Facebook | Post 1 — the essay's confession or hook angle. |
| **Thursday morning** | PCN | The pastor note: two paragraphs, pastor-to-pastor, "hand this to your people" framing. Thursday because sermon prep is underway and Sunday is not yet on fire. |
| **Friday morning** | Facebook | Post 2 — the essay's hardest or most contrarian true sentence. |
| **Sunday evening** | Facebook | Post 3 — the pastoral angle, landing when the weekend's ache is freshest (and when pastors themselves are lowest). |

**Monthly variation:** the first Tuesday of each month, the Substack leads
with a book — the sample chapter, not a pitch — and the week's posts draw from
the book instead of an essay.

**The floor, not the ceiling:** if a week collapses, the Tuesday Substack is
the one thing that must ship. Everything else can be skipped without apology
and without doubling up later.

## 2. The transformation pipeline (the template)

For every essay, produce in this order:

1. **Substack issue.** Subject line that states the essay's claim plainly (no
   curiosity gap). An opening of 150–250 words that is *new writing in the
   essay's voice* — not the essay's first paragraph reused — which earns the
   click by giving something complete, then links: "The whole essay is here."
   One link, once. The book the essay funnels to gets one quiet line at the
   bottom, after the signature, never inside the body.
2. **Three Facebook posts, three different angles.** (a) The confession — the
   self-implicating opening. (b) The verdict — the essay's hardest true
   sentence, allowed to stand nearly alone. (c) The pastoral turn — written to
   the person carrying the thing, ending with the link. Each post is complete
   in itself; a reader who never clicks was still served. Quotes from the
   essay are verbatim or absent.
3. **The PCN note.** Two paragraphs, pastor-to-pastor. First: why this essay
   exists and who in their congregation it is for. Second: permission and
   provision — "hand it to them," with the link. Never "share this to grow the
   platform." The note passes the test from the constitution: would a pastor
   trust the platform with their congregation after reading it?

The Revision Pass applies to every line: forbidden-language sweep, no
exclamation outside verbatim Scripture, every quotation checked against the
essay, the skeptic read last.

## 3. The book path

Every essay already ends at a topically matched book on the site
(`KeepReadingBook`). Distribution respects the same order: the content serves
first, the book follows.

- **Substack:** one line after the signature — "This essay is part of the
  argument of *[Book]*. The opening chapters are free." Never in the body.
- **Facebook:** no book links in posts 1 and 2. Post 3 may carry the essay
  link only; the essay page does the book work.
- **PCN:** books appear only when the book is the tool — a sermon series, a
  study, a giveaway for elders. Pastors are a channel because they are served,
  and they stop being one the moment they feel used.

The rule that governs all three: **the content must be worth the reader's
time if they never buy anything.** If a caption only works as a funnel, cut it.

## 5. What this platform refuses

Named plainly, with the cost of refusing each, because each one works.

- **The curiosity gap.** "You won't believe what this pastor said" gets the
  click and spends the trust. Cost: slower growth. Paid gladly.
- **Outrage harvesting.** The algorithm pays for indignation, and a platform
  built against tribalism cannot take that money. Every post that would make
  one tribe cheer gets the same test as the essays: both sides, same
  instrument, same grief. Cost: virality. Paid.
- **Volume over weight.** Daily posting outruns the writing and trains
  readers to skim. Four touches a week, all from one essay, is the ceiling.
  Cost: feed presence. Paid.
- **Comment-section combat.** The essays argue; the account does not. Answer
  honest questions, thank honest objections, never win a thread. Cost: the
  dopamine of the dunk. Paid.
- **The bait-and-switch.** No post that promises help and delivers a pitch.
  The seeker who came for the marriage post gets the marriage help. Cost:
  conversion rate. Paid.

Depth first. Reach is downstream, or it is not worth having.
