## Substack issue — Tuesday

**Subject:** There is an atheist in my pulpit every Sunday

**Body:**

He is not a visitor slipping in at the back. He is me — the man I was for
most of my life, whose reasons I can still recite without notes.

Most conversion stories kill the earlier man off by the second paragraph. He
becomes a cartoon, a set-up, a before picture. I have never been able to do
that to him, because he was not a fool. His atheism was not a wound he was
nursing. It was a conviction he held the way serious people hold serious
things, and some of you reading this hold it now, and you can smell a setup
from a long way off.

So this week's essay does something I have not tried in print before. It puts
his reasons on the table at full strength — Russell, the crimes on religion's
ledger, the fatherless boy underneath the arguments — and then tells the
truth about what moved, slowly, against my preference. No checkmate. No altar
call. The essay ends with the only thing I know how to ask of the man I was,
and it costs nothing and risks everything.

Grace and peace,
James

## Facebook post 1 — Wednesday (the confession)

There is an atheist in my pulpit every Sunday morning.

He is not a visitor. He is me — the man I was for most of my life, whose
reasons I can still recite without notes, standing inside the man who now
preaches.

Most conversion stories have the earlier man dead by the second paragraph. I
will not do that to him. "He was not a fool, and if I treat him like one, I
will deserve the distrust of every reader standing where he stood."

Some of you are standing there now. This one is for you. [link]

## Facebook post 2 — Friday (the verdict)

A word to my unbelieving friends, from a man who was one of you far longer
than he has been this:

"Your arguments may be better than the arguments of the believers around
you. Mine were."

Nobody was ever loved into anything by a checkmate. I do not think you are
stupid, and the pressure you are braced for is not coming. [link]

## Facebook post 3 — Sunday evening (the pastoral turn)

I did not hold my atheism as a wound. I held it as maturity. After a
childhood built around a promise that was not kept, a universe that made no
promises felt like the first honest thing anyone had offered me.

If that is where you are tonight — certain, clean, and colder than you let
on — I wrote down what I could not have known then:

"I was, for years, standing in the clean cold, certain of one thing above
all the others: that no one was coming.

Somebody was watching the road." [link]

## PCN note — Thursday

Brothers — most of our people have someone they love standing outside the
faith, and most of what we hand them makes it worse, because it treats the
unbeliever as a fool waiting to be corrected. I was that unbeliever. My
reasons were good ones, and the essay I published this week puts them on the
table at full strength before it says one word about what changed. The
church's failures are in there too, uncorrected, because "they are facts the
believer needs to carry."

If there is a deacon's son who stopped coming, a wife whose husband sits in
the car, a college kid home with better arguments than the youth group ever
gave them — this is the one to hand them. It will not embarrass you, and it
will not corner them. That was the whole design. [link]
