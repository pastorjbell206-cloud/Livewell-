## Substack issue — Tuesday

**Subject:** God is angrier about it than you are

**Body:**

If you are reading this, there is a real chance you have a specific face in
your mind. A pastor. An elder. A congregation that closed around something it
should have exposed. And you have spent years being told, directly or by
implication, that the wound is your problem. That you are bitter. That you
left because you wanted to sin.

I am a pastor, and this week's essay says the thing I am afraid the church
has not said to you nearly enough: what happened to you was real. Your anger
is not your faithlessness. And the God you are afraid you have offended by
leaving is, on the matter of what was done to you, angrier than you are.

The essay walks through what Scripture actually says about shepherds who eat
the sheep — it is more violent than you would dare to be — and about the
difference between the bride and the men who betrayed her in her name. It
does not tell you to get over it. It does not give you a timeline. It was
written so that the people who hurt you do not get to keep the one thing they
were never standing between you and.

The whole essay is here: [link]

Grace and peace,
James

## Facebook post 1 — Wednesday (the confession)

Some of you have a specific face in your mind when you hear the word
"church." A pastor. An elder. A room you can still smell.

And you have spent years being told the wound is your problem.

I am a pastor, and I want to say what my profession has not said to you
nearly enough: "What happened to you was real. Your anger is not your
faithlessness." [link]

## Facebook post 2 — Friday (the verdict)

Read Ezekiel 34 sometime — God's own words about religious leaders who used
the flock instead of feeding it. It is angrier than anything you have ever
posted.

"The thing you have felt — the fury that someone in God's name used God's
people for their own appetite — is not your bitterness corrupting your
faith. It is the echo of God's own fury running through you." [link]

## Facebook post 3 — Sunday evening (the pastoral turn)

To the one who left, and meant it, and still aches on Sunday nights:

"You do not have to walk back in tomorrow. You may not be able to for a long
time, and a God who keeps millstones is not going to rush a wounded person
across a threshold. But do not let the people who hurt you do the one thing
they could not do while you were still in the room. Do not let them take
Jesus too. They took enough." [link]

## PCN note — Thursday

Brothers — every one of our churches has an empty seat that used to hold
someone another church wounded, and most of them will never tell us why they
flinch at the word "elder." This week's essay is for them, and I will be
honest: it is also a mirror for us. It takes Ezekiel 34 and Matthew 18 at
full strength — the shepherds who ate the sheep, the millstone — and it
refuses every reflex we use to hurry wounded people back through the door.

Hand it to the person who told you they are done with church. Not with a
follow-up question, not with an invitation attached. Just the essay. It asks
nothing of them, which is exactly why it might be the first church thing in
years they can read to the end. [link]
