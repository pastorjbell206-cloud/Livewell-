# LiveWell Editorial Constitution

> This is the governing document for LiveWell by James Bell. It sits above
> every other brief, audit, and plan in this repository. `CLAUDE.md` governs
> voice, palette, and engineering; this document governs editorial mission.
> When a content decision is unclear, it is settled here. Everything else —
> articles, site improvements, SEO, visuals, series, newsletters, family
> content, the Blind Spots work, the Reconstruction Review — nests underneath
> it.

## The central question

Every article, page, series, image, category, and design decision must help
answer one question:

**How can Christians learn to live faithfully and wisely in a post-Christian
America while recovering the depth, wisdom, practices, and imagination of
historic Christianity?**

If a piece does not help answer this question, it is probably not central to
the mission. Evaluate everything against it.

## What LiveWell is

LiveWell exists to help Christians see clearly. Modern believers inherit
assumptions they never learned to recognize: political, cultural,
psychological, economic, individualist, consumer, national, even churchly.
Much of modern Christianity has been read through these lenses so long that
many mistake the lens for the faith.

LiveWell helps readers identify those lenses, examine them honestly, and
recover a deeper, older, more historically grounded faith.

The goal is not deconstruction. The goal is reconstruction. Not of a political
movement, a denomination, or a culture war. Reconstruction of Christian vision.

## The core thesis

The crisis facing American Christianity is deeper than politics, deeper than
Christian nationalism, deeper than progressive Christianity, deeper than
denominational decline or attendance. The deeper crisis is that many Christians
no longer recognize the assumptions through which they interpret Christianity.

The future does not belong to stronger tribes. It belongs to deeper disciples.
Every piece of content should reinforce this.

## What success looks like

Not traffic. Not virality. Not outrage. Not platform growth. Those matter, but
they are not ultimate. Success means readers become more thoughtful, more
humble, more historically aware, more rooted in Scripture, more resistant to
ideological captivity, more faithful, and more able to see reality clearly.

## The five pillars (editorial frame)

The publication should be balanced across five pillars, each receiving roughly
equal investment, quality, depth, and editorial attention.

1. **Christianity After Christendom** — post-Christian culture, secularization,
   Charles Taylor, meaning, identity, belonging, the future of Christianity.
   *How do Christians live faithfully when Christianity is no longer culturally
   assumed?*
2. **The American Church and Its Blind Spots** — Christian nationalism,
   progressive Christianity, evangelicalism, political captivity, institutional
   decline, church history, cultural Christianity. *What assumptions distort the
   church's witness?*
3. **Discipleship and Spiritual Formation** — prayer, worship, Scripture,
   disciplines, habits, formation, virtue, holiness. *What kind of person is
   Christianity trying to create?*
4. **Marriage, Family, Parenting, Fatherhood, and Manhood** — marriage,
   parenting, family discipleship, masculinity, fatherhood, motherhood,
   technology, formation in the home. *How do Christian families remain faithful
   in a post-Christian society?* **This pillar is to be expanded significantly
   until it stands alongside every other pillar in depth, quality, and volume.**
5. **Pastoral Leadership and Church Renewal** — leadership, ministry, pastoral
   wisdom, revitalization, ecclesiology, church culture. *What kind of churches
   and leaders are needed for the next generation?*

> **Implementation note (open decision).** These five are the editorial frame
> for balance and investment. The *code* taxonomy spine is a separate, still-open
> decision: the site currently runs three overlapping taxonomies (the six-pillar
> `PILLARS_V2`, a legacy five-pillar set, and a 13-track layer — see
> `docs/audits/content-expansion-audit.md`). Reconciling these five editorial
> pillars with the code taxonomy has not been decided and must not be done
> silently. Until it is, balance and depth are judged against these five; routing
> and tagging continue under the existing system.

## Writing and article standard

Depth over speed. Never publish to fill a schedule. Prefer one excellent article
over five mediocre ones. Expand, deepen, strengthen, clarify, historicize,
theologize — but never merely lengthen. Add substance.

Every major article should carry: a compelling opening observation, a deeper
cultural diagnosis, biblical engagement, historical perspective, theological
reflection, practical implications, pastoral application, reader
self-examination, and a memorable conclusion. The article moves from symptom to
cause, from surface issue to deeper reality, from information to wisdom.

## Family content

One of the highest priorities. Treat it as theological formation, never
lifestyle content. The working line of inquiry: marriage after individualism,
parenting after Christendom, raising children in a secular age, family
discipleship, technology and formation, fatherhood, masculinity, the home as a
formative institution, emotional maturity, marriage and sanctification, sports
and school and screens and identity, family life as resistance to cultural
fragmentation.

## Visual identity

Every image reinforces the tone: editorial, thoughtful, human, historic, warm,
serious, grounded. Preferred: historic churches, families in ordinary moments,
natural landscapes, books, architecture, meaningful human moments, visuals that
communicate reflection rather than performance. Avoid: generic Christian stock,
megachurch aesthetics, artificial imagery, influencer polish, political
symbolism unless necessary. Every image should belong in a serious publication.

## The James Bell voice

Write as a pastor who diagnoses rather than denounces. Build by accumulation.
Steelman opposing positions. Challenge assumptions. Avoid tribalism, cheap
certainty, outrage, slogans, partisan talking points. Keep pastoral concern and
theological seriousness visible. The reader should feel: this person understands
what is happening, why it is happening, and what faithfulness requires.

(Binding mechanics live in `CLAUDE.md`: the forbidden-word list and the
punctuation mechanics — em-dash and semicolon are part of the voice; the
exclamation point is reserved for direct Scripture quotation.)

## Content decision filter

Before publishing anything, ask: Does this deepen understanding? Does this
strengthen discipleship? Does this reveal unseen assumptions? Does this help
readers live faithfully? Does this contribute to the larger LiveWell project?
Would this still matter five years from now? If not, revise or reject.

## The final test

Every article, page, image, category, recommendation, and series should leave
the reader with this impression:

> Christianity is deeper than my politics. Older than my culture. Wiser than my
> assumptions. And more demanding, and more beautiful, than I realized.
