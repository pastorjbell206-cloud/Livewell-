# The Voice Audit: Final Report of the Board

Eight seats read the corpus: books, essays, guides and curriculum, the leadership layer, the reference layer. Every quotation below is verbatim from the files as the seats captured it. Where an em-dash appears in this report, it appears only inside quoted offending text in the Excision Ledger, where the em-dash is the exhibit. Nowhere else.

One sentence before the findings. The man is real on every page, and a second, mechanical hand has been laid over roughly half of them.

---

## A. The Voiceprint

The voice, as the corpus actually reveals it, is prophetic and pastoral in the same breath, and it dies the moment it becomes only one of them. It is plain on the surface and load-bearing underneath: Otto, Bellah, Taylor, Chalcedon, the Sixth Ecumenical Council of 681, all carried without apparatus, the idea always named alongside the name. It implicates the writer before it indicts anyone. It reaches past the proximate cause to 312, to 1517, to 1845, and then walks the reader back to a Tuesday. It clears ground with a hard period, gathers evidence in one long sentence, and lands a verdict of three to eight words. Its endings do not resolve. They hand the reader something to carry out.

The corpus revealed two things the brief did not know. First, the voice survives the em-dash ban completely: the cleanest stores on the site, written without a single dash, are among the most alive pages in the library. The dash was never the voice. It was a pipeline. Second, the voice cannot be narrated. Wherever a guide says "Bell confesses" instead of "I confess," self-implication turns into a case study about a man named Bell, and the room goes cold.

Three exemplars, quoted exactly as the seats captured them. First, from Born Again From Atheism, chapter 5:

> "What did I do with Otto? Nothing, at first. That is the truthful answer, and it is the answer that best describes how a mind actually moves. I finished The Idea of the Holy, put it on the shelf, and went on being an atheist for years. If you had asked me at the time, I would have told you it was an interesting phenomenology weakened by its author's commitments. But something had shifted in the load-bearing structure, and I can name it now. Before Otto, my unbelief held the experiences at arm's length, comfortably labeled. After Otto, the labels required maintenance. Every time the ordinary tilted, every time the something enormous stood just behind the music, I heard a second voice alongside my own explanation, saying, structured like address, wholly other, dread and desire together, you have read the catalogue, you know this is not what noise looks like. I want to be careful not to dramatize. There was no voice. There was a category, standing open, that I could no longer close by calling it aesthetics, and a man can live a long time with a category standing open. But he lives differently. He lives like a man in a house where one door will not stay shut, who tells his guests it is nothing, the frame is warped, old houses do this, and who believes it less each time he says it, and who has begun, though he would deny it, to listen at night for what the door is for."

Second, from "Where Is God on Holy Saturday?":

> "Saturday is not Sunday either. This is the part we will not sit still for. [...] All of it may be true. None of it is Saturday. On Saturday the resurrection has not happened yet, and to a person living inside their Saturday, our Sunday talk sounds like someone narrating the ending of a film to a man who is still watching, in the dark, the part where the hero dies. We are not comforting him. We are refusing to be in the theater with him."

Third, from the closing of "The Constantinian Turn":

> "It has. Every generation since has faced its own version of 312, some throne or party or public offering the church protection in exchange for blessing, and the offer never stops sounding like providence. Sometimes it even is. The gains were real, and it is cheap to sneer at legal worship from inside a country that has never burned our Scriptures. But the ledger of the fourth century sits there to be read. The church gained the world's ear and began, slowly, to speak in the world's accent. It gained the power to stop persecution and used it, within a lifetime, to persecute. Nothing in that ledger says favor must corrupt. Everything in it says favor will try. The age of the martyrs asked the church whether it would die for its Lord. The age of Constantine asked a harder question, whether the church could hold power without worshiping it. We are still being asked. The answer is not yet in."

The test every one of these passes, and the flat pages fail: a competent Christian blogger could not have written them. A man who came to faith from atheism, was raised without a father, and is raising five sons could not have written them any other way. Not one of the three contains an em-dash, and not one misses it.

## C. The Cohesion Map

The sweep counted roughly 43,000 em-dashes and called it a corpus problem. The seats' close reading says otherwise. It is a pipeline problem, and the seam between the two hands runs down to chapters inside a single book.

**The clean hand.** The life domains (zero em-dashes across 70 files), the context guides (zero across 32), the history essays (zero), the table studies (zero), the theology library (3 across 50), the study guides (5 across 63), the 131 leadership articles (zero), 120 of the 132 how-tos, and three of the five audited books: Born Again From Atheism, Deep Roots, and Character Before Competence, all at zero. This hand quotes ESV with the reference traveling, ends on verdicts, and is not sterile. The context guides do the driest work in the library and are among the most alive pages on the site.

**The saturated hand.** The sermons store at 4,961 em-dashes, the largest single concentration in the corpus. The Hidden Life of a Pastor at 458. The marriage book's HTML chapters at 300, ten of thirteen. The twelve dm-* how-tos carrying all 83 of that store's dashes, along with contractions the other 120 files refuse. The published essays at 12 to 40 per piece, 3,835 across the database. The drafts folder at 27,427, and the two essays checked in both stores are byte-identical between draft and database, so one pass upstream cleans both. The servant-leadership handbook (181), the formation guides (166), whole-bible-sermons (117), and the creeds store's four confession summaries (paraphrase in the site's hand, not historic text) round out the account. The saturated hand travels with its own markers: HTML markup, unmarked NIV quotation, uplift closings, self-interrupting appositives. Whatever produced one produced them all. Fix them as one batch.

**One internal seam the sweep could not see.** The study guides run three narrator conventions in one corpus: the skeptic guide and the leadership formation units speak as "I"; the pastoral-health guide speaks entirely about a man named Bell; marriage and doubt blend the two, and the mask slips mid-paragraph, which proves the material was written to be spoken in the first person and then wrapped in a lecturer's frame.

**The correction the board owes the sweep.** The raw count charged the leadership library with 110 exclamation points, the most of any store, and the number read as pep. It is not. Every one was walked programmatically and each sits inside verbatim quoted Scripture: Job 2:4, Mark 4:39, Galatians 3:1, Psalm 139:23, and their kin, spot-checked against the ESV. The same holds for the 26 in the study guides. The rule says the exclamation lives only inside quoted Scripture. That is exactly where it lives, everywhere, without exception. The charge is withdrawn; the sweep's raw count overstated the tell, and no excision is needed.

**The reference voice, named.** The whole corpus should converge toward four bodies of work: the context guides, the Constantine essay, the leadership formation units, and Born Again From Atheism. They prove the same fact from four registers: the voice at full strength needs no dash, no bold, no narrator, and no template. The Reformation essay is the one warning inside the clean hand: mechanically spotless and, through its middle sections, written by no one. Subtraction alone is not the voice. The voice is a writer standing inside his own sentences.

## E. The Integrity Findings

The board's most important result comes first, stated plainly: no fabricated quotation and no invented citation was found anywhere in the corpus. Roughly a hundred and fifty dates, councils, figures, studies, and verbatim quotations were checked, from Otto 1917 to the Milvian Bridge to Lewis's Trinity Term sentence word for word, and the floor held. Where the writing did not know a number, it refused to invent one: "The studies vary and the definitions vary, but the floor of every honest estimate is sobering, and the ceiling is not high." That refusal is the trust contract kept, and it is worth more than any score in this report. What follows are the items that need a hand laid on them, each with its fix.

1. **The little foxes are not Jesus.** The marriage draft (`content/drafts/marriage/the-slow-drift-that-ends-marriages.md`) attributes the line to Jesus; it is Song of Solomon 2:15. The swept-and-empty house in the same sentence is genuinely Jesus (Matthew 12:44). Fix: attribute each to its source, per Ledger practice; the rewrite stands in seat report two.
2. **The Bonhoeffer line blurred toward Moltmann.** In `theology/impassibility.json`, "Only the suffering God can help" sits after "He says it plainly" inside the Moltmann steelman; the sentence is Bonhoeffer's, from the prison letters, quoted approvingly by Moltmann. The suffering life domain attributes it correctly, so the corpus disagrees with itself. Fix: "And he reaches for Bonhoeffer's line from a Nazi prison, that only the suffering God can help."
3. **The unnamed Wiesel.** The same page retells the gallows scene from Night without naming its witness. The most terrible scene in the literature does not get borrowed anonymously. Fix: name Elie Wiesel and Night, then let Moltmann answer.
4. **The SBC self-identification.** `context/guides/the-slavery-texts.json` says "My own denomination, the Southern Baptist Convention." If that is biographically true of James Bell, it is one of the bravest sentences in the corpus. If it is not, it is a fabricated self-implication, which is worse than none. Fix: confirm with James before anything else ships from this list.
5. **The parable arithmetic.** "Sixteen of his thirty eight parables turn on possessions" and the heaven comparison (`leadership/articles/money-and-the-church.json`) trace to popular stewardship literature; the tallies vary by counter and do not survive checking as stated. Fix: replace the arithmetic with the checkable list: the treasure in the field, the barns, the talents, the unforgiving debtor, the rich man and Lazarus.
6. **The unnamed Gottman.** The fight-fair how-to leans on "researchers who have watched thousands of couples" for four corrosive behaviors that are Gottman's own. Fix: name him and his actual finding, that contempt predicts divorce better than anger does, or drop the borrowed authority and let the pastoral observation stand on its own feet. Chapter 3 of the marriage book makes the same anonymous borrowing that chapter 10 corrects; name him in both.
7. **NIV where the house reads ESV.** The Hidden Life of a Pastor and the marriage book's HTML chapters quote NIV exclusively and silently, at least ten passages, and in the worst case the argument leans on the translation: Psalm 86:11 as "give me an undivided heart" where the ESV reads "unite my heart to fear your name." Fix: convert to ESV, or name the translation where its wording is doing the work. This travels with the em-dash batch; the same pipeline produced both.

Smaller items the seats logged, fixes in the seat reports: the Lewis paraphrase on petitionary prayer needs a named work or a loosened attribution; the Willard counsel is honestly secondhand through Ortberg and must not tighten into a quotation later; "Karl Barth, centuries later" reads wrong beside Bavinck; the Luther timeline conflates 1522 and 1525; the unanchored "1948" entry; the Mere Christianity broadcast dates; "It is love with nowhere to go" used as original prose under contested attribution; 1 Peter 5:7 and Proverbs 22:7 adapted without the reference traveling; and the money pages need a plain line that they are not financial advice.

## F. The Priority Fix List

Ranked. Each with the reason and the scale.

**1. The em-dash purge.** Roughly 43,000 instances: 27,427 in drafts, 6,245 in the books (458 in The Hidden Life of a Pastor, 300 in the marriage HTML chapters, zero in the three clean books), 5,425 in the leadership store (4,961 in the sermons alone), 3,835 in the published essays at 12 to 40 per piece, 83 confined to the twelve dm-* how-tos, about 30 leaked into the creeds store's confession summaries. The checked drafts and their database copies are byte-identical, so one pipeline pass covers both stores. First because it is the largest humanity defect, purely mechanical, and the clean stores prove the voice loses nothing in the cure. Batch it with the NIV conversion; the same hand made both marks.

**2. The "not just X" reflex.** 1,517 instances corpus-wide. It is the counterfeit of the ground-clearing move: the real move kills the false claim with a hard period and then states the true one; the counterfeit hedges both halves into one breath, and at this frequency it is the most audible machine cadence in otherwise human prose. The fix is always the same: commit to the second half and let the first die unmentioned.

**3. The narrated author.** 149 instances of "Bell says," "Bell confesses," "Bell has watched" across four study guides (59 in pastoral-health, 36 in marriage, 32 in doubt, 22 in skeptic). One find-and-recast pass to the first person, reserving the third for the leader primer and the promo kit, the two places a host genuinely speaks about him. The material already wants this; the mask slips mid-paragraph twice, and the two strongest guides in the corpus never wore it.

**4. The template parts bin.** The reused steelman scaffold opening three flagship essays; the bolded thesis in eight of ten essays at the same structural position; "at full strength" 25 times in one guide; "Then the reversal" 27 times across three; "Land it on X, not Y" stamped across three guides; sixteen identical "Hold X. Hold Y." triage closings; the farmer sentence and the honesty sentence verbatim in two life domains; the Lazarus tomb image in at least eight files. None of these is a bad sentence. Each is a fingerprint repetition is bleaching into a stamp, and the template is the one tell no word-counter catches and no reader unnotices. Ration each signature to one home; break the essay skeleton's fixed order.

**5. The integrity items of Section E.** Seven named, the SBC confirmation first because it gates a live sentence, the little foxes and the Bonhoeffer blur next because they are misattributions on the page today. Smaller in scale than everything above and heavier in kind: mechanics cost polish, sourcing costs trust.

**What does not need fixing.** The theology: the confessional floor held everywhere the board pressed, first-order lines hard and second-order questions left honestly open. The structure: symptom to cause to wisdom, executed in every register. The steelmans: read blind, the contested-doctrine pages do not reveal where the author lands. The endings: one marriage-essay exception, already in the ledger; the rest leave weight. The heart of this corpus needs no surgery. The surface needs a barber with a straight razor and one afternoon of nerve.

## G. The One-Page Style Bible

Hand this to any writer. Paste it into any tool. It is the whole standard.

**The voice in one paragraph.** Write as a working pastor who came to faith out of atheism, was raised without a father, and is raising five sons. Prophetic and pastoral in the same sentence, never one without the other. Plain on the surface, load-bearing underneath: name the idea alongside the thinker, date the turn, quote Scripture verbatim with the reference traveling. Implicate yourself before you indict anyone. Reach past the proximate cause to the full arc, then walk it back to a Tuesday. End on a verdict that leaves weight. Never resolve what the reader must carry out.

**The structural moves.** The ground-clearing correction on a hard period, once per movement, never once per paragraph. Short, long, short: a declaration, a sentence that gathers the history and pulls the reader inside it, a verdict of three to eight words. The triplet only when the thinking produced three things of equal weight; a true pair beats a limping three. Self-implication in the first person, always: "I did this." Confession cannot be narrated.

**Banned outright.** The em-dash, at zero; where you would reach for one, decide instead: period, comma, colon, or split. The correction cliche in every form: "not just X," "not only... but also," "more than just." Hollow uplift and abstraction nouns: delve, tapestry, landscape, realm, testament, beacon, symphony, unlock, foster, navigate, leverage, holistic, nuanced, multifaceted, transformative, resonate, elevate, embrace, empower, unpack, pivotal, "journey" as metaphor. Throat-clearing and signposting: "here's the thing," "let me explain," "it's worth noting," "in today's world," "now more than ever." The tidy wrap-up and the summary paragraph. Therapy-speak: "hold space," "your truth," "do the work," "lean into," "showing up." Unearned Christian filler: "God's got this," "blessed" as an adjective, "authentic community." Hedges that fear their own claim: "it could be argued," "in many ways," "perhaps," "arguably." Bolded thesis sentences. Announced turns ("Then the reversal"). Templates of any kind: a signature sentence lives in one place at full strength or it is a stamp.

**The humanity rules.** Burstiness: a two-word sentence beside a forty-word one, paragraphs of unequal weight, an expected rhythm broken on purpose. Specificity over abstraction: a named church, a real Tuesday, an actual number, a dated event. A position arrived at and defended, with the strongest objection named in the form its defenders would sign. The semicolon rarely, only where it truly binds two close clauses. A rougher human sentence beats a smoother machine one, every time.

**Scripture and sources.** ESV by default, verbatim, reference traveling with the quote; name the translation only when its wording does work. Cite specifically or not at all; no secondhand authority; paraphrase offered as paraphrase. If a quotation, date, or study cannot be verified, cut it. Inventing one is the single unforgivable act.

**The exclamation rule.** The exclamation point appears only inside a verbatim Scripture quotation. Nowhere else. Outside quoted Scripture, the verdict lands on a period.

**The final test.** Read the sentence aloud and ask one question. Could any competent Christian blogger have written it? Then it is not finished. Could only a man who came to faith from atheism, was raised without a father, and is raising five sons have written it? Then keep it, and do not sand it smooth.
