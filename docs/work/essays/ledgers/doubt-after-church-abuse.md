## Donatists and the Second Helvetic Confession

- North Africa, early fourth century, after the persecution under Diocletian, some clergy handed over copies of Scripture to the authorities to save their lives. Unverified this session (textbook: the "traditores"; Diocletian's persecution began 303).
- A party later called the Donatists held that baptisms and ordinations performed by such men were worthless. Unverified (textbook).
- Augustine of Hippo spent years arguing against them, on the ground that baptism belongs to Christ and not to the minister. Unverified as to "years" and the summary (textbook). Creeds.json confirms the confession's own statement that "Augustine, the blessed servant of God, many times argued from the Scriptures against the Donatists."
- The Second Helvetic Confession was written in the 1560s by the Zurich reformer Heinrich Bullinger. Creeds.json dates it 1562. Bullinger's authorship and Zurich: unverified this session (textbook; published 1566).
- The confession teaches that the sacraments remain effectual for believers even when unworthy ministers administer them. Creeds.json, chapter 18, section "EVEN EVIL MINISTERS ARE TO BE HEARD": "the sacraments ... are effectual to the godly, although they be administered by unworthy ministers." Paraphrased in the essay. Verified locally.
- Quotation: offenders who cannot be corrected "are to be deposed, and like wolves driven away" from the flock. Creeds.json, chapter 18, section "SYNODS": "Offenders who can be cured are to be rebuked by the elders and restored to the right way, and if they are incurable, they are to be deposed, and like wolves driven away from he flock of the Lord by the true shepherds." Verified locally. The quotation stops before "from he flock", a typo in that edition. This is the modern English translation the repository carries; the verifier may check it against the published translation.
