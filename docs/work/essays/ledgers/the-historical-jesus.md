## Berlin, 1910 (opening)

- The German Monist League (Monistenbund) held a public debate on Drews's *Die Christusmythe* in Berlin's Zoologischer Garten on January 31 and February 1, 1910. Search-checked (Wikipedia "The Christ Myth", citing the published lectures: "Berliner Religionsgespräch des Deutschen Monistenbundes am 31. Januar und 1. Februar 1910 im Zoologischen Garten").
- Arthur Drews was professor of philosophy at the Technische Hochschule in Karlsruhe. Search-checked (Wikipedia; Deutsche Biographie: habilitated there 1896, extraordinary professor 1899; English title page "Professor of Philosophy in the Techn. Hochschule, Karlsruhe").
- *Die Christusmythe* published 1909; argued Jesus was not a historical person. Search-checked (Wikipedia; Cambridge *Church History* article). The essay's gloss, "a figure of religious myth who had later been given a human story," is a general paraphrase of Drews's thesis; unverified as to his exact formulation.
- Bruno Bauer advanced a version of the idea in the nineteenth century. Search-checked (Wikipedia on Drews: "amplifying and publicizing the thesis initially advanced by Bruno Bauer").
- Drews carried his case from city to city; theologians answered him. Search-checked (B. B. Warfield, 1910, via monergism.com summary: "he made his progress from city to city, lecturing and debating"; Church History article: "a wide range of Protestant theologians attempted to refute Drews").
- For several years it was a public controversy in Germany. Search-checked (Cambridge *Church History* article title: "The Christ Myth Debate, Radical Theology and German Public Life, 1909-1913").

## Survey

- Barna, 2015: 92 percent of U.S. adults said Jesus was a real person who actually lived. Search-checked (barna.com "What Do Americans Believe About Jesus? 5 Popular Beliefs").
- Among the youngest adults surveyed (Millennials) the figure was 87 percent. Search-checked (secondary summary of the Barna report on bciowa.org: Elders 96, Boomers 95, Gen X 91, Millennials 87). Confirm on barna.com.

## Overstatements Christians make

- "The evidence for Jesus is better than the evidence for Julius Caesar" and "no serious person has ever doubted him" are described as claims some Christians make, and both are said to be untrue. Interpretive; no source cited. (Drews, a professor, doubted him, which disproves the second.)

## What is missing

- No document written during Jesus' lifetime mentions him; no inscription, coin, official letter or trial record; he left no writings. General scholarly consensus; unverified as phrased.
- Every surviving account of his life was written by people who believed he was raised. Interpretive generalization about narrative accounts (Josephus and Tacitus are not accounts of his life); unverified.
- Mark usually dated around 70, some forty years after the crucifixion. Unverified (standard dating, c. 65-75; crucifixion c. 30 or 33).
- Paul says little about where Jesus went, his miracles, or most of his teaching. Unverified (standard observation).

## Carrier

- Richard Carrier, *On the Historicity of Jesus*, 2014, Sheffield Phoenix Press, a biblical studies publisher. Search-checked (Wikipedia; richardcarrier.info).
- Uses probability theory (Bayesian reasoning). Search-checked.
- Argues the first Christians believed in a heavenly Jesus known through visions and Scripture, later given an earthly biography in the Gospels. Unverified (memory of his "minimal mythicism" model; search summary confirms only the minimal-mythicism vs minimal-historicity framing).
- Paul's near-silence carries much of the weight in his case. Unverified (characterization).
- Carrier grants that the near-universal judgment of qualified experts is against him. Search-checked (summary of Carrier's own framing: "near-universal consensus of well-qualified experts"). Paraphrased, no quotation marks.

## Pilate

- Pilate governed Judea for ten years (prefect c. 26-36). Search-checked (Pilate stone sources).
- His name appears in only one known inscription. Search-checked (Wikipedia/Madain Project on the Pilate stone: "the only known occurrence of the name Pontius Pilate in any ancient inscription").

- Context: Paul's first visit to Jerusalem after his conversion (Galatians 1:18). Unverified.
- Mythicists read "brother of the Lord" as a title for any believer. Unverified (memory of Carrier's argument).
- 1 Corinthians 9:5, "Do we not have the right to take along a believing wife, as do the other apostles and the brothers of the Lord and Cephas?" ESV from memory; unverified.
- Mark 6:3, "Is not this the carpenter, the son of Mary and brother of James and Joses and Judas and Simon?" ESV from memory; unverified. Context: the people of Nazareth take offense at him (Mark 6:3b). Unverified.
- James was a leader of the church in Jerusalem. Unverified (Galatians 2:9, Acts 15).

## Josephus

- Josephus was a priest's son, commanded Jewish forces in Galilee in the revolt, went over to the Romans, and spent the rest of his life in Rome writing his people's history. Unverified (memory of *Life* and *Jewish War*).
- *Antiquities of the Jews* has twenty books and was finished in the 90s (93/94). Unverified.
- Book 20: high priest Ananus acted between the death of the governor Festus and the arrival of Albinus, around 62. Verified locally for the narrative (Eusebius, *Church History* 2.23.21-24, NPNF2 McGiffert translation, quoting Josephus): "when he learned of the death of Festus, sent Albinus ... Ananus ... supposing that he had a favorable opportunity on account of the fact that Festus was dead, and Albinus was still on the way, called together the Sanhedrim." The year 62 is unverified (standard dating).
- Quotation: "brought before them the brother of Jesus, the so-called Christ, James by name, together with some others." Verified locally (McGiffert's translation of Eusebius's quotation of *Antiquities* 20.200). Whiston's translation reads "the brother of Jesus, who was called Christ, whose name was James"; the essay uses McGiffert.
- He had them stoned. Verified locally ("condemned them to be stoned").
- Most historians, including many with no Christian commitment, accept the James passage as genuine. Unverified (standard; e.g. Feldman, Meier).
- Quotation of the longer passage (*Antiquities* 18.63-64) as Eusebius quoted it: "a wise man, if indeed it be proper to call him a man" and "He was the Christ." Verified locally (Eusebius, *Church History* 1.11.7-8, McGiffert).
- Eusebius quoted it in the fourth century. Unverified (his *Church History* is usually dated to the early fourth century).
- Many specialists think Josephus wrote a shorter notice that Christian copyists improved; the argument continues. Search-checked in part (Feldman's view that the text is substantially Josephus with Christian alterations, via Wikipedia "Josephus on Jesus" summary). "Many" kept deliberately loose.

## Jesus son of Ananias (*Jewish War* 6.300-309)

- A countryman who began about four years before the war, at the Feast of Tabernacles, to cry out against Jerusalem and the temple. Search-checked (Whiston translation via lexundria.com summary: "four years before the war," Feast of Tabernacles, "a voice against Jerusalem and the holy house"). The essay drops the Tabernacles detail; "countryman" from memory of Whiston's "plebeian and an husbandman," unverified.
- Leading citizens had him beaten and brought him to the governor Albinus, who had him flogged until his bones showed. Search-checked (Whiston: "whipped till his bones were laid bare"; Schaff: "scourged till his bones could be seen").
- He would not answer; Albinus released him as a madman. Search-checked.
- He kept crying for years until a stone from a siege engine killed him during the siege. Search-checked (Josephus: a stone from a ballista; seven years and five months). "During the siege" of 70.

## Tacitus (*Annals* 15.44)

- Written early in the second century. Unverified (usually dated c. 115-120).
- The fire burned much of Rome in 64; Nero blamed the Christians. Unverified for the year (64, standard); Nero blaming Christians search-checked (passage context).
- Quotation: "Christus, from whom the name had its origin, suffered the extreme penalty during the reign of Tiberius at the hands of one of our procurators, Pontius Pilatus, and a most mischievous superstition, thus checked for the moment, again broke out." Search-checked (Church and Brodribb translation; multiple sites incl. earlychristianwritings.com, neverthirsty.org, and the Aquila Report quoting the "superstition" clause). The essay paraphrases the rest of the sentence ("spreading from Judea to Rome itself").
- Tacitus despised the Christians. Supported by the quoted passage.
- Skeptics argue he may have repeated what Christians said. Unverified (standard objection; not attributed).

## Celsus via Origen (*Against Celsus*)

- Celsus wrote late in the second century; his book is lost; it survives through Origen's reply. Unverified for date (usually c. 170-180); survival through Origen is standard.
- Celsus puts charges in the mouth of a Jewish critic. Verified locally (*Against Celsus* 1.28, ANF: "he introduces a Jew").
- Quotation "invented his birth from a virgin." Verified locally (1.28).
- Quotation "born in a certain Jewish village, of a poor woman of the country, who gained her subsistence by spinning." Verified locally (1.28).
- Husband a carpenter who threw her out for adultery; Jesus hired himself out as a servant in Egypt, acquired miraculous powers, came home calling himself a god. Verified locally (1.28 paraphrased; ANF: "turned out of doors by her husband, a carpenter by trade, because she was convicted of adultery ... hired himself out as a servant in Egypt ... acquired some miraculous powers ... proclaimed himself a God"). No quotation marks on the paraphrase.
- "The first pagan writer we know of who attacked Christianity at length." Unverified characterization (the early-church pilot calls his the earliest full-length attack that survives in any quantity).
- As far as the surviving sources show, no ancient opponent said Jesus never lived. Interpretive; unverified. Flag for the verifier (Justin, *Dialogue with Trypho* 8, is sometimes cited by mythicists, but it concerns the Messiah being unknown, not a denial of Jesus).

## Sanders and Ehrman

- E. P. Sanders, *Jesus and Judaism*, 1985, lists "almost indisputable facts" near the start of the book. Search-checked (Wikipedia "Jesus and Judaism"; TGC Themelios review).
- The list: baptized by John; Galilean who preached and healed; called disciples and spoke of twelve; confined activity to Israel; controversy about the temple; crucified outside Jerusalem by Roman authorities; followers continued as an identifiable movement; some Jews persecuted parts of it. Partly search-checked (baptism, Galilean preacher/healer, confined to Israel, temple controversy confirmed by review summaries; "eight facts" confirmed). The twelve, the crucifixion, the continuing movement and the persecution items are from memory of the book's introduction (p. 11), unverified.
- Sanders described as one of the most influential scholars of Jesus' Jewish world in the last century. Characterization; unverified.
- Most historians would add the kingdom of God, parables, reputation as healer and exorcist. Interpretive; unverified.
- Bart Ehrman, agnostic; *Did Jesus Exist?* (2012, HarperOne), written against the mythicists; concludes Jesus was a Jewish preacher crucified under Pontius Pilate in the reign of Tiberius. Search-checked (Wikipedia; publisher descriptions). Ehrman's agnosticism: unverified here (stated in why-trust-the-bible draft as well).
- The consensus holds across Jewish, Christian and secular scholars. Interpretive; unverified.

## The quests and the inventions

- Gotthold Lessing published fragments by Hermann Samuel Reimarus, a Hamburg scholar, after Reimarus's death (1774-78); Reimarus argued Jesus was a failed political messiah. Unverified (memory; also in the old site essay, which is not trusted).
- Nineteenth-century German lives of Jesus often set aside miracles and eschatology. Unverified (general).
- Albert Schweitzer published his survey in 1906, in Strasbourg, as a young theologian (aged 31). Search-checked in part (Christian History Institute: published at 31; 1906 date standard). Strasbourg unverified.
- Quotation (Montgomery translation): "each individual created Him in accordance with his own character." and "There is no historical task which so reveals a man's true self as the writing of a Life of Jesus." Search-checked (Wikiquote-derived listing, p. 4; Wikipedia "Quest for the historical Jesus").
- Paraphrase that the liberal Jesus "founded the kingdom of God on earth and died to consecrate his work" never existed. Search-checked (Schweitzer, "Results," p. 396: "The Jesus of Nazareth who came forward publicly as the Messiah, who preached the ethic of the Kingdom of God, who founded the Kingdom of Heaven upon earth, and died to give His work its final consecration, never had any existence.").
- Quotation: "He is a figure designed by rationalism, endowed with life by liberalism, and clothed by modern theology in an historical garb." Search-checked (Southern Cross Review full chapter; Logos/Faithlife and Biblia, p. 396).
- Schweitzer's own Jesus: a prophet who expected the imminent end and went to his death to hasten it. Unverified (standard summary).
- Bruce Barton, *The Man Nobody Knows*, 1925, Jesus as founder of modern business. Search-checked (Wikipedia; Encyclopedia.com; Christian History Institute).
- Barton was an advertising executive. Unverified (memory: co-founder of the agency later BBDO).
- Quotation: "picked up twelve men from the bottom ranks of business and forged them into an organization that conquered the world." Search-checked (Encyclopedia.com and Commonweal give this wording; no page number found).
- It topped the nonfiction best-seller list. Search-checked.
- Warner Sallman, *Head of Christ*, 1940. Search-checked (Wikipedia; Covenant Church; Calvin University).
- Description "soft-lit Jesus with light skin and long brown hair." Unverified (description of the image from memory).
- USO and Salvation Army handed out pocket-sized copies to soldiers in WWII. Search-checked (also the YMCA).
- Reproduced more than half a billion times by the end of the century, "by some estimates." Search-checked (Wikipedia: over half a billion by the end of the 20th century; Calvin: 500 million to 1 billion estimates).
- Jesus Seminar founded by Robert Funk in 1985; voted with colored beads, red for words Jesus surely said, black for words he did not. Search-checked (Wikipedia; Encyclopedia.com bead definitions). Note some sources name Crossan as co-founder.
- *The Five Gospels*, 1993, concluded most of the words attributed to Jesus were not his. Search-checked in part (Richard Hays's criticism cites "18 percent" as authentic; the book's own "82 percent" figure not reached, so the essay says "most").
- The Seminar's Jesus was a sage of subversive parables with little apocalyptic preaching. Unverified (standard characterization).
- Richard Hays objected that the votes came from a self-selected group with a shared point of view. Search-checked (Wikipedia "Jesus Seminar" summary of Hays). Paraphrased.
- Contemporary church portraits of Jesus (fighter who votes with the congregation; gentle progressive). General characterization, no source.
