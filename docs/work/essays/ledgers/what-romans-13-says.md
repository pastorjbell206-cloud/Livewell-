## The Case for Resistance

- Jonathan Mayhew, pastor of the West Church in Boston, preached the sermon on the Sunday after the anniversary of Charles I's execution, "1749/50", and it was published in 1750 as *A Discourse Concerning Unlimited Submission and Non-Resistance to the Higher Powers*. Mayhew TCP: title page ("the Substance of which was delivered in a Sermon preached in the West Meeting-House in Boston the Lord's-Day after the 30th of January, 1749/50"; "Pastor of the West Church in Boston"; "BOSTON, Printed and Sold by D. FOWLE ... 1750"). Verified. Note: the printed *Discourse* expands the sermon ("the substance of which"); the essay says "the sermon that became".
- Charles I executed January 30 (1649). Textbook. Unverified this run; the title page and preface tie the occasion to "the 30th of January".
- The Church of England kept January 30 as a solemn day in the king's memory. Mayhew TCP preface: "the near approach of the Thirtieth of January ... on which solemnity". Verified as Mayhew's description; the Prayer Book service for the day is textbook (unverified this run).
- Quotation: "the slavish doctrine of passive obedience and non-resistance, is often warmly asserted." Mayhew TCP preface: verbatim (comma as printed). Verified.
- Mayhew went through Romans 13 verse by verse. Mayhew TCP: the *Discourse* comments on the verses in order with marginal notes. Verified.
- Paraphrase: Paul's case for submission rests on what rulers are for. Mayhew TCP: "his reasoning, in order to show the duty of subjection to the higher powers, is ... built wholly upon the supposition, that they do, in fact, perform the duty of rulers." Verified as paraphrase.
- Quotation: "the apostle was no sycophant, or parasite of power." Mayhew TCP: verbatim, in the comment on "rulers are not a terror to good works" denying that Paul gives "the true character of Nero". Verified.
- Quotation: "Rulers have no authority from God to do mischief." Mayhew TCP: verbatim. Verified.
- Quotation: "It is blasphemy to call tyrants and oppressors, God's ministers." Mayhew TCP: verbatim (comma as printed). Verified.
- Paraphrase: every ruler is fallible; if a government's being ill-managed in some instances justified disobedience, no government could stand; what forfeits obedience is acting against the public good habitually. Mayhew TCP: "Nor is it sufficient to legitimate disobedience to the higher powers that they are not so administred; or that they are, in some instances, very ill-managed; for upon this principle, it is scarcely supposeable that any government at all could be supported"; and "This does not intend, their acting so in a few particular instances, which the best of rulers may do through mistake, &c. but their acting so habitually". Verified as paraphrase.
- Calvin allowed that magistrates appointed to restrain kings, like the Spartan ephors, would betray the people if they did not resist a king's tyranny (*Institutes* 4.20.31). Search: a scholarly abstract (pubs.ufs.ac.za) reports Calvin's insistence that the ephors were set up in Sparta to check kings and tribunes against consuls. "Would betray the people" is the writer's paraphrase of Calvin's wording (from memory: their connivance would be a betrayal of the people's liberty); unverified against the text.
- Calvin closed the *Institutes* with Peter's words (Acts 5:29). Search: Ligonier summary (chapter ends returning to Acts 5:29) and a forum quoting the final sections (Daniel 6:22). Verified by search summary.
- Jonathan Boucher, Anglican rector in Maryland, taught that obedience to government is commanded by God; preached with loaded pistols on the pulpit; sailed for England in September 1775. Search: Wikipedia "Jonathan Boucher", Mount Vernon digital encyclopedia, constitution.org title "On Civil Liberty, Passive Obedience, and Nonresistance" (summary: obedience "enjoined by the positive commands of God"; "his last sermons in America were preached while loaded pistols lay on the pulpit"; "He left for England in September, 1775"). Verified by search summary. The search did not confirm that Boucher's sermon quotes Romans 13 by chapter and verse, so the essay does not say so.
- Benjamin Franklin proposed (summer 1776, as a member of the seal committee formed July 4, 1776) a seal showing Pharaoh overwhelmed by the Red Sea with the motto "Rebellion to Tyrants is obedience to God." Search: greatseal.com (committee and reverse design), Founders Online note on "Bradshaw's Epitaph" (1775). Verified by search summary.
