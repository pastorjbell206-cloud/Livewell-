## John of the Cross

- Spanish Carmelite friar in the reform movement led by Teresa of Ávila. Source: EWTN, Britannica, Vatican saint-of-the-day pages; a Teresa letter about his imprisonment. Search.
- Seized in December 1577 (one source: the night of 3 December 1577) by friars opposed to the reform (Calced Carmelites / Carmelites of the Observance) and taken to their priory in Toledo. Search.
- Held about nine months. Source: Catholic Encyclopedia via search ("more than nine months close imprisonment in a narrow, stifling cell"). Search.
- Cell so small Teresa wrote he could hardly fit in it. Source: Teresa of Ávila letter via search ("in a little prison cell where small as he is, he could hardly fit"). Paraphrase. Search.
- Composed poetry in the cell (the Spiritual Canticle). Search.
- Escaped in August 1578 (sources differ: night of 16–17 or 17–18 August). Search. The essay says only "August 1578".
- In the years after his escape he wrote a poem about a soul going out on a dark night to meet the one it loves ("En una noche oscura"), then a long commentary on it (*Dark Night*, usually dated c. 1584–85). Unverified (textbook). The essay gives no dates for these.
- That commentary is where the English phrase "the dark night of the soul" comes from (the treatise's usual English title). Unverified (textbook).
- John's teaching: God withdraws sweetness in prayer to wean beginners from loving consolation more than God; beginners love the comfort of devotion. Paraphrase of *Dark Night* Book I (chs. 1–8). Partially supported by search summaries (Catholic Exchange "Aridity and Contemplation"; CCEL chapter summary). Not quoted.
- John gave signs for telling this night from other dryness, and warned that dryness can come from sin, lukewarmness, or a bodily disorder ("humours"). Source: *Dark Night* I.9 via CCEL chapter summary and Catholic Exchange (search summary: "aridity caused by sin, weakness, lukewarmness, or bodily humors"). Paraphrase only. Search. Not quoted, because the Peers text could not be opened.

## J. L. Schellenberg

- First set out the hiddenness argument (argument from reasonable or nonresistant nonbelief) in 1993 in *Divine Hiddenness and Human Reason*. Search (Wikipedia "Argument from nonbelief"; Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy "Hiddenness of God").
- Restated it in 2015 in *The Hiddenness Argument*. Search ("The Hiddenness Argument: Philosophy's New Challenge to Belief in God", 2015).
- The argument as summarized (a loving God seeks relationship with all capable persons; relationship requires belief; so no nonresistant nonbelief; there is nonresistant nonbelief; so no perfectly loving God). Paraphrase of the SEP and secondary summaries. Search.
- Schellenberg's reply to the freedom defense (belief in God's existence does not force love). Paraphrase. Unverified as to exact place in his writings, but a standard part of the debate (SEP covers it).
- The usual critics' route is to deny that nonresistant nonbelievers exist. Search (summary: "That premise ... is the usual target of critics").
