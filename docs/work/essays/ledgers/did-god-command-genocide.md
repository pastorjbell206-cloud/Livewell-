Method key. "WDB" means read verbatim on 2026-10-26 in files fetched from raw.githubusercontent.com/HistoricalChristianFaith/Writings-Database (NPNF, ANF and Benziger translations, except where noted). "Local BSB" means the reference and sense were cross-checked against the Berean Standard Bible text in client/public/bible/ch/ (not the ESV). "Local TAGNT" means checked against the STEPBible Greek tagging in the same files. "Memory" means quoted or stated from memory and NOT checked this session: the shared WebSearch budget (200 per turn) was exhausted before this essay began, and esv.org, digitalcommons.unl.edu and similar hosts are blocked by the sandbox proxy. ## Marcion, Faustus, Augustine, Aquinas, Origen

- Marcion, from Pontus, in the middle of the second century (Tertullian: "a heretic of the Antonine period," i.e. Antoninus Pius, 138–161). WDB (Tertullian, *Against Marcion* 1.1, 1.19, ANF).
- Marcion's *Antitheses* set the law against the gospel to argue for two gods. WDB (*Against Marcion* 1.19: "Marcion's special and principal work is the separation of the law and the gospel ... These are Marcion's Antitheses ... they may contend for a diversity of gods also"). Paraphrase.
- Marcion taught that the God of the Old Testament could not be the Father Jesus revealed. WDB (*Against Marcion* 1.2, two gods). Paraphrase.
- Marcion kept only an edited Gospel of Luke and ten of Paul's letters. Textbook (Tertullian, *Against Marcion* books 4 and 5). Not checked this session. Unverified.
- "The church rejected him." Textbook. Unverified this session.
- Tertullian wrote five books against Marcion. WDB (folder "The Five Books Against Marcion").
- Quotation: "that Christ Jesus is the revealer of none other god but the Creator." WDB, *Against Marcion* 1.19 (ANF; the source has a footnote marker after "revealer"). Verified verbatim.
- Creed confesses "one God, the Father almighty, maker of heaven and earth" (Nicene Creed). Paraphrase without quotation marks. Textbook.
- Faustus, a Manichaean bishop in North Africa (Faustus of Milevis), late in the fourth century. Textbook. Unverified this session.
- Quotation (Faustus): "destroyed thousands of men for a slight offense, or for nothing". WDB, Augustine, *Reply to Faustus* 22.4 (NPNF). Verified verbatim.
- Quotation (Faustus): "threatened to come with a sword and spare nobody, righteous or wicked." WDB, same passage. Verified verbatim.
- Quotation (Augustine): "not in cruelty, but in righteous retribution". WDB, *Reply to Faustus* 22.74 (NPNF: "God in giving the command, acted not in cruelty, but in righteous retribution"). Verified verbatim. The context is "the wars of Moses." Verified.
- Quotation (Augustine): "Is it the death of some who will soon die in any case, that others may live in peaceful subjection?" WDB, 22.74. Verified verbatim.
- Thomas Aquinas, thirteenth century. Textbook.
- Paraphrase: all, guilty and innocent, die by God's power on account of original sin. WDB, *Summa Theologiae* I-II q.94 a.5 ad 2 ("All men alike, both guilty and innocent, die the death of nature: which death of nature is inflicted by the power of God on account of original sin"). Verified.
- Quotation (Aquinas): "by the command of God, death can be inflicted on any man, guilty or innocent, without any injustice whatever." WDB, same reply. Verified verbatim.
- Origen preached through Joshua in the third century; the homilies survive in Rufinus's Latin translation. WDB file header (Baehrens, GCS 30). The essay no longer mentions the Latin; "third century" is textbook.
- Origen paraphrase: unless these physical wars were figures of spiritual ones, he doubted the apostles would have handed these histories to the church to be read by followers of a Christ who came to teach peace. WDB, *Homilies on Joshua* 15.1 ("Unless these carnal wars bore the figure of spiritual wars, never, I think, would the books of the Jewish histories have been handed down by the Apostles to be read in the churches by the disciples of Christ, who came to teach peace"). Note: this English is the repository's own public-domain translation from Baehrens's Latin, possibly machine-assisted; the essay paraphrases and does not quote it. Verified in substance.
- Origen read the enemies as sin and spiritual powers. WDB, same homily ("spiritual wickednesses in the heavenly places"; "the soul dies when it has received a wound of sin"). Verified.
- Ephesians 6:12 (partial) "For we do not wrestle against flesh and blood". Local BSB. ESV memory. Unverified verbatim. (Origen cites Ephesians 6 in the same homily. WDB.)
