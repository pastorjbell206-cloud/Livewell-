## The word "deconstruction"

- The term came from the French philosopher Jacques Derrida, who used it in the late 1960s for a way of reading texts against their own assumptions. Unverified (textbook; *Of Grammatology*, 1967). Note: "came from" is used rather than "coined" because the French word predates him.

## Nicaea and Chalcedon

- Around 318 Arius, a presbyter of Alexandria, began teaching that the Son was a created being. Unverified (textbook; dates given as 318-319).
- The teaching was summed up in the slogan "there was a time when he was not" (given without quotation marks), which the creed of 325 condemned by name in its anathemas. Unverified (textbook).
- The Council of Nicaea, 325, confessed the Son "begotten, not made," of one being (homoousios) with the Father. "Begotten, not made" quoted from the standard English translation of the creed of 325; unverified.
- The creed was expanded at Constantinople in 381 into the form most churches still say. Unverified (textbook).
- Chalcedon, 451: one person in two natures, truly God and truly man, without confusion, without change, without division, without separation (given without quotation marks; standard translation of the four adverbs). Unverified (textbook).
- The creed says God became flesh, was crucified under Pontius Pilate, rose on the third day, will come again to judge the living and the dead. Paraphrase of the Nicene Creed; unverified.
- Second-order list (baptism, millennium, women as pastors, Genesis 1) follows the platform's own classification in CLAUDE.md; no position asserted.

## Augustine

- Born 354 in Thagaste, in modern Algeria, to a Christian mother, Monica. Unverified (textbook).
- At nineteen he read a book of Cicero's (the *Hortensius*) that set him on fire for philosophy (*Confessions* 3.4). Unverified; check the age.
- He found the Bible crude beside Cicero (*Confessions* 3.5). Paraphrase; unverified.
- Nine years among the Manichees (*Confessions* 4.1). Unverified.
- The Manichees promised a religion of reason and mocked the Old Testament (*Confessions* 3.6-7; *On the Usefulness of Believing* 1.2). Paraphrase; unverified.
- Faustus came to Carthage and could not answer his questions (*Confessions* 5.3-7). Unverified.
- He drifted toward the Academic skeptics (*Confessions* 5.10, 5.14). Unverified.
- 384: moved to Milan to teach rhetoric; went to hear Ambrose to judge his eloquence (*Confessions* 5.13-14). Unverified.
- Ambrose showed that passages crude on the surface had a deeper sense and often repeated "the letter kills, but the Spirit gives life" (*Confessions* 6.4). Paraphrase plus ESV quote; unverified.
- 2 Corinthians 3:6, partial quote: "the letter kills, but the Spirit gives life." Unverified (ESV from memory).
- Ambrose received him kindly, like a father (*Confessions* 5.13, "received me as a father"). Paraphrase; unverified.
- Summer 386, garden in Milan, child's voice chanting "take up and read," Romans 13:13-14 (*Confessions* 8.12). "Take up and read" is the standard English rendering of *tolle lege*; unverified.
- Baptized by Ambrose at Easter 387 (*Confessions* 9.6). Unverified (textbook).
- "More than a decade" (from about age 19, 373, to 386). Arithmetic from the above.
- Preached the Bible for the rest of his life (bishop of Hippo from 395/396). Unverified (textbook).
- Monica: a bishop she begged to argue her son out of his errors told her to leave him alone for a while and pray, and said the son of so many tears could not be lost (*Confessions* 3.12). Paraphrase, no quotation marks; unverified.
