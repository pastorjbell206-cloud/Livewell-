## The Church's History

- George Whitefield, the best-known preacher of the First Great Awakening, campaigned to make slavery legal in Georgia. Verified. GITenberg, Tyerman vol. 2: Whitefield's letter to the Georgia Trustees, "GLOUCESTER, _December 6, 1748_," written when "the trustees were about to allow the employment of slaves in Georgia," argues "Georgia never can be a flourishing province, unless negroes are employed" and asks for "a limited use of negroes"; Tyerman vol. 1 says one public reason Mr. Seward, Whitefield's companion, returned to England was "To convince the Trustees of Georgia that three things were necessary to establish the Colony, viz.: 'an allowance of negroes, a free title to the lands, and an independent magistracy.'" Search (2026-10-27): the ban was lifted in 1751, partly through his campaigns and pleas to the Trustees (among the results: Wikipedia's Bethesda Academy entry, the Penn and Slavery Project's "George Whitefield", the Museum of Gloucester). "Best-known preacher": verified, Search (third close): the New Georgia Encyclopedia ("George Whitefield (1714-1770)") calls him the most popular preacher of the Evangelical Revival in Britain and the Great Awakening in America and the most visible figure of the Great Awakening; Britannica's biography says he played a leading part in the Great Awakening.
- Whitefield owned enslaved people whose labor supported his orphanage there (Bethesda). Verified. GITenberg, Tyerman vol. 2: his letter "it is impossible for the inhabitants to subsist without the use of slaves ... purchasing, in this province, a plantation and slaves, which I purpose to devote to the support of Bethesda ...
