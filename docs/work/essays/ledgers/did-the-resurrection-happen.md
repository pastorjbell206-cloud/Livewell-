# Claims ledger: did-the-resurrection-happen

Writer's ledger, 2026-10-26. "Search" means WebSearch result summaries (pages themselves not opened; esv.org, biblegateway, biblehub, davidhume.org and fordham were unreachable from the sandbox). "HCF" means the public-domain ANF text in github.com/HistoricalChristianFaith/Writings-Database, read locally from a sparse clone. Anything marked "unverified" is from memory and still needs checking.

## Dates, people, events

- Paul wrote 1 Corinthians from Ephesus in the mid-50s. Source: 1 Cor 16:8 (Ephesus); mid-50s is the standard scholarly date. Unverified (textbook).
- Some in the Corinthian church said there is no resurrection of the dead. Source: 1 Cor 15:12. Text checked from memory; see Scripture list.
- Jesus crucified around AD 30 or 33. Source: standard scholarly range. Unverified (textbook).
- Paul visited Jerusalem about three years after his conversion, stayed fifteen days with Cephas, saw only James of the other apostles. Source: Gal 1:18-19 (search summary of ESV text on biblia.com).
- 1 Corinthians written "some twenty years after the crucifixion." Derived from the two dates above. Unverified.
- Most scholars (believing and skeptical) think Paul received the 1 Cor 15:3-8 summary within a few years of the events. Source: general scholarly consensus (Ehrman, Dunn, Wright, Allison all date it early). Unverified; stated generally on purpose.
- Tacitus, who despised Christians, recorded the execution of Christus under Pontius Pilate early in the second century. Source: Tacitus, Annals 15.44 (written c. 115-117). Unverified (textbook).
- Bart Ehrman grants that followers came to believe very soon after the crucifixion that God had raised Jesus, and denies the belief was true. Source: Ehrman, Jesus: Apocalyptic Prophet (1999) and How Jesus Became God (2014). Paraphrase only, no quotation. Unverified.
- Ehrman, in How Jesus Became God, argued Jesus probably did not receive a decent burial / was not laid in a known tomb because Romans left crucified bodies or disposed of them as part of the punishment. Source: search summary of ehrmanblog.org posts (2014) describing the book's argument.
- Craig Evans answered Ehrman (Judea in peacetime; Josephus on pre-sunset burial; Yehohanan). Source: search summary of ehrmanblog.org posts and trinities.org podcast; Evans's essay "Getting the Burial Traditions and Evidences Right" in How God Became Jesus (2014). "Judea in peacetime" framing: unverified wording, from memory of Evans's argument.
- Ehrman replies that one case among hundreds of ossuaries proves little. Source: search summary of ehrmanblog.org (he cites "some 900 bone boxes"). Essay says "hundreds."
- Josephus says Jews took down even the crucified and buried them before sunset. Source: Josephus, Jewish War 4.317 (Whiston wording seen in search query/summary via lexundria.com). Paraphrase.
- 1968, construction crew at Giv'at ha-Mivtar, Jerusalem, broke into family tombs; ossuary of Yehohanan, young man (20s), iron nail through heel bone. Source: search summary (cojs.org, Wikipedia "Jehohanan", Biblical Archaeology Review). Tombs date 2nd c. BC to AD 70; essay says "from around the time of Jesus."
- Joseph of Arimathea "a respected member of the council." Source: Mark 15:43. Unverified ESV wording.
- The council the Gospels hold responsible for handing Jesus over. Source: Mark 15:1. Unverified.
- Josephus, summarizing the law, said women's testimony was not to be admitted. Source: Josephus, Antiquities 4.219. Paraphrase. Unverified.
- Celsus attacked Christianity late in the second century. Source: standard date c. 170-180. Unverified (textbook).
- Celsus quotation "A half-frantic woman, as you state." Source: Origen, Against Celsus 2.55, ANF (Crombie) translation, HCF file "Origen of Alexandria/Against Celsus/Book 2.html". Verified verbatim. Note: Celsus speaks through an imagined Jewish critic.
- Celsus quotation "had either dreamed so, owing to a peculiar state of mind, or under the influence of a wandering imagination had formed to himself an appearance according to his own wishes, which has been the case with numberless individuals." Source: same, Against Celsus 2.55, HCF. Verified verbatim (HCF text has the OCR typo "bad formed"; the ANF reads "had formed").
- Celsus quotation "is whether any one who was really dead ever rose with a veritable body." Source: same, Against Celsus 2.55, HCF ("But the question is, whether any one who was really dead ever rose with a veritable body."). Verified verbatim.
- Celsus compared the story to Greek and Egyptian legends of figures said to have returned from the underworld. Source: Against Celsus 2.55, HCF (Zamolxis, Pythagoras, Rhampsinitus, Orpheus, Protesilaus, Hercules, Theseus). Verified.
- Celsus's book is lost; it survives in Origen's point-by-point answer written around the middle of the third century. Source: standard (Contra Celsum c. 248). Unverified (textbook).
- In the oldest manuscripts, Mark ends at 16:8. Source: search summary (ESV note that some of the earliest manuscripts lack 16:9-20). Textbook.
- Matthew's guard story; "this story has been spread among the Jews to this day." Source: Matt 28:11-15. Unverified ESV wording.
- Justin Martyr, mid-second century, in Dialogue with Trypho, complained that men had been sent throughout the world to say the disciples stole the body from the tomb by night. Source: Justin, Dialogue 108, ANF, HCF file "Justin Martyr/Dialogue with Trypho.html" ("you have sent chosen and ordained men throughout all the world to proclaim ... his disciples stole him by night from the tomb"). Verified; paraphrased. Mid-second century date (c. 155-160) unverified (textbook).
- Many scholars doubt Matthew's guard story. General; unverified.
- W. Dewi Rees, Welsh doctor, 1971 British Medical Journal study, 293 widowed men and women, almost half reported experiences of the dead spouse, some for years; Rees thought them normal and helpful. Source: search summary of BMJ abstract (bmj.com/content/4/5778/37: 227 widows and 66 widowers; "almost half"; lasting years; normal and helpful) and Church Times obituary.
- Peter denied knowing Jesus on the night of his arrest. Source: Mark 14:66-72. Textbook.
- Ehrman thinks some disciples had visions and that resurrection belief grew from them. Source: How Jesus Became God (2014), ch. on the resurrection. Paraphrase. Unverified.
- Dale Allison, a New Testament scholar and a Christian, collected reports of apparitions of the dead including some to more than one person, and warns believers against claiming visions are always private; he judges the empty tomb (with hesitation) historically likely. Source: search summaries of reasonablefaith.org Q&A on Allison's Resurrecting Jesus (2005) and Church Times review of The Resurrection of Jesus (2021). The "warns believers" wording is my paraphrase of his critique of apologists. Unverified against Allison's text.
- Acts 12: Peter freed from prison, church praying, servant girl Rhoda. Source: Acts 12:5, 12-15. Unverified ESV wording except as listed below.
- N.T. Wright, historian and former Anglican bishop (Bishop of Durham 2003-2010), The Resurrection of the Son of God (2003), "some eight hundred pages," tracing the meaning of resurrection among Jews and pagans; argues empty tomb and appearances together (not either alone) produced the belief. Source: memory of the book (817 pp. in the Fortress edition). Unverified.
- Sadducees denied the resurrection. Source: Mark 12:18. Unverified ESV wording.
- Martha: Lazarus four days in the tomb. Source: John 11:17, 24. Unverified ESV wording.
- Firstfruits as first sheaf of the harvest. Source: Lev 23:10 background. Unverified.
- Simon bar Kosiba led a revolt against Rome in 132; Rabbi Akiva hailed him as messiah and named him Bar Kokhba, "son of the star"; killed at Betar in 135. Source: search summary (Wikipedia "Simon bar Kokhba", New World Encyclopedia).
- Maimonides (twelfth century, "a thousand years later") explained the sages believed he was the messiah until he was killed, and his death showed he was not. Source: search summary quoting "The Rambam explains that the sages of that generation were convinced that bar Kokhba was the Messiah. However since ... he was killed, the sages realized he was not the Messiah" (Mishneh Torah, Laws of Kings 11:3). Location of the passage unverified.
- James the brother led the Jerusalem church and was stoned there in 62. Source: Josephus, Antiquities 20.200 (search summary: Ananus II, between Festus and Albinus, c. 62). Note: a 2024 article (Nicholas List, JECS) argues "called Christ" is interpolated; most scholars hold the passage authentic. Leadership: Acts 15, 21:18; Gal 2:9 (unverified).
- Nobody called James the messiah. Wright's argument; unverified as phrased.
- Herod Agrippa killed James the brother of John with the sword. Source: Acts 12:1-2 (Acts says "Herod the king"; identified as Agrippa I). Unverified ESV wording.
- 1 Clement, a letter from the church in Rome to Corinth late in the first century, says Peter and Paul suffered martyrdom. Source: 1 Clement 5, ANF, HCF file "Clement of Rome/First Epistle to the Corinthians.html" ("when he had at length suffered martyrdom"; Paul "suffered martyrdom under the prefects"). Verified. Date (c. 96, some argue earlier) unverified; essay says "late in the first century."
- Swoon theory popular among rationalist critics two centuries ago. Source: memory (Bahrdt, Venturini, Paulus, c. 1780-1830). Unverified.
- David Friedrich Strauss answered the swoon theory: a half-dead man needing bandages and nursing could not have persuaded anyone he had conquered death. Source: memory of Strauss, A New Life of Jesus (1864; ET 1865). Paraphrase, no quotation. Search did not locate the passage. Unverified.
- Joseph of Arimathea knew where he had laid the body. Source: Mark 15:46-47; Matt 27:60. Unverified.
- David Hume, "Of Miracles," published 1748 (Section X of what became An Enquiry Concerning Human Understanding). Source: search summaries (davidhume.org E 10.4). Textbook.
- Hume quotation "A wise man, therefore, proportions his belief to the evidence." Source: search summary confirming the sentence at Enquiry 10.4 (davidhume.org; homepages.uc.edu PDF). Not opened.
- Hume's argument paraphrased (uniform experience; witnesses deceived or deceiving). Source: search summary of the Fordham sourcebook ("whether it be more probable, that this person should either deceive or be deceived"). Paraphrase.
- Hume's Queen Elizabeth example: all historians of England agree she died on 1 January 1600, seen by physicians and court, buried a month, reappeared and governed England three years. Source: search summary (stafforini.com quotes page; carneades.pomona.edu). Paraphrase.
- Hume quotation "should not have the least inclination to believe so miraculous an event." Source: search summary confirming the wording via stafforini.com. Not opened.
- Biography: "I was an atheist before I was a Christian." Source: NOTES.md (came to faith as an adult from atheism). Confirmed.
- Biography: evangelical churches are "the ones I know best." Source: NOTES.md (James pastors a Baptist church). No denomination named.
- Marcus Borg, widely read mainline scholar, co-wrote a book with Wright setting their views side by side (The Meaning of Jesus: Two Visions, 1999), treated the empty-tomb stories as parables whose truth did not depend on what happened to the body. Source: search summaries (LibraryThing listing of the book; Christian Century and bobcornwall.com reviews describing Borg's reading). Paraphrase. Partly verified (secondary sources only).
- Apostles' Creed confesses "the resurrection of the body." Source: client/public/creeds/documents/apostles-creed.json in this repo. Verified against site text.
- Nicene Creed: "we look for the resurrection of the dead, and the life of the world to come." Source: client/public/creeds/documents/nicene-creed.json in this repo. Verified against site text.
- Paul's "Let us eat and drink, for tomorrow we die" quotes Isaiah 22:13, words heard in Jerusalem. Source: memory of Isa 22:13 ESV. Unverified.
- Mary Magdalene at the tomb; Jesus said her name. Source: John 20:11-16. Unverified ESV wording (not quoted).
