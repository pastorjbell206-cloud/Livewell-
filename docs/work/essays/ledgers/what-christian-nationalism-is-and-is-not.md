Methods, in the words used below:

- "Search-checked" means the claim matched WebSearch result summaries run on 2026-10-26 (the sites named are the ones the results cited). No primary page was opened: esv.org, Wikipedia, amacad.org, orwellfoundation.com, libcom.org and similar hosts refused connection from the sandbox, and WebFetch could not resolve them.
- "Library-checked" means the passage was read in the ANF/NPNF text of the HistoricalChristianFaith Writings-Database (git clone, read with `git show`) on 2026-10-26.
- "Not checked (textbook)" means the claim was not searched; it is kept because it is standard reference knowledge, and it is marked so a later reader can check it.
- The run's shared web-search budget ran out before the last two planned searches (the Baylor survey year behind the four-group figures, and the year Augustine began *The City of God*). Both are handled below.

Not in scope here and left for the voice and audit step: the essay's em-dashes and the Closing Texts layout (the pilot predates the no-em-dash rule).

## Corrections made in the essay

1. Opening: "a few politicians now use the label about themselves with pride" became "a few public figures, a member of Congress among them, have claimed the label for themselves with pride." Search results confirmed one sitting member of Congress (Marjorie Taylor Greene, July 2022) plus non-politicians (Michael Flynn, Sean Feucht, Doug Wilson); "a few politicians ... now" was not confirmed, and Greene's July 2022 remarks are past, not current.
2. Sociologists: "chiefly the 2017 Baylor Religion Survey" became "chiefly the Baylor Religion Survey." Search results confirmed the six items were asked in the Baylor survey in 2007 and 2017; they did not confirm which wave the book leans on most.
3. Baptists: "Virginia jailed Baptist preachers for preaching without a license" became "Virginia jailed Baptist preachers who preached without a license." Search results show the formal charge was usually disturbing the peace, with unlicensed preaching as the underlying offense; the new wording states the fact without naming the legal charge.
4. Baptists: "the Baptists of Danbury, Connecticut" became "the Danbury Baptist Association of Connecticut." The 1801 letter came from the Association, not from the town's Baptists alone (search-checked: founders.archives.gov, au.org, Wikipedia "Baptists in the history of separation of church and state").
