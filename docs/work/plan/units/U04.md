## The notice, with its source

Text used (one constant, `client/src/lib/esv.ts`):

> Scripture quotations are from the ESV® Bible (The Holy Bible, English
> Standard Version®), © 2001 by Crossway, a publishing ministry of Good News
> Publishers. Used by permission. All rights reserved.

Source and method, stated exactly: Crossway's permissions page,
https://www.crossway.org/permissions/, **was not opened**. crossway.org,
esv.org, api.esv.org and static.crossway.org all fail from the sandbox (DNS
failure in the fetch tool; the agent proxy answers 403 to CONNECT). The text
was read on 2026-10-26 from web search results for that page (five searches,
some limited to crossway.org and esv.org). The search results gave "Scripture
quotations are from the ESV® Bible (The Holy Bible, English Standard
Version®), © 2001 by Crossway, a publishing ministry of Good News Publishers."
as the notice that "must appear as follows on the title page or copyright page
of printed works quoting from the ESV, or in a corresponding location when the
ESV is quoted in other media", and gave "Used by permission. All rights
reserved." as part of Crossway's credit line, but never showed the two as one
continuous verbatim sentence from the page. Variants seen in the same results,
not used: "Unless otherwise indicated, all Scripture quotations are from ...";
"copyright © 2001" for "© 2001"; an "ESV Text Edition: 2016" or "2025" line on
some Crossway Bibles.

**James: please open https://www.crossway.org/permissions/ and paste the
notice exactly as it stands there.** If it differs, `ESV_NOTICE` is the one
line to change (and its test's two checks).

Quotation limits noted at the same time (same method, same caveat): the ESV
may be quoted in print, digital and audio formats up to and inclusive of five
hundred (500) verses without a formal license or written permission, provided
the verses quoted do not amount to more than one half of any one book of the
Bible and do not account for 25 percent or more of the total text of the work
in which they are quoted, and not in a commentary or other biblical reference
work. Non-saleable media (bulletins, orders of service, posters) may carry
"(ESV)" after each quotation instead of the full notice. "ESV" and "English
Standard Version" are registered trademarks of Crossway. One result also said
the ESV text may not be quoted in a publication made available under a
Creative Commons license; another search could not confirm that sentence on
the page. Nothing in the essays approaches these limits.

## Which essays carry the notice

`owesEsvNotice(body, { rewrite })`:

- A **rewrite** (`client/public/essays/<slug>.json`) is written to the house
  rule (ESV verbatim, BRIEF), so it carries the notice once it quotes
  Scripture: a closing quotation mark (italics allowed) followed by a
  parenthesized chapter-and-verse reference, or a block quotation ending in
  one. The loose reference shape is a stand-in for U09's parser. Note for U05:
  the pilots' closing texts currently set the reference after a dash rather
  than in parentheses; once U05 rewrites them to `*"Quote."* (Reference)`
  (NOTES) they match too, and every pilot already matches through its in-text
  quotations.
- An **older database essay** carries it only where its own text names the ESV.

Why the second rule: the 15 live shelf essays were read from production
(read-only, `posts.getBySlug` through the Vercel connector, 2026-10-26). Eight
quote Scripture with a reference; none names a translation. Most of the
wording reads like the ESV (from memory, not checked), but at least one does
not: Amos 5:21-24 in `how-the-bible-gets-translated-by` ("let justice roll on
like a river") reads like the NIV. Putting Crossway's line under it would be a
false claim, and an agent cannot certify the others. So today **no live essay
shows the notice**; the pilots will, as soon as U05 adds them. The live
essays' translations belong to question 4 (rewrite or keep): a kept essay gets
the notice by naming the ESV in its text, or by a rewrite.
