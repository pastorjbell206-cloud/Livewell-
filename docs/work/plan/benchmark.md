# Benchmark and Audience Research

Prepared 2026-10-26 for the plan that answers James's direction of 2026-10-26
("tools and family devotions and topics that we need to deal with and truly
help people live in a post-Christian nation"), read together with his
2026-10-14 church history brief and the 2026-10-12 simplification. Companion to
`inventory-content.md` and `inventory-family.md` in this folder, which say what
LiveWell already has. This file says what readers need, who already serves
them well, and where the open ground is.

**Method and limits.** Web search only. Direct page fetches were refused by
this sandbox's egress proxy (barna.com, relevantmagazine.com, and every Google
property, including Trends and Keyword Planner), so no figure here was read off
a primary report page. Each number is given as the cited source reports it,
with the source named, and figures that reached me only through a press
release or a secondary outlet are marked *(secondhand)*. Under CLAUDE.md's
integrity rule, **no number in this file should be printed on the site until
someone has checked it against the primary report.** "Demand" is ranked from
converging proxies (surveys, platform data, sales, chart positions, book runs),
not from search volume, which I could not reach. The best real search data for
this site is its own Google Search Console query report and Vercel analytics;
pulling those is the obvious next step and would correct this ranking where it
is wrong.

## 0. The short verdict

The country James is writing to is post-Christian in its institutions and
still hungry in its people. Christian identity fell for fifteen years and has
now flattened at about six in ten; the young remain far less religious than
their parents. Yet Bible sales are at a twenty-year high, weekly Bible reading
jumped in 2025 (most of all among Gen Z and younger men), and the most-engaged
verse on the largest Bible app was "fear not, for I am with you" for the fourth
time in six years. Whether this is a revival is disputed by the most careful
sociologist in the field, and that dispute is itself something the site can
report honestly where others will not.

What readers ask for, by the weight of the evidence: how to read and trust the
Bible; what to do with fear, suffering, and loneliness; how to hand the faith
to their children; what phones and now AI are doing to their kids and to them;
whether Christianity is true after the deconstruction wave; what to do with
church hurt and scandal; how to be a Christian in politics without making the
flag a god; and, rising from a low base, where the older church went and how
to get back to it.

The best products each do one of these things very well. BibleProject owns the
Bible's story and owns it free. New City Catechism, Long Story Short, and the
Jesus Storybook Bible own the household's doctrine and story. Axis owns the
teen culture briefing. Ligonier owns the structured course, including church
history, inside one tradition. Holy Post and Christianity Today own honest
talk about evangelical politics and failure. Hallow and YouVersion own the
daily and seasonal habit.

None of them joins the Bible, the church's long memory, and the present
American fight in one place, at one level of honesty, for both the skeptic and
the family at the kitchen table. Nobody brings exile, the flag, politics at
dinner, and the church's own failures to children with Scripture and a witness
from church history beside them. Nobody teaches church history to laypeople as
an interactive, primary-source course with a family edition. Nobody reports
the "revival" and "Christian nationalism" numbers from both sides and lets the
reader weigh them. That is LiveWell's ground, and most of the raw material to
hold it is already in the repo.

## 1. What Christians and seekers in America need and search for

### 1.1 The moment, measured

**The decline has paused, not reversed.** Pew's third Religious Landscape Study
(36,908 adults, July 2023 to March 2024, released February 26, 2025) puts
Christian identity at 62%, down 9 points since 2014 and 16 since 2007, but
holding in a 60 to 64% band since 2019. The religiously unaffiliated have also
plateaued. Daily prayer (about 44%) and monthly attendance (about 33%) are
steady. Pew's own caution: the young remain far less religious than older
adults, so further decline is likely. ([Pew Trusts summary](https://www.pew.org/en/trust/archive/summer-2025/the-decline-of-christianity-has-slowed))

**Most Americans feel the loss, and half regret it.** In February 2024, 80% of
U.S. adults said religion is losing influence in American life, the highest in
Pew's surveys; 49% said it is losing influence and that this is a bad thing.
([Pew, 2024](https://www.pewresearch.org/religious-landscape-study/2024/03/15/8-in-10-americans-say-religion-is-losing-influence-in-public-life/))
That 49% is a large, under-served reader: people who are not sure what they
believe but sense something has gone missing.

**The counter-signals are real.**
- Bible sales rose 22% in 2024 (Circana BookScan; "a 20-year high") and about
  11 to 12% more in 2025, with a 36% one-month spike in September 2025.
  ([Christian Century](https://www.christiancentury.org/news/bible-sales-keep-growing-even-many-us-lose-their-religion), [Crosswalk](https://www.crosswalk.com/headlines/contributors/michael-foust/us-bible-sales-climb-again-2025-people-are-hungry.html))
- Barna and Gloo's State of the Church 2025 (12,116 online interviews, January
  to October 2025): weekly Bible reading rose to 42% of adults from 30% in 2024;
  Gen Z from 30% to 49%; Millennials to 50%; younger men now read Scripture more
  than younger women, reversing the historic pattern. ([Barna](https://www.barna.com/trends/bible-reading-trends/), [Gloo](https://www.gloo.com/press/releases/millenials-and-gen-z-are-driving-a-bible-reading-comeback), [RNS](https://religionnews.com/2025/11/06/new-study-shows-how-millennials-and-gen-z-are-driving-a-bible-reading-comeback/))
- The same series found men outpacing women in weekly attendance (43% to 36%),
  the widest gap Barna has tracked, and only 24% of single mothers attending
  weekly. ([Gloo](https://www.gloo.com/press/releases/new-study-shows-men-now-surpass-women-in-church-attendance-in-major-reversal), [ChurchLeaders](https://churchleaders.com/news/2206222-men-significantly-outpacing-women-in-church-barna.html))
- American Bible Society, State of the Bible 2025 (2,656 adults): Bible use
  outside church rose to 41% from 38% (about 10 million people); Gen Z
  "Scripture engagement" rose from 11% to 15%, Millennials from 12% to 17%. ABS's
  own John Plake: "It's not yet a trend." ([Scripture Engagement](https://scripture-engagement.org/content/state-of-the-bible-usa-2025/), [UCA Insights](https://insights.uca.org.au/?p=32404))
- YouVersion: more than 19 million people opened the app on the first Sunday
  of November 2025, an all-time high; nearly 19 million on Easter 2025; 1 billion
  installs across its apps in November 2025; more than 3 million subscribed to
  one-year plans on New Year's Day, up 18%. ([ChurchLeaders](https://churchleaders.com/news/2209898-youversion-2025-verse-of-the-year-bobby-gruenewald.html), [Christian Post](https://assets.christianpost.com/news/youversion-reveals-top-bible-verse-of-2025.html), [Crosswalk](https://www.crosswalk.com/11923935))
- Catholic dioceses report heavy increases in adults entering at Easter 2026,
  and Hallow reported campus OCIA participation up 87.6% year over year across
  nearly 60 colleges *(secondhand)*; Religion News Service notes the "young men"
  story varies by region and one official suspects a post-pandemic rebound.
  ([National Catholic Register](https://www.ncregister.com/news/catholic-converts-surge-us), [RNS](https://religionnews.com/2026/04/03/catholic-revival-among-gen-z-what-young-adults-say-about-returning-to-the-church/))

**The skeptic's reading of the same data.** Ryan Burge, working from the GSS,
the Cooperative Election Study, and Pew, says there is "zero convincing
evidence" of a Gen Z return: 42% of Gen Z was unaffiliated in the 2023 CES, the
same as Millennials; 38% of Gen Z never attends, against 31% of Millennials at
the same age. He also points out that Barna's headline frequency figure (1.9
attendances a month for Gen Z churchgoers) describes churchgoers only.
([ChurchLeaders](https://churchleaders.com/news/2211572-ryan-burge-no-gen-z-revival.html/2), [MinistryWatch](https://ministrywatch.com/is-america-having-a-revival/))
The UK's much-quoted "Quiet Revival" figures are from the Bible Society for
England and Wales, not the United States.
([Religion Media Centre](https://religionmediacentre.org.uk/news/the-quiet-revival-huge-increase-in-young-people-attending-church/))

**The frame most American Christians now use.** Aaron Renn's "three worlds"
(First Things, February 2022; *Life in the Negative World*, Zondervan, 2024):
a positive world before about 1994, a neutral world to about 2014, and a
"negative world" since, in which Christian conviction costs status. It is
widely used and widely contested: TGC's review calls it "too simplistic," and a
Baptist News Global critique argues the negative world is partly of
conservatives' own making. Renn himself calls it "a simplification ... designed
primarily for practical purposes." ([CT](https://christianitytoday.com/ct/2024/may-web-only/aaron-renn-life-negative-world-evangelicals-anti-christian.html), [TGC review](https://thegospelcoalition.org/reviews/negative-world-aaron-renn), [BNG](https://baptistnews.com/article/aaron-renns-three-worlds-and-the-negative-world-of-conservatives-own-making/))
For LiveWell this matters because the frame invites a grievance posture.
James's mission (the church that lived without power before and was faithful
anyway, with the writer inside the indictment) is the alternative to that
posture, and it is rarely offered.

**What this means for the reader we are building for.** Not a nation walking
out of church in a body, but one that has thinned out and gone quiet: curious,
anxious, reading the Bible more than it did two years ago, and badly
under-equipped to read it in context, to answer its children, or to tell
Christianity apart from the political tribe that borrows its name.

Three readings of the table matter for the plan.

First, **the top of the list is the Bible itself.** The single largest, best
documented demand is people reading Scripture and wanting help to understand
it. LiveWell's strongest owned asset, the Study Bible with chapter notes and 41
context guides, sits exactly on that demand, and the January 1 reading-plan
spike (3 million one-year plans on YouVersion alone) is nine weeks away.

Second, **fear is the emotional key of the moment.** The verse people reach
for most is a promise of presence against fear, and the question they type
most is about suffering. A post-Christian guide that leads with argument
before it has met fear will lose most readers on the first screen.

Third, **parents are the hinge audience.** They carry three of the top four
topics at once (the Bible, fear, and phones) on behalf of someone else, they
feel unequipped (86% in the Barna and Awana study), and the research says the
cheapest thing they can do, talking about faith at home often, is the thing
that most predicts their children keeping it.

## 2. The best products in the space, and what makes each work

Each entry: what it is, what makes it work, what it leaves undone. The
"leaves undone" lines are the openings section 3 collects.

### 2.1 Family devotion and catechesis

**New City Catechism** (TGC and Crossway; adapted by Tim Keller and Sam Shammas
from Calvin's Geneva Catechism, the Westminster catechisms, and Heidelberg).
Fifty-two questions, one a week; every answer in an adult and a short
children's form; kid songs for every answer with a background playlist; an ESV
reading, a short prayer, and commentary from pastors such as Keller, Piper, and
DeYoung. Free on iOS and Android. ([NCC](https://newcitycatechism.com/mobile-apps), [App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/new-city-catechism/id564035762))
*Works because:* one question a week is a bar any family can clear; two
registers let a six-year-old and a parent learn the same truth; music does the
memorizing; the source documents give it the weight of the Reformation.
*Leaves undone:* timeless doctrine with no word on the world the child walks
into; the app is modest in reach (176 App Store ratings); commentary is for
adults.

**Long Story Short** and **Old Story New** (Marty Machowski, New Growth Press).
Ten-minute devotions, five days a week, 78 weeks for the Old Testament and 78
for the New; a weekly rhythm of three days on the story, one on how it points
to Jesus, one on a related passage; each week keyed to the Gospel Story Bible
and the Gospel Story church curriculum; preschool through high school, with a
home supplement (78 assignments and coloring pages) for ages 4 to 12.
([New Growth Press](https://www.newgrowthpress.com/long-story-short/), [Goodreads](https://www.goodreads.com/book/show/9649138))
*Works because:* the time is promised on the cover; the parent is scripted, so
a nervous father can lead; church and home teach the same story the same week.
*Leaves undone:* story only, no present culture, no church history.

**The Jesus Storybook Bible** (Sally Lloyd-Jones, illustrated by Jago, 2007;
subtitle *Every Story Whispers His Name*). More than 2 million copies in 19
languages by Wikipedia's count; Zonderkidz claimed 2.5 million in 2017.
([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Jesus_Storybook_Bible), [Publishers Weekly](https://publishersweekly.com/pw/by-topic/industry-news/religion/article/69357-jesus-storybook-bible-sells-two-million-copies.html))
*Works because:* it tells one plot across the whole canon, written with
literary care and never talked down; adults read it for themselves (an adult
edition followed). CT called it "a very grown-up children's bible."
*Leaves undone:* it is a book, not a practice; no questions, no habit, no
culture.

**Training Hearts, Teaching Minds** (Starr Meade, P&R). The Westminster
Shorter Catechism's 107 questions, each with six short daily readings, about
two years end to end; aimed at elementary and junior high. Reviewers find it
forced at times and hard for six- to eight-year-olds.
([Ligonier store](https://store.ligonier.org/training-hearts-teaching-minds-paperback), [Challies](https://challies.com/resources/resources-for-learning-your-catechisms))
*Works because:* doctrine taught as reasons, daily, with Scripture beside each
point. *Leaves undone:* no age bands, and no bridge from doctrine to the week
the child is having.

**Family Worship** (Donald S. Whitney, Crossway, 2016). Three elements: read,
pray, sing. Catechism and memory work are optional extras. Brevity,
regularity, flexibility; a chapter for homes with an unbelieving father or
very young children.
([9Marks](https://9marks.org/review/book-review-family-worship-by-don-whitney/), [Modern Reformation](https://www.modernreformation.org/resources/articles/the-mod-family-worship-by-donald-whitney))
*Works because:* it lowers the bar and gives permission to be short, and
Whitney's point that keeping it short increases frequency is the whole
strategy. *Leaves undone:* it is a method without content.

**Lectio 365** (24-7 Prayer; launched Advent 2019) and **Lectio for Families**.
Three daily devotionals: morning on the P.R.A.Y. rhythm, midday on the Lord's
Prayer, night on the Ignatian Examen; read or listen with optional music; a
week downloadable offline; four languages. Past 2 million downloads (March
2026), about 330,000 monthly users, over 50 million prayer times in a year
*(company figures)*. ([Lectio 365](https://lectio365.com/), [faith.tools](https://faith.tools/app/69-lectio-365), [Lectio for Families](https://faith.tools/app/70-lectio-for-families))
*Works because:* a rhythm, not a reading; audio for people who will not read;
it borrows openly from the older church (Examen, Lord's Prayer).
*Leaves undone:* devotional warmth without the hard topics or the history
behind the practices it borrows.

**Dwell** (audio Bible). Twenty-plus voices (27 narrators by its own listing),
14 to 17 translations, optional ambient music, 75+ listening plans, read-along
text that scrolls with the narrator, playlists by theme (hope, forgiveness),
offline; freemium. ([App Store](https://apps.apple.com/app/id1343917374), [Dwell](https://dwellapp.io/llms.txt))
*Works because:* Scripture as something heard, which is how most of the church
received it, and which suits the car and the table. *Leaves undone:* no
teaching, context, or family structure.

**YouVersion** and the **Bible App for Kids** (with OneHope, 2013). The Bible
App: 1 billion installs across its apps; 2.6 million joined its 30-Day Bible
Challenge in November 2025, with daily use up 19%. The kids' app: 100 million
installs, 85% outside the U.S., 66 languages, 41 animated stories with
tap-to-animate scenes, questions, and rewards.
([ChurchLeaders](https://churchleaders.com/news/2209898-youversion-2025-verse-of-the-year-bobby-gruenewald.html), [K-LOVE](https://klove.com/news/faith/bible-app-for-kids-hits-100m-installs-worldwide-families-learn-the-bible-together-42328), [Scripture Engagement](https://scripture-engagement.org/content/bible-app-kids/))
*Works because:* free, everywhere, social (plans with friends), seasonal
challenges, and a verse a day. *Leaves undone:* plans are supplied by many
publishers and vary widely in depth; nothing on reading a text in its world or
on the church's history; engagement mechanics (streaks, rewards) that
LiveWell's brand contract rules out.

**Hallow** (Catholic). More than 1.4 million joined its 40-day Lent challenge
in 2025 and it reached No. 2 among free apps; $9.99 a month or $69.99 a year;
parish pages where members share requests and join challenges; a Kids' Lent
Challenge and Family Mass Prep. Appfigures notes the Lent download surge
peaked in 2024 (about 2 million) and the business is shifting to retention.
([ChurchLeaders](https://churchleaders.com/news/507269-million-join-hallow-prayer-challenge-lent.html), [Appfigures](https://app.appfigures.com/resources/insights/hallow-lent-surge-prayer-app-revenue), [App Store](https://apps.apple.com/app/1405323394))
*Works because:* the church year is the engagement engine, communal
challenges with a start date, parishes as the distribution channel.
*Leaves undone:* Protestant households have no equivalent built on the
church calendar.

**Axis** (Culture Translator). A weekly email on what is trending in teens'
music, shows, and feeds; 100+ free parent guides (mental health, technology,
social media, suicide), 50+ conversation kits, short videos; no account
needed; framed as teaching "how to think" rather than "what to think."
(Described by a school library page: [TBCS](https://www.tbcs.org/fs/pages/992).)
*Works because:* it meets the parent at the news cycle, every week, with a
conversation ready to use. *Leaves undone:* built for speed by design; the
church's long memory and the Bible in context are not its medium.

**Natasha Crain**, *Talking with Your Kids about God* (Baker, 2017; 30
conversations, a guide per chapter, elementary to high school) and *about
Jesus* (2020), *Faithfully Different* (2022).
([TGC store](https://store.thegospelcoalition.org/product/9780801075520))
*Works because:* apologetics cut to conversation size for parents.
*Leaves undone:* the titles (*Keeping Your Kids on God's Side*, *Faithfully
Different: Regaining Biblical Clarity in a Secular Culture*) frame the task as
defense against a secular culture; on the evidence of the descriptions I could
reach, it does less with the church's own failures or with history as a
witness (not read in full).

### 2.2 Study and learning

**BibleProject.** Free, donor-funded animated explainers, podcasts, reading

**Ligonier Connect.** 115+ interactive video courses (biblical studies,

([Ligonier](https://www.ligonier.org/posts/ligonier-connect-grow-with-your-group), [Ligonier](https://www.ligonier.org/posts/save-30-on-ligonier-connect-for-your-group))
*Works because:* real course structure, group cohorts, and church history as a

a paywall, and little steelmanning of the other side.

**RightNow Media.** Churches subscribe, priced by size (one example: $389.99 a
month for a church of 1,000), and every member gets free access to 25,000+
video studies; partner counts of 20,000+ churches *(partner pages)*.
([RightNow FAQ](https://web.rightnowmedia.org/us/faq), [Potomac Ministry Network](https://www.potomacag.org/right-now-media))
*Works because:* distribution runs through the pastor, which is the PCN model

voice.

**The Gospel Coalition.** About 30 million users have used TGC.org resources
(2023 editor's note); 16.4 million unique visitors in 2016; the Keller Center
for Cultural Apologetics (2023) runs online cohorts for parents, pastors, and

([TGC](https://thegospelcoalition.org/article/editors-choice-best-2023), [TGC 2016 report](https://media.thegospelcoalition.org/wp-content/uploads/2017/10/26164321/2016-annual-report.pdf), [TGC](https://www.thegospelcoalition.org/article/tgc-announces-keller-center/), [CT](https://christianitytoday.com/2023/02/tgc-tim-keller-center-cultural-apologetics-collin-hansen))

([RNS](https://religionnews.com/2021/02/24/phil-vischer-veggietales-holy-post-big-idea-jethani-metaxas-evangelical-ken-ham-bobtomato/), [Holy Post](https://www.holypost.com/about))

### 2.3 Engaging long-form reading on the web

**Length is not the enemy on a phone.** Pew and the Knight Foundation (117
million phone interactions with 74,840 articles, 2015 data): articles of 1,000+
words held readers 123 seconds against 57 for short ones; 5,000+ words held
270 seconds; 36% of long-form phone visits lasted over two minutes, against
10%; and long pieces were opened at nearly the same rate as short ones.
([Benton summary of Pew](https://www.benton.org/headlines/long-form-reading-shows-signs-life-our-mobile-news-world)) Old data, but the
direction supports James's essays as they are.

**Multimedia earns its place only when it serves the text.** The New York
Times' *Snow Fall* (December 20, 2012) drew about 3.5 million page views and
2.9 million visitors in six days, a third of them new to the Times, and won the
2013 Pulitzer for feature writing; within a year Slate called the copies that
followed "a whole lot of bells, way too many whistles."
([OpenNews](https://source.opennews.org/articles/how-we-made-snow-fall/), [Coloradan](https://www.colorado.edu/coloradan/2013/06/01/breaking-ground), [Slate](https://www.slate.com/articles/technology/technology/2013/08/snow_fall_the_jockey_the_scourge_of_the_new_york_times_bell_and_whistle.html))

**Interactivity raises engagement and recall, not comprehension.** A CHI 2026
study (N=454) found a scrollytelling format raised engagement and lowered
cognitive load while comprehension stayed level with plain text; an older
Mainz study found scrollytelling and video improved recall, but text was the
most efficient medium per minute.
([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.04367), [Mainz](https://openscience.ub.uni-mainz.de/handle/20.500.12030/409?mode=full))
The lesson for LiveWell: keep essays as text; spend interactivity where recall
matters (a timeline, a myth check, a catechism recite) and not inside the
argument.

**A human signature and an email beat a feature set.** Wait But Why paired
very long, researched prose with crude stick figures and launched into a web
full of listicles; it averaged about 1.5 million unique visitors a month and
300,000+ email subscribers *(self-reported)*.
([Next Big Idea Club](https://nextbigideaclub.com/magazine/tim-urban))

**Themed issues, art, and conviction.** *Plough Quarterly* builds each issue
around one theme, mixes long essays with poetry and art, carries no ads, and is
praised for "a visible convictional agenda"; *Mere Orthodoxy* is a
reader-supported journal of "serious Protestant thought" with a keepable print
issue. ([Plough](https://www.plough.com/quarterly))

## 3. What none of them does

Each gap below names the products that come closest, why they stop short, and
the LiveWell asset that already points at it.

**1. Nobody holds the Bible, church history, and the present American fight in
one place, at one level of honesty.** BibleProject reads the canon and stays
out of the culture and the centuries after Acts. Ligonier teaches history
within one tradition, in lectures. Holy Post and CT name the present failure
but not as formation. Axis meets the culture without the church's memory. NCC
and Long Story Short teach doctrine and story without the culture. LiveWell
already has all three strands (the Study Bible and 41 context guides; the
history page, 22 history essays, 14 creeds; the Christ and the Nation pages and
the politics care page); the work is joining them so each page hands the
reader to the other two.

**2. Nobody brings the post-Christian moment to the kitchen table.** Every
family product above is either timeless (catechism, story Bible) or reactive
(the weekly teen-culture email). None has a weekly devotion on exile
(Jeremiah 29, 1 Peter 2:11), on "Jesus is Lord and the flag is not," on
politics at dinner, on why Christians do bad things, on the phone in your
pocket, each with Scripture, a witness from church history, three questions,
and a prayer. The parent research (top topic 3) says this is where faith is
handed down, and `inventory-family.md` shows LiveWell does not have it either.
The 72 weekly devotions are the right format to extend.

**3. Nobody writes toward the skeptic and the family at the same time.**
Apologetics for parents (Crain, McLaughlin, the Keller Center) is written to
defend. Skeptic-respecting conversation exists mostly as British podcasts
(Brierley) and secular history (Holland). No American site steelmans the
objection in the form its defenders would sign, puts the writer inside the
indictment, and then gives a parent the version of that answer a twelve-year-old
can carry. James's biography (atheist to pastor, fatherless, five sons) is the
rare voice that can do both honestly.

**4. Nobody reports the numbers from both sides.** Barna and Gloo announce a
Bible-reading comeback; Burge says there is no statistical evidence of a
revival. PRRI finds a third of Americans sympathetic to Christian nationalist
ideas; Pew finds 10% favorable. Most outlets pick the figure that suits their
audience. A short, maintained page ("What the numbers say about faith in
America, and what they do not") that sets each pair side by side, with dates
and samples, is exactly the trust the skeptic is looking for and exactly what
pastors need before they quote a statistic from the pulpit.

**5. Nobody teaches church history to laypeople as an interactive,
primary-source course with a family edition.** What exists: lecture courses
(Ligonier), a free magazine (CHI), five-minute podcasts, YouTube explainers.
What does not: sessions that open with a myth check, set the story beside
verbatim primary sources, steelman the losing side, hold up a mirror ("we did
this too"), and connect people, councils, places, and dates in a registry the
reader can explore, plus a household version a family reads at dinner. The
rising interest in the older church (topic 13) is real but has nowhere
Protestant, honest, and free to go. The judges' consensus for the lost design
(`docs/work/NOTES.md`) is already this shape.

**6. Nobody builds tools that help people think instead of tools that keep them
tapping.** The habit products run on streaks, badges, rewards, and counts
(YouVersion, Hallow, the kids' app). LiveWell's brand contract forbids that,
and it can turn the constraint into the product: private tools that ask a
question before and after (a myth check), compare two people's answers
(the parent-and-teen tool and the marriage check already do this well), and
remember a family's place on the device without scoring it.

**7. Nobody is teaching discernment about AI as a spiritual authority.** A
third of adults already trust AI's spiritual guidance as much as a pastor's,
and 12% of pastors feel ready to teach about it. Logos shows the honest form of
AI help (answers grounded in named sources with citations). The opening for
LiveWell is not a chatbot. It is a short, plain guide ("Can I trust what an AI
tells me about the Bible?") that teaches a reader to check the source, the
context, and the translation, and a family devotion on what is real; and,
quietly, being the well-sourced page that AI tools cite (`llms.txt` and the
context guides already serve that).

**8. Nobody uses the church year for Protestant households.** Hallow proved
that a dated, communal season (1.4 million in a Lent challenge) brings people
in, and YouVersion's 30-day and New Year plans prove the same. No
Protestant-family product does Advent, Lent, Easter's fifty days, Pentecost,
and All Saints as household practice joined to the church's history. LiveWell
has Advent and Holy Week sets already.

**9. Nobody hands the pastor a weekly thing to give his people.** RightNow
proves churches will distribute a resource that serves members, and PCN is
LiveWell's channel. What is missing everywhere is a one-page weekly sheet
(this week's family devotion, a question for the table, the essay behind it)
that a pastor can print in the bulletin or forward on Sunday night, with no
login and no fee.

**10. Few have a single voice people follow.** The Bible Recap and Fr. Mike's
podcast show people will follow one trusted voice through the whole Bible.
Most institutional sites are a crowd of writers. LiveWell is one man's work,
and the essays, a recorded reading of them, and a weekly letter in his voice
are the moat no publisher can copy.

## 4. What this suggests for the plan

In order of value against the evidence, each with what to borrow and from
whom. These are proposals for the planning step, not decisions.

1. **The weekly table.** Extend the family devotions with a post-Christian set
   in James's register, in two bands (younger and teen), on the ten subjects in
   gap 2. Borrow the promised time and the scripted parent from Long Story
   Short, the read-pray-sing minimum from Whitney, two registers from NCC, the
   one-big-story spine from the Jesus Storybook Bible. Add what none has: a
   witness from church history each week, Scripture opening in the Study
   Bible, a table mode (one step per screen, read aloud), and a printable card.

2. **When the news comes to dinner.** A small set of conversation cards for the
   moments that arrive unannounced (an election, a shooting, a church scandal,
   a friend who comes out), with Scripture, one historical witness, questions,
   and the care-page guardrails. Axis's speed with the church's memory. With
   the midterm on November 3, 2026, the politics card and the existing
   `/help/politics` page are the most time-sensitive items in this file.

3. **Hard questions in the words people type.** Retitle and front the 35
   Honest Answers and the care pages by the "asked as" column of section 1.2
   ("Why does God allow suffering?", "Is the Bible reliable?", "Can I trust the
   church after what it did?", "What is Christian nationalism?", "Should my kid
   have a phone?", "Can AI answer my questions about God?"), each a short
   honest answer that hands the reader to an essay, a study guide, and a family
   devotion. Add the AI discernment guide (gap 7).

4. **Read the Bible in its world, starting January 1.** A thirty- to
   ninety-day plan that pairs Study Bible chapters with the context guides, for
   the new Bible readers the 2025 data describes, ready before the New Year
   spike. BibleProject's whole-canon story with LiveWell's context depth; Dwell
   and Lectio's lesson that audio matters.

5. **Church history as a course, with a family edition.** Build the course the
   judges' consensus describes, borrowing Ligonier's session and group
   structure and BibleProject's single visual grammar (for the timeline and
   the registry), and keeping prose as the medium of the argument. Add a family
   track that carries the same story to the table (gap 5).

6. **The essay page, per `inventory-content.md` section 7,** now backed by the
   evidence here: length is fine on phones (Pew); spend interactivity on recall
   (myth checks, a timeline), not inside the argument (CHI 2026, Snow Fall's
   lesson); give the skeptic sources to check; add questions and a group guide
   for the pastor; offer a recording in James's voice if he will make one.

7. **A church-year rhythm for households,** dated and without streaks:
   Thanksgiving (Thursday, November 26), Advent (from Sunday, November 29),
   the New Year plan (January 1), Lent (Ash Wednesday, February 10, 2027),
   Easter (March 28, 2027) and its fifty days. Hallow's season-as-door without
   Hallow's mechanics.

8. **The weekly sheet for pastors,** generated from whatever the household got
   that week and sent to PCN on Sunday night; the "Take it with you" print and
   send machinery from #534 already does most of the work.

9. **A maintained "what the numbers say" page** (gap 4), with every figure
   checked against the primary report before it is published, both readings of
   each contested trend, and dates and samples shown.

**What not to copy:** streaks, badges, rewards, or completion counts
(YouVersion, Hallow, the kids' app); paywalls on the core material (Ligonier,
CT); volume over curation (RightNow); a tribe's settled frame on every question
(TGC on one side, Holy Post as many readers hear it on the other), since the
guardrail must fire in both political directions; multimedia that interrupts
the argument (Snow Fall's imitators); celebrity voices in place of James's own.

## 5. Figures to verify before any reaches the site

Everything here is secondhand to some degree (see Method). These are the ones
most likely to be quoted and most in need of a primary check:

- Barna and Gloo's 2025 Bible-reading jump (30% to 42%; Gen Z 30% to 49%) and
  the 2024 baseline, which secondary outlets call both a "15-year" and a
  "25-year" low; one outlet's 3% figure for Boomers looks like an error.
- The Communio and Harvard "7.5 times" and "2.5 times" odds (press-release
  figures; the paper reportedly appeared in the *Journal for the Scientific
  Study of Religion*).
- The Barna and Gloo AI figures (34%, 44%, 12%), released May 2026.
- The Bible Recap's 300 million downloads; Mars Hill's 14 million; Hallow and
  Lectio user counts (all company or secondhand).
- Pew's April 2026 Christian nationalism figures (59%, 10%, 31%, 17%), which
  reached me through OSV News and a Pew chart page.
- *The Great Dechurching* return-willingness figure, which sources give as both
  65% and 51%.
- Any "most-Googled" list: the Explore God seven questions are a popular
  framing, not a published search dataset, and Relevant's 2025 list was not
  readable from this sandbox beyond its first item.

The Google Search Console query report for www.livewellbyjamesbell.co, and the
Vercel analytics for the same period, would show which of these subjects real
readers already arrive on. That is the one data source that should outrank
everything in this file.
