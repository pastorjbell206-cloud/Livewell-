# Product and interaction critique: LiveWell as a learning tool

Lens: product and interaction design for learning tools (the standard set by the YouVersion and ESV Bible apps, STEP Bible, Logos, Sefaria, Readwise, Brilliant). The question is not whether the pages are handsome or the writing is good. It is whether a person who sits down to study the Bible, a doctrine, or the church's history gets further, faster and deeper here than anywhere else, and whether the site remembers and builds on what they do.

Evidence: every `-phone-first.png`, the full-page phone and desktop shots of the Study Bible, John 1, the tools hub and three tools, search, church history and an era essay, the Nicene Creed, a context guide, the essay, Family Tonight, Find Help and the anxiety page, plus a reading of the page code where a state was not photographed (the verse study panel, search sources, translations). Interactive states past first load were not captured, so claims about the verse panel come from `client/src/pages/StudyBible.tsx`, not a screenshot.

## Verdict

The raw material is seminary-grade and some of it is better than anything in the niche: notes on all 1,189 chapters, the Greek and Hebrew under every English word from STEP Bible data, a chapter outline, a story placement, a resurrection walkthrough that argues one move at a time, a family devotion that actually gets done at a table. But as a product LiveWell is a library of pages, not a study tool. Reading John 1 here is slower than on ESV.org (no section headings in the text, notes thirteen collapsed accordions below the last verse), it connects less than STEP or Sefaria (cross-references only inside a panel, essays and history never link back to the text), and it remembers nothing the reader thinks (no highlight, no note, no verse bookmark; only "Mark as read" and page-level Save). The same job is done two or three times by parallel tools with different data and different translations (a "flagship" Bible companion that covers 37 passages, two glossaries, three verse finders, BSB here, WEB there, KJV elsewhere, ESV in the essays), and the site search cannot see the best study content at all: "grace" returns a page on aging and an eight-week porn-recovery plan, and no doctrine, no glossary entry, no charis. The fix is not more tools. It is one study surface, the Study Bible chapter, that every other piece plugs into: one translation, the outline as headings in the text, tap any verse to open notes, cross-references, words and the reader's own note in a panel that sits beside the text, one search that knows passages, words, doctrines and people, and live references everywhere that lead back to it. Do that and LiveWell stops being a site you read and becomes one you study in.

## Findings

### Critical

**1. `/study/bible/john/1` (and every chapter): the notes are severed from the verses they explain.**
What is wrong. The 51 verses render as one unbroken block of prose (`study-bible-john-1-phone-00` to `-03`), with no section headings, no paragraphing, and the prologue (1-18) not set as the poetry most translations set it. The chapter's own outline ("1-5 The Word in the beginning", "6-8 John, a witness", eight sections) exists, but only in the desktop side card; on phone it is not rendered at all (the aside is `wide`-only in `StudyBible.tsx`). The notes sit after the last verse as thirteen collapsed accordions ("The historical setting", "Key Greek words", "Verse by verse", "A hard question (1 of 2)" and so on), so a reader who wants the note on 1:14 scrolls past 37 more verses, opens "Verse by verse", and hunts. The only way to reach a verse's note in place is to tap its number, which is a 12px superscript with a 24px hit area in running text, well under a 44px touch target. On desktop, below the outline card the right column is 380px of empty cream for the full height of the text (`study-bible-john-1-desktop-00`, `-01`).
Why it costs. Studying is reading with a question in your hand. Every time the answer is three screens away the reader loses the sentence they were holding. This is the flagship surface and it currently teaches people to read the text or the notes, not both.
World-class version. The ESV Study Bible in the ESV app and Logos keep notes in a pane that scrolls with the text; Sefaria opens a Connections panel beside the exact segment you touch. Here: insert the outline as pericope headings in the text at every width (the data is already there); tap or click anywhere on a verse, not just its number, to select it; mark verses that have a verse note with a small dot; on desktop make the sticky panel follow the reader, showing the note for the section in view until a verse is chosen; on phone use a half-height sheet that leaves the selected verse visible above it. Keep the accordion notes, but lead with a one-screen "Read this chapter well" summary (setting, structure, the key word) above the text, not below it.

**2. The Study Bible, essays, guides: the reader cannot leave a mark.**
What is wrong. There is no highlight, no personal note, no verse bookmark, no copy-verse-with-reference, and no in-place translation comparison anywhere in the Study Bible (confirmed in code: the verse panel offers notes, word-by-word, cross-references, related pages and "Go deeper", nothing the reader writes). The only memory is "Mark John 1 as read" and the page-level Save. Comparing translations means leaving the site: "Read John 1 in other translations: ESV · NIV · NASB · CSB · KJV" opens five external tabs (`ChapterNotes.tsx` line 307). Essays offer quote-sharing on selection (desktop only) but nothing is kept.
Why it costs. Study is what the reader does to the text, not only what the text does to the reader. A tool that keeps nothing of the reader's thinking is a reference shelf; people return daily to YouVersion and Readwise because their own marks are there. It also forfeits the site's best retention loop.
World-class version. YouVersion's verse action bar (highlight in a few colours, note, bookmark, copy, compare) and Readwise's highlight-then-review loop. Within James's no-accounts decision (2026-10-14, recorded in `MyLibrary.tsx`): keep highlights and notes in the browser through the existing `lib/storage.ts` helper, show them in My Library grouped by book, and give an Export (Markdown and a file to import on another device) so a reader's year of notes is never trapped. Add a "Compare" row in the verse panel that shows two or three translations inline for that verse only, where licensing allows, rather than sending the reader to five other sites.

**3. `/tools`, `/tools/deep-bible`, `/theology/passage`, `/tools/glossary`, `/theology/glossary`: the same jobs are done by parallel tools with different data.**
What is wrong. There are three book-level Bible study surfaces: the Study Bible (66 books, 1,189 chapters), the "Flagship tool" Deep Bible Study Companion (20 books, 37 passages hard-coded in the component), and the Bible Study Guide ("12 essential books"). There is a fourth passage surface, the Passage Context Tool (`/theology/passage`), with its own 230 contested-passage notes and a different translation fetched at runtime, and the Study Bible's own chapter page links out to it ("The passage guide to John 1"). John 1:1-18 therefore has two separate deep treatments in two places. There are two glossaries with different term counts (114 in `public/theology/theology-glossary.json` behind `/theology/glossary`, 104 hard-coded in `/tools/glossary`). There are three "find Scripture for what I'm facing" tools (Bible Verse Finder, Wisdom Finder, What the Bible Says About) and a fourth, Misused Verses. There are two family devotion systems (72 written devotions and a generating Builder). The tools hub (`tools-desktop-00`, `-01`) presents all 36 at equal weight, each ending "Open the tool".
Why it costs. James asked for the most organized site in the niche. A reader who finds three answers to "study a book of the Bible" cannot tell which is the real one, assumes the others are thinner, and stops trusting the shelf. Every duplicate is also a second copy of notes and definitions to keep correct.
World-class version. Sefaria: one library, many lenses onto it. Make the Study Bible the single home for passage study and fold the others into it: Deep Bible's 37 worked passages become verse notes and a "worked passage" section on those chapters; the Passage Context Tool's 230 notes merge into the chapter notes; the Bible Study Guide's book themes become the book introduction; one glossary, linked from the verse panel and the doctrines. Retire the duplicates with the one-hop redirect pattern the site already uses. The tools hub then shrinks to the tools that are genuinely different instruments.

**4. `/search?q=grace` (and the five other search boxes): search cannot see the study content.**
What is wrong. `/search` ranks titles and blurbs over the static libraries and `search-index.json`, which covers Honest Answers, Christ and the Nation, family devotions, the catechism and What the Bible Says (`lib/siteSearch.ts`, `scripts/build-search-index.mjs`). It does not index the 62 doctrine studies, the 24 reading guides, the book introductions, the 1,189 chapter notes, either glossary, the church-history people, councils and heresies, or the lexicon. So "grace" (`search-q-grace-phone-00`, `-01`) returns Grace and Works, patronage, child discipline, "How to Age With Grace", the Thanksgiving study, the catechism answer, then "Eight Weeks Out of Hiding" (the pornography plan), blended families and willpower, with no result count, and no doctrine of salvation, no glossary entry, no Ephesians 2:8, no charis. Meanwhile there are six separate boxes with six scopes: the header search, `/theology/search` (doctrines, passage notes, glossary, creeds, figures; it sends references to the Passage Context Tool, while the header sends them to the Study Bible), "Search the essays" on `/writing`, "Find a tool" on `/tools`, "Type it in your own words" on `/help`, and "Go to" on the Study Bible.
Why it costs. On a study site search is the study tool. A reader who types a doctrine and gets an aging how-to concludes the doctrine is not here, when it is, in depth, one click away.
World-class version. One command bar, everywhere (the Logos "Go" box, Sefaria's search, a Cmd-K palette), that answers in typed groups: Passage (with the verse text), Word (charis, G5485, with its occurrences), Doctrine, Glossary, People and councils, Essays, Help, Tools. The header search, the Study Bible "Go to" and `/theology/search` become the same component; the Help box keeps its own care-first ranking but draws on the same index. Show counts and let the reader filter by those types, not by "Articles" and "Resources".

### Major

**5. Site-wide: one verse, four wordings.**
What is wrong. The Study Bible, family devotions and care pages quote the Berean Standard Bible; the Passage Context Tool fetches the World English Bible at runtime; the Family Devotion Builder and the tools glossary quote the KJV; the essays quote the ESV (`lib/esv.ts`). The same verse reads differently depending on the door the reader came through.
Why it costs. Memorization, quoting in a small group, and the reader's sense that this is one coherent work all depend on a stable text. The Scripture Memory tool cannot sit beside four wordings.
World-class version. One reading text site-wide (the BSB, already public domain and already under the Study Bible), with the translation named on every quotation, and comparison offered in place (see finding 2) rather than by switching texts between tools.

**6. Essays, church history, Find Help: Scripture references are dead text on the surfaces readers spend most time on.**
What is wrong. Live references (dotted underline, opening the verse in a sheet) are wired only into context guides and creeds (`Prose verseRefs="sheet"` in `ContextGuide.tsx` and `CreedDocument.tsx`), plus family and plans. In the essay, "(1 Samuel 8:5)" is plain text (`writing-strongman-theology-desktop-00`); the anxiety page's "(Psalm 55:13)", "(Matthew 8:26)", "(Deuteronomy 5:15)" are plain (`help-anxiety-desktop-02`, `-03`); the apostolic-age essay has no Scripture links at all and only three links of any kind, all back to the hub. Nor does any chapter page show where it is discussed on the site.
Why it costs. The essay is where a skeptic or a doubting Christian meets the text; if the reference does not open, the claim cannot be checked, and checking is the whole trust contract.
World-class version. Faithlife's RefTagger and Wikipedia's page previews: every reference everywhere opens the verse in place, with "Open in the Study Bible" one tap further. Then add the reverse: a "Discussed on LiveWell" list in the verse panel and at the foot of each chapter, built from the same references (Sefaria's Connections, Obsidian's backlinks). That one index turns 700 pages into a web around the text.

**7. `/study/bible`: the book list is a fifteen-screen wall, and progress is invisible.**
What is wrong. All 66 books are equal cards with a three-to-five-line blurb (`study-bible-phone-01`, `study-bible-desktop-01`); reaching Revelation on a phone is about thirteen screens. There is no chapter grid, so a book card leads to an introduction page and only then to chapters. "Mark as read" is stored, and once anything is read the page offers "Continue the story: <next chapter>" (code, `BookIndex` in `StudyBible.tsx`; not photographed, since the capture was a cold visit), but that button follows the story order, not the chapter the reader was last in, and nothing on the grid shows progress (no per-book mark). There is no Bible reading plan on `main`, though the site has plans for Proverbs, families and eight-week care.
Why it costs. Getting to a passage should take two taps. Getting back to where you were should take one. Everything else on the page is in the way of both.
World-class version. The YouVersion and ESV apps: a compact book grid of names (two or three per row on phone) that expands to a chapter-number grid in place; a thin progress ring per book from the existing read data; keep the Continue button but add "Pick up where you stopped" for the last chapter opened; the story of the Bible offered as a reading plan with a daily chapter and a quiet streak (BibleProject's plans), not only as an essay series. Move the blurbs to the book pages.

**8. `/` and `/study`: the front doors describe studying instead of starting it.**
What is wrong. The home page's first phone screen has one action, "About James" (`home-phone-first`); the first study affordance is a text link on screen two, and there is no passage field, no "Continue", no "Tonight". `/study` (`study-phone-00`, `-01`) is a directory of 19 text links, three of which go to the same `/study/bible`, and it ends by sending tools away: "Looking for the study tools ... They are all in the tools." Doctrine, one of the four things James named, has no door in the navigation: the 62 doctrine studies appear as one card on `/study/bible` and one line on `/study`, and the Study menu (`header-menu-study-open-phone-first`) does not list them.
Why it costs. A first visit decides whether this is a place you use or a place you read about. Right now it reads like a brochure for a tool that lives two clicks deeper.
World-class version. Sefaria's home: a working surface. One field ("A passage, a word, a question") at the top of `/` and `/study`, then Continue (the last chapter or plan), Tonight (the family devotion), and three entry points with real counts: The Bible, Doctrine, Church history. Put "Doctrines" in the Study menu.

**9. `/theology/history`: two overlapping essay series, a timeline that is not a timeline, and a mis-filed shelf.**
What is wrong. "The era essays, 22 essays" (`theology-history-desktop-00`, `-01`) are two series printed as one list: fourteen titled by period ("The Apostolic Age", "The Reformation") and eight more covering the same ground again ("The Apostolic and Persecuted Church", "The Reformation and Its Wars"). Nothing tells the reader which to read or how they differ. "The timeline" is a stack of seven accordions with dated bullet lists (`-02`, `-03`), not something you can see across. The figures (36), councils (11) and heresies (10) are tabs of text. And "Everything on this: Church history, 42 pieces" opens with "How Should Christians Think About Sickness and Health?", "How Do You Date Wisely and Choose a Spouse?" and "How Do You Discipline Children With Grace?" (`-03`), which are not church history.
Why it costs. History is learned as shape before detail. Without a picture of the two thousand years, the 22 essays are 22 essays, and the mis-filed shelf tells a careful reader the filing cannot be trusted.
World-class version. A horizontal, scrubbable timeline at the top (the pattern of Knight Lab's TimelineJS and viz.bible's Theographic), with eras as bands, councils as marks, people as dots you can tap, and each era band opening its essay. Choose one series as "the story" and present the other as "the short version" with a sentence saying so. Fix the filing feeding "Everything on this" before showing it again.

**10. `/theology/history/the-apostolic-age`: an excellent essay that is a dead end.**
What is wrong. The page (`theology-history-the-apostolic-age-desktop-00` to `-03`) has three links, all back to the hub. No "Next: The Age of the Martyrs", no linked figures (Clement, Ignatius, Polycarp, Paul are plain text in "Key figures"), no Scripture links though it narrates Acts, no reading time, none of the reader tools the essay page has (Listen, text size, Focus, Save), and no primary text, though the figures it names left letters. The right half of the desktop page is empty for its whole length.
Why it costs. A series is read in order or not at all. Each era essay that ends without a way forward loses the reader at the end of every chapter of the story.
World-class version. Previous and next era at the foot and a slim era rail at the side (the empty gutter is the place for it, with the era's dates and where you are in the two thousand years). Link each figure to their card. Add a short "In their own words" excerpt per era from a public-domain translation, so readers meet Ignatius, not only a summary of him, which is what Sefaria does for every rabbinic source. Give it the same reader toolbar as the essays (finding 13).

**11. `/resources/creeds/nicene-creed`: the annotations live in a list below the text they annotate.**
What is wrong. The notes on "begotten, not made", "of one substance", "who proceedeth from the Father" are superb, but they are a separate block after the creed (`resources-creeds-nicene-creed-desktop-01`), each repeating the phrase it explains, so the reader reads the creed, then reads the phrases again, matching by eye. The only Scripture link is 1 Corinthians 15:3-4. Nothing shows what Constantinople (381) added to Nicaea (325), though the intro tells that story, or lets the reader toggle the filioque.
Why it costs. A creed is learned line by line. Separating each line from its meaning makes the reader do the indexing the page should do.
World-class version. Genius-style inline annotation: each annotated phrase in the creed is underlined, and tapping it opens the note beside it (the same panel pattern as the Study Bible). Add a "325 / 381" switch that highlights the added clauses, a filioque toggle, a "Scripture behind this line" reveal, and a memorize mode that reuses Scripture Memory's first-letter drill. The family catechism already proves the site knows how to teach something to be learned by heart.

**12. `/tools`: 36 equal cards, overlapping instruments, no routing.**
What is wrong. The hub (`tools-desktop-00`, `-01`; 12.5 phone screens) shows every tool as an identical card with a paragraph and "Open the tool". The self-assessments overlap without explanation: "Where Are You With God?", "The Whole-Life Assessment" (30 statements across inner life, body, relationships, work and the world), "Life Audit" (24 questions across 8 life areas), "Emotional Health Self-Check", "Marriage Health Self-Check", "Financial Health Check", "Spiritual Gifts". Naming varies (Self-Check, Assessment, Audit, Health Check) for the same kind of instrument. No card shows that a tool is in progress on this device, though My Library knows.
Why it costs. A hub this flat makes the reader do the product manager's job of deciding what each thing is for, and most will pick the first card.
World-class version. Brilliant's course map and Apple Fitness's small set of clearly different workouts: group by the job ("Read Scripture", "Understand a doctrine", "Examine yourself", "Practice this week", "For the household"), one recommended starting tool per group, consistent instrument names, an "In progress" or "Your last result" line on cards from the existing device storage, and the consolidation in finding 3 so the list is shorter.

**13. `/tools/deep-bible`: a flagship that is smaller than the thing it points to.**
What is wrong. Labelled "Flagship tool", it covers 20 books in no canonical order (Genesis, Psalms, Exodus, Proverbs, Isaiah, Daniel, Ruth, Job ... ; `tools-deep-bible-desktop`), 37 passages in all, fourteen of whose "text" fields are excerpts ending in "...", and it closes by telling the reader to go to the Study Bible for "every book and every chapter".
Why it costs. The word flagship makes a promise the tool then retracts, and the arbitrary book order makes it feel unfinished to anyone who knows a Bible.
World-class version. Fold it into the Study Bible as finding 3 says. If it stays for a while, drop "Flagship", order books canonically, and print the full passage from the site's one translation.

**14. Long-form surfaces: the reader tools change from page to page, and Scripture cannot be heard.**
What is wrong. The essay has Send to my phone, Save, Print, Listen, text size, Focus, an "In this essay" outline, Cite this and quote-share (`writing-strongman-theology-desktop-00`, `-04`). The context guide has only Send, Save, Print. The church-history era essay has none. The Study Bible chapter, the text most worth hearing, has no Listen at all, while the family devotion can read Philippians 4:5 aloud.
Why it costs. Readers learn a tool once and expect it everywhere; inconsistency reads as unfinished. Audio is how many people actually get through the Bible (Dwell, YouVersion audio).
World-class version. One reader toolbar component on every long-form surface (essay, era essay, context guide, creed, chapter), with Listen on every Bible chapter and the outline/TOC everywhere a page has sections.

**15. `/help/anxiety`: the week's practice is written as paragraphs, and the tools it needs are 20 screens away.**
What is wrong. "What to do this week" (`help-anxiety-desktop-03`) is six excellent numbered steps rendered as static prose. Step 1, "Name it on paper", is exactly what The Worry Journal tool does, but the journal is offered only under "Go deeper" much further down. Step 5, "Tell one person", gives a sentence to say but no way to send it. Step 4 names Psalm 131 without opening it. The jump links ("What to do this week", "When to get more help" ...) sit only in the hero of a page that is 38 phone screens long.
Why it costs. A person in a hard week needs to do one small thing now, not read about six. The page already does the hard part (the words); it stops one step short of the action.
World-class version. Headspace and Calm turn a practice into a step you can start and tick. Make each step actionable in place: "Start the list" opens the Worry Journal inline; "Tell one person" offers the sentence as a prefilled text (the page already uses `sms:` links for the hotlines); Psalm 131 opens in the verse sheet; steps can be ticked and are remembered on the device; a "Remind me tomorrow" uses the calendar helper the Proverbs tool already has. Make the section links a slim sticky bar once the hero scrolls away.

### Minor

**16. `/tools/test-the-case`: on phone, nine case buttons stand between the reader and the first move.**
The opening paragraph, then nine stacked case buttons (`tools-test-the-case-phone-00`), push "Move 1 of 5" to the bottom of the first screen. "Close to the common ground of the historians who work on this" names no one, and the skeptic this is written for will ask who. The walkthrough does not persist (no storage in `TestTheCase.tsx`), so a reload starts over. World-class: Brilliant's one-step-per-screen with a visible progress rail; move the case picker behind a "Other questions" control; name the scholarly positions the move relies on in a "Who says so" disclosure (only sources James has verified); keep position in sessionStorage as Which Lens does; end with a one-screen summary of the path the reader took.

**17. `/tools/which-lens`: progress is a line of text.**
"1 OF 13" (`tools-which-lens-phone-00`) is the only sense of distance. A thin segmented bar would do more. Otherwise this is the right shape: one question, two honest answers, private.

**18. `/family/tonight`: "69 of 72 · Weekly devotions · Week 44 of the year" reads as two contradictory counters.**
And "We did this tonight" records the night but nothing shows the record back. World-class: one counter ("Week 44"), and a small year strip on `/family` showing the nights done, without streak pressure.

**19. `/study/bible/john/1`: "Go deeper on LiveWell" is loosely matched.**
Under John 1 the cards are "Baptism and Communion", "The Creeds", "Why Follow Jesus", "Creation and Providence", "The Trinity", "Eutychianism" (`study-bible-john-1-desktop-02`). Several fit; the first and last read as keyword matches. World-class: two or three hand-picked next steps per chapter where James has them, the matched list below and labelled as such.

**20. `/search`: the filters and results do not say what they are.**
"All / Articles / Resources" are not categories a reader holds in mind; there is no count; "See all in Church History" appears though no church-history result is shown. Fold into finding 4.

## What to keep

- **The Study Bible's depth and honesty.** Notes on all 1,189 chapters, the Greek and Hebrew interlinear with lexicon and parsing from STEP Bible data (credited in full at the foot of each chapter), the verse study panel (notes, word by word, cross-references, related pages), "This chapter at a glance", "Act Nine of the story" placement, Previous and Next in the story, the "Continue the story" button once a reader has started, and the candid "verse-by-verse notes are written for Genesis and the four Gospels so far". This is the platform's crown; the critique is about how it is presented, not what it holds.
- **The "Go to" parser** that takes "John 3:16", "jn 3 16" or "Romans 8" and lands on the passage. Make it the one search box.
- **Test the Case's pedagogy.** One move at a time, the reader's own objection as a button, "What this move does not settle" stated plainly, "You can walk away unconvinced", no altar call. That is Brilliant-grade teaching design written in James's voice.
- **Which Lens Has You?** One question per screen, two uncomfortable answers, answers kept in the tab and seen by no one.
- **Family Tonight.** "Start at the table" and Table mode, the passage read aloud, Read / Talk / Ask / Do / Remember / Pray, the catechism question of the week tied to the devotion, "We did this tonight", Before and Next. The best product thinking on the site: a daily habit for real households without gamification.
- **Find Help's front door.** "Type it in your own words", the plain-language needs ("I can't stop worrying"), crisis lines first with call and text links, Exit site, and the instruction on the anxiety page to "put the worries on paper and leave the list by your bed" before anything else.
- **Context guides.** Live verse references that open in a sheet, named sources (deSilva, Georges, Bailey, Richards and O'Brien), Keep reading within the series, and a PDF. This is the pattern to spread to the essays and history.
- **The Nicene Creed's notes** as writing. Change where they sit, not what they say.
- **Church-history Sources and Key figures blocks.** Named books by named historians on every era essay.
- **My Library without accounts.** Saved and started things on the device, said plainly, with a way to email the list. Build highlights and notes on the same promise.
- **The essay reader toolbar.** Listen, text size, Focus, "In this essay", Cite this, Send to my phone. Make it the standard everywhere.
