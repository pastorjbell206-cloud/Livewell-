# Judge: James's seat

One of the judges of the four design directions in this folder (`study-desk.md`,
`field-guide.md`, `long-memory.md`, `the-week.md`), step 3 of the design program
in `docs/work/NOTES.md`. Prepared 2026-10-27.

**The seat.** James Bell himself: his brief of 2026-10-27, his standing asks
(the best church history study available and very interactive; family
devotions; tools and topics "to truly help people live in a post-Christian
nation"; the doors Study, Topics, Family, Essays, About), his mission as the
Decision Log states it ("living well in post-Christian America and
understanding the church's long history"), his voice (`docs/VOICE-JAMES-BELL.md`,
which governs), and the integrity rules. The question the seat asks of each
direction: would he be proud to send this to the pastors of PCN?

**What this is not.** An agent judging from his brief, not James's own verdict.
Nothing below is his first person, and every choice that touches the contract,
the Decision Log or his byline is his to make (section 8).

**Read for this judgment.** `CLAUDE.md`; `docs/EDITORIAL-CONSTITUTION.md`;
`docs/VOICE-JAMES-BELL.md`; `CRITIQUE.md`; the syntheses' verdicts, rankings and
tensions (`A-`, `B-`, `C-SYNTHESIS.md`, sections 0 to 3 and 4.5); `MASTER-PLAN.md`
sections 0 to 3 and 10.6; `NOTES.md`; all four directions in full; the current
screenshots (home, Study, John 1, the strongman essay, church history and the
apostolic age, Tonight, Find Help, Test the Case, About).

**Checked in the repo for this judgment** (section 7 has the detail): the 2027
plan's days 302 to 308, the registry's dates for Polycarp and Ignatius, the BSB
text of John 1:14, the John 1 and John 3 church-reading notes, seven catechism
questions, two care-page titles. Two of the four directions carry a factual
error in an example; one direction caught the error the others repeated.

## 3. Each direction, from this seat

### 3.1 The Long Memory: 87

**Strongest.** The Line. Twenty centuries drawn to scale on the home page in
cream hairlines on black, the long bracket of Christendom dotted where its end
is argued, the short American row at the right, one mustard tick: *You are
here.* Its caption is James's own sentence from #533, *The early church knew.
So did believers in every age who lived without power.* No other direction
turns his headline's claim into something a stranger can see in three seconds,
and it does it with a still, checkable drawing rather than motion. The learning
critic's line is the case for it: "That one image teaches the post-Christian
thesis better than a paragraph can."

**Weakest.** It makes every date a claim, and its own showcase breaks its own
rule. Section 4.3 offers, "in the registry's own dates," that "Polycarp was a
boy when Ignatius died." The registry gives Polycarp "around 69 to 155" and
Ignatius "around 35 to 108": Polycarp was about forty. Section 3.2's returning
reader reads "Day 302 of 365 · The Prophets and Exile"; day 302 of the
generated plan is Luke 8 to 9. A direction whose risk list says "a to-scale
drawing turns every year into an assertion a skeptic will check" cannot ship
an example a skeptic would catch. The validator and ledger it proposes are the
right answer; they have to be in force before the first drawing, not after.

**By criterion.**
1. *Brief (17).* All five aims, entered through the story. Doctrine taught as
   "the words the church was forced to find" (4.5, the creed's date rail) is the
   best doctrine idea in the four. "How to live it" comes through the topic
   page's long arc and *Asked before* (4.7) and the Year clock for the
   household. The Bible keeps its text first (5.1, "Scripture is read as
   Scripture first"). It loses a point to the Study Desk on "a useful tool to
   study": the study surface is good here but specified in less depth.
2. *History (10).* The only direction that builds what he asked for twice as
   the centerpiece and makes it interactive in ways that teach: the hinge sheet
   (*the world before, what happened, the world after, what we still carry*),
   *Who was alive* with its year rule, the facing page with the losing side
   first, *Compare the witnesses* on Thessalonica from three stored sources,
   the gap ruler in Test the Case, *Twelve weeks, twelve turns*.
3. *Mission (14).* "Older than my culture" made visible. History is the one
   subject the skeptic will read without first conceding the faith, and the
   history hub's new line speaks to him ("Whether or not you believe it, this is
   the story that made the country you live in"). Its stated limits are written
   the way James argues: the church's failures (Callinicum, Thessalonica, the
   Crusades, the slave trade, the German Christians beside Barmen) drawn in the
   same ink as its councils; "the rhyme is not identity," with one sentence
   wherever the two ends of the line are set side by side; the "not yet" past
   today marked plainly as confession. That is reconstruction, not nostalgia.
4. *Voice and integrity (12).* The method is the most rigorous of the four
   (certainty drawn, solid or dotted; a source and a ledger line for every
   mark; *Not yet written* drawn on purpose; the registry's two women shown as
   the gap it is). Docked for the two errors above, and for setting James's
   sentence in muted cream on the home hero (3.1, item 4), the gray-for-primary
   text the editorial critic faulted.
5. *Decisions and time (7).* It keeps his headline, his hero and his words. It
   asks to reverse the doors he chose a day earlier and to move About off the
   header into the masthead, although his face and his atheist story are the
   skeptic's strongest reason to trust the site. It says plainly that nothing
   breaks if he keeps his five. Readings introductions, the twelve hinges and
   the bracket's wording add to his review load, but most of the history
   writing is already in the plan.
6. *Attention (10).* The most arresting true object in all four documents,
   and the most distinctive: nobody else in the niche draws this.
7. *Organized (8).* One periodization replacing three, one ordered reading
   list, a series rail on every era, dates as the shared key across the
   relation model, "type a year" in search. Docked because the parent and the
   seeker do not look for things by date, and the Study and History split will
   send some readers to the wrong door for a creed.
8. *PCN (9).* *Twelve weeks, twelve turns*, dated for a church, with leader
   notes, a 45- and 75-minute plan and a handout, is an adult class a pastor
   would run this winter. *The church remembers* gives him a witness a week for
   the bulletin.

### 3.2 The Study Desk: 85

**Strongest.** The verse opens the library (4.1) at three depths (4.2): one
study surface that every page plugs into, each layer labeled by who is
speaking, the church's dated witnesses on the verse, and at *Teach* depth a
one-page leader's sheet for every one of 1,189 chapters. It is the clearest
answer to "the most organized" and to "a useful tool for people to use to
study," and it is the thing a PCN pastor would open every week.

**Weakest.** It makes the site a believer's instrument first. The home hero is
"This week · John 1:14," a verse card in a white sheet; the post-Christian
subject and "how to live it" are rooms off the desk; church history, asked for
twice, is "already planned in depth" and receives the desk's grammar rather
than its attention. It names the risk itself ("the skeptic reads 'a Bible
app' and leaves"). It also repeated an unchecked example: "Days 302 to 308:
Jeremiah" (3.4), which the generated plan reads as Luke 8 to 22.

**By criterion.**
1. *Brief (18).* Bible and doctrine are the best specified in the four: the
   doctrine room with 76 catechism question pages, *In brief* with proofs, the
   ladder from Scripture to creed to debate to witnesses; one search that
   answers by kind; the instruments by job. Post-Christian America and how to
   live in it are served, honestly, by keeping Topics and Find Help in front of
   the desk.
2. *History (7).* The river, lives as lines, the church on this verse. Good
   grammar, deferred content.
3. *Mission (12).* The church's long memory beside the verse is "older than my
   culture" at the scale of a sentence, and James's voice is placed after the
   text, the witnesses and the reader's own answer, which is the humility the
   Constitution asks for. The first screen leads with the product rather than
   the subject.
4. *Voice and integrity (13).* Labels of who is speaking, mapped from fields
   the 4,311 verse notes already carry; no supplied first person; stage-two
   witnesses only through the U07 verifier; "Not yet written for this verse"
   said in one line. The John 1:14 BSB text it quotes and the John 1 church
   readings it cites (Heracleon, Irenaeus, Origen) match the repo. Docked for
   the Jeremiah example.
5. *Decisions and time (8).* Keeps his five doors and gives doctrine a named
   room in the Study menu instead of a sixth door. No page redesign before
   Advent. Asks to replace his black, biography-first hero (#533) with a cream
   desk hero. Every new piece of his writing ships first with existing text.
6. *Attention (8).* Real and earned (a hard question in Cormorant at the head
   of a chapter, Trace the argument, Compare), but closer to Sefaria and Logos
   in kind; less of a fresh vision than the Line.
7. *Organized (10).* The best in the four: one surface, one search, one
   relation model, four passage tools folded into one.
8. *PCN (9).* Teach depth, the leader's sheet, room mode, study sheets.

### 3.3 The Week: 75

**Strongest.** *For Sunday* (4.9, 5.13): the week on one printed page with a QR
code to its address, and courses a pastor starts for his whole church on a
Sunday he picks, with one link, one calendar file and one printed schedule
(4.7). That is the best PCN artifact in the four. Beside it, the discipline of
computing every example from the generated data: it alone found that A 13's
"days 302 to 308 are Jeremiah" is Luke, the error two other directions
repeated.

**Weakest.** The skeptic, whom the contract ranks first, meets a devotional
rhythm: a dateline, a week card of Read, Learn, Table, Think and Pray, a daily
office with an Examen, saints remembered on their days. For a Baptist pastor's
site these are identity choices (the direction says so), and the office frame
and some thirty Examen lines are new words on his platform. Church history
and the post-Christian subject become strands of a week rather than doors.

**By criterion.**
1. *Brief (15).* Strongest on "how to live it" (living after Christendom as
   keeping a different week, with James K. A. Smith's argument offered as
   paraphrase) and on the family, which the Constitution says must grow until
   it stands beside every other pillar. Weaker on understanding what
   post-Christian America is.
2. *History (5).* *Remember* and *This week's source*; the era template.
3. *Mission (12).* "Recovering the practices of historic Christianity" is in
   the Constitution's central question, and the Formation Standard is this
   direction's whole argument. The skeptic risk is real and named.
4. *Voice and integrity (12).* The best data discipline and the clearest
   anti-streak rules, written as checks. Docked for the Examen and office lines
   as new writing, and because its home sentence drops "raised without a
   father," one of the two facts `CLAUDE.md` says shape everything he writes.
5. *Decisions and time (7).* Keeps his doors and is built for a cadence one
   writer can keep. Adds a dateline to the header, four new top-level
   addresses, and a Home change targeted at Advent Sunday, his busiest week.
6. *Attention (7).* The date as the hook is honest and calm; as a first
   impression of an elite site it is a list.
7. *Organized (7).* It makes four clocks one week, which is real; it also
   lays a second structure across the doors.
8. *PCN (10).* The best.

### 3.4 The Field Guide: 71

**Strongest.** One Guide registry (`guide/index.json`) built from the four
separate question lists (the 40 care pages, the topics, the 35 Honest
Answers, the nine cases), every page a view of one file, never a copy. With
care graded by urgency and an exit finished to GOV.UK's standard, it is the
best answer to "the same job built several times" at the level of the reader's
question, and the best care discipline of the four.

**Weakest.** It turns James's signature move into a field on every entry. The
"field marks" (4.3) put a "Not this. This." line under *In brief* on every
question page, drafted for his read ("Christendom is not the faith. What has
ended is the arrangement."). His own voice standard puts repeated antithesis
and "not this but that" constructions on the avoid list once their work is
done and says those moves are "no longer the default gait"; NOTES records that
shelf-wide stock phrases "became a stamp when the set was read together." A
cap of two per page does not help when the page type repeats a hundred times.
Its question-first front door also risks the help-desk reading the visitor
critic warned about, and it renames and reorders the doors he chose a day
earlier.

**By criterion.**
1. *Brief (16).* Excellent on how to live it and the post-Christian subject
   (region 2, *What happened to Christian America?*); Study, doctrine and the
   chapter template are sound but second.
2. *History (5).* The river in Christendom mode, the witness card.
3. *Mission (12).* Symptom, cause, wisdom made into page structure is the
   Formation Standard taken literally, and the skeptic's region comes first.
4. *Voice and integrity (10).* Region heads that use only his published lines
   verbatim are right; the care cards and the rule that sensitive free text
   never enters a URL are right. The field marks are not.
5. *Decisions and time (5).* "Questions" for his word "Topics," moved first;
   field entries raised from the plan's cap of six to twelve; 35 steelmen and a
   re-sort of every care page for his review.
6. *Attention (7).* Recognition of one's own question is a real hook, but the
   whole reads like a well-run public service rather than a serious house.
7. *Organized (9).* The registry is excellent.
8. *PCN (7).* The leaders view and *Host a table* are useful; a pastor would
   hand the care pages to his people, less likely the front door.

## 4. The winner: The Long Memory

From this seat the Long Memory wins, narrowly, for three reasons the scores
only partly carry.

1. **It is James's thesis, drawn.** His headline is a claim about time; his
   hero paragraph says the early church knew; the Decision Log names "the
   church's long history" as half the site's purpose; the Constitution's final
   impression begins "older than my culture." The Long Memory is the only
   direction whose organizing idea is something he already preaches.
2. **It is the history study he asked for, and nobody else holds it.** Wave A's
   verdict ("the church's long memory set beside the verse, dated, attributed
   and verified") and wave B's (the shape of two thousand years, the facing
   page, the source in the reader's hand) both land here, and U07's verifier
   is what makes it checkable rather than a mood.
3. **It is the one he would put on the screen at a PCN gathering.** A pastor
   in a shrinking church sees the bracket of Christendom end and the line keep
   going, with one tick that says *You are here*. That reframes decline as
   exile without a word of flattery, and it names the church's failures in the
   same ink.

The Study Desk is the better tool and the better organization, and the merge
must take its chapter page whole (section 5.1). Where the two disagree, on the
spine of the site and the home page's first screen, the Long Memory's choice
stands; where they agree, on the verse panel, the depth switch, the church on
the verse, one search and one long-read template, the Desk's specification is
the more complete and should be the one built.

**Conditions on the win.** Three, each from this seat.

- **The doors stay his five** (Study, Topics, Family, Essays, About) unless he
  changes them himself. The Long Memory's own fallback holds: Study's menu
  opens with "The whole story, Pentecost to now," church history is the first
  section of `/study`, and About stays a door, because his face is the
  skeptic's reason to trust the rest. Its History door goes to him as a
  question with pictures, not as a plan.
- **No drawing ships before its ledger.** Every mark on the Line, every hinge,
  every lifeline passes `validate-history.mjs` with a certainty and a source,
  and every example in a design document, a test or a caption is computed from
  the generated data, never typed. The Polycarp line and the day-302 line are
  corrected before any of this goes to James.
- **The sentence on the hero is set in full-strength cream,** not muted
  (the Field Guide's correction, 3.1 item 4), and keeps "raised without a
  father."

## 6. What must not be lost in any merge

Whoever wins, these hold.

1. **Find Help in every header**, crisis lines first, and the care pages' first
   screen as it is (practical door first, the depth behind it).
2. **His headline words, his face, and his atheist sentence on the first
   screen,** with "raised without a father" kept; About one tap from every page.
3. **The skeptic's door on Home in his own words**, and every page the skeptic
   lands on asks him for nothing.
4. **His five doors**, until he changes them.
5. **Tonight at the table in two taps from Home**, table mode, "We did this
   tonight" as a plain button that counts nothing.
6. **No supplied first person.** Nothing in his voice that he has not written
   or read and approved; no invented scene, congregant, or biography; no book
   count, title or years of ministry printed.
7. **Every quotation verbatim from a stored, verified text, and every date with
   its certainty and source.** Paraphrase marked as paraphrase. Anything that
   cannot be verified is cut. Examples in design documents are computed from
   the data.
8. **The church's failures drawn in the same ink as its councils,** and the
   guardrail firing in both political directions with the same instrument
   (`validate-politics.mjs` on every arc, hinge and region head).
9. **Honest gaps shown:** *Not yet written*, uneven depth said in one line, the
   registry's thin coverage of women drawn rather than hidden.
10. **Position, never performance:** no streaks, badges, counts of people,
    percentages of a person, push notifications, pop-ups, autoplay, feeds or
    "latest."
11. **His voice placed and labeled:** after the text, after the church's
    witnesses, after the reader's own answer; never a bot speaking in his
    name.
