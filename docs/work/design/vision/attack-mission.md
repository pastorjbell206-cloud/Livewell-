## The verdict

VISION is honest in its sentences. Every quotation it prints is real and verbatim, it invents no biography, it prints no count, and its list of refusals (no streaks, no push, no modals, no feeds, no AI box, no counts of people) is the strongest in the niche. Where it fails is where honesty has to survive composition and process rather than wording. Its signature drawing argues one side of a contested reading of history, tells a Western story as "twenty centuries of the church," and is sold as "a picture of a fact"; the guard it names cannot see the problem. The first reckoning it builds is the one that flatters the church, though the one that does not is already in the corpus. The promises it makes to readers ("Nothing counts you," "counts nothing," "nothing recorded about his answer," *James Bell* as "his signed judgments," "never supplied by an agent") are not true of the site and the review process it specifies. Its answer template lands on questions the contract and James have left open. It files the skeptic's questions under Care, puts a newsletter form and the household clock on the pages it promises will ask him nothing, and specifies care pages whose traces outlast their own Exit button. One break is fatal, ten serious, and one bundle minor.

## The breaks

### 1. The Line is an argument drawn as a fact, and it argues in one direction (fatal)

**Claim.** 3.7: "the first object is a picture of a fact a stranger did not know he could see: how long the church lived without power, how long the bracket of Christendom ran." 7.1: "Every one of these is a true object." 4.1, *The honesty it carries*: the reckonings "Callinicum and Thessalonica, the Crusades, the slave trade, the German Christians beside Barmen"; "Wherever the two ends of the Line are set side by side, one sentence says where the likeness breaks"; "`validate-politics.mjs`'s both-directions check runs on every hinge title and arc." 3.2: the phone Line with "Without power" to 313, the Christendom bracket from 313, "In America," *You are here*, captioned "The early church knew. So did believers in every age who lived without power." Risk 3 names "the Line as persuasion" and lists these as what holds it.

**Problem.** Five failures, each against the contract's own words.

- *Fact or reading.* Where Christendom begins and whether its beginning was a fall are argued among historians and theologians (Yoder's "Constantinian shift" against Leithart's *Defending Constantine*). The vision knows it (risk 3; judge-skeptic: "the Line is an argument drawn as if it were a fact") and then calls the drawing a fact in 3.7 and 7.1. The validator in 4.1 fails a bracket "whose end is not drawn uncertain," but its start is drawn solid at 313, which is the most argued date on the drawing (toleration in 313, Theodosius's edict in 380, the ban on sacrifice in 392). The drawing has no speaker label, though 6.5 requires one on every block and the bracket's words are James's.
- *It contradicts James's own caption.* His sentence says believers "in every age" lived without power. The drawing marks "without power" only before 313. Inside the bracket it draws no one: not the Church of the East under Persia and then Islam, not the Ethiopian and Coptic churches, not the Anabaptists, not enslaved Black Christians in America, not the church under Communism. "Twenty centuries of the church, drawn to scale" is the Latin West and then white American Protestantism. The shelf's own *Christianity Was Never a Western Religion* and the era essay `the-global-church` say otherwise, and the skeptic who asks "is this a white man's religion?" will see it first.
- *Home skips the sentence that keeps it honest.* The phone Line sets the two ends side by side under a caption that asserts the likeness, and 3.2 gives no sentence saying where the likeness breaks. The most-seen instance is the one exempt from 4.1's rule. Without it the drawing tells a believer "we are the early church now," the persecution story the shelf's own *Is the American Church Really Being Persecuted?* takes apart, and tells the skeptic that the most powerful Christians on earth are casting themselves as martyrs. That is the flattery loop and the certainty trade in one picture.
- *Every named reckoning is the same kind of failure.* Callinicum, Thessalonica, the Crusades, the slave trade and the German Christians are all failures of the church holding power. None is drawn from the other families the guardrails name: accommodation, subtraction, the therapeutic turn, prosperity teaching. The constitution's thesis is that the crisis is "deeper than Christian nationalism, deeper than progressive Christianity." The front page compounds it: the essays band reads from the five pilots (`fea345a4`), three of which are on Christian nationalism, a Christian nation and post-Christian America, and the moment band and the Line say the same. The guardrail fires in one direction on the page where it matters most.
- *The named guard cannot see this, and if forced to, it would fail the other way.* `validate-politics.mjs` checks topic pages that carry `bothWays.right` and `.left` within a 55% word band, plus style (header lines 14 to 31). A hinge title such as "The slave trade" has no right or left to weigh, so the check passes a one-sided selection trivially. Extended to hinges as a word-band symmetry, it would force false balance onto history, which `VOICE-JAMES-BELL.md` puts on its avoid list ("Symmetrical criticism of right and left when the evidence does not support symmetrical responsibility"). The "at least two" reckonings out of twelve reads as a quota, not a judgment.

A drawing that every page repeats, that faces the skeptic first, and that is held by a check unable to see what it claims to hold is the site's identity resting on a tribal argument. That is why this break is fatal, not serious.

**Fix.** (1) Strike "a picture of a fact" and "a true object" from 3.7 and 7.1. The bracket, the band and the caption carry the *James Bell* label (6.5), with one line that says historians argue where Christendom began and whether it was a fall. (2) Draw the bracket's start as a range (313 to 392) and make `validate-history.mjs` fail a bracket with either end drawn solid. (3) Draw the caption's own claim: a thin row under the bracket for believers without power inside Christendom (the Church of the East, Ethiopia, the Anabaptists, the enslaved church in America), each mark ledgered. If that row cannot be drawn yet, retitle the drawing honestly ("the church in the West, and then in America"). (4) Make the rhyme-break sentence mandatory on Home, under James's caption, in his words (D5), and tag it *Draft for James* in the prototype until he writes it; the Line does not go on Home without it. (5) Replace "the both-directions check on every hinge title" with a reviewed selection rule: hinges chosen by historical weight with a ledger line, reckonings drawn from more than one family of failure the guardrails name, and the set read by a named person against the guardrail, with no word-count symmetry. Candidates from the other side are for the ledger to check, not claims made here: the 1914 manifesto of German intellectuals that Karl Barth said broke his trust in his teachers' theology, and the eugenics sermons of 1920s American pulpits. (6) Home's essays band is chosen by the reader's question, as *Start where you stand* is, not read from whichever pilots landed first.

### 2. The first reckoning built is the one that flatters the church (serious)

**Claim.** 4.8: "The first built is Thessalonica in 390, from three texts U07 already stores." Section 9, screen 5, sets Ambrose's "I dare not offer the sacrifice if you intend to be present." 4.10: on the politics page, the witness is "Ambrose and Theodosius after Thessalonica." 5.15 and LM 5.9: turn 5 is "The bishop and the emperor, Thessalonica, 390." NOTES' surviving consensus: "Callinicum and Thessalonica as the first reckoning."

**Problem.** The corpus already stores the other half: `ambrose-letter-40.html` and `ambrose-letter-41.html` (PROVENANCE.md, "on the synagogue at Callinicum"). In 388 Christians burned a synagogue, Theodosius ordered the bishop to rebuild it, and Ambrose pressed him to withdraw the order. Letter 40 has Ambrose saying, verbatim in the stored text, "I declare that I set fire to the synagogue, or at least that I ordered those who did it, that there might not be a place where Christ was denied," and calling the synagogue "a home of unbelief, a house of impiety, a receptacle of folly, which God Himself has condemned." The vision builds only the scene where the bishop shames the emperor, offers it to the politics page as the model witness, and gives James twelve candidate turns in which the church is never the wrongdoer except as the backdrop to a hero (Thessalonica, Barmen). That is the flattery loop the contract names, in the history study James wants to be the best available, and the skeptic who knows Callinicum will judge the whole study by its absence.

**Fix.** Build the first *Compare the witnesses* as one pair, Callinicum in 388 and then Thessalonica in 390: the same bishop and the same emperor, the same hold over him used once to shield violence against Jews and once to rebuke a massacre, Letter 40 first. The politics page's *Asked before* carries both or neither. Ask James to choose at least one of the twelve turns in which the church is the wrongdoer, named as such.

### 3. The promises made to the reader are not true of the site specified (serious)

**Claim.** Section 0, read aloud: "Nothing counts you, chases you, or asks you to sign up." 4.11 and screen 9: "We did this tonight" as "a plain button that counts nothing." 4.6, on Test the Case: "nothing recorded about his answer." 7.3: "nothing on any page changes while the reader looks at it." 6.7 keeps `lib/telemetry.ts` "as built"; 10.6 adds events and names the measures "returning devices by week ... sessions and devotions completed."

**Problem.** As built and kept: `App.tsx` line 709 calls `trackReturnReaderOnce()` on every load, which writes `lw-seen` to every device on its first visit and counts every later visit as `return_reader`. "We did this tonight" fires `family_devotion_complete`. Test the Case fires `argument_objection_raised` with the index of the pushback the skeptic opened ("which pushback a reader actually holds," says the module) and `argument_case_complete`. The site's own `/privacy` says "We count page views ... with a random identifier stored in your own browser" and does not mention the objection or devotion events. Aggregate, nameless counting is defensible and MASTER-PLAN 9.9 allows it. Telling the reader the opposite is not. A skeptic who opens the network panel on the page that says nothing is recorded and watches his chosen objection go to Vercel Analytics has caught the site in the one kind of dishonesty he came looking for. The 7.3 line fails too: the header and bottom bar hide and return, the margin "follows the reading," and the history door's tick "follows the Part in view."

**Fix.** Make every promise literally true. Section 0: "Nothing here knows who you are. We count, without names, how often pages are opened and finished, and nothing else." Drop `argument_objection_raised` or say at Test the Case's start panel that the site counts, without names, which objections are opened. Change "counts nothing" to "keeps no count you will ever see," or drop the event. List every event name on `/how-we-write` and `/privacy`, with a test that fails when `lib/telemetry.ts` adds a name the pages do not list. Strike or narrow 7.3's sentence.

### 4. The *James Bell* label promises more than the review process gives (serious)

**Claim.** 6.5: *James Bell* means "his signed judgments." Rule 17: his voice is "never templated; never supplied by an agent; never a bot." 4.6: "Chapter answers gain the four labels only through James's sample review, never by an agent splitting his text." 4.2: "any new connecting line is drafted for James's sample read." D13 lists the writing the merge asks of him (76 *In brief* answers, the four moves on chapter answers, his readings under every *Read it closely*, the rhyme sentences, *Read Mark with a friend*, Trace the argument, the parent's answers, *If your table looks different*). 5.29: *How we write* "says who reads every piece (James)." The newsletter line: "Written slowly."

**Problem.** James's answer of 2026-10-26 (NOTES, item 1) is "sample per section": he reads a few pieces from a batch and the rest merges. An agent dividing 1,033 chapter answers into four labeled moves while James samples a handful is an agent splitting his text, which 4.6 says never happens. At the bandwidth risk 1 describes, D13's volume will be drafted by agents, and under sample review most of it will reach readers under a label that says James signed it. MASTER-PLAN 2's byline rule ("No piece published under James's name goes live until he has read it as a reader would") already strains against sample review; the vision then builds a label that claims more than either. The labels are the vision's answer to the skeptic's first question, "who is speaking?" A label that misstates who is speaking undoes the honesty the rest of the vision builds. Whether AI drafting is acceptable is James's call; that the page says what actually happened is not optional.

**Fix.** Define the label by process. *James Bell* goes only on text James wrote or read and approved individually, recorded by a ledger line with the date; a validator fails a *James Bell* block without one. Text approved by sample carries *Study note* (or "LiveWell editors"). *How we write* says who drafts as well as who reads. Strike "never by an agent splitting his text" or make it true by keeping the four labels off chapter answers until he has read each. "Written slowly" stays only if it is true of how the letter is made.

### 5. The answer template lands on questions the contract leaves open (serious)

**Claim.** 4.6 and 5.18: every Honest Answer in four moves, *Put at its strongest · What it gets right · The answer · What this does not settle*; the first screen is "H1, the steelman, the first lines of the answer in brief"; "On the six second-order answers, a positions block at equal length before the answer." U87: "the four moves as fields and a validator." 5.8: "the four moves on contested positions" on the debate pages. 4.8: the facing page puts "the losing side first ... and James's verdict below both."

**Problem.** `CLAUDE.md` on a contested doctrine: state each position so the reader "should not be able to tell, from the steelman alone, where the author lands"; name the order first; "Leave second- and third-order questions open." The answers in `client/src/data/answers.ts` include "Can you lose your salvation?", "Does God choose us, or do we choose him?", "Is hell forever, or does it end?", "What does the Bible say about divorce and remarriage?", "How should a Christian read Genesis 1?" (held for James in MASTER-PLAN 2) and "Did God command genocide in the Old Testament?" (held for his position in NOTES). A validator that requires an `answer` field, and a first screen that shows "the answer in brief" before the positions, make the template land where James has not, and rule 20 ("James lands only where he has said so") cannot survive its own validator. *Put at its strongest* is singular: it is the shape of a skeptic's objection, not of a dispute between faithful churches with two or more positions. The facing page's "losing side" names a verdict, and its verdict slot invites one on every controversy, including second-order ones (the Anabaptists in 1525, East and West in 1054). The doubting Christian's "Never: being resolved too fast" (2.4) is broken by an answer in brief on her first screen.

**Fix.** Two templates, keyed on `order`. First-order questions and the skeptic's objections keep the four moves. Second- and third-order answers open by naming the order, then the positions at equal length, then *Where James lands* only with a ledger record that he has said so, otherwise nothing. The validator forbids an answer-first first screen when `order` is 2 or 3. Questions held for James stay unpublished as pages until he answers. On the facing page, "the side the council rejected" for first-order disputes, and a verdict slot only where the question is first-order or James has written one.

### 7. The skeptic's "never" list is broken by the site-wide chrome (serious)

### 8. Care pages leave traces their own Exit button cannot clear (serious)

**Claim.** 4.12: on care pages "the Worry Journal opens inline, 'Tell one person' becomes a prefilled text, ... steps tick and are kept on the device," and *Exit this page* is "finished to GOV.UK's tested behavior."

**Problem.** An exit button exists because the reader may be watched on that device. **Fix.** On high-sensitivity pages, keep nothing by default: ticks and the journal live for the session unless the reader chooses *Keep this on this device*. ### 10. "The strongest case against, quoted from its strongest named defender" invites invented quotations (serious)

**Claim.** 5.24: "Where the essay turns on an objection, *The strongest case against* sits at that hinge, quoted from its strongest named defender with a source." 5.18: *The strongest work on each side*, "every one verified and ledgered."

**Problem.** The verifier covers the stored public-domain corpus to about AD 450. Modern defenders have no stored text, and the sandbox cannot reach most sources (NOTES). A slot that asks for a named defender's own words at every objection hinge across the shelf will be filled by agents under schedule, and an agent asked for a quotation it cannot fetch is pulled toward plausible words, the one act `CLAUDE.md` calls unforgivable. The slot also inserts new material into essays James approved "as written" on 2026-10-28.

**Fix.** *The strongest case against* is quoted only where the essay already quotes it, or where a ledger records the quotation checked against a primary text, with the method named. Otherwise it is a paraphrase labeled as paraphrase, with the work cited. Any addition to a published essay goes back through James's read.

### 11. The canon's fill is a number to protect (serious)

**Claim.** 5.3: "chapters read on this device filled in `--ink`"; on a phone "each book a 44px row with a thin bar for its length and its progress." Plate II: "read chapters filled." Rule 6: "Position, never performance ... no percentage of a person ... no filled dots for days done." Risk 9 names "the canon's fill" as streak creep, held by "rule 6's copy check and the position-only component tests."

**Problem.** A canon of 1,189 cells filled as they are read is a completion meter, and a per-book progress bar is a percentage drawn instead of printed. C-SYNTHESIS tension 1 gives the rule ("the reader is never handed a number to protect") and DH's tests (does it measure the practice or the person; would it make sense if nobody ever saw the number). The fill fails both. The holding measures cannot catch it: a copy check reads strings, not fills, and a test that a strip "reads the date or a position" does not cover a canon that reads completion. For the doubting reader, a mostly empty canon is a ledger of what he has not done.

**Fix.** The canon shows position: the last chapter opened marked, *Pick up where you stopped*. "Show what I've read" is a toggle, off by default, never on Home. Drop the per-book progress bar. A component test asserts the canon renders no fill unless the reader turned it on.
