# Judge: the skeptic's seat

Step 3 of the design program (`docs/work/NOTES.md`, "James's redesign brief"),
2026-10-27. One of six judges scoring the four directions in this folder:
`study-desk.md`, `field-guide.md`, `long-memory.md`, `the-week.md`.

**Who sits here.** An intelligent reader who does not believe, or no longer
does, a little wounded by a church, who has learned to look for the seam. He
typed a question into a search box ("what does post-Christian mean," "did the
resurrection actually happen," "can you trust the Bible") and landed somewhere
other than the home page. He decides on the first screen whether he is the
audience, in the first minute whether he is being met or handled, and over a
week whether anything here is worth coming back to argue with. The contract
ranks him first and gives him the tie.

**How I judged.** I read the four directions in full; `CRITIQUE.md` and above
all `critique/visitor.md`, which is this seat's evidence; wave B's synthesis and
its skeptic cluster (patterns 1, 2, 5, 6, 9, 15 and 19 to 22, and the cluster's
avoid list); A 3, A 4 and A 9; C 1 and C 8; MASTER-PLAN 0 to 3 and 10.6; and
NOTES. I looked at the current phone screens a skeptic would meet:
`home-phone-first.png` (the atheist sentence at the foot of the first screen,
under two paragraphs addressed to believers), `writing-the-rise-of-the-nones-phone-first.png`
(the essay named for his question answers "James is revising this essay"),
`tools-test-the-case-phone-first.png` (the best skeptic instrument on the
site, its first move below the fold), `theology-history-phone-first.png` ("The
story you were born into"), `about-phone-first.png` ("Author of 21 books") and
`header-menu-open-phone-first.png` (a mustard "Find Help for What You Are
Facing" as the first menu row). Then I walked each direction through four
arrivals: (1) a search for "what does post-Christian mean" onto the subject's
page; (2) "did the resurrection happen" onto Test the Case or an Honest Answer;
(3) a friend's link to the home page; (4) the same phone a week later.

## Each direction from this chair

### The Long Memory: 89

**The walk.** From the search, he lands on `/topics/living-after-christendom`
(with `/post-christian` and `/exile` re-pointed to it, P9). The first screen
holds the question, the definition first in James's own sentences from `/about`
if he approves ("Nobody owes Christianity a hearing anymore"), and the top of
the long arc. Below it come dated turns that open era essays already written,
*Asked before* with a verified witness (the Letter to Diognetus), *The
strongest case against*, and one sentence saying where the rhyme with the early
church breaks. That is the visitor critic's world-class page almost word for
word. From "did the resurrection happen" he reaches Test the Case with Move 1 on
the first screen, *Next move* beside *Not convinced, but go on*, and, at the
moves that rest on sources, *How far from the event?*: each source placed by
when it was written, with its certainty, *What this move does not settle*
beneath, and no date on the ruler that the ledger cannot stand behind (4.8). He
argues with the data instead of the pastor, which is wave B's resolution of
credentials against method (B tension 9). On Home, the first picture is a
proportion he can check. A week later the device shows his place in the course
and one ink tick on the Line; nothing is counted, and his door moves down a row
but never disappears (3.2).

**Strongest.** Honesty is built into the method, not the tone. The facing page
sets the losing side first in its own words and says so when a side survives
only in its enemies' quotations (4.6); *Compare the witnesses* states the gap
from the event; every event carries a witness label (participant, within a
generation, a later account, tradition); *Read it closely* puts four questions
before James's reading; every myth check corrects at least one myth believers
tell (4.10); the church's failures (Callinicum and Thessalonica, the Crusades,
the slave trade, the German Christians beside Barmen) are hinges drawn in the
same ink as the councils; the registry's gaps are drawn as *Not yet written*;
the confession past today is marked as confession. Each is a seam shown on
purpose, the opposite of what a church-wounded reader expects. And history is
the one subject he will read before conceding anything: "Whether or not you
believe it, this is the story that made the country you live in."

**Weakest.** Two things. First, the first phone screen spends James's best
credential badly: the atheist sentence in `--charcoal-fg-muted` beside a 56 x 70
portrait, the size the capture shows today and the critique asked to grow,
under a caption about "believers in every age" and a promise ("the church has
been here before") spoken to insiders. Second, the Line is an argument drawn as
if it were a fact. "Without power," the Christendom bracket and "You are here"
carry James's After Christendom thesis, and a reader who knows the history will
ask who decided where Christendom began and ended in America. The direction
knows this (dotted ends, the bracket's words left to James, the rhyme sentence,
the both-directions validator on every arc and hinge), but the merge must hold
it, or the most arresting object on the site becomes its slickest piece of
persuasion. About leaving the doors is acceptable only because "by James Bell"
is a live link on every page and the desktop portrait is at real scale.

### The Study Desk: 83

**The walk.** The topic page leads with its short answer, then *The texts this
question turns on* (Jeremiah 29, Daniel 1, 1 Peter 1, Acts 17). Test the Case
cites only sources James has verified ("Who says so") and opens every piece of
evidence on the desk (1 Corinthians 15:3 to 8). Home puts James's sentence in
ink beside a 64 x 80 portrait at the top, then Find, then "Not sure you believe
any of this? *Start here.*", then the week's verse, John 1:14, as the first
screen's main object. A week later, one Continue line; nothing counted.

**Strongest.** It is the most checkable direction and the one that separates
voices best. Five labels in small caps (Scripture with its translation named,
The church's witness, Study note, James Bell, Your notes), mapped onto the typed
fields the 4,311 verse notes already carry and enforced by a validator (6.6),
mean he never mistakes the pastor for the text or a paraphrase for a quotation.
The chapter's hard question set in Cormorant at the head of the notes with its
four labeled moves (4.4), hostile witnesses on the time rule beside friendly
ones, authorship and dating both ways on book pages, and the one-line empty
state ("Not yet written for this verse: the church's reading") are exactly the
habits that disarm him. Its rebuilt skeptic track (5.9: one screen, four rows
including *Hurt by church*, no email, the only offer a host kit for his friend)
is the best single skeptic page in the four documents.

**Weakest.** The first screen's hero is a verse to tap. "THIS WEEK · JOHN 1:14"
set large tells a man who does not believe that he has arrived at a Bible app
for people who do; the direction names the risk itself (8.5, risk 6) and its
guard is the door above the verse, not the page's center of gravity. It also
keeps Find Help as the one colored word in the header with no descriptor beside
the name, so the wellness reading the visitor critic described stands, and it
offers him little reason to return other than study: no letters, no reckoning
stops, no correspondence.

### The Field Guide: 78

**The walk.** The field entry ("Questions · What happened to Christian
America?") puts In brief on the first screen, then Underneath with the river in
witness mode, the strongest case against, and "Asked of both sides" on politics.
An Honest Answer becomes its own page: Put at its strongest, The answer in brief
on the first screen, What it gets right, The answer, What this does not settle,
the case opened at the matching move, and *The strongest work on each side*, two
to four books per side, verified and ledgered (5.3). Home gives him the sentence
in full cream with a 64 x 80 portrait, Find, then "Or start from where you are"
with his row first, in his words ("I don't believe, but I'm curious."). At the
end of a case, a private "strongest point against this" with *Send this to
James*.

**Strongest.** It answers him in his own grammar faster than any other
direction. His question is a door ("Questions"), his row is first on Home, his
region is first in the Guide and is headed by the best sentence James has
written for him ("If you do not believe any of this, I am not going to pretend
your reasons are stupid."), and every Honest Answer ends in the strongest books
on both sides. Its masthead descriptor and its rule that free text matching a
sensitive care page never enters a URL show the most care for how a wary
stranger reads the site.

**Weakest.** It makes his doubt one situation in a triage. The Guide merges the
Honest Answers into the Find Help registry; `/help` is the same component; four
of seven regions are care; the Guide index puts a crisis line on its first
screen and offers two sorting chips ("For whom?" "What kind?"). The learning
critic found that filing the skeptic's plan beside grief and addiction "tells
the skeptic his questions are a pathology" (`learning.md` C2); the Guide
generalizes that filing into the architecture, and putting his region first
does not undo it. The field marks ("Christendom is not the faith. What has ended
is the arrangement.") would set James's ground-clearing move on every entry as a
two-line slogan, the register a church-wounded reader has heard most and trusts
least ("not a religion, a relationship").

### The Week: 66

**The walk.** Arrivals (1) and (2) are protected: argument first, the season
only as a kicker, never the office or the table on his way in, *What's new and
corrections* offered as a next step (2.5). One small respect stands out: his own
written objection to Test the Case can come back in a week, if he asks (5.11).
Home gives a dateline ("TUESDAY, DECEMBER 1 · ADVENT, THE FIRST WEEK"), the
headline, James's sentence and his door, then the week card: Read, Learn ("Why
and how should we pray?"), Table, Think, Pray. A week later: "Last here: Sunday."

**Strongest.** Its refusals are the most enforceable of the four: six rules
written as a copy check and a strip test (no streak, no "you missed," no filled
dots, no push; 6.10), coherence shown only where the data makes it, never a
forced bridge (2.4), and seasons and courses that end on a stated date, which
give him a shape in time instead of a feed (B 4).

**Weakest.** The site's center of gravity is a devotional practice he does not
keep. A church calendar in the header and a prayer-and-catechism card as the
first screen's main object tell him, before he has read a sentence, that this is
a companion for the faithful. The direction names this as its first risk and
asks this seat to test it (8.4, risk 1); on the home page the test fails. Its
machinery for honest argument (steelmen, positions at equal length, the church's
failures) is thinner than the other three, because its effort went into the
week.

## The winner: The Long Memory

It is the one direction a skeptic can read without first deciding the faith is
true, and the one whose structure keeps showing him its seams on purpose. The
Study Desk is a close second and is the better instrument at the verse; the
right merge takes the Long Memory's spine and method and the Desk's labels and
skeptic page. The Long Memory wins on three conditions, which grafts 1, 7 and 12
below meet: every block says who is speaking; the home's first screen gives
James's sentence full weight and a face; and the Line's progress marks can never
turn into a score.

## What must not be lost in any merge

1. **His door on Home's first phone screen**, in his own words, directly under
   James's atheist sentence moved from the bottom of the screen to the top, with
   a portrait large enough to read a face. All four directions agree; the merge
   must not trade it for a hero object.
2. **Nothing asked of him.** No email, sign-up, verdict picker or "Where do you
   stand now?" on any skeptic-facing page; the lead magnet and in-page signup
   gone from `/skeptic-track`; one newsletter form per page, in the ending.
3. **The four moves on every skeptic-facing answer**, the objection in a form its
   holders would sign before the answer, and *Not convinced, but go on* in place
   of "That's fair, keep going."
4. **Verified or labeled.** Every verbatim excerpt passes `verify-sources.mjs`;
   paraphrase is labeled as paraphrase; a witness whose text is not stored
   appears as a narrated line and says so; hostile witnesses sit on the same
   rule as friendly ones ("Tacitus, no friend of the faith").
5. **Uncertainty drawn, confession marked.** Dotted ends, both dating schemes,
   authorship both ways, witness labels; the church's confession stated plainly
   and marked as confession in one clause, neither hidden nor softened.
6. **The same instrument in both directions.** The church's failures drawn in
   the same ink as its councils; every myth check corrects a believers' myth
   beside the skeptic's; `validate-politics.mjs` on every arc, hinge, region and
   topic.
7. **Positions at equal length before James lands** on every second-order
   question, with who has held each and since when, and the order of the
   question named first.
8. **Everything checkable where it is read.** Every reference opens its text;
   notes beside the text; the essay's record block (first published, revised,
   corrections, cite, report an error); `/whats-new` with its corrections view;
   *How we write*.
9. **The ending allowed to end.** End mark, silence, one next read, the book as a
   quiet line; "Send to my phone" on desktop only.
10. **Position, never performance.** No streaks, percentages, badges or counts of
    people anywhere; nothing on Home that counts the reader.
11. **His writing stays his.** Everything he types stays on the device and says
    so at the field; nothing about a person enters a URL or telemetry.
12. **The subject defined first.** One page for post-Christian America that opens
    on the definition in James's own words, with `/post-christian` and `/exile`
    pointed at it (James's call), and the long arc after it.
13. **Find Help on every page, but not the loudest thing a stranger sees**, and
    a plain descriptor beside the name.
14. **History addressed to every reader**: "Whether or not you believe it, this
    is the story that made the country you live in," not "The story you were
    born into."

## What no direction carried far enough

These come from the research and the critique, not from any of the four
documents, and the merge should pick them up because this reader will look for
them.

- **One fact sheet about James** (CRITIQUE #10 and question 10; NOTES). `/about`
  says "Author of 21 books" and `/books` says "Three books. Every word mine."
  The visitor critic calls this the most likely moment for him to close the tab
  with a knowing smile. No direction reconciles it. Until James settles the
  numbers, no page prints a count (MASTER-PLAN 2).
- **Who holds the writer to account** (B 5). A short, plain statement on About,
  and a contact for concerns that does not go to James, only if he names one.
  After RZIM, this is the question a skeptic asks of any single-voice ministry.
  None of the four puts it on the page.
- **Equal chairs** (B 22). One or two written exchanges a year with a named
  unbeliever, in fixed rounds of equal length, the same type and width for both.
  No direction carries it, and it would do more for this reader than any
  drawing.
- **Letters answered in public** (B 19). The Long Memory and The Week mention
  letters "if James wants them." It is the honest way to show him the argument
  continues; *Send this to James* (graft 9) is its intake.
- ***Believe* first for a reader arriving from the skeptic's door** (visitor
  critic, `/books`). The book written for him sits third today.
