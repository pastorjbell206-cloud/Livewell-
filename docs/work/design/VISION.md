## 0. The vision

**To read aloud.**

LiveWell is a place to study the Bible, the faith and the long story of the church, made for a country that no longer hands anyone the faith. Open any chapter and the words come first, set the way Scripture is set in a good book. Touch a verse and the library gathers around it, every voice labeled: what the words say, what the first readers heard, what the church said about it and when, in quotations anyone can check, and your own answer, asked for before anyone else's. Open the history and meet the people who carried the faith, in their own words, with both sides of each quarrel heard and the church's failures told in the same voice as its councils. One drawing holds it together: twenty centuries on a single line, drawn to scale, with the stretch of Christendom marked above it as James's reading and not as a fact, the churches that lived without power drawn beneath it in every century, and one small mark at the end that says *You are here*. Bring a hard question and find it named in plain words, answered at full strength where the creeds have answered it, and left open where faithful Christians differ. At the end of the day, tonight's devotion for the family table is two taps from the front page, and on Sunday a pastor can print the week on one sheet. Nothing here knows who you are: the site counts, without names, which pages are opened and finished, says so on its privacy page, never chases you, and asks nothing of you until it has given you something. The hope is a reader who leaves more thoughtful and more humble than they came, and able to say what the platform's constitution hopes every reader will say: *Christianity is deeper than my politics. Older than my culture. Wiser than my assumptions. And more demanding, and more beautiful, than I realized.*

**The promise, in one sentence.** Open any verse, any question or any year, and LiveWell sets it in the long memory of the church: the text first, every voice labeled and dated, the strongest objection heard, open questions left open, and one mark that says where you are standing.

**What you are approving.** A direction, not a finished site. Five things, in the order they would reach readers: the chapter page rebuilt so the text comes first and the notes sit beside the verse, before the 2027 reading plan sends readers there every day from January 1; church history given a shape, the Line, on its own door first; the care pages made safer and easier to act on, starting with three large crisis-line buttons in November; the skeptic's pages made to ask nothing of him; and the front page reordered so the skeptic and the tired parent each have a door on the first screen. You will see it as six screens on your phone (section 9). Everything ships in small pieces, each merged only when its checks pass, and nothing a family uses at the table changes during Advent.

### 1.2 The base: the Long Memory as the spine and the face, the Study Desk as the engine

The base is **the Long Memory**: its organizing idea (time, with the American present read against twenty centuries), its signature drawing (the Line), its drawing grammar, its history study, and its claim on the home page. Its reading surface is replaced, whole, by **the Study Desk's**: the chapter page, the verse panel, Read · Study · Teach, the labels of who is speaking, the search, and the build order. The other two give their best systems as bounded modules: the Week its household and congregational machinery, the Field Guide its question registry and its care discipline.

This is not the highest total, and the reasons the judges gave are why.

1. **The Study Desk's lead is the shared lane, not a spine.** The engineer said it plainly: all four directions propose the same reading surface, text serif, search and folds, and "the best base is the direction whose spine is the shared lane." That is an argument for building the Desk's surface first, which this vision does (section 10), not for making a desk the site's identity. On identity, the Desk lost the seats that judge identity: James's ("it makes the site a believer's instrument first"), the skeptic's ("tells a man who does not believe that he has arrived at a Bible app"), and the director's ("the signature is shaped like an interface").
2. **The Long Memory is James's thesis, drawn.** His headline is a claim about time; his own hero paragraph says "The early church knew. So did believers in every age who lived without power"; the Decision Log names "the church's long history" as half the site's purpose; he asked twice for "the best church history study available ... very interactive." No other direction makes that the centerpiece (judge-james 4). It is also the one opening no one in the niche holds: "the church's long memory set beside the verse, dated, attributed and verified, for a lay reader" (A verdict).
3. **The skeptic wins the tie, and he chose it.** History is the one door he will open without conceding anything first, and the Long Memory builds honesty into the method (the losing side first, witness labels, the church's failures drawn as hinges, gaps drawn as *Not yet written*) rather than into tone (judge-skeptic).
4. **Its low scores are about build and the household, and those are exactly what the grafts fix.** The engineer scored it 69 for a new core over a registry not yet built and every tick a public claim; the parent scored it 53 because the Line pushed Tonight down the first screen and the care pages went untouched. This vision takes the Desk's build order, puts the Line on the history door first and only after its ledger, lets it onto Home only with James's own words under it, holds Tonight high on Home's first screen, and takes the Field Guide's care work and the Week's household machinery whole.
5. **The attack on the mission found the Long Memory's real danger, and the merge now guards it.** A drawing of Christendom with the reader at its end is an argument, not a fact, and drawn carelessly it argues one direction and tells a Western story as the whole church's. The Line now carries two labeled layers (the record, and James's reading), draws the churches that lived without power in every century, and draws reckonings from more than one family of failure (4.1).

Read the merge as one sentence: **the text on a desk, the story on a line, the week at the table, the question at full strength.**

### 2.5 Search

One index and one box everywhere (critique #3): the header field, Home's Find, the Study Bible's Go to, `/search` and `/theology/search` are one component over one index. The local boxes on `/writing`, `/tools` and `/help` become scoped views that keep the query when widened; Find Help keeps its care-first ranking over the same index.

**The answer depends on the kind of query** (FG 2.6, resolving 1.4 row 13):

1. **Crisis words** outrank everything, every pin and every group (U15's rule).
2. **A reference** ("John 1:14," "jn 1 14," "1 cor 15") opens the desk at the verse with its panel open.
3. **A year** ("325," "AD 325," "1517," "the fourth century") opens the Line at that year (`/theology/history/timeline?year=325`): what happened, who was alive, which Part, the session that covers it. The recognizer runs after the reference parser and is fenced to three- or four-digit numbers, an explicit "AD" or "BC," or a century form, so "John 3:16" or a stray "23" never becomes a date (judge-engineer).
4. **A doctrine word** ("grace," "justification," "homoousios") opens with a definition card: the glossary's short line and the catechism answer, verbatim ("Grace is the free and undeserved favor of God ..."), then Doctrine, Passage (Ephesians 2:8-9), Word (charis), History, then the rest. The card reads a hand-filed `doctrine-words.json` (term, glossary id, catechism question, doctrine study) with a validator, so search does not wait on the doctrine room; U90's joins absorb it later (AF 4).
5. **A plain sentence** ("how do I talk to my kids about God," "my marriage is falling apart") shows *In Topics* first, matched on the Guide's `askedAs` phrasings (Help's `searchNeeds`, generalized), then Essays, Study, Tools.

Every result says where it lives ("Study › Church history › Part II") and carries its date where it has one ("Nicaea · 325 · Council"). On any list of dated results, *In history's order* sorts Scripture first, then the dated witnesses, then LiveWell's own writing, last and labeled (LM 2.5, A 23). Pins and synonyms live in a hand-edited `search-bestbets.json` with a validator that every pin is live; queries to pin come from Search Console, never from logged queries. Free text that matches a high-sensitivity care page is never written into a URL; the result carries the page's slug instead (Smart Answers' ADR 002). The empty field shows the reader rows from Home. ## 6. The design system

The palette, the three color roles, square corners, hairlines instead of shadows, no gradients, no stock photography, and "Unhurried. Weighted. Grown-up." all stay. What follows is how they are held, and what is proposed to James where the contract itself would change (6.10).

### 6.3 Grid, the reading grid and the phone shell

- **Phone:** four columns, one gutter token, `--gutter: 16px`, on every page (today 16, 24, 32 or 48px by page). **From 768px:** eight columns. **From 1100px:** twelve columns inside `--w-default` (1180px), 24px gutters.
- **The reading grid,** one page shape a reader learns once, used by the chapter, the essay, the session, the era essay, the creed, the context guide and the topic page: an optional left rail of 200px from 1280px · 48px · the text column at 640 to 680px (`--w-prose`) · 48px · a right margin of 280 to 320px.
- **One margin per side** (FG): the right margin holds only the page's own notes and the verse panel; the left rail holds only contents (a chapter's movements, a session's parts, a long care or topic page's stations). Essays get no contents rail (C 4.5: refused on essays); their "In this essay" sits in the head, closed.
- **Every apparatus has one home** (C tension 3): the kicker line, the head's tool row, the margin, the foot. An item that cannot name its home is not added; a new panel needs a line in its template's fixed order first (MASTER-PLAN 6.2).
- **The phone shell:** the first screen begins with the thing itself; on reading pages the header hides on the way down and returns on the way up; an auto-hiding bottom bar carries the page's own controls (a chapter: previous, the picker, next, Aa, Listen; a care page: Help now and the contents; an essay: Aa and Save) within thumb reach; the menu is a bottom sheet over a scrim.

### 6.4 Color, inside the contract

- **Cream is the room.** Every page background is `--bone`. `--bone-warm` alternates on Home and the hubs. Inside a long read the background never changes, from title to end mark (P5).
- **Black is weight, spent in five places only** (P4): Home's hero, the ending merged with the footer on every page, at most one verdict band on a page that has earned it, room mode on a television, and About's hero, where the man is the subject. Every other page head moves to cream through `PageHead`, `/help`'s included (U103).
- **White** (`--card`) is for things lifted: the *In brief* card, the verse panel's sheet, inputs, the print preview of For Sunday.
- **Ink** sets body text. `--ink-muted` is for metadata only, never primary text; on black, primary text is full `--charcoal-fg`.
- **Mustard means *now* and *this*** (P3). Its marks: the *You are here* tick, one per drawing; the selected verse's 2px rule; the essay's versal and end mark; the current step's underline in a stepper; a page's first kicker and its short leading rule. Link underlines are 1px and do not count (never thicker than 2px on hover). Find Help in `--mustard-text` is the one standing exception, at the doors' size and weight. **One non-link mark per viewport:** where a drawing's tick shares a screen with a versal or a kicker rule, the tick wins and the other drops. `--mustard-text` (5.27:1 on cream) is the only mustard for text on a light surface; bright `--mustard` is text only on charcoal (7.33:1). Never a button fill, a background, a band, a card bar, a highlight color or a word in a headline. Section labels after the first kicker are `--ink-muted`.
- **No color coding.** Position, label and one tick carry meaning (A tension 5). The `--accent-*` and `--pillar-*` colors stay out of new work, and the misleading aliases (`--oxblood` and `--rust` resolve to mustard) retire as their files are touched.
- **The gate** (U70; AC 13, AF 16). Inputs under 16px and inline `outline: none` go to zero at once: one global input rule, and the 37 declarations removed or moved into classes that restore `:focus-visible`. Mustard cannot be checked statically, because a color in a component does not say whether it sits on cream or on charcoal; so its gate is an allowlist of components that sit on charcoal plus a render test that samples computed colors on the captured routes. U70 fixes what that test finds there (`/search`'s 2.09:1 labels among them); a baseline holds the rest, and each touched file pays its share.

### 6.5 Labels of who is speaking

## 7. How it draws attention, and stays unhurried, weighted and grown-up

James asked for a site that draws people's attention; the brand asks for a quiet room where the light is good. The three research waves resolved the tension the same way, and this vision adopts their sentence as its rule: **attention is earned by what the page shows the reader (about the text, about the past, about himself, about the day) at the moment he reaches for it, and never bought by motion, counters, rewards or novelty** (A 3, B 3, C 3).

### 7.1 What draws attention, honestly

Every one of these is real and opens real text; the one that is an argument, the Line's bracket, says whose argument it is.

- Twenty centuries drawn to scale, James's reading of Christendom labeled as his, the churches that lived without power drawn in every century, and one tick that says *You are here* (4.1).
- A verse that opens: "pitched his tent" under John 1:14, Irenaeus around 180 quoting it against teachers who denied the Word took flesh, in his own words from a stored text, with the date and the translation named (4.3, section 9).
- The chapter's hardest question, set in Cormorant at the head of its notes, at full strength, in 1,033 chapters (4.6).
- The creed with a hairline down its edge, ticked at the year each article was fought for (4.7).
- The same bishop and the same emperor two years apart, at Callinicum and at Thessalonica: the bishop's hold over the emperor used once to shield a mob that burned a synagogue and once to rebuke a massacre, told by the witnesses in the order they wrote, with the gap from each event stated (4.8).
- The two Gregorys, Ambrose, Jerome, Chrysostom and Augustine alive in the same year, 386, on one rule (4.9).
- A letter from the second century saying of Christians that every foreign land is to them as their native country, beside the question an American asks this year (4.10).
- The guess the reader made, sitting beside the answer (4.5).
- A care page that tells a frightened reader in one heading what to do now (4.12).
- Tonight's verse, set large enough to read aloud at a table (4.11).
- A week on one printed page in a church lobby, with a QR code to its address (4.11).
- A dated list of what the site got wrong (5.29).

### 7.2 The rules

Each rule names how it is checked, so it survives many hands.

1. **One signature drawing, still and to scale.** The Line is never drawn on, faded in or animated on load, never zoomed, never panned. *Check:* the pattern page for `Line`; a render test that the static SVG has no animation.
2. **Every mark opens real text, every date is a claim with a source, and every reading says whose it is.** No mark that opens nothing; certainty drawn; a ledger line for every tick; the bracket and its sentence labeled *James Bell*. *Check:* `validate-history.mjs` (U30, U75, U108).
3. **Every example is computed from data.** Weeks, days, ages, plan days and counts in captions, tests, prototypes and design documents. *Check:* real-date tests in `week.test.ts` and `line.test.ts`; section 9's data list.
4. **Ask before telling.** The guess before a reveal, the myth check before a session, the reader's answer before the notes (4.5). The reply to anything a reader does is information with its source, never praise.
5. **Interaction lives in three places only:** before a reveal, after the argument, and in a separate instrument. Nothing in an essay's body asks the reader to act: no fields, polls, quizzes or prompts between its first sentence and its last (C tension 2, AC 23); its references are doors, and opening one is the reader's choice. *Check:* the long-read template has no slot for it.
6. **Position, never performance.** No streak, no count of days, no percentage of a person, no badge, no "in a row," no "you missed," no "don't break," no filled dots for days done. Position is said in words and dates ("Week 3 of 8 begins Sunday," "Last here: Tuesday"). The canon shows where the reader stopped, not a fill; what he has read shows only if he asks (AM 11). *Check:* a copy check in `validate-design.mjs` that fails those phrases in `client/src` strings, a component test that any strip or tick reads the date or a position and nothing else, and one that the canon draws no fill unless the reader turned it on (U70, U79).
7. **Counts are of library things, never people.** No "join 10,000 others," no reader counts, no trending. *Check:* review, and the copy check.
8. **Nothing moves on its own, and nothing pushes.** No autoplay, parallax, scroll-triggered reveals, animated counters, pop-ups, modals or push notifications; reminders only as calendar entries the reader writes to his own calendar. *Check:* `prefers-reduced-motion` turns every transition off and every state is still reachable.
9. **A date is the hook, not freshness.** No "Latest," no feed, no infinite scroll; new things arrive on a calendar and stay where they were put; every page ends (C tension 4). *Check:* the shelf and Home have no "latest" slot.
10. **Mustard means now and this.** One non-link mark per viewport. *Check:* `validate-design.mjs`'s mustard rules; screenshots at 390 and 1440 in every UI unit.
11. **Black only where weight is earned.** Five places (6.4). *Check:* `PageHead` is the only page head; a black head needs its reason in the PR.
12. **Images are witnesses or absent.** Credited, licensed, captioned with their point. *Check:* the art program's ledger (P10).
13. **Every apparatus has one home.** Kicker, head row, margin, foot. *Check:* a new panel needs a line in its template's fixed order first.
14. **The skeptic's pages ask nothing.** No email field, sign-up, lead magnet or verdict picker anywhere on the rendered page of `/skeptic-track`, `/answers`, Test the Case or the Doubt essays, footer included (the footer on every page carries a link to the newsletter, never a form); the second clock (Tonight, the year, *The church remembers*) appears neither in those pages nor in their menu's thumb zone. Home is the stated exception: it holds Tonight in its hero because the parent needs it there, a trade made in the open (AM 7). *Check:* a test over those routes, rendered whole, for forms and the household components.
15. **Care raises its voice in one card,** by words and position, never by color or motion; the crisis lines are buttons, not banners.
16. **The ending is allowed to end.** End mark, silence, one next read, the book as a line, the newsletter once. *Check:* the essay foot renders in one fixed order (U24).
17. **His voice is placed and labeled, and the label says what happened.** After the text, after the church's witnesses, after the reader's own answer; never templated; never a bot. *James Bell* marks only what he wrote, or read and approved one piece at a time, with a ledger line (D16). *Check:* the validators that fail a *James Bell* block without its ledger line (6.5), and the byline rule.
18. **The same instrument both ways.** The church's failures drawn in the same ink as its councils, from more than one family of failure; myth checks correct believers' myths beside skeptics'; the selection read by a named person against the guardrail, without word-count symmetry. *Check:* `validate-history.mjs` (every reckoning names its family, and the set names at least two), and `validate-politics.mjs` on the topic pages and region heads that carry `bothWays`, never on history (AF 16).
19. **Baptist identity respected.** The church's witnesses are remembered through Hebrews 12:1, as remembrance and never veneration; the great seasons go by their plain names only (Advent, Christmas, Lent, Holy Week, Easter, Pentecost); no lectionary vocabulary; never an implied Southern Baptist membership.
20. **Contested questions left open.** Second-order questions get positions at equal length with the order of the question named first, and James lands only where he has said so; the essays held for him stay held. *Check:* the answers validator fails an answer in brief on a second- or third-order answer's first screen, and a *Where James lands* without its ledger record (AM 5).
21. **One translation per surface, named where it is quoted.** The BSB on study and household surfaces, the ESV in essays and sessions with Crossway's notice copied verbatim, references in link mode where the two meet (D4).
22. **What a reader writes stays his.** It stays on the device and says so at the field; nothing about a person enters a URL or telemetry; free text that matches a sensitive care page never becomes an address.
23. **Sensitive pages leave no trace by default.** On high-sensitivity care pages nothing is kept unless the reader chooses to keep it; a prefilled text never names the subject; Home's *Where you are*, search's recents (off until turned on) and My Library never list a high-sensitivity page, a recovery plan or a self-check result; Exit says what it cannot clear. *Check:* a component test against each page's `sensitivity` (AC 12, AM 8).
24. **Every promise to the reader is literally true.** What a page says about counting, keeping or sending is true of the code, and every counted event is listed by name on the privacy page and in *How we write*. *Check:* a test that fails when `lib/telemetry.ts` adds an event those pages do not list (AM 3).

### 7.3 Why it stays unhurried, weighted and grown-up

**Unhurried,** because nothing on any page moves unless the reader moves it, the week holds still from Sunday to Saturday, and every row says how long it takes and what is enough (ten minutes at the table, three on a hard night). **Weighted,** because black is spent on a headline, a man's face and an ending, Scripture is set as Scripture, and the type is large where it should be said aloud. **Grown-up,** because the site speaks to every reader on the same terms, shows its seams on purpose (*Not yet written*, two women among 36 people, the dates it is unsure of, the things it got wrong), asks nobody for anything on the pages where trust is being decided, and leaves the last word of every piece with the reader.

## 10. The build plan

### 11.2 Answered objections: where a critic was not followed, and why

1. **A line above the headline in James's words from About (AC 9).** Not taken. Out of its paragraph, "If you do not believe any of this" has nothing for "this" to point to, and a front page that opens on a defense reads as braced for a fight. The skeptic is met about 160px lower by James's own atheist sentence and by his own door, before anything addresses believers. The headline is James's; whether it should speak to the doubter is his to write, and screen 1 puts the question in front of him.
2. **One reckoning tick on the phone's Line (AC 9).** Not taken. The Home variant draws no events at all, so it cannot select them one-sidedly, while a single tick chosen to show balance would be the token gesture the mission attack warns against. The reckonings live where hinges are drawn, on the history door, from more than one family of failure.
3. **Every design gate at a zero baseline, mustard included (AC 13).** Taken for inputs and focus rings, which are mechanical. Not taken for mustard: a static checker cannot see whether a mustard color sits on cream or on charcoal (AF 16), and a blind swap would darken mustard exactly where bright mustard is correct. U70 fixes what its render test finds on the captured routes, `/search`'s labels among them, and the baseline holds the rest while each touched file pays its share.
4. **Compare in U77, before January 1 (AC 7).** Not taken. It needs public-domain texts the site does not yet ship, each with its shards and a drift check, and U77 already carries the desk's critical path to the freeze. *Copy*, *Save*, the note and export need no new data and are in U77; *Compare* follows in U106.
5. **A unit to move the fifteen live database essays into JSON overrides (AF 5).** Not taken. An override shadows its database row, so James's own edits in the admin would stop showing, which breaks the Decision Log's promise that his posts appear on their own. The essay program already moves them one at a time, as each is rewritten.
6. **No question pages until James has chosen one home for doctrine (AC 2).** Not taken as stated. The question pages are not a third doctrine system; they are the catechism's one copy, and `/family/catechism` becomes a list that links to them. The choice between the doctrine studies and the debate pages stays his (D3), and the question pages presume nothing about it.
7. **Five prototype screens (AF 12).** Six. The sixth, the doubter's door and two Honest Answers, is where James sees how his name labels writing (D16) and how an open question keeps its shape (AM 5), and both are his to judge. All six are static and the whole is sized L.
8. **The full tagline at 11 to 12px under the wordmark (AC 10).** Taken in substance with one change: at 390px a descriptor of 51 to 63 characters cannot sit beside the header's controls, so P8 asks James for one under 28 characters, set at 10px caps. The long tagline stays in the footer and on About.
9. ***The short version* kept where a longer essay covers the same span (AC 4).** Went further: the broad essays are longer, not shorter, so the label retires and *Overview* marks only the four that span finer essays.
10. **Two figures in the attacks.** "30 high-sensitivity care pages" (AC 12) is 15 of 40 in `client/public/needs/`; the draft's "35 Honest Answers" is 36. Neither changes a fix.
