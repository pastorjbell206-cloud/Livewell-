## Read

**1 Timothy 2:1-4** (Berean Standard Bible)

> First of all, then, I urge that petitions, prayers, intercessions, and
> thanksgiving be offered for everyone— for kings and all those in
> authority—so that we may lead tranquil and quiet lives in all godliness and
> dignity. This is good and pleasing in the sight of God our Savior, who wants
> everyone to be saved and to come to the knowledge of the truth.

## Talk

**God asks us to pray for the people in charge, the ones we chose and the ones
we didn't, because He wants them to know Him too.**

Paul wrote these words to Timothy, whom he had left in the city of Ephesus to
care for the church there. In those days the man over everyone was the emperor
of Rome. No one in Timothy's church had chosen him. He didn't worship Jesus, and
the officials under him could make life hard for Christians. Paul himself had
been beaten and jailed by some of them.

Even so, Paul tells the church to pray for kings and everyone in authority, and
to do it first of all. He asks for thanksgiving too, which is harder. And he
gives the reason: God wants everyone to be saved, and everyone includes the
people at the top.

Praying for someone changes the way you talk about them. It's hard to mock
someone at breakfast when you prayed for them by name the night before.

*If the people you voted for won*

Thank God, and keep praying for them. Pray that they tell the truth, keep their
word, and remember the people who didn't vote for them. Don't ask them to be
your savior. They are people, and sometimes they will let you down.

*If the people you voted for lost*

Pray for the ones who won, by name. Pray for their families, and ask God to give
them wisdom when they decide things. You can disagree with a leader and still
pray for them. Paul asked the first Christians to pray for an emperor nobody
chose.

## Ask

*Younger (about 4 to 10)*

1. Who are some of the people in charge of our town, our state, and our
   country?
2. Is it easier to pray for someone you like or someone you don't? Why?
3. What is one good thing we could ask God to do for a leader this week?

*Older (about 11 to 17)*

1. Paul asks for thanksgiving for kings, not only requests. What could you
   honestly thank God for about a leader you didn't want?
2. How might praying for a leader by name change the way you talk about them?
3. Paul says God wants everyone to be saved. What would it look like to want
   that for a leader you disagree with?

## Do

Write down the names of three people in charge: one in your town, one in your
state, and one in the country. Make sure at least one is someone your family
didn't vote for. Keep the list where you eat, and pray for one name each night
this week.
