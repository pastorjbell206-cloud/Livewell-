## Read

**Matthew 10:2-4** (Berean Standard Bible)

> These are the names of the twelve apostles: first Simon, called Peter, and
> his brother Andrew; James son of Zebedee, and his brother John; Philip and
> Bartholomew; Thomas and Matthew the tax collector; James son of Alphaeus, and
> Thaddaeus; Simon the Zealot, and Judas Iscariot, who betrayed Jesus.

## Talk

**People who love Jesus can vote differently and still belong to each other,
because they belong to Him first.**

Read the list slowly, because two of the names should surprise you. Matthew had
been a tax collector. He collected money for the rulers of the land, and many
people looked down on tax collectors for it. Simon was called the Zealot, a name
for someone on fire with zeal for God. In those days that zeal sometimes meant
wanting the Romans gone, though we don't know exactly what Simon believed.
Still, it's hard to picture these two agreeing about much at dinner.

Jesus called them both. He didn't ask them to settle their arguments first. He
asked them to follow Him, and following Him put them side by side.

After an election you may find out that some of the grown-ups you love voted
one way and some voted another. That can feel like a crack running through the
family. It doesn't have to become one.

*If you are glad about how it went*

Be glad without gloating. Someone you love may be sad tonight, and their sadness
is real. The people who won can't save anyone; only Jesus can do that. So be
gentle with the ones who are hurting, and pray for the winners, because they
will need it.

*If you are sad about how it went*

Be sad without despair. Someone you love may be glad tonight, and that doesn't
make them your enemy. The people who won can't take Jesus off His throne. So be
gentle with the ones who are happy, and pray for the winners, because God asks
us to.

## Ask

*Younger (about 4 to 10)*

1. Matthew and Simon probably didn't agree about a lot of things. Why do you
   think Jesus wanted both of them?
2. Who is someone you love who thinks differently from you about something?
3. What could you say to someone in our family who feels sad this week?

*Older (about 11 to 17)*

1. What do you think it was like for Matthew and Simon to eat, walk, and pray
   together every day?
2. What is the difference between disagreeing with someone and looking down on
   them?
3. Is there anyone you have stopped listening to because of how they vote? What
   would it take to listen again?

## Do

Write the names of two people your family loves who see politics differently,
from each other or from you. Pray for both by name tonight, and this week do one
kind thing for one of them: a call, a note, or a visit.

together when we disagree. Make us glad without gloating and sad without
despair, and help us love the people who voted differently from us. Amen.

### Notes for James

  both-directions part. They name no party and no issue, and the politics gate
  holds them to the same length (47 and 47 words). Whichever way the vote goes,
  every family is in one of them, and many tables hold both.

  that later fought Rome, or simply a man zealous for God's law; scholars are
  not sure which Simon was (see the ledger).
