Addresses that appeared in results (none fetched):

- https://www.thegospelcoalition.org/article/new-city-catechism-print-devotional-apps-music/
- https://thegospelcoalition.org/article/final-installment-songs-new-city-catechism
- https://apps.apple.com/us/app/new-city-catechism/id564035762

What the results said:
- 52 questions and answers, one a week for a year. (S)
- Each question carries a Scripture reading (ESV), a short commentary from a past preacher, a commentary from a living one, and a short prayer. (S)
- A "Children's Mode" with shortened answers. On the web it is a switch on the same page: the children's version of question 1 lives at the same address with `?childrens_mode=true`. A "No Animations" switch sits beside it. (S, addr)
- All 52 answers are set to music word for word, playable in the app, with lyric videos elsewhere. (S, 3P)
- Three parts; Part 1 is "God, creation and fall, law," twenty questions. (S)
- Free app on iOS and Android. A printed children's booklet is aimed at ages 4 to 11. (3P, S)
- A translations section exists. (addr)

**Best at.** One question a week with a full kit under it: a long answer and a short answer on the same page behind one switch, a verse, two commentaries, a prayer, and a song that sings the exact words of the answer. A family and an adult study the same question in the same week at two depths.

**Borrow.**
1. **One switch, same address, two depths.** LiveWell's catechism already has an answer "for the little ones" of 3 to 16 words. Put the switch at the top of every catechism question and carry it in the address (`?little=1`) so a parent can send the child's version to a spouse. Use the same switch on the nightly devotion once the "Little ones (3 to 6)" lines are written (audit F2).
2. **The full kit under every question.** Each of the 76 questions gets the same five parts in the same order: answer, one verse (opening in the Study Bible), a short "why this matters" for the grown-up, a prayer of two sentences, and "go deeper" linking to the matching one of the 62 doctrine studies. The pieces exist; they are not joined.
3. **Audio of the answer.** The brief already lists "the catechism read aloud." Do it with a recorded human voice if James will read them (76 answers is about twenty minutes of recording), with the device's speech as the stand-in. Song is the stronger memory aid, but LiveWell has no songs and must not copy these. List it for James as a decision: commission simple melodies later, or do not.
4. **A week number.** "Week 14 of 76" with a start date kept on the device, so a family begins at question 1 and keeps its own place. This is audit fix F4 stated as a feature.
5. **Parts with plain names.** Group the 76 questions into three or four named parts so the index is a short page and not a list of 76.
6. **A reduced-motion switch as a courtesy.** Honour the device's reduced-motion setting everywhere.

**Refuse.**
- An app. LiveWell is a website that works on a phone; a second product splits the work.
- A panel of celebrity commentators. LiveWell is one voice.
- Copying any question, answer, commentary, prayer, melody or lyric. LiveWell's catechism wording changes only on James's word.

## 2. The Village Church: Family Discipleship (time, moments, milestones)

Addresses that appeared in results (none fetched):
- https://www.thevillagechurch.net/resources/articles/time-moments-and-milestones (seen as cdn. and checkin. mirrors of the same path)
- https://thevillagechurch.net/ministries/milestones

- A copy of the 2016 guide hosted by another church: https://fbcdrumheller.nucleus.church/download/3c736a916fe35e8b04e687d241a54b0d33a19f41.pdf (3P host)

What the results said:
- A three-word framework for a household. **Time**: a set block built into the family's routine. **Moments**: ordinary chances a parent notices and uses. **Milestones**: marked occasions that remember God's faithfulness. (S, and 3P copy of the guide)
- A free guide from 2016 titled "Helping Your Household Establish a Sustainable Rhythm of Time, Moments and Milestones," covering the biblical basis, parents as models, then the three parts. (3P host)
- The same three words organise the seasonal guides: an Advent guide for families with young children and a summer activity book. (S)
- The family ministry page speaks of each family as a "little church." (S)
- The framework is also a 2020 Crossway book by Matt Chandler and Adam Griffin. One reader called the three core sections useful but slim. (3P)
- I could not confirm a weekly guide sorted by age or a list of milestones by age.

**Best at.** Giving a parent three plain words that cover the whole job, and then using those same three words on every seasonal guide so the parent never has to learn a second system.

**Borrow.**
1. **A three-part frame for the Family page, in LiveWell's own words.** Not their words. Something as plain as "At the table. Along the way. On the big days." At the table = tonight's devotion, the catechism, the reading plan. Along the way = the 24 parent how-to guides that the audit says are not linked from the family page (F8), plus the Honest Answers a child will ask. On the big days = the next item.
2. **Big days.** LiveWell has nothing for a baptism, a first Bible, a first communion, a thirteenth birthday, leaving home, a death in the family. One short page each: a reading, three sentences to say, a prayer, something to give. Needs James for doctrine on baptism and the Supper. This is new writing, about eight short pieces.
3. **Along-the-way cards.** Twenty to thirty short prompts for the car, bedtime, after a fight, after bad news: one question, one verse, one sentence. Drawn from the 36 Honest Answers and the family devotions already written. Printable on one sheet (audit F12).
4. **One frame on every seasonal set.** Advent, Holy Week and Thanksgiving already exist; Lent, Easter week and Pentecost are missing (F11). Lay each out the same way so a parent who has done one knows them all.
5. **"For your church" page.** The Village's material is built to be handed out by a church. LiveWell needs the page the audit names in F18: how a pastor gives the family devotions to his people, with a one-page handout and a link he can put in the bulletin.

**Refuse.**
- Tying the material to one congregation's calendar, sermon series or classes. LiveWell serves any church.
- Their three words as a brand. Borrow the idea of a frame, not the phrase.
- The words "leverage" and "gospel-centered," which appear in the guide's own definitions and are on LiveWell's forbidden list.

- A family devotional of 15 chapters, each in three moves: instruct the mind, engage the heart, influence the will. Families may split into teams. (S)

1. **A one-page map of the family year.** LiveWell has 72 weekly devotions, 40 seasonal ones and 76 catechism questions, and a "year map" already generated in the build. 3. **Three moves in every devotion, named for the parent.** LiveWell's Table mode already has six screens (Read, Talk, Ask, Do, Remember, Pray). Keep them; add one grown-up line saying which screen teaches, which reaches the heart, which asks for a response, so a parent in a hurry knows which three to keep.

5. **A page for the children's pastor.** Same as the "For your church" page above: the chart, the print pack, and permission to copy.

**Refuse.**
- Selling the core. Here curriculum is paid and only the framing is free. LiveWell gives the whole thing away.
- Grade labels. "3rd grade" is an American school term and shuts out home-schoolers and other countries. Use ages.
- A catalogue as a home page. The search results for this publisher were almost all product files. LiveWell's family door opens on tonight's devotion, not a list of products.

- Drag and drop as the only way in. On a phone, taps and a list work for everyone and pass accessibility checks.
- A programme that can read as method without doctrine. Every LiveWell practice page ties to a text and to the creed.
