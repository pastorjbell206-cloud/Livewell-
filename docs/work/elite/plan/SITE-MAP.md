# The site map: how LiveWell is reorganized

Written 2026-11-18. One of four planning documents for the program to make LiveWell one of the best Christian study sites. This one covers the map: what a stranger sees, where every page sits, what folds into what, and the order to do it in so that nothing breaks.

Nothing in the site was changed to write this. It is a plan.

**How to read it.** Sections 1 to 9 are for James. They use plain words. Section 10 is for the builders and holds the files, the data shapes and the order of work. Section 11 lists what only James can decide. Section 12 says what this plan rests on and what was not checked.

---

## 1. The short version

The site already holds nearly everything James named. All of it is within three taps of the home page today. The trouble is the opposite of a gap: the same job is done at two, three or five addresses, the menus are long, and 45 live pages are reached by no menu or link.

So this plan adds almost nothing to the map. It does four things.

1. **Keeps the five doors James chose on 2026-10-26**: Study, Topics, Family, Essays, About, with Find Help on every page. No door is added or renamed.
2. **Cuts every door to six rooms or fewer.** The menu and the door's own page show the same rooms in the same order. No room sits under two doors.
3. **Folds every double into one survivor.** There are 41 doubles in the table in section 6. Each old address keeps working and sends the visitor to the survivor in one step.
4. **Adds five small indexes** that the site is missing: a page for each Honest Answer, one Questions library, one page of Bible reading plans, one list of people and one timeline inside church history, and a plain map of the whole site.

In numbers, if every fold is approved:

| | Today | After |
|---|---|---|
| Links in the Study menu | 9 | 6 |
| Links in the Topics menu and footer group | 11 | 5 |
| Links in the About menu and footer group | 11 | 5 |
| Tiles on the tools page | 36 | 29, in five groups, none doubled |
| Places to look up "what the Bible says about" a subject | 5 | 1 |
| Indexes of hard questions | 4 (and ten stray question pages) | 1 |
| Glossaries | 3 | 1 |
| Timelines | 2 | 1 |
| Lists of people in church history | 3 | 1 |
| Search boxes a reader has to know about | 3 | 2 (the site, and words in the Bible) |
| Live pages nothing links to | 45 | 3 (the three thank-you pages a book buyer is sent to) |

**Two rules hold the map together.**

- **Six or fewer.** A door never shows more than six rooms. Everything else is one tap inside the room that holds it.
- **One home.** A page has one door and one room. Every page says where it is in a small line at its top ("Study › Church history › Lesson 4"), whatever its address says. Addresses do not move to make this true; the line at the top is the map.

### 5.2 What folds into what

Each line is a fold: two or more things become one. The full list of doubles, with the survivor of each, is the table in section 6. The tools are in section 7. The big folds, in plain words:

1. **One Questions library.** Today hard questions live in the Honest Answers (35), a second list of 112 "questions people ask," ten stray question pages, and 1,033 hard questions written inside the Bible chapter notes that are listed nowhere. After: one index at Hard questions, one page per answer, and a third shelf, "Hard questions in the Bible, by book," where each question opens in its chapter.
2. **The Study Bible takes in three older tools.** The 20-book study, the 12-book study and the "verse in context" tool each cover part of what the Study Bible covers in full. Their good notes move beside the chapters and onto the book pages. The outside service one of them calls today is dropped.
3. **One "What the Bible says about..."** Five tools do this job in four Bible wordings. The one with 208 subjects in the site's own Bible text survives and takes the best of the others.
4. **One list of people and one timeline in church history.** The 31 "witnesses" join the 36 people, making 67. The 45 events of the injustice timeline join the 68, making 113, as a layer a reader can switch on. The old addresses open the same page with that layer or that group already showing.
5. **One church history course.** The fourteen lessons are the course. The four older long essays that retell the same centuries fold into the lessons they repeat. The four long essays on 1648 to today become a second unit: "The American church, 1648 to today."
6. **Topics become the place where the libraries meet.** The twelve "pathways," the 22 pieces on justice and on the church and power (unreachable today), and the three politics pieces that repeat shelf essays fold into topic pages, one at a time, with the writing work.
7. **Reading plans in one place.** They sit in three places today. One page, "Bible reading plans," under the Study Bible, lists them all. The family plans stay under Family and are linked from it.
8. **One tools list.** The self-checks page, the tools page and My Library each keep their own list with their own names for the same tools. One list feeds all three.
9. **Four pairs of study guides** cover the same ground and become four guides (decision 8).
10. **Questions for an essay sit under the essay.** The 90 separate "group guide" pages fold: where the essay is public, its questions print at its foot; where it is not, the address sends to the study guides.

Two joins belong to the other planning documents and are named here only because the map depends on them. One page is asked about and not planned: a page **for pastors and churches** saying how to hand the site to a congregation. It needs words only James can give (decision 16).

## 8. The order to do it in, so nothing breaks

Six stages. Each stage ends with every check green and a link for James to look at. A stage never starts a fold it cannot finish.

**Four rules that keep old links alive.**

1. **Carry first, then send.** No address is sent anywhere until the page it lands on holds everything worth keeping from the old one. The 112 questions are the clearest case: if their page were sent to the answers page today, 61,000 words would vanish.
2. **What a reader kept comes too.** Before a tool folds, whatever a reader saved in it (notes, a devotion series, past results, saved verses) is made to show up in the survivor or in My Library.
3. **One step, never two.** An old address goes straight to its final home. When a target changes, every address that pointed at the old target is re-pointed the same day.
4. **No dead ends inside the site.** Every link on the site that pointed at a folded page is changed to point at the survivor in the same piece of work, so a reader never takes the extra step.

**Stage 1. Lay the floor (nothing a visitor sees changes).** One list of rooms that the menus, the footer, the "you are here" line, the map page and the search all read. One list of tools that the tools page, My Library, the search and the start page all read. One ledger of every move in this document, with a check that fails if a move is half done.

**Stage 2. The moves no one has to decide.** The sign-up forms come off the study guides and the skeptic page (a standing rule already requires it). The stray addresses are sent home: `/quiz`, `/start/quiz`, `/roadmap`, the old reading paths, the self-checks page, the three orphan reading-list files, the 20 book samples. The old discipleship and pastors' addresses land where they should. Start here leaves the Topics menu. Everyday life and For skeptics get their rooms. The politics library is linked from its topic page. The ten stray question pages are read against their targets, topped up, then sent. The stale line about `/connect` in the project's own rulebook is corrected.

**Stage 3. The doors.** The three long menus are cut to the rooms in section 2. The Study, Topics and Family pages show the same rooms as their menus. The "you are here" line goes on every page. The tools page, the downloads page and "Everything on one page" are rebuilt from the lists made in stage 1. The new names in 5.1 go in.

**Stage 4. The new indexes.** A page for each Honest Answer, with the old bookmarks still working. The 112 questions carried into the Questions library, then their old page sent. The Bible reading plans page. People and the timeline joined inside church history (the data is joined now; the old addresses change what they open only on James's word).

**Stage 5. The folds James approves.** One at a time, each paired with the work that makes its survivor ready: the glossary; saved items; the whole-life check; the five verse-by-subject tools; the 12-book study and the verse-in-context tool; the devotion builder; the quiz; the second creeds page; the second search; the off-shelf books. The 20-book study goes last, after its scholar claims are checked.

**Stage 6. The folds that ride with the writing.** These move at the pace of the rewriting, a few at a time: the twelve pathways into topic pages; the justice and church-and-power pieces, if they come back; the politics doubles; the four older history essays into their lessons; the four study guide pairs; the questions under each essay as it lands.

Stages 1 to 3 need nothing from James except a yes to "six rooms a door" (decision 1). Stage 4 needs nothing. Stage 5 is his list. Stage 6 follows the writing plan.

## 9. What the best sites do that this map borrows

A plain caution first. The research for this program could not open any of the sites it studied; the session's network blocked them all. Everything it reports comes from search results: page titles, addresses and short summaries. That is fair evidence for how a library is organized, and no evidence at all for how a page looks. So this section cites only patterns of organization, and only as "search results showed."

| Pattern | Where search results showed it | Where this map uses it |
|---|---|---|
| The title of the page is the question, in the asker's words; one index of all questions; a trail at the top of each answer back to the index | GotQuestions (its own blog says the trail and the regrouping were added in 2020) | One Questions library; a page for each answer; the "you are here" line |
| Everything filed two ways, by Scripture and by topic; topic pages that open with a short definition, then group what follows by kind | The Gospel Coalition; Desiring God; Ligonier | "Also on this chapter"; topic pages as the place where the libraries meet |
| Church history as numbered lessons by period, each with its own address | Ligonier (one course in six parts); The Gospel Coalition (courses by period); Christian History Institute (numbered modules by era) | One course of fourteen lessons and a second unit; nothing else called a course |
| A short trail on every page saying where you are, and addresses that never change | New Advent's Fathers pages | The "you are here" line; old addresses kept for good |
| One start page that walks a newcomer through, one step at a time | The Bible Recap's Start page; Practicing the Way's "getting started" | Start here as one road, listed once |
| A big one-author library carried with two keys and one hand-picked "start with these" shelf | The Marginalian | Each room's index by subject and in order, with a short chosen shelf at the top |
| People and places as ways in, beside passages | Bible Odyssey | One list of people in church history; places wait on James's word about maps |
| One timeline, drawn from one list of dated events, with a view for each subject | World History Encyclopedia | One timeline of 113 events, with the hard record as a layer |
| The terms said on the door: free, no advertising, no account | The Gospel Coalition's courses section | One quiet line at the top of Study, Family, Topics and Downloads |
| One teacher's whole work in one place, entered by a Bible text or by a question | Gospel in Life | The whole map |

What the research says no site in its results does, and this map is built to do: put the Bible text, the notes, the history, the hard questions and the family table in one place, in one voice, free, with no account, and let a reader walk from any one to the others.

What the map refuses, because those same results showed the cost: a store in the path of study, anything behind a sign-up, a menu of many media types, long paged archives, and names for things that only an insider understands.
