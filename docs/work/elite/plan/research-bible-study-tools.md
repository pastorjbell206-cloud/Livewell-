# Research: Bible study tools (Blue Letter Bible, STEP Bible, Bible Hub)

Written 2026-11-18 for the LiveWell elite plan, step 1 (learn from the best).

## Read this first: what I could and could not see

**No page on any of the three sites was fetched.** All three hosts (www.blueletterbible.org, www.stepbible.org, biblehub.com) failed to resolve from this session: they are outside the session's allowed network domains. I did not substitute other sites, because the block is on the network, not on these sites, and a substitute would fail the same way. To do this research properly, those three hosts have to be allowed in the session's network settings and the pages fetched again.

Everything below comes from **14 web searches** (the cap), that is, from search-result titles, addresses and snippets, plus the search tool's summary of them. Some snippets are from the sites' own help pages; others are third-party reviews and app-store listings. Each claim is marked with where it came from. I describe nothing from memory. Nothing here says how a page looks (type, colour, spacing, density), because I saw no page.

What that means for the plan: the patterns below are sound as ideas, because they are attested by the sites' own help text. Claims about clutter, ads and phone behaviour rest on third-party reviews only and should be re-checked by someone who can open the pages.

## 1. Blue Letter Bible (blueletterbible.org)

### Seen (search results only; no page fetched)

Addresses that appeared in results, with the snippet content I relied on:

- https://www.blueletterbible.org/help/using_blb.cfm ("What is in the Blue Letter Bible?"): a Tools button beside every verse reference; six tabs under it: Interlinear, Bibles, Cross-Refs, Commentaries, Dictionaries, Miscellaneous. Cross-Refs is the Treasury of Scripture Knowledge. Commentaries gathers text commentaries, audio sermons and other study material for that verse. Dictionaries links related entries in dictionaries, encyclopedias and topical works. What Miscellaneous holds was not shown.
- https://www.blueletterbible.org/tools/interlinear/mgnt/jhn/3/16-18 : an interlinear address for a verse range in a Greek text; snippet showed a word with its parsing (tense, voice, mood, person, number) and a display mode called "Inline Interlinear."
- https://www.blueletterbible.org/lexicon/g26/niv/tr (and /csb/, /rst/mgnt): a lexicon page for one Strong's number, with the address carrying both a translation and an original-language text; snippet gave an occurrence count that differs by text ("117x" in the KJV, 116 times in 106 verses in one Greek text) and a "Line-By-Line Order" option.
- https://www.blueletterbible.org/help/lexiconc.cfm and https://www.blueletterbible.org/help/bible_search.cfm : a word search's results have four tabs: Primary, Dictionaries, LexiConc, FAQs. LexiConc lists each Hebrew and Greek root behind an English word with the verses that use it. A search can be limited to ranges (the help example limits a word to Matthew 5:1-15 and Romans 7-16) under an "Adv. Options" button.
- https://blogs.blueletterbible.org/blb/2025/10/23/new-tools-functionality/ : the Tools layout gained Previous and Next buttons, so a reader moves to the next verse's tools without closing and reopening.
- App store listings and aggregators (apps.apple.com/app/365547505, applevis.com, appfollow.io): the phone app is free with no in-app purchases; a "verse tap menu" offers interlinear and translation comparison; highlights in several colours; notes; a two-version parallel view. Reviews were mixed on screen-reader support and one reported freezing when swiping chapters. **Not confirmed by any result:** offline use, absence of ads, donor funding.

### Best at

One button beside every verse that opens everything known about that verse, sorted into the same six tabs every time, so a reader never has to learn where things are. And a word search that answers a second question the reader did not know to ask: "which Hebrew and Greek words stand behind this English word, and where is each one used" (LexiConc).

### Borrow

1. **One fixed set of tabs per verse, in the same order on every verse.** LiveWell's verse panel already holds notes and Greek. Give it fixed tabs: Notes, Words, Cross-references, Other translation, Go deeper. A tab with nothing in it for that verse is shown greyed, not hidden, so the layout never shifts.
2. **Previous and Next inside the panel.** Let a reader step verse by verse through notes and words without closing the panel. This also fixes the audit's finding that the only way in is a 24-pixel number: once the panel is open, the reader never has to hit a small target again.
3. **Search results in tabs by kind.** A search for "grace" returns Bible verses first, then tabs for LiveWell's own shelves: doctrine studies, Honest Answers, church history, study guides, family devotions. This is the audit's item 5 (search cannot find the site's own things) solved with a pattern readers of Bible tools already know.
4. **A word-behind-the-word tab.** LiveWell has Greek and Hebrew under every word. From that data, a search for an English word can list each original word it translates, with a count and the verses. No lexicon text needs to be copied; the counts and verse lists come from LiveWell's own Bible data.
5. **Limit a search to part of the Bible.** A simple chooser: whole Bible, Old Testament, New Testament, one book. Plain names, no range syntax.
6. **A "Go deeper" tab that points at LiveWell's own writing.** Where Blue Letter Bible attaches other people's commentaries and sermons to a verse, LiveWell attaches its own: the doctrine study, history essay, Honest Answer or study guide that treats this verse. This is the thing none of the three sites can do, because none of them has one author.

### Refuse

- Six tabs of other people's material of uneven quality and age. LiveWell is one voice; the panel holds what James wrote and the Bible text, and links out by name where it must.
- Codes and abbreviations as labels (a button reached by a letter, a tab called "Misc"). Every label is a plain word.
- Address schemes and options that expose the machinery (translation plus Greek text edition in the address, "Line-By-Line Order"). A reader should not need to know what a text edition is to see a word's meaning.
- An app. Reviews show freezing and screen-reader trouble; LiveWell's promise is that the website itself works on a phone.
- Audio sermons and outside media attached to verses.

## 2. STEP Bible (stepbible.org), Tyndale House, Cambridge

### Seen (search results only; no page fetched)

- https://stepweb.atlassian.net/wiki/display/SUG/Quick+overview and the STEP User Guide space (stepweb.atlassian.net/wiki/spaces/SUG): feature list including comparing versions, interlinear displays, vocabulary, New Testament grammar, transliteration, and a "Quick lexicon." A reader can open several passages and searches at once with a + symbol. The search tool noted these guide pages are old, so button layout may have changed.
- https://stepweb.atlassian.net/wiki/spaces/SUG/pages/12877895/Vocabulary+boxes : clicking a word opens a vocabulary box. Two numbers sit beside the word: its count in the current book and its count in the whole Bible (a second guide says book and testament). Clicking the first number searches every use of that word in the current book. A "related words" control links to words that share meaning. Everything in blue or red in the box is a link. Counts vary with the version chosen.
- https://stepweb.atlassian.net/wiki/spaces/SUG/pages/17694726/Searching+from+the+Lexicon and https://stepbibleguide.blogspot.com/p/finding-words-and-subjects.html , /p/advanced-search.html (a third-party guide): one search box takes an English word and offers the matching Greek or Hebrew entry to pick; it also takes Greek, Hebrew or a Strong's number. Subject searches return whole passages on a topic and draw on Nave's Topical Bible; word searches find one original word.
- https://stepweb.atlassian.net/wiki/spaces/SUG/pages/20512791/Word+cloud : a word cloud feature exists (title only; contents not shown).
- thegospelcoalition.org (Justin Taylor, 2013) and blogs.efca.org: made and supported by scholars at Tyndale House, Cambridge; free.
- App reviews (apps.apple.com STEP Bible id1476903313, justuseapp.com, appshunter.io): praised as clean, ad-free, with dark mode, and good for quick word studies. Complaints: offline mode does not always load all verses, some chapters stop after a few verses, readers ask for highlighting and for column views on a tablet, and one says the website does more than the app.
- **Not confirmed by any result:** hover behaviour, the colour-coded grammar display, how the website lays out on a phone.

### Best at

Making the original languages usable by someone who has never studied them: tap a word, and the meaning arrives with two numbers that are themselves links, so the next step of a word study ("where else does this book use this word?") is one tap. It is scholar-made, free and, by reviewers' account, without ads.

### Borrow

1. **Counts that are links.** In LiveWell's word panel, show "used 9 times in John, 116 times in the New Testament," and make each number open the list of verses. LiveWell has the word data under every verse to compute this at build time. This turns the Greek and Hebrew from something to look at into something to follow.
2. **One search box that asks what you meant.** Type "love" and the box offers: the passage (if it reads as a reference), the English word in the Bible, the Greek and Hebrew words translated "love" (each with a one-line meaning), and LiveWell topics by that name. LiveWell's Go to box already understands "jn 3 16" and "Revelations 21"; this extends the same box, it does not add a second one.
3. **Related words.** From a Greek or Hebrew word, link to the words built on the same root (the noun and the verb for love, for example), computed from LiveWell's own data.
4. **Topic search that returns passages, not single verses.** For LiveWell this is already written: the Topics index, Honest Answers and doctrine studies are the topical layer. A subject search should land on those pages with their key passages, not on a borrowed topical index.
5. **A second passage open beside the first.** On a wide screen, let a reader open a cross-reference or a second translation in a column beside the chapter and keep both. On a phone, the same thing as a half-height sheet. This is on the project list already (a second translation in a column); the borrowed part is that a cross-reference opens the same way.
6. **Say who made it and what it rests on.** STEP's standing comes from naming Tyndale House. LiveWell's chapter heading should name the translation (the audit found it does not), and the word panel should say plainly where its word data comes from.

### Refuse

- A workspace of many panels a reader has to manage. LiveWell's readers are a parent, a skeptic, a new Christian; one text and one panel is the limit, two columns on a wide screen.
- Typed search codes and prefixes, Strong's numbers as something a reader must know, and grammar codes shown raw. LiveWell already gives grammar in English words; keep that.
- Counts that change with a setting the reader did not know they chose. One text, one count, stated.
- An offline mode that half works. If LiveWell offers "keep this for later," it keeps a whole chapter or says it could not.
- A word cloud. It decorates; it does not teach.

## 3. Bible Hub (biblehub.com)

### Seen (search results only; no page fetched)

- https://biblehub.com/john/3-16.htm (also under www. and mail.): the verse page lists the verse in many translations, then a Cross References block listing related verses with their text printed beneath each reference; a commentaries section follows.
- https://biblehub.com/parallel/john/3-16.htm : translations grouped by kind (literal translations, New Testament translations, majority-text, Catholic, from the Aramaic), with a "Top of Page" link, which implies a long page.
- https://biblehub.com/interlinear/john/3-16.htm : word-by-word Greek with transliteration, English gloss, Strong's number and a grammar code per word (the snippet showed a word labelled "Adv").
- https://biblehub.com/strongs/john/3-16.htm : a per-verse list of each word with its Strong's entry and a short definition.
- https://biblehub.com/greek/26.htm and /thayers/26.htm : a word page with previous and next arrows to the neighbouring entries, a definition, and links to Interlinear Greek, Interlinear Hebrew, Strong's Numbers, Englishman's Greek and Hebrew Concordance, and Parallel Texts. A mirror showed the concordance listing every occurrence (116 for this word) verse by verse.
- https://biblehub.com/commentaries/john/3-16.htm : a strip of abbreviations for many commentaries (the snippet showed Lange, MacLaren, MHC, MHCW, Meyer, Parker, PNT, Poole, Pulpit, Sermon, SCO, Teed, TTB, VWS, WES, TSK).
- https://biblehub.com/m/john/3-16.htm : a separate mobile address exists for the same verse (address only; contents not shown).
- https://biblehub.com/study/john/3-16.htm and /library/john/3-16.htm : addresses only; contents not shown.
- Third-party reviews (learnofchrist.com, bestbiblecommentaries.com, a Google Play summary page): described as lining up dozens of translations with interlinears, commentaries and lexicons, "built for verse-level comparison and word research more than for daily reading"; called "busy and ad-supported," "functional but a bit dated," and on a phone "like using a desktop site," "not always thumb-friendly." Its commentaries are older public-domain works. The search tool noted it found no first-hand, Bible Hub-specific ad complaint, so the ads claim rests on reviewers' summaries.
- **Not confirmed by any result:** the top menu bar, the home page, the search box.

### Best at

Every verse has its own address, and every way of looking at that verse (translations, interlinear, word list, commentaries) has a matching address built the same way. That makes each verse a landing page that search engines find and that a reader can link to, and it makes cross-references readable in place because the verse text is printed under each reference.

### Borrow

1. **A page for every verse that has something to say.** LiveWell has verse notes and words under every verse but only chapter addresses. Give a verse a shareable address (the chapter page opened at that verse with its panel showing, with its own title and description for search and link previews). This is how a person searching "John 3:16 meaning" would arrive at LiveWell, and it feeds the share cards on the project list.
2. **Cross-references with the verse text shown.** Never a bare list of references. Each cross-reference shows its verse in full beneath it, tappable to go there. LiveWell's VerseRef sheet already fetches verse text; use it here.
3. **Previous and next on every study page.** Word to next word, verse to next verse, chapter to next chapter, history lesson to next lesson. The audit found church history essays have no "next"; the same control should be one component across the site.
4. **A verse-by-verse word list in reading order.** Under a verse, each word in order: the English, the original, how it is said, a short meaning, the grammar in plain English, and a link to everywhere it appears. LiveWell has this data; the borrowed part is laying a whole verse out as a list a beginner can read top to bottom.
5. **"Every place this word appears" as a page.** One page per Greek or Hebrew word listing each verse with the word marked. Built from LiveWell's own data at build time, it is also the Bible word search's result page for an original word.
6. **Translation comparison limited to what teaches.** Bible Hub shows dozens. LiveWell should show two or three chosen for contrast (a word-for-word, a thought-for-thought, and the one the site reads from), with one line on why they differ where they do. Check the licence of any translation before adding it.

### Refuse

- Ads. The reviewers' main complaint, and against the brief (everything free, nothing sold).
- Dozens of translations and a strip of sixteen abbreviated commentaries. Volume is Bible Hub's product; judgment is LiveWell's. No abbreviation appears as a label.
- Nineteenth-century commentary as the default depth. LiveWell's notes are its own; old commentators are quoted by name where a note needs them, checked against the text.
- A separate mobile site. One page that works at every width.
- Thousands of thin pages made only to be found by search. A verse page exists where LiveWell has a note, a hard question or a word worth teaching, and it carries real content.
- Long pages that need a "Top of Page" link. The audit already counts LiveWell's index pages at 12 to 18 phone screens.

## What the three have in common, and where LiveWell can pass them

From the search evidence, all three answer the same chain of questions in the same order: what does this verse say in other translations, what are the words underneath, where else does this word or idea appear, and what has someone wise said about it. The differences are in the door (a Tools button with fixed tabs, a tap on the word itself, or an address per view).

None of the three, as far as these results show, does what LiveWell can:

- **One author's notes on every chapter**, with a hard question on most, in place of a shelf of other people's commentaries.
- **A next step that is not another reference tool**: from a verse to the doctrine it teaches, the century that fought over it, the question a skeptic asks about it, and the devotion a family could read on it tonight.
- **Plain words throughout**: no Strong's numbers, grammar codes or abbreviations required to use it.

So the path to build, verse first:

1. Tap anywhere on a verse (not a 24-pixel number). A sheet opens at about 40 percent height on a phone, a side column on a wide screen.
2. Fixed tabs: Notes, Words, Cross-references, Compare, Go deeper. Previous and Next step through verses.
3. Words: the verse as a list in reading order; each word's counts are links.
4. Cross-references: each with its text shown.
5. Compare: two or three translations, chosen.
6. Go deeper: LiveWell's own doctrine study, history lesson, Honest Answer, study guide or devotion for this verse.
7. Each verse has an address a person can share, and search returns verses first with LiveWell's shelves in tabs.
