# Research: the Bible reading experience (group: bible-reading)

Written 2026-11-18. Sites: ESV.org (Crossway), Bible Gateway, YouVersion / bible.com, NET Bible (netbible.org).

## Read this first: what I could and could not see

**No page on any of the four sites could be fetched.** www.esv.org, www.biblegateway.com, www.bible.com and netbible.org all failed to resolve from this session (the hosts are outside the session's allowed network). They have to be allowed in the session's network settings before anyone can look at the real pages. I did not swap in other sites, because the block is on the network, not on these four, and an equivalent site would fail the same way.

Everything below therefore rests on **search results only** (14 searches, the full allowance): titles, addresses and short summaries of each site's own help pages, the publishers' own articles, app store listings and a few third-party reviews. I never saw a rendered page. That means I can report which features exist and how the sites describe them, and I **cannot** report anything that needs eyes: the typeface, line length, how a psalm is indented, where a note sits on a phone, how crowded the page is. Where the brief asked for those, this file says "not seen". Nothing here is described from memory.

The typography and poetry-layout questions stay open and need a second pass once the four hosts are allowed.

## 1. ESV.org (Crossway)

**Seen (search results only, no page fetched):**
- crossway.org/articles/esvorg-how-to-adjust-how-the-bible-text-is-displayed/ (summary)
- crossway.org/articles/12-things-you-might-not-know-about-esv-org/ (summary)
- crossway.org/articles/series/esvorg-tutorial/ (index summary)
- crossway.org/articles/introducing-subscriptions-for-esv-org (summary)
- crossway.org/articles/untitled-12/ ("Using Reader's Mode in the New ESV Bible App", summary)
- esv.org/Ps.%2023%3A1%2C%205/ (a snippet showing only the opening verse and the labels "Layout Options" and "Text Settings")
- App Store listing for the ESV Bible app; learnofchrist.com review (third party)

**What the results showed.**
- One settings menu (a gear at the top of the page) holds: font size, font type, red-letter mode, book illustrations, verse-by-verse mode, reader's mode, audio speed, and copy and paste options.
- Reader's mode removes verse and chapter numbers; the alternative is verses separated one per line. In the app, reader's mode hides verse numbers, section headings, cross-references and translation footnotes.
- A free account gives the full text, streamed audio, interactive reading plans and personal notes; highlights, notes and plan progress need a login and are saved to the account.
- Crossway's tutorial series includes building a Greek and English interlinear from a New Testament passage, and listening to the text read by a named reader.
- A paid tier adds study Bibles and commentaries. The sources disagree on the price ($2.99 a month in Crossway's article, about $5 in a review), so no figure should be quoted.
- The addresses are readable passage references (esv.org/Psalm+73/, esv.org/Colossians+3:19;Colossians+3:21/), and one address can hold two separate passages.

**Not seen:** the actual typeface, the poetry indentation, where footnotes sit, the phone layout.

**Best at.** Giving the reader control of the page from one place: a single settings menu that turns the same chapter into a clean reader's text with no numbers, or a verse-per-line study text. The address of a passage is the passage's name.

**Borrow.**
1. **One "Text settings" menu on the chapter page.** Size, reading typeface, verse numbers on or off, section headings on or off, note marks on or off, one verse per line. Kept on the device with the storage helper. This answers the audit's "Scripture printed as one block" from the reader's side.
2. **Reader's mode as a named switch.** One tap hides numbers, note marks and the drawers, leaving only Scripture. LiveWell's notes are heavy; a reader needs a way to put them away and simply read.
3. **Verse-per-line as the study view.** In this view each verse is its own tappable row, with a small mark on verses that have a note or a hard question. It solves the audit's 24-pixel tap target.
4. **Two passages in one address.** The Go to box already understands "Mt 5-7". Let it accept "Col 3:19; 3:21" and show both, so an essay, an Honest Answer or a devotion can link one address that opens every verse it cites.
5. **Copy options.** "Copy verse" with choices: with or without the reference, with or without verse numbers, and always the translation name (Berean Standard Bible) appended.
6. **A playback speed control**, if and when audio is added (see "Audio" at the end).

**Refuse.**
- A paid tier for study notes. LiveWell's notes are free and stay free.
- An account as the price of a highlight or a note. LiveWell keeps them on the device and says so.
- Book illustrations as decoration inside the text, unless James commissions them.

## 2. Bible Gateway (biblegateway.com)

**Seen (search results only, no page fetched):**
- biblegateway.com/help/tutorial/ and beta.biblegateway.com/help/tutorial/ (summaries)
- biblegateway.com/support/KA-01390, KA-01443, KA-01444 (help article summaries)
- biblegateway.com/share/ (summary)
- biblegateway.com/plus (summary)
- Bible Gateway blog posts on features (summaries)
- App Store listing; learnofchrist.com and bibleinyear.com reviews (third party)

**What the results showed.**
- A "Page Options" menu (gear) on every passage page toggles verse numbers, footnotes, headings and cross-references. Footnotes, verse numbers and headings are on by default.
- A parallel icon above the passage opens a second Bible window beside the first, with its own version list. Passage Lookup accepts a passage and up to five translations at once.
- Cross-references are marked in the text with capital letters in parentheses, such as (A); following one goes straight to that passage. Footnotes are the translators' own. Not every translation has either.
- Basic reading is free. Notes and highlights need a free account and are saved to it. An account can follow a reading plan and track progress.
- The free site carries banner ads. A paid membership, Bible Gateway Plus, removes the ads and opens more than 60 study resources. Sources give four different prices, so none should be quoted. One third-party review says a serious share of the reference content is behind the subscription.
- Verse of the Day has share buttons and a free email sign-up.

**Not seen:** the passage page itself, how crowded it is, where the ads sit, how poetry is set.

**Best at.** Putting any passage in any translation on screen fast, and setting two to five translations side by side. It is the web's reference desk for "what does this verse say in another version."

**Borrow.**
1. **A second translation in a column.** The brief already asks for this. The pattern to copy is the control: one icon above the text opens the second column, with its own version picker, and closes again. On a phone the second version should sit under each verse, not in a squeezed column.
2. **"Page options" as plain toggles with sensible defaults.** Headings and verse numbers on, note marks on, cross-references off until asked. Same menu as the ESV pattern above; build one, not two.
3. **Cross-references as a visible, followable mark.** LiveWell has a VerseRef component that opens a verse in a sheet. Use it for cross-references in the chapter: a small mark, tap, read the verse in a sheet without leaving the chapter.
4. **A look-up that takes several passages and several versions**, for the serious reader and the pastor preparing a lesson. One field, results stacked.
5. **A verse of the day with a share line**, only if James wants one; the family "tonight" line already does this job better for LiveWell's readers.

**Refuse.**
- Banner ads, on any page, ever.
- A subscription that removes the ads or opens the study library. Paying to make the Bible page quiet is the opposite of the brief.
- A study sidebar that advertises locked resources beside the text.
- Dozens of translations. LiveWell needs one main text and one or two for comparison, all free to show, each named on the page.
- An email sign-up placed in the reading path.

## 3. YouVersion / bible.com

**Seen (search results only, no page fetched):**
- help.youversion.com articles: Verse of the Day (Android), Guided Scripture (iOS and Android), Streak (iOS, Bible Loop, Bible App Lite) (summaries)
- blog.youversion.com/getstarted/ and the 2017 Streaks announcement (summaries)
- App Store listings for the Bible app (summaries)
- bibleinyear.com review (third party)
- **No result described the bible.com web reader.** Everything here is about the app.

**What the results showed.**
- Highlights in custom colors, bookmarks, private or public notes, prayers, and verse images a reader makes and shares.
- A free account syncs notes, highlights, bookmarks and plans across devices. No paid tier, no in-app purchase.
- More than 1,600 reading plans (one help page's figure; an app listing says "thousands"), short daily selections, plans done together with friends and discussed.
- Verse of the Day, with a ready-made image that can be downloaded and shared, and past days reachable from Home. It follows the last version the reader used.
- "Guided Scripture": a daily sequence of a short video, a devotional, the verse, and a prayer, sometimes with a reflection question; the prayer can be saved to a prayer list.
- Streaks: a count of consecutive days. On iOS a missed day resets the count to zero; one variant offers "streak freezes".
- Select versions download for offline reading. Sources conflict on whether audio can be downloaded.

**Not seen:** the web reader, the verse selection menu, the typography, any sign-in or app-download prompt.

**Best at.** Making daily Bible reading a habit for ordinary people: a short guided sequence each day, a verse image ready to share, and plans a group can do together, all free.

**Borrow.**
1. **A guided daily sequence.** LiveWell's Table mode already runs six screens (Read, Talk, Ask, Do, Remember, Pray) for families. Offer the same shape for one adult on the reading plan: read today's chapter, one note, one question, one prayer, done. It gives the audit's "shelves and no road" reader a road.
2. **Share cards for verses.** The brief lists these. The pattern: pick a verse, get a finished image in the site's own look (cream, serif, thin mustard rule, reference and translation named), with Download and Share. Built on the device; no account.
3. **Highlights in a few colors plus a note on a verse**, opened by tapping the verse. Keep them on the device, and give the reader one page, My Library, that lists every highlight and note with a link back and an export to a file, since there is no account to sync them.
4. **A plan a group can do together without accounts.** A share link that carries the plan and the start date, so a small group or a family all see the same "today". No feed, no comments.
5. **"Past days" on tonight's devotion and the reading plan**, so a missed night is one tap back and not a failure.
6. **Offline reading for a book**, since the chapter text already ships as files: a "Keep this book on this device" button.

**Refuse.**
- Streaks that reset to zero, streak freezes, badges, and any count that turns Scripture into a score. A parent who misses Tuesday should meet "Pick up where you left off," not a broken chain. This is the certainty-and-reward loop the brief's Formation Standard warns against.
- Public notes, friends lists and a social feed.
- Daily video as the front of the experience.
- A library of thousands of plans by many authors. LiveWell has one voice; a dozen good plans beat a catalogue.
- Push to install an app or make an account before reading.

## 4. NET Bible (netbible.org)

**Seen (search results only, no page fetched):**
- classic.net.bible.org/bible.php?book=Psa&chapter=23 and classic.net.bible.org/verse.php?book=psa&chapter=23&verse=2 (summaries)
- bible.org/sites/bible.org/resources/netbible/psa23.htm (summary)
- netbible.com resource pages on the notes and the translation (summaries)
- bible.org/news item on the app (old; summary)
- biblicaleldership.com article quoting bible.org's own description; learnofchrist.com review; app review sites (third party)
- **No result described the current netbible.org screen.** I could not confirm a notes pane beside the text, a Constable's Notes tab, or hover highlighting of Greek and Hebrew. Those are unverified.

**What the results showed.**
- Three kinds of note, each labelled: **tn** (translator's note: why it was translated this way, with other possible renderings), **sn** (study note: history, culture, context), **tc** (text-critical note: where the manuscripts differ).
- Notes are keyed to a specific word with a small superscript mark that links to the note. One review calls them inline, collapsible, and tagged so a reader can scan for the kind he wants.
- The full set of translators' notes is free online.
- The site's own description (via a secondary source) says the text sits beside the notes, commentaries, Hebrew and Greek word studies and Strong's references on one screen.
- On Psalm 23 the notes do two visible jobs: a study note explains the shift of picture from shepherd to host, and translator's notes explain a Hebrew verb form and the phrase traditionally rendered "shadow of death".
- User reviews of the app say the notes are valuable and the layout makes them hard to read, with the text sometimes smaller than the notes.

**Not seen:** the two-pane layout, tabs, the phone view, typography.

**Best at.** Honesty about translation. It shows its work: every hard choice has a note saying why, what else the words could mean, and where the manuscripts disagree, free, attached to the exact word.

**Borrow.**
1. **Label every note by kind.** LiveWell's notes sit in fourteen closed drawers. Give each note a short plain tag a reader can filter by: "Meaning" (what the word or phrase says), "Background" (history and setting), "Hard question", "Greek / Hebrew". A reader taps the kinds he wants and the rest go quiet.
2. **Notes beside the text, tied to the word.** On a laptop, text in one column and the notes in a second that follows as the reader scrolls; a mark on the word or verse lights its note. On a phone, a sheet that opens at about 40 percent, as the audit asks. LiveWell already has the verse panel; this makes it the main way in.
3. **"Why it is translated this way" on the hard verses.** LiveWell has Greek and Hebrew under every word. For the verses where translations differ, add one short note that names the choice in English and shows the second translation's wording. This is where the compare column and the word data meet, and no free site does it in a pastor's voice.
4. **Say where the manuscripts differ, plainly.** For the handful of well-known places (the ending of Mark, John 7:53 to 8:11), a calm "About this passage" note. A skeptic who finds this stated openly trusts the rest. Only with sources that can be checked.
5. **Keep the text bigger than the notes.** The reviews' complaint is the lesson: Scripture is set largest, in the reading typeface; notes are smaller and clearly secondary.

**Refuse.**
- Abbreviations a new reader must learn (tn, sn, tc). Use words.
- Sixty thousand notes at one volume. A superscript on every other word makes a psalm unreadable; marks should be off in reader's mode and sparse otherwise.
- A screen with four panes open at once. One text, one companion pane, the reader chooses what is in it.
- Strong's numbers shown as numbers. LiveWell already gives the grammar in English words; keep that.

## Across the four: what this means for LiveWell

1. **One settings menu, two named views.** "Read" (no numbers, no marks, paragraphs and poetry lines) and "Study" (a verse per row, marks on verses with notes, companion pane open). Every site here has some version of this; none pairs it with notes written in one voice.
2. **Tap the verse, not the number.** A tap opens one small menu: Notes, Greek or Hebrew, Highlight, Add a note, Copy, Share card, Compare. Three of the four sites hang these actions on the verse.
3. **Highlights and notes without an account.** All three account-based sites keep a reader's marks on their servers. LiveWell's promise ("lives in this browser on this device") is rarer and more honest; it needs a My Library list and a file export so the promise is not a trap.
4. **The compare column is expected.** It is ordinary on these sites. LiveWell's edge is the note that explains why the two differ.
5. **Plans that know today, and forgive.** Past days one tap away; no streak.
6. **Free with no ads is a real distinction.** Two of the four charge for study material and one shows banner ads on the free pages. A study Bible with notes on all 1,189 chapters, free, with no advertising, is something a visitor will notice in the first minute. Say it once, near the text.

## Open questions for a second pass (need the hosts allowed)

- How each site sets poetry: indent depth, second-line indent, stanza breaks, what happens on a narrow phone.
- The reading typeface, size, line length and line height each uses for Scripture.
- Where footnotes and cross-references sit on a phone.
- Whether netbible.org really shows notes in a pane beside the text, and how it behaves on a phone.
- What the bible.com web reader offers when a verse is selected.
- **Audio.** ESV.org streams audio free with a speed control; Bible Gateway and YouVersion offer audio Bibles. LiveWell has no recorded audio that I know of. Whether to add any (a licensed recording of the Berean text, or the device's own read-aloud voice for the catechism and devotions) is a decision for James, and the licence must be checked before anything is built.
