1What LiveWell is for

You said you want the site to be known for one thing: how to study and understand the Bible. So the site says that, in one sentence, on the first screen:

A free place to study the Bible, understand its world and the church's story, and work through hard questions.

That sentence is going under your headline on the home page now. Change the words and I will change the page.

It is built for five people. Most of them are in their twenties to forties, and most will arrive on a phone.

  • The skeptic, who has one hard question and expects to be handled.
  • The new believer, who does not know where to start.
  • The person who has taken their faith apart, and is not sure what is left.
  • The believer who wants depth, and is tired of thin answers.
  • The parent at the table, with ten minutes and tired children.

Every page is judged by whether those five get what they came for without being told how the site works.

Four rules that keep it tidy

  • Six or fewer. No menu ever lists more than six things. Everything else is one tap inside the room that holds it.
  • One name. A tool or page is called the same thing in the menu, on its own page, in search and in My Library. Today one tool goes by three names.
  • Tools have their own door, and are grouped by the job a person would name.
  • One page shows everything. "Everything on LiveWell" lays out the whole site as pictures and tiles, with a box to narrow it as you type. It is one tap from every page. Being built now

Old addresses keep working. Nothing is deleted.

5Easier to disciple someone

The site has plenty to read. What it lacks is a way to walk through it with another person. Six pieces fix that.

  1. A road that remembers your place. The road for a new Christian exists as six steps. It will remember which step you are on, on your own phone, with no account.
  2. A first course for two people. One Gospel in seven sittings: a passage, a few questions, a prayer. Made for a kitchen table or a coffee shop.
  3. A course you can share by a link. Pick a start date and send the link. Both of you see this week's reading first.
  4. A weekly rhythm on one printed page. What to read, what to pray, what to ask each other.
  5. Group guides with nothing in the way. The 68 study guides already have a leader's guide and a handout. The handout asks for an email first today. That comes off.
  6. "Go deeper" on every page. A Bible chapter will show the doctrine study, the history lesson, the hard question and the family devotion that touch the same passage. Today 719 of the 1,189 chapters show nothing beside them. This is one index joining the libraries together.

None of these keep score. No streaks, no badges, no "you are 47 days behind". A parent who misses Tuesday meets "Pick up where you left off."

6One home for all you do

You said you want LiveWell, Substack, Facebook and the ENDS Initiative to feel like one thing, and that you do not know how. Here is a way to hold it in your head. One home, and several roads leading to it.

  • LiveWellThe home and the library. Everything lives here, free, and stays here.
  • SubstackThe letter. New writing arrives in the inbox and points back to the library.
  • FacebookThe front porch. Short notes, each pointing to one thing on the site.
  • Following the WayThe podcast.
  • ENDS InitiativeThe mission work: training pastors in remote regions.
  • Books and speakingThree books on the shelf, and how to invite you.

What I would build

  • One page that holds all of it. "Find James" becomes the single link you can put anywhere: every channel as a picture tile, with one line saying what it is.
  • Your newest Substack pieces listed on the site, by title, each linking to Substack. They are linked, not copied in.
  • Share to Facebook, done well. A share button on every essay, and picture cards for a verse or a quotation, with the site's name on them, made to be posted.
  • The same two lines everywhere: "Read James on Substack" and "Follow James on Facebook", at the foot of every page and the end of every essay.

What only you can do, in about ten minutes

  • Put the one link in your Facebook profile, on your Substack "About" page, and in the podcast notes.
  • When you post on Facebook, link one thing on the site. One post, one page.

Three limits you should know

  • Facebook does not let a website pull posts from a personal profile. Your posts stay as links unless you paste me the ones you want featured.
  • I cannot open Substack or Facebook from where I work, so I have not seen your pages, and the Substack list will need checking once it is live.
  • Your message was spoken, and it came through as "the Ant initiative". I have read that as the ENDS Initiative. Tell me if I have that wrong.

8The order of work

  1. The frame. Seven doors, short menus, the Everything page. One shared page top, the Bible door rebuilt as the model, the purpose sentence on the home page. Being built now
  2. The doors. Tools, in groups and listed once. The Bible pages. History with its timeline. Questions with the ask box and a page for each answer.
  3. The pieces people will remember. Study a chapter in seven steps. Family, with the catechism said aloud. Essays. Start where you are. Find James, as the one home for all you do.
  4. Joining it up. "Go deeper" on every page. Then fresh reviewers go through the whole site on a phone, and the nine-part audit is run again for scores.
  5. The writing, alongside: three study guides for your read, then the rest.

Each step lands on the same preview link and I tell you when it does. The building is running on a second model until 24 November, because the first one's usage limit ran out. The home page you approved was built on the second.
