# LiveWell: a review of the whole site, and the prompt to act on it

Written for James on 2026-11-19, after he asked: "review the entire site ... find ways to improve it, improve the design or writing ... Is there more interactive? What needs to be done, what needs to be completed. Give me a prompt and a review."

Two reviewers did the work and changed nothing. One walked the live site as a visitor (about 70 pages, phone and desktop widths, every menu, four tools completed). One audited the content behind it by script (counts of what is finished, an editor's read of fourteen pieces, every open question in the project notes). What each could not check is listed at the end.

## The verdict

The look is right. The cream, black and mustard, the engraved plates and the type feel unhurried and grown-up, and the home page, Church History, the Mark book page and the chapter study are better than most paid Bible sites. Nothing is badly broken.

The weak spots are of four kinds:

1. **Older corners never brought into the new design**: the three quizzes, the theology quiz's results, the essay page header, the glossary, the downloads page.
2. **Pictures that decorate but do not yet teach**: about twelve plates carry the whole site, and the longest pages (the Bible's Story, the world portraits, Genesis, the Reformation) have no map or diagram.
3. **Good interactive features that are hidden**, and two that are missing: reading plans that keep your place, and verses you can tap anywhere on the site.
4. **Writing that is unfinished or uneven**: 41 of the 72 family devotions never name Christ; two essay sections are empty; the Bible door's new plain-words layer covers 26 of 1,189 chapters.

By the auditor's count of 2,017 pieces, about 36 percent of the site is in its finished form. Leave out the Bible chapters and it is about 86 percent. Nothing is empty: every chapter has its full older notes.

## The ten best things (to match, not to change)

1. Home: the whole Bible on one shelf, the seven-age history line, "Tonight at the table".
2. Mark's book page: the tappable outline, the timeline, the map, "Mark in a minute".
3. The chapter study: tap a verse number for notes, word meanings, cross-references, highlights; the Greek view.
4. The guided seven-step chapter walk.
5. Test the Case.
6. Find Help: crisis lines on top, plain words, numbers with the date they were checked.
7. The church history lessons and their source boxes.
8. The Honest Answers.
9. Family table mode and the catechism's "Read it to me".
10. Search that answers in Bible verses first.

The best prose on the site, by the editor's read, is the new plain-words layer (Mark 4 was the sample) and the argued answers.

## What to fix, in order

### 1. Broken or contradictory (all small)

- The theology quiz recommends essays that have been taken down, and its time label reads "12 min min read". It also has three different names and tells the reader "YOUR PRIMARY PILLAR".
- A wrong address shows the friendly "no page here" but tells search engines the page is fine.
- The empty My Library mentions an email button that is not there.
- Home says "TEN DOORS"; the menu has seven. "Context and creeds" on Home goes to a page called "Study the Bible".
- Home shows catechism question 47 while the Family page shows question 1, with no label saying why.
- /start offers five roads; /start/quiz uses a different set of six topics; the two never link.
- The party scorecard says "No team scores highest" and then prints a tally (4 Left, 0 Center, 3 Right). The tally is what gets screenshotted.
- One essay asks for its text twice and gets "not found" behind the scenes (the reader still sees it).
- The ebook download address answers with a raw error on a bad link. Terms and Privacy still describe ebook sales. The old network sign-up address still exists in the server code.
- Contact messages are saved and never emailed to James.
- Small wording slips: "practise", "Born" with a capital, "TAP TO TURN" on desktop, labels that show the team's filing words ("THE INTEGRATED LIFE").

### 2. Design

- **Three quizzes, three looks**, with off-palette purple, maroon and green. One question card and one result layout.
- **The chapter page on a phone** has about seven rows of controls before verse 1, and two different things called "Study".
- **"Mark ı"**: the heading font's 1 looks like a small Roman I.
- **Very long pages with no way to jump**: the glossary (114 cards, no A to Z bar), downloads (599 files, no filter), the two long world portraits.
- **Two of the three book covers** look like placeholders.
- **Dark mode is built and looks good but is switched off.** Evening devotions and bedtime reading are when it is wanted.
- The essay header is the one page top that does not follow the system; its drop cap breaks on a first word of "In".
- On desktop reading pages about 40 percent of the screen is empty beside the text.
- Home's lower sections arrive late and the page jumps.
- Mustard overused on the quiz results and the diagnostic.
- Table mode's type is small for a phone propped on a table.
- Tap targets too small: verse numbers, book spines on a phone, the reading-plan chips.

### 3. Pictures

- **The same plates are reused too often.** The quill stands for five different things; every Honest Answer carries the empty tomb, including "Is it okay to be angry at God?"
- **Pictures that would teach, most of them drawable from data the site already holds:**
  - an outline strip at the top of every chapter (the outline with verse ranges is already in all 1,189 notes)
  - maps for the other Gospels, for Genesis (Abraham's route), for Acts and Paul's journeys on the Roman world portrait
  - for Jewish life: Herod's sons' territories, a plan of the Temple courts, a cutaway of a Galilean house, a coin chart
  - one whole-Bible timeline, and a small one for the years between the Testaments
  - for history: a lifespan chart of the 36 people and 11 councils, a "family tree of the church" (451, 1054, 1517), a map of the four councils and of Reformation Europe, portraits of eight key figures
  - a kings-and-prophets chart, a family tree of the Herods, a festival year wheel
  - one plate for each of the eleven acts of the Bible's Story
  - a larger portrait of James on About

### 4. Interactive

- **Reading plans that keep your place** (only the 2027 plan does today; the other eleven only print).
- **Tap any verse reference anywhere** to see the verse, using the panel the chapter page already has.
- "What to read next" chosen by subject (today it simply follows the list).
- A one-time hint, "Tap any verse number", so readers find highlights, notes and the Greek view.
- A four-bar chart on the diagnostic result.
- Compare translations side by side inside the verse panel (the free ones: BSB, KJV, WEB).
- Share cards: the quote as a cream-and-black image for Facebook.
- A scrubbable Bible timeline (large).

### 6. Checking still owed

- Scripture in the study guides was quoted from memory of the ESV and never checked against an ESV text.
- About a dozen quotations printed as exact have not been checked against a source; four numbers disagree between essays.
- The site mixes four Bible translations (ESV, BSB, NIV in older notes, KJV in one tool) and "He" and "he" for God.
- Never tested on a real phone, in Safari, or with a screen reader.

## Decisions only James can make

1. Which facts the site may state about him, and whether his church is Southern Baptist. Several unconfirmed first-person lines are still live.
2. One Bible translation for the site, and "He" or "he".
3. Dark mode: follow the phone's setting, add a toggle, or leave off.
4. Whether the party scorecard and the theology quiz stay.
5. His position, or "no side", on: losing salvation, election, divorce and remarriage; the three held essays.
6. Which Gospel a new Christian reads first (/start says John; his plan says Mark).
7. Whether to commission ten to fifteen new plates, and proper covers for two books.
8. A mail sender so Contact reaches him.
9. His own sentences for the Find Help pages.

## What the reviewers could not check

James's photo on About (its host was blocked from the test machine); any form that sends or saves; all 599 download files (five were opened); audio quality; installing the app on a real phone; real slow-network speed; a screen reader; Safari on a real iPhone. The newest pages (Matthew, the Roman world) were confirmed live but not read closely by the visitor.
