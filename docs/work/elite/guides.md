# Three study guides rewritten: a pilot for you to read

James, these are the first three of the sixty-two older group study guides, rewritten whole so you can judge the approach before the rest are done. Nothing about their shape changed: same titles, sessions, session titles, Scripture passages, downloads, and the "When someone in the group is carrying this" help box. What changed is the writing.

## Which three, and why

Grief, Lament, and Suffering. The writing plan said to start with the guides for people in crisis, and these are the first three of the five it names for despair and the 988 line (grief, lament, suffering, aging and legacy, anxiety). They are also the three that most needed to sound like a pastor sitting with someone, and least like a template.

## What changed in all three

- Your teaching is in your own voice. No narrator calling you "Bell".
- Each teaching is seven or eight ordinary paragraphs, not one block of 900 to 1,500 words.
- Contractions wherever a leader will speak (about 50 to 130 per guide before, 430 to 560 after).
- Scripture quoted exactly, with the reference beside it, woven into the teaching instead of paraphrased loosely. The guides already used the ESV, so I kept it.
- The rulebook is gone: no "steelman", "verdict", "grant all of it", "inside the indictment", "the method is the mercy".
- Your life story is gone from all three. It was used as a credential ("written by a man who came to faith out of atheism"). None of these guides needed it to make its point.
- "Honest" no longer vouches for itself every few lines (62, 47 and 86 uses before; 5, 9 and 7 after).
- Case studies that read as true stories now begin "Suppose...", like the six newer guides.
- Every "Leader to confirm" note is gone, each book either checked or corrected. Every quotation and date was checked; the full record is `docs/work/elite/guides-ledger.md`.
- Every 988 line is still there, word for word, where the leader meets it.

## Grief

**Before (session 1 teaching):** "So here is the verdict, and the leader must hold it cleanly all the way through. Grief is love meeting death and recoiling ... The law names the writer before it names the reader."

**After:** "So grief isn't unbelief, and it isn't nothing. It's love meeting death and recoiling, and God did it first. If you were told your sorrow was weak faith, you can put that down tonight."

**Before (summary):** "Most of all it implicates the leader and the writer. Bell does not stand outside this. Pastors stand at gravesides and say the right words and drive home undone ... He writes from inside that ministry, not above it. So will you."

**After:** "Christians have written this way before. C. S. Lewis kept a notebook in the weeks after his wife died ... This study keeps company with them, and with every person in the room who is still carrying someone."

**Before (session 1, John 11:33):** "the word underneath that is not gentle sadness. It is closer to a snort of anger, the sound of a warhorse."

**After:** "The Greek word there isn't a soft one. Mark uses the same word when the disciples 'scolded' the woman who poured perfume on Jesus (Mark 14:5). It carries indignation."

Cut and why: the FAQ that asked "Why does the study keep implicating the writer?" (it was about the method, not grief); a wrong Tennyson date; a book listed under "various, in the commentary tradition" (it has real authors, now named); the Bonhoeffer book that does not contain the passage cited.

Quotations: Julian of Norwich's "all shall be well" kept (short and well known). Bonhoeffer on the gap a loved one leaves is now marked as paraphrase, because I could not confirm the English wording. No other person is quoted.

## Lament

**Before (summary):** "James Bell does not stand outside this. He was raised without a father, and he came to God out of atheism, and he does not exempt himself from the unanswered prayer and the graveside silence this study names."

**After:** "In 1986 the Old Testament scholar Walter Brueggemann wrote about what he called the costly loss of lament: what a church gives up when it allows only praise."

**Before (session 2 teaching):** "And here is the verdict this entire session exists to protect. Psalm 88 is in the Bible too."

**After:** "Psalm 88 is in the Bible too. The same God who kept the turn in Psalm 13 kept the dark of Psalm 88, with no note telling us this is the wrong way to feel. Its heading marks it as a song for the choirmaster. Israel sang it."

**Before (facilitator script, for a volunteer to read aloud):** "I have prayed prayers that got no answer I could see. I have stood at gravesides with nothing true to say."

**After:** "None of us is here as an expert. We're people who have prayed and not heard an answer, or love someone who has."

Cut and why: a Luther entry I could not support as written; a King James Bible entry that was filler; a "circa 1440 BC" date for Job that no one can know. Corrected: Brueggemann's The Message of the Psalms is 1984, not 1986.

Quotations: Bonhoeffer's "only a suffering God can help" now marked as paraphrase (the English wording varies by translation). Brueggemann's essay title is quoted as a title. No other person is quoted.

## Suffering

**Before (session 1 teaching):** "Take the strong case for the other side now, the atheist's case, because it deserves to be stated at full strength rather than knocked down as a cartoon ... Grant its weight."

**After:** "The atheist's case should be heard here at its strongest, because it isn't a cartoon. A world with this much undeserved agony ... is hard to square with a God who is perfectly good and fully able to stop it."

**Before (session 4):** Paul "was beaten with rods, whipped near to death five times, shipwrecked, stoned and left for dead."

**After:** Paul's own words: "Five times I received at the hands of the Jews the forty lashes less one. Three times I was beaten with rods. Once I was stoned. Three times I was shipwrecked" (2 Corinthians 11:24-25).

**Before (session 2 goingDeeper):** "A group should sit with his hard sentence, that faith is not the absence of the wound but the trust that keeps holding on inside it." (Presented as Wolterstorff's sentence; I could not find it.)

**After:** Cut. Wolterstorff's book is still recommended for what it does: it "refuses every comfort that would make the death smaller."

Cut and why: the Wolterstorff "sentence" above; "a book he would later find harder to live than to write" about Lewis (an opinion stated as fact). Added: Wiesel was not making a Christian point in the gallows scene Moltmann draws on, which a skeptic would rightly want said.

Quotations: Stott's line about not believing in God but for the cross is now described, not quoted. Bonhoeffer marked as paraphrase. Leibniz, Lisbon and Voltaire dated and kept.

## What only you can supply

1. **A memory, if you want one.** All three guides now speak in your voice without any story from your own life. If there is a graveside, a hospital room, or a prayer that went unanswered that you would want in one teaching (grief session 1 or lament session 1 would fit), it would make them yours in a way I can't.
2. **Your biography.** I used none of it. The brief allowed it once per guide where it does real work; in these three it only ever served as a credential. Tell me if you want it back anywhere.
3. **The leader's "tell me tonight."** The 988 lines in the closing scripts are spoken by the volunteer leader ("tell me tonight"). I kept them word for word as instructed. You may want a leader to say it differently.
4. **Translation.** These guides use the ESV; the Study Bible and the six newer guides use the BSB. Should the older guides move to the BSB as they are rewritten?
5. **Impassibility.** Suffering session 3 sets the classical teaching that God cannot suffer beside Moltmann's case that he did, and leaves it open. If you hold a position, tell me whether to state it.

## What I would do the same way for the other fifty-nine, and what I would ask you first

The same way: read the whole old guide; keep every session, passage and part; rewrite each teaching as seven or eight paragraphs in your voice; quote Scripture exactly with the reference; cut every rulebook word, biography stamp and "Leader to confirm"; reframe case studies as "Suppose"; check every person quoted, keep only what I can verify, paraphrase the rest with the source named; keep every help line word for word; run the validators and look at it on a phone. One guide takes roughly an hour and a half of careful work, so the fifty-nine are a multi-week job, best done crisis-facing first and then by theme.

What I would ask first: whether these three sound like you (if not, which paragraphs miss); the five questions above, especially translation and biography; and for the eleven guides that call themselves companions to books not on your three-book shelf, whether those books exist for readers or the tie-ins should come out.
