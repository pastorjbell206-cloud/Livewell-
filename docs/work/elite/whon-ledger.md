# Ledger: Honor and shame (portrait at /resources/context/world/honor-and-shame)

Every specific claim in `client/public/context/worlds/honor-and-shame.json`,
its source, and how it was checked (2026-11-19). 54 web searches were run with
the WebSearch tool; a direct fetch of ccel.org was refused by this machine's
network, so ancient texts were matched through search results that quote or
summarize named translations. Bible references in the prose and every quoted
passage were read against the Study Bible's BSB text in
`client/public/bible/text/` (the passages are copied from those files by the
build script, so they are word for word). "Site guide" means one of the five
Honor, Shame, and Culture guides or the Greco-Roman guides already on the
site, whose facts the portrait keeps consistent. "Standard" means a fact found
in every reference work and not separately searched; these are listed again
at the end.

No ancient author is quoted in English on the page except: Cicero, Against
Verres 2.5.165 ("the cruelest and most disgusting penalty", one published
rendering of crudelissimum taeterrimumque supplicium, confirmed in search);
Lucian, Peregrinus 13 ("that crucified sophist", A. M. Harmon's Loeb wording,
confirmed on the Perseus text in search); Sirach 42:9 and 11 (NRSV wording,
confirmed on biblia.com and Bible Gateway, and named as NRSV on the page).
Everything else from an ancient writer is paraphrase.

## Cut, or never written, because it could not be verified

- Quintilian on crucifixion along the busiest roads (the search found
  Declamation 274 is a different case; the reference could not be confirmed).
- Bailey's kezazah ceremony for a returning son (its claimed Talmudic basis is
  disputed).
- Bailey's reading of the younger son's request as wishing his father dead
  (not confirmed in the searches; the page says only that it was a hard break
  with the honor a son owed).
- Any claim that Simon the Pharisee meant his omissions as a deliberate insult
  (the page says interpreters differ).
- Junia Theodora's gold crown and the 1954 date of the stele's discovery (one
  blog only).
- Malina's term "dyadic personality" (not searched; left out).
- The year of Jayson Georges's The 3D Gospel and of Bailey's Through Peasant
  Eyes (not confirmed; neither is cited on the page).
- Philo's "crowded gatherings" (in the first draft; Colson's wording was not
  seen, so the page names only markets, council halls and law courts).

## Standard facts not separately searched

Seneca's death in AD 65; Plutarch's birth about AD 46; Justin's date and
place; Tertullian in North Africa; the Didache's usual date; Ben Sira's date;
Mary Douglas's year and the body-as-boundary idea; the Greek kataphroneo and
the Hebrew of Psalm 4:2; the title euergetes; "few duties weighed more than
burying a father"; the exact list of goods in the limited good article.
