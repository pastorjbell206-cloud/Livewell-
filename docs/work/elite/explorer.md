# Bible Explorer: the pilot, and the plan for the rest

James asked (2026-11-19) for a way to explain the whole Bible to young people aged 7 to 16 through pictures: the overarching story, the core doctrines, why Christians believe what they believe, something to click on and work through. This is the pilot of that room, at `/study/explorer`. The drawings, wording and structure are the site's own, in the engraved hand of the art kit; nothing is modelled on another ministry's videos, diagrams or scripts.

## What is built

- **The map** (`/study/explorer`): one road drawn down the page, from the garden to the city, with a roundel for each of thirteen stops. Every stop is named, placed and summed up in one line at both reading levels, so the whole arc can be read end to end today. A finished stop opens its own page; a stop still being drawn says so and has no link.
- **All thirteen stops finished** (four in the pilot, three added on 2026-11-19, six more on 2026-11-20), each with a drawn scene, the story at both levels, "What this shows us about God", one or two verses quoted exactly from the BSB (each opens the site's verse panel), "Read it yourself" (Study Bible chapters), and one activity:
  - `/study/explorer/creation`: tap the picture to find the seven days.
  - `/study/explorer/abraham`: match each of God's three promises to where the Bible shows it kept.
  - `/study/explorer/rescue`: put four events of the rescue in order.
  - `/study/explorer/cross`: think, then look (four questions, each answer behind its own button).
  - `/study/explorer/fall`: "What broke", tap the picture to find four broken relationships (with God, with each other, with ourselves, with the ground), each with its place in Genesis 3 and a line on how God begins to mend it.
  - `/study/explorer/sinai`: "Walk into the tent", eight numbered marks on a plan of the tabernacle, from the gate of the courtyard to the ark, taken with Next (or tapped); each says what the part was and where the Bible takes it up.
  - `/study/explorer/land`: "Round and round", the judges' cycle as a wheel stepped round with Next, round again, ending with why the story needs a better king.
  - `/study/explorer/kingdom`: "What makes a good king?", five moments from the stories of Saul, David and Solomon; for each the reader taps "He trusted God" or "He grabbed for himself" and sees what the Bible says and the verse; it ends on the promise of a king who will not fail (2 Samuel 7:16; Luke 1:32-33).
  - `/study/explorer/exile`: "Promises in the dark", four promises from Jeremiah, Ezekiel and Isaiah matched to where the New Testament takes each up (Luke 22:20; Acts 2:1-4; Acts 8:32-35; Luke 1:32-33).
  - `/study/explorer/jesus`: "Who is this?", five things Jesus did by the Sea of Galilee in Mark's order, marked on the picture and taken with Next (or tapped); each says what he did, what people said, and who the Gospel says he is, ending on Peter's answer and handing on to the Cross.
  - `/study/explorer/return`: "From the decree to the voice in the wilderness", five events put in order (Cyrus's decree, the temple finished, Nehemiah's wall, the first Hanukkah, John the Baptist); each shows its date and one line once it is in place.
  - `/study/explorer/church`: "Out to the ends of the earth", Acts 1:8 as three faint rings on the picture and five numbered places walked with Next (the upper room, Samaria, the desert road, Caesarea, Antioch and the ship); each says what happened there and which ring it is.
  - `/study/explorer/new-creation`: "The end of the road is the beginning, made new", four pictures from Genesis 2 and 3 (the guarded tree of life, the river, God walking in the garden, the curse) matched to what Revelation 21 and 22 say of each. Its foot leads to the whole map and to "Why we believe".
- **Two reading levels**, "Ages 7 to 11" and "Ages 12 to 16" (the Family door's younger and older bands, `lib/familyTable.ts`), one tap, kept on the device. The older level adds the why, an honest hard question with its answer, and links to go deeper (Honest Answers, context guides).
- **Two threads**, lit across the whole map, each with one line and its verse at each of the thirteen stops. "God with his people": a solid line where God lives among his people, a broken line where the closeness is lost or waited for (the fall, the exile, the return). "The king" (2026-11-20): solid where God's rule is shown or his king is on the throne (creation, the sea, Sinai, David, Jesus, the cross, the right hand, the throne in the city), broken where the king is promised or the throne stands empty (the fall, Abraham, the judges, the exile, the return). A thread now names its own states in its key, and `broken` lists the states drawn broken (default "apart"), so the first thread renders exactly as before. Three more lit on 2026-11-20 (evening): the Holy Spirit, the promise and the lamb (see below); "A blessing for every nation" is named as coming.
- **"Why we believe"**: eight finished cards (The Bible; Jesus, God and man; from 2026-11-20 evening, The resurrection and The church; from 2026-11-20 night, God, People made in God's image, Sin and The cross) with a picture, a plain statement at both levels, a quoted verse, the passages it rests on, a question people ask, the stops that teach it, and the family catechism questions that say the same. Two more (The Holy Spirit; The life to come) are named as coming, without links.
- **Progress** on the device (`livewell-progress-explorer-v1`, through `lib/storage.ts`): stops visited, activities done, the level, and "You are here" on the map. No account, no API procedure.

## The stops, and why there are thirteen

The site's Bible's Story (`/study/bible/story`, the eleven acts in `client/public/theology/biblical-theology-storyline.json`) is the spine; every stop is filed under one act, and the map shows the act's name. Two acts are split into two stops each, because a young reader needs them as places of their own:

- **Act Four (Exodus and Sinai)** becomes *The Rescue from Egypt* and *Sinai and the Tent*: the rescue and the law are two different lessons, and the tabernacle is where the thread of God dwelling with his people turns.
- **Act Nine (Christ, the Center)** becomes *Jesus Comes* and *The Cross and the Empty Tomb*, as the brief asked: the cross and the resurrection need a stop of their own.

### 1. Paul's travels (an interactive map)

The site already has the frame and the places: the "mediterranean" frame in `client/src/pages/study-bible/BookMap.tsx` (`MAP_FRAMES` in `lib/bible-notes.ts`) and 24 checked places with cards in `client/public/bible/notes/acts/intro.json` (`teaching.map[1]`, "Paul's travels and the road to Rome"), with their ledger in `docs/work/elite/wmap-ledger.md`. What a stepper adds:

- **Data** `client/public/explorer/travels.json`: trips, each an ordered list of steps `{ place: <id from intro.json>, refs: ["Acts 13:4-5"], younger, older, by: "sea" | "land" }`. Places are referenced by id, never copied, so a card is checked once.
- **Route lines**: BookMap draws marks and labels but no routes. Add an optional `route` layer: a path through the step points in the frame's own projection, sea legs dashed, land legs solid, the walked part inked and the rest faint. Where the way between two towns is not known, a straight dotted line and a note saying so; no invented roads.
- **The stepper**: "Previous" and "Next" buttons (44px), a step counter ("Step 4 of 11"), the current town ringed, its card below with what happened there and the Acts reference opening the verse panel; arrow keys move too; the trip chooser is a set of radio buttons (first trip, second, third, the road to Rome). The map is a picture, the stepper is the control, so a keyboard or screen reader reader follows the steps as a list.
- **Wording**: the forbidden-word gate rejects "journeys" even in "missionary journeys" (checked: `forbiddenRe` matches it; only "journey to Jerusalem" is set aside). So the room is called **Paul's travels**, and the trips "Paul's first trip" and so on. If James wants the searched-for phrase used once, it needs an entry in `ALLOWED` in `scripts/lib/forbidden-language.mjs`, his call.
- Reuses: BookMap's frame and projection, `intro.json` places, `VerseRef`, the order activity's button pattern.

### 6. Simplicity: Next, all the way through

A young reader must be able to start at the first stop and tap "Next" to the last without being lost. How the design holds that:

- **One order.** Every stop has a number (1 to 13) and one "Next stop" and "Previous stop" at the foot, in the map's order, always in the same place; the stop's head says "Stop 4 of 13". Today, while stops are still being drawn, "Next" skips to the next finished stop (a stop being drawn has no link); when all thirteen are drawn, Next walks every stop. A test will hold that the chain from stop 1 reaches stop 13.
- **One way home.** Every page in the Explorer has "The whole map" and the "Bible Explorer" label at the top, both to the map, and the map marks "You are here".
- **One place kept.** The map and "Continue where you left off" read `last` from the one progress key.
- **The other rooms hang off the road, not beside it.** Paul's travels opens from the Church and the Spirit stop; the library and salvation pictures open from the map's foot and from the stops that teach them; each has "Back to the road" and returns to the stop it was opened from.

### What is built (the pilot)

- **The name: "Watch and learn"**, used everywhere (the section on the map, its anchor `#watch`, every film page, search, llms.txt). It says what to do, in a child's words. James may want another; it is one string in five places.
- **The player** (`client/src/pages/explorer/films/FilmPlayer.tsx`, `film.css`, `motion.tsx`): a framed 16:10 stage in the art kit's frame, a thin progress line under it, one Play. Before Play it is a poster: the film's first drawing with its title on a paper label and a round Play. Play runs the frames, each 5 to 8 seconds by the length of its caption, and stops on the last ("Play again"); it never loops or starts by itself, and pauses when scrolled out of view or the tab is hidden. Pause, Back, Next, Replay and numbered frame buttons (two rows of 44px buttons on a phone, one row wider) move by hand; the left and right arrow keys move while the picture has focus; a step by hand is announced politely, the frames Play moves through are not. Captions are real text under the picture at the reader's level (the Explorer's one switch), with any verse quoted from the BSB and its reference opening the verse panel; all captions sit in one grid cell, so the box never changes height while it plays (CLS 0 measured).
- **How the motion is made**: a film's drawing is a function of the frame number. Each part says from which frame it shows (and, if it leaves, from which it is gone); moving to a frame changes classes only, and CSS transitions carry the change: lines draw themselves (stroke-dashoffset), parts fade, rise or grow (opacity, transform), marks travel (transform). Nothing else animates, no library is used, and no timer runs except the one that moves Play to the next frame. With reduced motion asked for, the stage gets `is-still`, every transition is off, Play is not offered, and the reader steps with "Start" and Next through finished frames; each frame's `still` sentence (what the finished picture shows) is the picture's accessible name, so nothing the motion shows is lost.
- **Content is data**: `client/public/explorer/films.json` (the four groups and every planned film, in order; a film not made yet has only slug, title and group), and `films/<slug>.json` for a finished one (title, group, mark, line at both levels, poster, frames with `name`, `still`, `younger`, `older` and an optional `verse`, "Think about it", Study Bible chapters, the stops and cards it belongs to, where churches differ, a care line, deeper links, and where it sends the reader at the end). The drawing is `films/scenes/<slug>.tsx`, one lazy chunk per film (`films/scenes/index.ts`). `build-explorer-index.mjs` copies the catalogue and each finished film's line, mark and frame count into `index.json`; `validate-explorer.mjs` checks the four groups, that a coming film has no file and no link, 5 to 9 frames, both captions and a `still` on every frame, BSB quotations exact, links live, the voice rules (no em-dash even in quoted Scripture, no forbidden words, "!" only in quoted Scripture, no emoji), and that each finished film has a drawing.
- **Where they live**: the dark band "Watch and learn" on `/study/explorer`, between the map and "Why we believe" (the one charcoal section on the page: a screening room), in four groups; a finished film is a card with its small picture, a coming one is named as "Coming" with no link. Each film is at `/study/explorer/watch/<slug>`, prerendered with its own head and every caption at both levels, in the sitemap, the search index and llms.txt. Stops link their films under "Watch and learn" (Creation: the whole Bible in two minutes and Who made God?; the New Creation: the whole Bible in two minutes; the Church and the Spirit: the Holy Spirit). The "Why we believe" cards show a film once one belongs to a finished card (the Holy Spirit film is filed under the coming card "The Holy Spirit", and Who made God? under "God").
- **The three pilot films**: the big story, *The whole Bible in two minutes* (9 frames); a big idea, *The Holy Spirit through the Bible* (8); a good question, *Who made God?* (6). Their claims are in the ledger.

### What narration would need

(Superseded on 2026-11-20 by Listen and the voice slot in the video export, below; kept as the record.) The films have no sound by design in this stage, and every word is on the page. Narration would need: (1) a script per film at one reading level (the captions are written to be read aloud, but the older level is long for listening; a spoken script would be the younger level, or a third, spoken one); (2) a voice: **James's own voice**, recorded, or another person's, or a synthesised voice; this is his call (see the questions below), and a synthesised voice should be named as such on the page; (3) one audio file per frame (small, Opus or AAC, about 20 to 60 KB each), so Back, Next and the frame buttons keep working and each frame's sound starts with its frame; (4) a mute button, sound off by default and never playing without a tap, captions kept visible; (5) the timer would follow the audio's length, not the caption's word count; (6) a transcript is already there (the captions), so access is met. Recording one film is about an hour of a reader's time plus an hour to cut and check it; synthesis is faster but needs a decision on whose voice, and on whether a made voice is acceptable for Scripture.

### Listen: the device's own voice

- A **Listen** toggle under every film's controls (off by default; the choice is kept on the device, `livewell-explorer-listen-v1`, through `lib/storage.ts`), with one line under it: "Read aloud by your device's voice." It is the browser's built-in speech synthesis, the same approach as the essays' Listen and the catechism's "Read it to me": nothing is recorded or sent, and it is not narration by a person.
- **What it says** (`client/src/lib/film-voice.ts`): each frame's `say` text at the reader's level, or the caption where there is none, then the frame's verse with its reference in words ("First Corinthians chapter 15, verses 3 and 4: that Christ died ..."), skipped where the caption already quotes it. 35 caption-and-level pairs whose captions carried a reference got a `say` with the bracketed reference left to the caption (two by hand: Ezekiel 10:18 and 11:23, and the Romans 8:15 inside a bracket); nothing else in any wording changed. The validator now fails any spoken line that still holds a reference in figures, and holds its quotations to the BSB as it does the captions'.
- **How it plays**: with Listen on, Play says the frame and moves on only when the voice says the sentence has ended (then a 0.7 second breath); if the end never comes, it moves on after a wait of 600 ms a word plus 4 seconds (at least 8). Pause stops the voice; Play on says the frame again from its start (Android's own pause does not resume reliably, as the essays' Listen found). Back, Next and the frame buttons stop the voice and say the new frame; a playing film keeps playing from there, a paused one stays put after saying it. Leaving the page, scrolling the player out of view or hiding the tab stops the voice. With reduced motion there is no Play, and each step by hand is read. Where the device has no voice, or the voice reports an error, the film falls back to its timed captions and the line under the toggle says "Sound is not available on this device, so the captions carry the words."
- **Screen readers**: with Listen on, the polite live region that announces a step by hand is left empty, so a screen reader user does not hear the device voice and the screen reader at once; with Listen off it announces as before. Captions always stay on screen.
