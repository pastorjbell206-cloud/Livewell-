# Four first-time visitors on a phone: the walk, and the ten worst

James, 2026-11-23: "Walk the site as four first-time visitors on a phone: a skeptic, a Christian with doubts, a pastor, and a parent. Write down every place each one gets lost, bored or stuck, then fix the ten worst." And: "Using what you learned, give each of the four readers one obvious next step from the home page and /start."

Branch `elite/nav`, merged with `claude/todo-implementation-hhp4bm` at 228fc9ba before the walk.

## How it was walked

The live site (www.livewellbyjamesbell.co), in Chromium through Playwright, at 390 by 844 with touch on, device scale 1, nothing stored, each visitor starting cold at the home page. The browser's clock was the machine's: Monday 23 November in UTC, which is Sunday evening, 22 November, in Michigan (the time zone the walk used). The nights around Advent Sunday were checked with the clock set to 24, 29 and 30 November and 1 December.

A "tap" is one tap, or one thing typed and sent. Opening the phone menu counts as a tap. Times are from the tap to the page settling (load, then the network quiet), so they are upper bounds; the time to the page's own heading was measured separately, from a cold start, and was under two seconds on all ten pages measured (Home, two Find Help pages, a search, /studyguides, /questions, /skeptics, /family/tonight, /theology/compare, /topics); the slowest to show its text was /family/tonight, at 3.3 s. No page walked was slow enough to count as a finding. The client code on the live site is the code on this branch, apart from three Home lines that live has and the branch does not (a "who is writing" line, a signup and an ENDS line in Find James), so every finding below was checked against this branch's code before it was written down.

Screenshots are in `/home/user/elite-shots/walk/`. The name of each file is `<visitor>-<step>-<what>.png`; `w2-` and `w3-` are second and third passes that timed a step or checked one thing. Every screenshot cited below was opened and read.

How bad a finding is: **who it loses** (which of the four, and whether they give up or only slow down) and **how often** (every visit, every visit by one kind of reader, or a few nights a year).
