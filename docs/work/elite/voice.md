# The six-lens pass on your first five essays

James, you asked for your six editing prompts to be run over your writing. This is the first run: the first five essays on the shelf, in this order: why-does-god-feel-far-away, doubt-after-church-abuse, excavation-not-demolition, what-post-christian-means, the-rise-of-the-nones. The full record, sentence pairs and all, is in docs/work/elite/voice-ledger.md.

## What changed

Not much, and on purpose. These five had already been through two voice readers held to your own standard (docs/VOICE-JAMES-BELL.md), and most paragraphs already sound like a man talking. The pass did four things again and again: it used contractions where the sentence was already spoken ("didn't", "it's", "I'll"); it turned a colon that sets up a reveal into a plain sentence; it cut "simply" where it was filler; and it folded the one-line paragraphs at the end of three essays into fuller ones. Two sentences were rebuilt because they read as built rather than said. For example, "A sixteenth-century friar refused to spiritualize everything." is now "That's a sixteenth-century friar refusing to make everything spiritual."

Length moved by less than half a percent in each essay (19,047 words to 19,009 in all). The tells your third prompt names, counted by a script, fell from 135 to 112 across the five.

## What did not change

A script compared every essay before and after: every quotation, Scripture reference, year, number, name, heading, link and book title. All five passed with nothing changed. Every help line and safety sentence is word for word, including all of them in the essay on abuse. Nothing about the abuse was softened, and nothing was made harder on the reader. Where an essay takes no side (forgiveness and repentance, the reply to Schellenberg), it still takes none. Your sentence about your father and your five sons is untouched.

## What only you can give

The prompts ask for writing that sounds like someone who went through it. In three places a true detail from you would do more than any edit: what the pull to protect the church's name has looked like for you ("I lead one, and I feel that pull too"); which of your own sermons wouldn't survive your sons' questions ("Which of mine would?"); and whether to say more after the sentence about growing up without a father. And an earlier reader cut a line about your coming to faith out of atheism from the essay on God's silence. That fact is confirmed now. Whether to put it back is your call.

## What was run

Every content gate in the CI workflow (36 commands) passed, as did the essays index check, pnpm check and pnpm test (146 test files passed, 7 skipped). There was no build, because no code changed.
