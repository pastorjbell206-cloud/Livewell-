# LiveWell: the project prompt

Written 2026-11-18 from James Bell's own words. This is the standing brief for everyone, human or AI, who works on the site. Read it before any work. When a task and this page disagree, this page wins; when this page and James disagree, James wins.

## The mission, in one sentence

LiveWell is the one place a person can come to follow Jesus and live well in post-Christian America: to study the Bible, understand its context and the church's history, get straight answers to hard questions, lead their family, grow as a disciple, and find everything James Bell writes.

## What James asked for

> "I want this website to look elite, world class. I want it to be one of the best premier Christian websites available... one of the best resources for people to study, to understand the scripture, to understand church history, understand the context, have a study Bible. I want you to do a full redesign... interactive things... expand the tools... I want the writings to be very strong... If you need to reorganize it, you can. I want this to be streamlined. I want the mission to be accomplished and the vision to be easy to understand."

## Who it is for

A parent at the table tonight. A skeptic with one hard question. A new Christian who does not know where to start. A serious reader who wants sources. A pastor deciding whether to hand it to his people. Every page is judged by whether those five people get what they came for, on a phone, without being told how the site works.

## What "done" means

The site is finished when all of these are true, checked by people who did not build it:

1. **Clear in five seconds.** A stranger on the home page can say what the site is and where to tap.
2. **Three taps.** Anything James named (a Bible chapter, a history lesson, an answer, tonight's devotion, an essay, a book, a way to reach him) is within three taps of the home page.
3. **A real study Bible.** A reader can read, search for a word, see notes beside the text, compare a translation, follow a cross-reference, and keep a highlight.
4. **Church history as a course.** One ordered path from the apostles to today, with sources a reader can check, a timeline to move through, and questions for each lesson.
5. **Tools that teach.** Every tool does one job a person would name, works on a phone, remembers its place, and leads somewhere next. No two tools do the same job.
6. **Writing that sounds like one man.** No piece reads as machine-made. Every piece scores 9 or better for sounding human and sounding like James.
7. **It looks like the best.** Set beside the best Christian and editorial sites, it holds its own in type, layout, and care.
8. **Fast and sound.** Every main page loads without jumping, passes accessibility checks, and has no dead link.
9. **Scored honestly.** The same nine-part audit that gave the site 6 out of 10 gives every line 9 or better.

## Rules that do not bend

- Nothing is invented: no quotation, date, number, scholar, or story about James. If it cannot be checked, it is left out.
- The site never states James's title, years in ministry, or number of books until he gives one set of facts.
- No membership fee. No giving page. The Pastors Connection Network stays outside the site.
- Facebook and Substack are linked, not copied in, until James supplies the posts or approves an embed.
- Doctrine wording and the catechism change only on his word.
- Pages for people in crisis always keep a visible path to real help.
- Everything is free and nothing hides behind a sign-up.
- James reads a sample of any writing before it goes public. Code goes live when its checks pass.
- The engineering rules in CLAUDE.md apply to every change.

## The work, in order

**1. Learn from the best (research).** Study twelve to fifteen leading sites: Bible study tools, church history and theology publishers, answers sites, family discipleship sites, and the best-designed reading sites of any kind. For each: what it does better than anyone, what LiveWell should borrow, what it should refuse. End with one page saying where LiveWell can be the best in the world, which is the combination no one else offers: a study Bible, church history, hard questions, and the family table, free, in one voice.

**2. Take stock and reorganize.** List every tool, download, page, and library on the site. For each: keep, merge, rename, rewrite, or retire. Fold duplicates. Produce one map of the site a person could draw on a napkin, and move everything to fit it. Old addresses keep working.

**3. Redesign.** One design system: a reading typeface, one scale of headings and spacing, one set of buttons and cards, day and night. Rebuild the pages people live in first: home, a Bible chapter, a history lesson, an essay, tonight's devotion, an answer. James sees these on a preview before the look spreads to the rest.

**4. Build the interactive pieces and expand the tools.** In order of value: Bible word search; notes beside the text; a second translation in a column; highlights and notes kept on the device; the church history timeline; "ask a hard question" on the home page; a devotion for tonight chosen in three taps; the catechism read aloud; memory verse practice; a reading plan that knows today; "who was alive when"; the 66 books at a glance; compare the views; share cards for verses and quotations. Research may add to this list or reorder it.

**5. Make the writing strong.** First add the missing help lines. Then rewrite, library by library, from weakest to strongest: study guides, family devotions, Honest Answers, church history, Bible notes, main pages, Find Help. Each piece is read by two readers before and after. Where a piece needs James's own memory or judgment, it is listed for him and not faked.

**6. Deepen the study.** Verse-by-verse notes on more books, sources under every history claim, further reading for all 66 books, the church history course filled out where regions and centuries are missing.

**7. Check and score.** Every wave ends with independent reviewers, every gate green, a preview link, and a short plain report. The nine-part audit is run again after each major wave. Work continues until every score is 9 or better.

## How James is kept in the loop

Each wave ends with three things: a link to look at, a half-page of what changed, and a short list of decisions only he can make. Nothing waits on him unless it is on that list.

## What is needed from James

1. One set of facts the site may state about him, or the word to state none.
2. A few real memories and details for the essays (the voice audit lists where).
3. Which of his two voice descriptions governs: the saved Substack profile or the voice statement kept with the site.
4. His Facebook and Substack posts, if he wants them shown on the site and not only linked.
5. A yes or no on each preview.
