# Who wrote the Gospels and the New Testament, when, and who chose the books

The text pages in this library ask whether we can know what the New Testament's authors wrote. This area asks the questions that come before it. Who were the authors? How long after Jesus did they write, and how did what he said and did reach them in the meantime? Why does the church read these four Gospels and not the others that circulated under apostles' names, and are some of the letters in the New Testament written under names that weren't their authors'?

These are the questions on which skeptical scholarship has spoken most confidently to ordinary readers, and Bart Ehrman's books, *Jesus, Interrupted*, *Forged* and *Lost Christianities* among them, have done more than any to carry them out of the seminar room. They are also questions on which Christian scholars themselves disagree. Faithful Christians hold different views on when the Gospels were written, whether the apostle Matthew wrote the Gospel that bears his name, and whether Paul wrote every letter that carries his. The pages that follow say so where it's true, and they take no side where the church's confession doesn't.

What the pages argue is narrower than either a skeptic or an anxious believer might expect. They don't claim that the traditional authorship of every book can be proved. They argue that the Gospels were written within the lifetime of people who had known Jesus, that the tradition behind them was handed on with more care than a game of telephone, that the church chose its four Gospels long before any emperor took an interest and for reasons it gave in the open, and that the hard questions about disputed letters, though real, don't touch what the creeds confess. Each page states the objection as its strongest proponents would, says what is true in it, sets out the evidence with names and dates a reader can check, and ends with books on both sides.

## The questions

1. **The Gospels are anonymous. Weren't the names Matthew, Mark, Luke and John added later?** What the titles and the earliest witnesses show, and what "anonymous" does and doesn't mean.
2. **Weren't the Gospels written decades after Jesus by people who never met him?** The usual dates and the reasons for them, the case for earlier dates, Paul's letters from before the Gospels, and what a gap of a lifetime allows.
3. **Before anything was written, the stories about Jesus were passed by word of mouth for decades. Isn't that a game of telephone?** Form criticism, memory research, the debate over controlled tradition, and what changed and what stayed across the Gospels.
4. **There were other gospels: Thomas, Peter, Judas, Mary. Didn't the church pick four and suppress the rest?** The other gospels and their dates, how the four were received, what Nicaea did and didn't do, and what is true in "the winners write history".
5. **Weren't some New Testament letters forged in the names of Paul and Peter?** The disputed letters, the case against them at full strength, the replies, what the ancients thought of forgery, and what is and isn't at stake.
6. **The Gospels were written by believers to make believers. What kind of writing are they, and does their kind make them history, legend or something in between?** The form critics' "folk literature" and the turn to ancient biography, Plutarch, Suetonius and Thucydides on what their kind of writing allowed, Justin's "memoirs" and Luke's preface, and what the genre does and doesn't establish.
7. **If Matthew and Luke copied Mark, aren't the first three Gospels really one witness rather than three?** The Synoptic problem set out fairly, Mark's priority, Q and the main alternatives with no side taken, what copying shows and what it doesn't, the sources skeptics themselves count, and the witnesses outside the problem.

## Words these pages use

- **Synoptic Gospels**: Matthew, Mark and Luke, so called because they can be set side by side and seen together. Most scholars think Mark was written first and used by the other two.
- **Ancient biography**: the Greek and Roman *bios* or Life, a book about one person's public career, words and death, written to show his character; its writers selected, compressed and arranged by topic.
- **Q**: from the German *Quelle*, "source": a lost collection of Jesus' sayings that, on the most widely held solution to the Synoptic problem, Matthew and Luke both used; others hold that it never existed.
- **Papias**: a bishop of Hierapolis in Asia Minor early in the second century, whose reports about Mark and Matthew survive in quotations by the historian Eusebius.
- **Canon**: the list of books a church receives as Scripture, from a Greek word for a measuring rule.
- **Pseudepigraphy**: writing under the name of someone who did not write the work. Whether a given case was a deception or an accepted convention is debated.
- **Pastoral Letters**: 1 and 2 Timothy and Titus, Paul's letters to two coworkers, so called because they deal with the care of churches.
