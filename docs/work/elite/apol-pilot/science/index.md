# Science

Many people who have left the faith, or never come near it, would say that science is the reason. They don't usually mean a single experiment. They mean a story they've absorbed: that the church once explained the world and science has been taking that job away from it ever since, that the church fought the takeover and lost, and that what is left of God is a shrinking space where physics and biology haven't yet reached. The questions in this area are the ones that story raises when it is put by its strongest advocates, not its loudest.

The pages state the science at its mainstream strength. The sciences date the universe at about 13.8 billion years and the earth at about 4.54 billion, and hold that life has a long history; the laws of nature are found, tested and used, and they work. Nothing here disputes a consensus finding by insinuation, and nothing here treats a finding as a proof of God. The question each page asks is narrower: what the finding shows, what it doesn't, and whether it settles the question the skeptic says it settles.

The history is told exactly, including where the church was wrong. Galileo's condemnation was real, and the pages say so with its dates. So is the fact that the "war between science and religion" is largely a nineteenth-century construction that historians of science, believers and unbelievers alike, have spent fifty years correcting. The argument about miracles is taken from David Hume's own words. The arguments from the universe's beginning and its fitness for life are stated with their real limits and the best replies to them, the multiverse among them.

On the age of the earth, evolution, the details of a historical Adam and the extent of the flood, this site takes no position. Faithful Christians hold different views, and the page on evolution and Genesis sets out each in the form its own defenders would sign, with its strongest difficulty, and then states what all of them confess together. That confession is not up for negotiation on any page: God made all things, human beings bear his image, sin and death are real, and Christ redeems.

Five later pages take the same care with five more questions: how old the earth is and whether the Bible says, what the evidence of human origins means for Adam and Eve, whether the mind is just the brain, whether neuroscience has shown free will to be an illusion, and whether near-death experiences prove an afterlife. The first two take no side among the views Christians hold. The last three state what the science shows and what it doesn't, set out the Christian views of the person and of freedom without choosing among them, and rest the Christian hope where the creeds rest it, on the resurrection of the body. The page on near-death experiences is written for readers who may be grieving or afraid, and points to Find Help (/help) at its start and its end.

## The questions

1. **Science keeps explaining what people once put down to God. Hasn't it made God unnecessary?** The track record of natural explanation, the god of the gaps, what science presupposes, and what kind of question it can settle.
2. **The church condemned Galileo. Hasn't religion always been at war with science?** What happened in 1616 and 1633 and what Galileo was and wasn't sentenced to, where the church was wrong, and how the "war" was written in the nineteenth century and taken apart since.
3. **A miracle would break the laws of nature. Didn't David Hume show that no one can reasonably believe in one?** Hume's argument in his own words, what he granted, the replies from Lewis to Earman, and what the question of miracles finally turns on.
4. **Christians say the Big Bang and the fine-tuning of physics point to God. Can't physics explain both without him?** What the beginning of the universe and fine-tuning can and can't show, the multiverse at full strength, and why neither is a proof.
5. **Hasn't evolution disproved Genesis, and with it the Christian story of creation and the fall?** The evidence for evolution at its strength, the main Christian readings of Genesis each in its defenders' terms with its hardest difficulty, the questions of death and Adam, and what every reading confesses together.
6. **The sciences say the earth is about four and a half billion years old, and Bible chronologies put creation a few thousand years ago. How old is the earth, and does the Bible say?** What the sciences measure and how, what the text and its ancient copies say, the readings of the days each in its holders' terms with its hardest difficulty, and what all of them confess.
7. **Fossils and genetics trace human beings back through a long history in Africa, from a population that was never only two people. Doesn't that rule out Adam and Eve, and with them the fall and the need for a Savior?** The fossil and genetic evidence as standard reference works state it, what it rules out and what it doesn't, the Christian views of Adam from a recent first pair to a figure for every human, each with what it must explain in Romans 5 and 1 Corinthians 15, and why what the doctrine of sin requires is itself where Christians differ.
8. **Damage to the brain changes memory, character and even the sense of self, and neuroscience ties more of mental life to the brain every year. Hasn't science shown that the mind is just the brain, and that there is no soul?** What neuroscience shows and what it doesn't, the argument from causal closure at its strongest, the hard problem, and the three Christian views of the person, each with its hardest difficulty, under one hope of the resurrection of the body.
9. **Experiments show the brain preparing an action before a person is aware of deciding to act. Hasn't neuroscience shown that free will is an illusion, and with it moral responsibility?** Libet's experiments and what has become of their interpretation, determinism, compatibilism and libertarian freedom, the Reformed and Arminian accounts each in its own terms, and why responsibility before God is confessed by both.
10. **People revived after their hearts stopped report leaving their bodies, a light, and meeting relatives who had died. Do near-death experiences prove that there is life after death?** What is reported and how researchers explain it, as a reference work sets out both sides, why the evidence is at an impasse, and why Christian hope rests on the resurrection of Jesus, not on reports from the edge of death; with a word for the grieving and for anyone thinking of death.

## Words these pages use

- **Methodological naturalism**: the working rule of science, to look for natural causes and test them. It is a rule of method, not by itself a claim that nothing exists but nature.
- **Naturalism**: the view that nature is all there is, with no God or anything beyond it.
- **God of the gaps**: the habit of appealing to God only where natural explanations are missing, so that God seems to shrink as knowledge grows. The phrase was made popular by the physicist Charles Coulson in the 1950s.
- **Secondary causes**: the causes within creation, such as the laws of nature, through which, on the historic Christian view, God ordinarily acts.
- **Conflict thesis**: the view, made popular by John William Draper (1874) and Andrew Dickson White (1896), that science and religion have always been at war.
- **Fine-tuning**: the finding that some of the constants and starting conditions of physics fall within narrow ranges that allow life.
- **Multiverse**: the proposal that our universe is one of a vast number of universes or regions, perhaps with different constants.
- **Compatibilism**: the view that free will is compatible with determinism; **libertarian freedom** is the view that some free choices are not determined by prior events.
- **Readiness potential**: a build-up of electrical activity in the brain that precedes a movement, measured in Benjamin Libet's experiments.
- **Physicalism**: the view that everything that exists is physical, or rests wholly on the physical; applied to the mind, the view that mental states are, or depend entirely on, states of the brain.
- **Hard problem of consciousness**: the question, named by David Chalmers in 1995, why the brain's processing is accompanied by experience at all.
- **Original sin**: the church's teaching that all human beings share a condition of sin that began with the first sin; traditions differ on whether it includes inherited guilt (the Augustinian view) or inherited mortality and corruption (the Eastern Orthodox view).
- **Radiometric dating**: measuring the age of a rock or meteorite from the decay of radioactive elements, which turn into other elements at rates measured in the laboratory.
