# Morality, sexuality and the church's record

Some of these pages concern harm done to people who are still alive, abuse in churches above all. For anyone who has been harmed, Find Help (/help) lists places to turn now, and a crime against a child or anyone else is reported to the police, whatever a church says. Nothing here asks a person who has been hurt to stay silent, and nothing here is offered as an excuse for what was done.

The case against Christianity that most people meet first is not an argument about God. It is the record of Christians. The Crusades were preached by popes. Catholic and Protestant authorities burned and drowned people for their beliefs, and some of the church's greatest teachers wrote the arguments that justified it. Christian nations built the Atlantic slave trade and the conquest of the Americas, and churches defended both from the Bible. Church leaders hid the abuse of children to protect their institutions. Critics from David Hume and Thomas Paine to Christopher Hitchens have pressed this record against the faith itself.

These pages begin there, with the church's record, and they concede its guilt first and plainly. The church named here is not someone else's church: Catholics and Protestants, Baptists and evangelicals among them, stand inside the record. Each page then asks what the record shows. It sets out the evidence from sources that can be checked, gives numbers only where a source gives them and says how uncertain they are, and refuses the defense that someone else did worse. Where the history corrects a false claim, the page says so and says that the correction lessens nothing. And each asks whether the wrongs were the faith's own logic or a betrayal of it, judged by the teaching of Jesus and by the Christians who opposed them from inside the church.

Pages on morality itself and on sexuality, which the plan also places in this area, are still to be written.

## The questions

1. **Weren't the Crusades a holy war of conquest, and don't they show what Christianity does when it has power?** Hume's verdict, the preaching at Clermont and the promise of forgiveness, the massacres of 1096 and 1099 and the sack of Constantinople, the conquests that came before, and the cross made a soldier's badge.
2. **Christians tortured and burned people for what they believed. How can a faith that ran the Inquisition claim to be good?** Paine's charge, Augustine's turn and Aquinas's argument for death, the Spanish Inquisition and the *conversos*, the Protestant executions in Zurich and Geneva, the early church's refusal of force, and the case for liberty made from inside the faith.
3. **Christian nations conquered the Americas and built the Atlantic slave trade, and their churches blessed both. Doesn't that discredit the faith they claimed?** Douglass and Las Casas as witnesses, the papal grants of conquest and enslavement, the Baptists' founding in 1845 and their apology in 1995, what the Bible says of kidnapping and of one human family, and who first resisted.

## Words these pages use

- **Crusade**: a war preached by the medieval church as an act of devotion, whose fighters took the cross as their sign and were promised forgiveness of sins.
- **Heresy**: the teaching, by a baptized person, of what the church holds to contradict the faith.
- **Inquisition**: a church tribunal for finding and judging heresy; the Spanish Inquisition, founded in 1478 under the crown, was abolished in 1834.
- **Converso**: a Jew, or a descendant of Jews, baptized in medieval Spain, often under pressure.
- **Donatists**: a North African church that broke with the catholic bishops after the persecution under Diocletian, and was suppressed by imperial law from 405.
- **Christian nationalism**: the error of making the cross the badge of a people's power, so that its enemies are treated as God's enemies and devotion is measured by willingness to fight.
