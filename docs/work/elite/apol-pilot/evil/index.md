# Evil, suffering and judgment

These pages argue; they don't comfort, and they explain no one's grief. For someone who is hurting now, the site's pages on suffering (/wisdom/suffering), on losing a child (/wisdom/losing-a-child) and on grief (/wisdom/grief), and Find Help (/help), are the place to begin, and nothing below is offered as the reason any person suffered or died.

The argument from evil is the strongest reason people give for not believing in God, and it deserves to be met as an argument. Since David Hume's *Dialogues Concerning Natural Religion* (1779) asked whether God was willing but not able, or able but not willing, philosophers have given it several forms. J. L. Mackie argued in 1955 that God and evil contradict each other. William Rowe argued in 1979 that suffering with no point we can see makes God unlikely. Others have pressed the suffering of animals over ages before there were people, the silence of God toward those who seek him, and the doctrine of hell. Christians have answered with defences, theodicies and confessions, and some of their answers have been worse than silence.

These pages take the arguments one at a time. Each states the argument in the form its best proponents give it, concedes what is true in it, lays out the reply and its limits, and says what remains open. Each also says what the Christian answer is not: an explanation of any particular evil. What Christians confess is stated as confession: God is good and almighty, evil is real and his enemy, and in the cross and resurrection of Jesus Christ he has entered it and will end it.

## The questions

1. **If God is all-powerful and wholly good, why is there evil at all? Didn't J. L. Mackie show that the two can't both be true?** Mackie's argument and its two hidden premises, Plantinga's free-will defence, Mackie's own later concession, the objection from heaven, and what a defence can and can't do.
2. **Even if God and evil don't contradict each other, doesn't so much suffering that serves no purpose anyone can see make God unlikely?** Rowe's fawn and his argument, the inference from what we can't see, Wykstra's reply and skeptical theism, what it costs, Rowe's question about why God gives sufferers no assurance, and the answer Christians give in the cross.

## Words these pages use

- **Defence**: an argument that God and evil are consistent, which needs only a possible reason for God to permit evil, not his actual reason.
- **Theodicy**: an attempt to give the actual, or the most plausible, reasons God permits evil.
- **Logical problem of evil**: the claim that God and evil contradict each other, so that both can't be true.
- **Evidential problem of evil**: the claim that the evil we see makes God's existence unlikely, though not impossible.
- **Libertarian freedom**: the view that a choice is free only if nothing outside the chooser, God included, determines which way it goes.
