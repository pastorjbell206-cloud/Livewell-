# Corpus Gap Analysis — LiveWell by James Bell

What the body of work covers deeply, what it touches lightly, what it never
addresses — judged against the Editorial Constitution's central question, the
five pillars, and the ranked audiences (skeptics first, then questioning
Christians, then pastors via PCN, then live-well seekers). Read-only; the
full corpus was inventoried (roughly 115 library essays, 131 leadership
articles, 66 whole-Bible sermons, 39 study guides, 48 theology files, 70 life
domains, 31 context guides, 10 creeds, ~130 how-tos, plus the justice,
disruption, nation, history, and table tracks and ~40 book products).

---

## 1. The topic map

### Written deeply (multiple genres, multiple angles — well served)

- **Christian nationalism / faith and political power** — the most saturated
  single theme (nation track, disruption topics, study guide, ~8 essays).
- **Classic apologetics for the skeptic** — resurrection, science, hell, the
  unevangelized, theodicy, inerrancy, plus the skeptic plan and guides.
- **Suffering, theodicy, lament, grief** — the skeptic's hardest question is
  answered across theology, life domains, plans, prophetic lament, and guides.
- **Pastoral leadership, preaching, ecclesiology, revitalization** — the
  deepest pillar by volume; the PCN channel is richly served.
- **Systematic and biblical theology, creeds, church history** — including
  the 31 context guides, a genuine strength.
- **Money, poverty, economic justice** — deeper than stewardship basics.
- **Fatherhood** — signature ground, already claimed (guide, essays, book
  material, sermon series).
- **Aging, death, dying** — despite being an expected gap, well covered.
- **Discipleship and formation, practical marriage and parenting, race and
  reconciliation, friendship and loneliness** — all solidly served.

### Touched lightly (present but thin)

Singleness (one strong domain file, no essay register) · the sacraments
(practical pieces only; no doctrine entry) · sexuality and gender (one 13KB
file carrying a top-three objection) · church discipline (membership defended,
discipline barely treated) · mental illness vs. spiritual struggle (pastoral
care present, the diagnostic line untheologized) · constructive political
theology (critique strong, praxis light) · motherhood and womanhood (thin
against fatherhood's depth) · evangelism after Christendom (how-tos, no
signature essay).

### Never addressed

**AI and algorithmic formation** (screens covered; AI absent entirely) ·
**worship and liturgy as a subject** · **Catholic/Orthodox engagement** ·
**the atheist-to-pastor conversion as a signature essay** (it exists only as
a book sample — the corpus the #1 audience reads never meets it).

## 3. The three to fill first

1. **The atheist-to-pastor conversion essay.** Highest leverage per word:
   serves the #1 audience, unrepeatable authority, already drafted in sample
   form. Nothing else so directly earns the skeptic's trust.
2. **AI and algorithmic formation.** The one true absence on a subject the
   Constitution explicitly prioritizes; every audience needs it now; squarely
   in the five-sons formation lane.
3. **Sexuality and gender for the skeptic.** The largest mismatch between
   audience need and corpus depth, with the source material already written
   at book length.

## 4. Gaps Bell should not try to own

- **A culture-war sexuality/gender arsenal.** Address it pastorally and for
  the skeptic; do not become a sexuality-politics shop — that breaks the
  same-instrument-same-grief guardrail.
- **Motherhood/womanhood from the inside.** Claim the topic theologically;
  the lived-experience register belongs to a named woman contributor.
- **Frontier bioethics and technical academic biblical scholarship.** Real
  gaps in the abstract, outside a working pastor's authority. Better cited
  than authored.
