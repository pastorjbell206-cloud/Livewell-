# The kingdom of God: the facts in the script, and where each was checked

This film is a rebuild of the Bible Explorer film `client/public/explorer/films/kingdom-of-god.json`, rewritten as a "big idea" film about the kingdom Jesus announced. Its sources are the old film (older captions, think question, differ note), its rows in `docs/work/elite/explorer-ledger.md` ("kingdom 1" to "kingdom 7", "kingdom, differ", "kingdom, left out"), the doctrine page `client/public/bible/doctrines/kingdom.json`, and the Study Bible notes in `client/public/bible/notes/` for Daniel 2, Mark 1, Matthew 6, Matthew 13, Luke 17, Romans 14 and Revelation 11. The Daniel 7 note was read but nothing from it is used.

## Claims, by chapter

1. **Your kingdom come.** The Didache (8.2-3) quotes Matthew's form of the Lord's Prayer and tells Christians to pray it three times a day, so by around AD 100 it was fixed in daily church life; "an early Christian manual": Matthew 6 note, cultural (and interpretation: "The Didache (8.2-3) already prescribed it three times daily"). The question "if God is already king, why pray for his kingdom to come?" grows from the doctrine page's definition (God "has always been king" and yet Jesus taught "Your kingdom come ... because its fullness still lies ahead").
2. **It rules over all.** In one sense God has always been king; the story is that rule refused by his creatures and reclaimed; "God's rule breaking into a world in rebellion against him to set it right": kingdom.json, definition; old film frame "rule" (older); ledger "kingdom 1".
3. **Not by human hands.** Nebuchadnezzar's statue of four metals (gold, silver, bronze, iron with clay) stands for a series of kingdoms; a stone cut without human hands shatters it and becomes a mountain filling the earth: Daniel 2 note, summary, story and literary; kingdom.json "ot". Readers differ on which empires the metals mean; all agree every human kingdom is temporary: Daniel 2 note, hard ("All agree on the point the chapter makes most plainly: every human kingdom is temporary").
4. **The kingdom is near.** The first thing Mark reports Jesus saying is the gospel of 1:15: Mark 1 note, literary ("the first thing the Gospel reports Jesus saying is the gospel the title promised"). Basileia most often means kingship or reign, not a territory; God had begun to act as king; Gustaf Dalman argued this in The Words of Jesus (1898): kingdom.json, definition; old film frame "near"; ledger "kingdom 2". "Has come near" (ēngiken, a perfect tense): scholars debate at hand but still future, or already arriving; Mark's narrative suggests both: Mark 1 note, word study G0932.
5. **Come upon you.** The kingdom present in his healings, his welcome of sinners; it "healed the sick, fed the hungry, and forgave sinners": kingdom.json, definition and errors; old film frame "signs"; ledger "kingdom 3". Matthew 12:28 as the old film's verse.
6. **In your midst.** Asked by Pharisees when the kingdom would come: BSB Luke 17:20. Entos hymon can mean "within you" or "among you": Luke 17 note, word study G1787 and hard question 1; kingdom.json "nt". Origen read it as the reign of God within the soul; the King James has "within you": Luke 17 note, interpretation. Most modern translations (RSV, ESV, NRSV, NASB, current NIV) say "in your midst" or "among you", since Jesus is answering Pharisees who opposed him; both readings are defended by serious scholars: Luke 17 note, hard question 1.
7. **A mustard seed.** Every image comes from ordinary Galilean life: Matthew 13 note, cultural. Kingdom of heaven and kingdom of God name the same thing, God's reign, not a place in the sky: Matthew 13 note, historical; kingdom.json definition. The mustard seed was proverbially tiny; three measures of flour was "bread for a crowd": Matthew 13 note, cultural. The mustard seed and the leaven make one point about small beginnings and a great end; a kingdom "already present in Jesus but hidden, small": Matthew 13 note, literary and historical; old film frame "seed"; ledger "kingdom 4".
8. **Treasure in a field.** The treasure and the pearl, one found by accident and one by searching, make one point about joyful sacrifice for supreme worth: Matthew 13 note, literary (the narration gives only the merchant doing the same). The viewer question ("what would be worth everything you have?") is the film's own, with no claim in it.
9. **Already, not yet.** Johannes Weiss (1892) and Albert Schweitzer (1906) argued Jesus expected an imminent end; C. H. Dodd's The Parables of the Kingdom (1935) answered that the kingdom had already arrived; a mediating view, present and future, won wide agreement, developed by (among others) George Eldon Ladd: kingdom.json, history. "Already and not yet": come in Christ, present wherever his rule is received, complete when he returns: kingdom.json, definition; old film frame "already"; ledger "kingdom 7"; Matthew 13 note, doctrines (kingdom). Romans 14:17 defines the kingdom as righteousness, peace and joy in the Holy Spirit: Romans 14 note, doctrines (kingdom); kingdom.json "nt" ("present as").
10. **Where Christians differ.** The old film's differ note (they differ on how the kingdom and the church are related now, and how its coming in full will unfold); ledger "kingdom, differ". The five views: the church as the seed and beginning of the kingdom (Roman Catholic teaching, Lumen Gentium); the kingdom as God's reign and the church as the community of those who have received it (many Protestant scholars); two kingdoms (Lutheran and a Reformed strand); Christ's kingship over every sphere (Kuyper's tradition); a kingdom promised to Israel (dispensational): kingdom.json, differ. The millennium is named, not argued; kingdom.json differ sends it to the Return of Christ study; ledger "kingdom, left out" keeps any end-times scheme out of the old film, and the film takes no side. The church has rejected any teaching that identifies the kingdom with a nation, empire, party or political program, and equally the error that shrinks it to a private feeling: kingdom.json, errors; old film frame "not-of-this-world"; ledger "kingdom 6".
11. **Forever and ever.** At the seventh trumpet heaven announces the kingdom of the world has become the kingdom of our Lord and of his Christ: Revelation 11 note, summary; kingdom.json "nt". "Here the word is singular ... not two kingdoms coexisting forever but one rule reclaimed": Revelation 11 note, word study G0932. "Because it has come, there is no need to despair; because it is not yet complete, there is no need to pretend": old film frame "already" (older); kingdom.json, life; ledger "kingdom 7".

## Notes

- The old film has no care line (ledger "all four, care lines": "the kingdom of God: none"), so none is on the page or in the narration.
- Left to the sibling film the-king and not repeated: David and 2 Samuel 7, the Messiah as king, Zechariah's donkey, the crown of thorns, Mark 10:45 and "My kingdom is not of this world" (John 18:36). Left to the batch 17 siblings: the nations (all-nations, so the mustard tree's birds are not read as the Gentiles here), exile and return (exile-and-home), and "the dominion of darkness" of Colossians 1:13 (light-and-darkness).
- No modern nation, party or ruler is named. Views on Daniel's empires, on "within" or "among", on the kingdom and the church, and on the thousand years are named and left open.
