# The Church in the Middle Ages: the facts in the script, and where each comes from

Every fact comes from one file, the lesson `client/public/history/essays/the-medieval-west.json` (page /theology/history/the-medieval-west). Section keys: **O** = "Two False Stories" (two-false-stories), **M** = "The Monks Who Cleaned the Church" (cluny-and-canossa), **H** = "Christendom at High Noon" (christendom-at-noon), **W** = "What the Power Did" (the-sword), **B** = "Babylon and the Broken Papacy" (babylon), **L** = "What Made Luther Possible" (the-eve), **KF** = keyFigures, **DR** = dateRange, **ST** = subtitle. Paragraphs are numbered within a section (M2 = the second paragraph of M).

The one exception is the chapter "Where Christians differ". Its three readings use words LiveWell's own history lessons give for each tradition (all in `client/public/history/essays/`); they are listed in the table with their file.

## For James

**Left out on purpose (for length, or to keep violence named rather than described).** Burgundy and the duke's Cluny spreading to hundreds of monasteries; Gregory dying in exile, and the compromise at Worms in 1122; England's King John; the Fourth Lateran Council of 1215, both its rule of yearly confession and its order that Jews wear distinguishing dress (a real loss; it could come back if you want it more than another line); the crusade against heretics in 1209 and Beziers; Boniface the Eighth's claim of 1302 and his seizure at Anagni; Catherine's letters; the council at Pisa and its third pope; Wycliffe's bones; the Renaissance popes (mistresses, armor, luxury); indulgences stretched to purgatory in 1476; Erasmus and the egg Luther hatched. Every death is said as "killed", "massacred" or "put to death", never by its manner (the lesson says Hus and Savonarola were burned). The lesson's line "the men who told it the truth" and its closing "In 1517 the bill came due" are left out so the narrator does not rule on Hus and Wycliffe; the Protestant reading carries that view instead.

**Views named without choosing.** The two opening stories are given without the lesson's labels ("the old Protestant polemic" and "the romantic picture"), so neither is pinned on a tradition. Catholic, Orthodox and Protestant readings get one line each (24, 25 and 24 words) and one panel each of the same size, lit in turn, each with three labels:
- Catholic, in words from the-east-west-schism (Peter's successor, a center that held) and this lesson (the church grew its own reformers, Cluny to Francis).
- Orthodox, in words from the-east-west-schism (Rome honored as first; a patriarch a brother, not a sovereign; 1204 not forgotten).
- Protestant, in words from this lesson's subtitle (the refusal of reform) and from the-reformation and reformation-and-its-wars (Wycliffe and Hus; God's Word in the people's own tongue).
The failures are named with the same plain words for every party, and the film says "We aren't better than they were", as the lesson does.

**Points to check.**
- Acts 8:20. The lesson names "the magician in Acts who tried to purchase the power of the Spirit" but no verse. The film quotes Peter's answer to him and never names Simon. The verse is copied as the BSB file has it, so it opens a quotation mark that closes in verse 21.
- The Orthodox line joins two sentences of the-east-west-schism ("honored Rome as first" and "a brother, not a sovereign") into one. That lesson says the second of a patriarch; the film applies it to Rome.
- The Catholic line takes the first half of the lesson's "The medieval church grew them in every century"; the lesson goes on to say the structure absorbed or killed them, which the narrator also says in the chapter before.
- "can catch it" softens the lesson's "catches it".
- "the church we come from" is the lesson's phrase, written for Western readers. Orthodox viewers do not come from the medieval Western church; the film keeps the lesson's words.
- Word count 599, at the top of the 480 to 600 range.
