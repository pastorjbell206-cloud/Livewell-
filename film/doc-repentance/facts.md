## For James

- **Left out on purpose.** The Hebrew and Greek words (shuv, nicham, salach, metanoia, aphesis and the rest). Exodus 34:6-7, the golden calf and the Day of Atonement. Jonah and Nineveh, John the Baptist, Pentecost (Acts 2:38) and Luke 24:47, all cut to keep the film under 600 words. The hard texts in Hebrews 6 and 12. "At great cost to himself" (the atonement has its own study). Judas's death: the file names it, but the film stops at "gave back the silver" so it doesn't put suicide in front of a viewer who is carrying guilt. The Judas and Peter contrast still holds. From the history: the Mishnah and Maimonides, Hermas, Tertullian, Nicaea's canon 8, the Irish penitentials, Lombard, Lateran IV, the Vulgate and Erasmus on "do penance", the indulgence controversy, Luther's first thesis, Augsburg, Calvin, Heidelberg, Westminster, Trent, Watson, Wesley's sermons, Bonhoeffer's "cheap grace" and the 1973 Rite of Penance. The Reformation story about penance and indulgences can't be told fairly in one line, and a one-line version would lean toward one side, so it isn't told at all. The evangelical lordship and free grace debate (Ryrie, Hodges) is also left out: naming it fairly takes three positions and about forty more words. If you want it, it could replace the Lutheran confession line.
- **Named without choosing.** On repentance and faith: Reformed (God's gift to a new heart, rising with faith, lifelong), Lutheran (the law exposes sin and troubles the conscience, the gospel creates faith, a daily return to baptism) and Wesleyan (grace awakens repentance, ordinarily before justifying faith, and believers keep repenting). On confession: Catholic and Orthodox (Christ's authority given to the apostles, a sacrament after baptism; for Catholics contrition, confession, penance and absolution in Christ's name; for the Orthodox a holy mystery of healing, with the priest as witness and physician) and Lutheran (private confession and absolution kept as a gift). The six panels are the same size, each has one small touch of mustard, and the three confession panels each carry the same small cross. The closing verse (1 John 1:9) is Scripture every tradition reads. The film doesn't say whose way of confessing it means.
- **Care lines kept as the file has them.** The unforgivable sin (word for word). Forgiveness after harm: often slow, not forgetting or excusing, trust needs the offender's repentance and time, no return to danger, no hiding a crime from those charged with justice (word for word). Repentance is not self-punishment.
- **The Bible's own words for who speaks and acts.** "The LORD God calls out" (Genesis 3:9). "Nathan answers, The LORD has taken away your sin." "Isaiah urges the wicked to return to the LORD, for He will freely pardon." In the parable it is always "the father", and the film never says "God runs". "Jesus restored him" stands for John 21, where Jesus asks the three questions.
- **Unsure, for your eye.** (1) "Troubles the conscience" is gentler than the file's "terror of conscience" in the Lutheran view. (2) The Wesleyan line says "grace" for the file's "prevenient grace". (3) The Catholic and Orthodox line drops "and retain" from "forgive and retain sins". (4) "The first man and woman" stands for Genesis's "the man and his wife". (5) The narration writes LORD (four times) as the BSB does. The voice should read it as "Lord".
- **Names to listen for:** Nathan, Isaiah (eye-ZAY-uh), Zacchaeus (zack-KEE-us), Decian (DEE-shun), Cyprian (SIP-ree-un), Carthage, Novatian (no-VAY-shun), Wesleyans, absolution, unrighteousness.
