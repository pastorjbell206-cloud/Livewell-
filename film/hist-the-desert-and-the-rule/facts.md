## For James

**Left out on purpose (for length or care).** Antony "fighting what he described as demons" in the fort, his two walks into Alexandria, and "reportedly past a hundred". The two imperial officials who read the Life of Antony, "thirty years later" and the line "He says so in the Confessions". "White martyrdom" (it was in an early draft and fell to the 600-word limit; easy to restore as one line). Pachomius's conversion when Christians fed the conscripts, and "until they held thousands". Moses' later description as "one of the gentlest of the fathers", the boasting brother sent to the doctor, and the hermit who breaks his fast for guests. Monks who "despised the body" and "fled ordination". Gregory the Great as nearly the only source on Benedict's life (the film says nothing about Benedict's life beyond the Rule, so nothing in it rests on Gregory). The abbot listening to the youngest, idleness and murmuring. "Fifteen centuries". The lesson's thesis line that "a faith with no full-price option quietly stops being believed": I left it out because a "full-price" faith for some Christians can be heard as taking a side on whether the monk's calling is higher than other callings, which is the very point the three traditions weigh differently.

**Views named without choosing.** Three readings, each in one line of about the same length, each drawn in an identical panel (no colour or size favours one):
- Orthodox: monasteries honored as houses of prayer and counsel, rules carrying Basil's stamp.
- Catholic: the vowed life honored as a calling God gives some for the good of all, many houses keeping Benedict's Rule.
- Protestant: following the Reformers, every calling holy, the farmer's and the mother's as much as the monk's.

The lesson itself only says Basil's stamp is on the East and Benedict's Rule on the West. The Protestant line comes from the course's Reformation lesson. The two phrases marked "beyond the lesson" in the table are mine, added because review asked that each tradition be named in its own terms. Please confirm them or cut them back to the lesson's words (for example: "In the Orthodox East, monks still keep rules that carry Basil's stamp. In the Catholic West, many still keep Benedict's Rule."). The closing line, "the monks don't ask it", is the lesson's own and takes no side.

**Scripture.** The lesson quotes no verse, but it ties the whole era to "the words where Jesus tells a rich young man to sell everything, give to the poor, and follow him". It doesn't name the Gospel. The film uses Matthew 19:21 because Matthew alone calls him a young man (19:20 and 19:22); Mark 10:21 and Luke 18:22 tell the same story with "sell everything you own". Worth checking outside the lesson: I believe Athanasius's Life of Antony quotes Matthew's wording ("if you would be perfect"), but the film doesn't claim it.
