# The church fathers: the facts in the script, and where each comes from

Every fact about the era comes from one LiveWell lesson, `client/public/history/essays/the-fathers-at-work.json` ("The Church Fathers: The Teachers Who Carried the Faith Between the Councils"). Section keys: **BC** = "Where Doctrine Actually Gets Made" (`between-the-councils`), **CM** = "Athanasius Against the World" (`contra-mundum`), **CA** = "Three Friends and a Sister" (`the-cappadocians`), **GT** = "Augustine Against Pelagius" (`grace-on-trial`), **ME** = "How the Work Was Done" (`the-method`), **IN** = "What We Owe Them" (`the-inheritance`), **KF** = keyFigures.

The one exception is the chapter "Where Christians differ". The lesson describes no tradition's way of reading the fathers, so its three tradition lines come from LiveWell's own pages, marked **PS** = `client/public/history/essays/the-plain-sense.json` (section "Sola Scriptura and the Answer at Trent", `scripture-and-tradition`, and "Calvin's Brevity", `lucid-brevity`) and **DC** = `client/public/bible/doctrines/canon.json` and `revelation.json`. See "For James" below.

## For James

**Left out on purpose.** Jerome's line that the world "groaned and marveled to find itself Arian" (it needed a definition of Arian the lesson doesn't give, and the word budget ran out). Jerome in Bethlehem translating the Scriptures into Latin. The monks of the Egyptian desert who hid Athanasius. "Athanasius against the world" is the kicker, not a spoken line. Basil's answer to the prefect who threatened him. Gregory's reluctance and the borrowed chapel with its hecklers. Pelagius's fuller view (freedom kept, sin as habit, grace as law, teaching, forgiveness and Christ's example) and the lesson's line that he was "not wrong" about a church going soft. The film keeps only "his case was serious" and "God doesn't command the impossible". Also left out: Jerome and Augustine's letters, the lesson's line about a congregation reciting the creed in ninety seconds, and its two kinds of ingratitude (the fathers as "infallible oracles" or as "dead weight"). Those last two were cut so that no one could hear them as aimed at a particular tradition right after the tradition lines. The lesson's "two halves of a corpse" is also left out of the close.

**Views named without choosing.**
- *How the traditions hold the fathers.* You asked for each tradition's way of reading the fathers. This lesson doesn't give one, so the three lines come from LiveWell's own pages (PS and DC above), not from the lesson: Orthodox, within Holy Tradition, the church's living memory, with Scripture at its heart; Catholic, with Trent, Scripture is not to be read against the unanimous consent of the fathers; Protestant, Scripture the only infallible rule, with the fathers still read closely, as Calvin read Augustine. Each line is said in its own tradition's terms, at about the same length. In the picture the three churches hold the same Scripture and the same five fathers, drawn the same size and in the same ink. If you'd rather keep strictly to this lesson, cut those three lines and the framing line. The chapter then keeps only the predestination line, and the film falls to about 525 words.
- *Augustine's late teaching on predestination.* Named as argued "ever since", with no side taken. The lesson's own words that the church "did not follow him into every conclusion" are not spoken.
- *Pelagius.* His case is called serious, as the lesson calls it. The film reports the church's verdict at Carthage and Ephesus, which is history the lesson records.
- *Athanasius's character.* His enemies' charge and the historians' partial agreement are both kept, as the lesson keeps them.

**Check these.**
- The lesson quotes no verse. Isaiah 43:11 stands beside "Only God can save", and 1 John 3:18 beside the creed and the soup kitchen. Cut either if you'd rather quote only what a lesson quotes.
- "Orthodox Christians hear them within Holy Tradition" joins two things the site says separately: the Orthodox hold Scripture as the heart of Holy Tradition, and the fathers belong to that memory. The second is the film's own wording.
- "Augustine had prayed, in effect, give what you command" keeps the lesson's "in effect".
- The closing card reads "Truth and mercy, one life."
