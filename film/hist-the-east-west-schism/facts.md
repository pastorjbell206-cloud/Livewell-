## For James

**Left out on purpose.** The lesson's reason the West added the filioque in "the lands they were converting" (lingering Arian influence) would have needed a definition the film had no room for, so the film keeps only "to guard the full divinity of the Son". Also left out: "two proud men lost their tempers"; the looting and killing of 1204 beyond the word "attacked"; "at the time many Christians barely noticed"; the West's "stable rock" image. The pope of 1054 and the pope and patriarch of 1965 aren't named, because the lesson doesn't name them. No council is named and no date is given for the filioque, for the same reason. Protestant readings aren't mentioned, because the lesson has none. Matthew 16 (the rock) and John 15:26 (proceeds from the Father) aren't quoted, because each one sits at the centre of a disputed reading.

**Views named without choosing.**
- Who has the last word. Catholic: the bishop of Rome, successor of Peter, has real authority to govern and to settle disputes for the whole church. Orthodox: the bishops together in council, with Rome honoured as first and a patriarch a brother, not a sovereign. The film gives each answer its strength in the lesson's words and says "neither answer was foolish".
- The filioque. West: a reverent addition meant to guard the Son's full divinity. East: it blurs the Father as single source, and no one region may change the common creed without a council.
- Blame is spread as the lesson spreads it. Both men in 1054 probably overreached. 1204 was a Western army. The later unions were forced. The close says "no hero and no villain".
- In the picture, West is always on the left and East on the right. Both are drawn in the same ink, at the same size, and the two churches are identical. At "neither answer was foolish", the two answers sit on a scale that settles level.

**Check these.**
- "In the West, and in the Catholic Church today" and "in the Orthodox Church today" carry the lesson's West and East positions into the present. The lesson pairs West with Catholic and East with Orthodox and says the division remains, but it doesn't state today's teaching in so many words.
- The lesson cites no verse. John 13:35 was chosen because the lesson ends on two traditions that "lost the ability to love each other".
- At the close, a small mustard light sits between the two churches for "the same Lord", and a thread is drawn between their doors for "it has to be kept". No figure is drawn.

**Names to listen for.** Constantinople; Augustine; Photius (FOH-shus); Cardinal Humbert; Hagia Sophia (HAH-jee-uh so-FEE-uh); Michael Cerularius (seh-roo-LAIR-ee-us); filioque (fee-lee-OH-kway); Godhead.
