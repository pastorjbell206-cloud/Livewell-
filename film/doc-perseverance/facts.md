**On screen only.** The names on the five cards come from the file's history section, one row per card, the same size: the Synod of Dort (1618 to 1619); the Remonstrants and John Wesley; the Augsburg Confession (1530); the Council of Trent (1547); Zane Hodges and Charles Ryrie (also named in the differ entry). Each card's two short phrases restate its two spoken lines. "The New Testament", "John 10", "John 15", "Councils and confessions", "The early church" and "The Reformation" are from the nt and history sections. The end card, "Look to Christ, who holds His people.", is the life section's cure, which the file gives "across the traditions". Counts in the pictures (twenty-six in the people, six branches, four confession pages, nine marchers, six others round each person) are drawings, not claims.

## For James

- **Where the weight sits.** Seven of the nine chapters are what the file says Christians share: the two questions; the New Testament's two voices, which "every Christian tradition affirms"; the Old Testament setting promise and warning side by side; Jesus and Paul speaking both ways (Philippians 2:12-13 as the two voices in one sentence); the early church and the Reformation; the life section's word, given "across the traditions", to the careless and the anxious; and Psalm 73. The differ chapter is 257 of the 599 words.
- **Named without choosing.** All five views in the differ section, in the file's own order: Reformed (the perseverance of the saints), Arminian and Wesleyan (conditional security), Lutheran, Roman Catholic, and Free Grace (eternal security without necessary perseverance). Each gets exactly two lines, one of what its holders hold and one of why they hold it, every second line marked "they argue" or "they teach" (the Reformed one begins "They may fall"). Lengths run from 283 characters (Reformed) to 299 (Free Grace), 48 to 52 words, so spoken time differs by a second or so. On screen each is the same card, same size, same colours, the same single mustard rule, the same time in the centre; the others sit equally quiet in the row. The whole section is one chapter at one music level (0.7). The film never says which is right, and no view's critics speak (the file's line that critics in every other tradition fault Free Grace is left out, as are all critiques).
- **Order and last word.** The order is the file's, which puts the Reformed view first and Free Grace last. Your own church confesses the 1689 Second London Confession, which follows Dort, so you may want to judge whether first place reads as preference. Free Grace speaks last in the section; the next line is the shared one ("Wise pastors across the traditions ..."), so the following line about the careless does not sound like an answer to Free Grace. The film's last spoken words are the file's own sentence to the doubting, and its last verse is Psalm 73:26, which every tradition reads alike.
- **The fear of having fallen away.** The film says to it only what the file says: the New Testament's promises are meant to be trusted; the anxious need the promises (John 6:37, paraphrased); look away from yourself to Christ; no one holds on alone; and doubt, weariness and dry seasons are not proof of falling away, the question is where they turn. It makes no promise beyond these, and the walker in the last scene turns toward the light but is never shown arriving.
- **Two small simplifications to check.** The Lutheran line says "can fall from grace" without the file's "through unbelief or deliberate sin" (cut for length). The Catholic line says grace is "restored through penance" where the file says "through repentance and the sacrament of penance".
- **Left out on purpose.** The Hebrew and Greek words (shamar, chesed, hypomenō, menō, plērophoria, parrēsia, phroureō, phylassō, arrabōn, parapiptō, aphistēmi). Jacob, Deuteronomy 31, the wilderness generation, Solomon, Ezekiel 18 and 36, Jeremiah 32 (its "many interpreters see" reading leans toward one view), Psalms 121, 125 and 138, Isaiah 49:15, Lamentations 3:22. Judas; Peter appears only inside the Reformed view's own words. Luke 8:13, Romans 11, Colossians 1:23, 1 Corinthians 9:27 and 10:12, Galatians 5:4, Hebrews 6:4-6 and 10:26-39 (the most debated warnings, which each view reads differently), 1 Peter 1:5, 2 Peter 1:10, 1 John 2:19, Jude, 2 Timothy 4:7, Revelation 2:10. All names and dates of the history except the five card rows: Decius, Cyprian, Hermas, Augustine, Orange, Aquinas, Luther, Calvin, Arminius, Westminster, the 1689 Confession, the Puritans, Perkins, Brooks, Wesley's Aldersgate, Owen, Shank, Marshall, Schreiner and Caneday, MacArthur, the lordship salvation controversy, the Baptist Faith and Message, Four Views on Eternal Security, Picirilli and Ashby, and the split between Wesleyans and Reformed Arminians on whether apostasy can be reversed. The errors section's presumption paragraph is left out, because the file's own description of presumption sits close to what the Free Grace view allows, and naming it as a shared error would have taken a side.
- **Verses.** Philippians 2:13 and Psalm 73:26 are spoken in full. The words "continue to work out your salvation with fear and trembling" are the BSB of Philippians 2:12, quoted inside a narration line and shown on screen with the reference "Philippians 2:12-13". None of the verses the other films already use (John 13:35, Matthew 5:9, Romans 11:33, Revelation 7:9, Deuteronomy 7:7) appears.
- **Length.** 599 words, the top of the range; about three minutes forty-two of voice by the estimate.
- **Names to listen for:** Arminian (ar-MIN-ee-un), Wesleyan, Lutherans, Reformation, persevere and perseverance, penance, presumption, Philippians, psalmist (the p is silent), Zane Hodges and Charles Ryrie (on screen only, RYE-ree), Remonstrants and Augsburg (on screen only).
