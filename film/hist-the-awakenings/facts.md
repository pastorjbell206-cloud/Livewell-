## For James

**Left out on purpose.** Spener's name, Frankfurt, Pia Desideria and 1675 (the film says only "Pietism"); Francke and Halle; Zinzendorf by name; "sixty years before the English societies existed"; Wesley as "a failed missionary"; Edwards's 1737 account crossing to Britain; James Davenport's bonfires (1743) and retraction; Edwards's dismissal in 1750, Stockbridge and the Mohican and Mohawk peoples, Princeton, and his death in 1758 after a smallpox inoculation, with the lesson's "Revival had not made his people kind" (the biggest cut, about 40 words if you want it back); the coal miners' tears; Whitefield's fame and his death at Newburyport in 1770; Franklin "who loved the man and doubted the message"; Wesley preaching into his eighties; David Bebbington and his four marks (conversion, the Bible, the cross, spreading the message); Whitefield rebuking planters for cruelty and teaching the enslaved the faith, and his will passing people "on like furniture"; that Edwards "condemned the ocean trade" (fairness asks you to weigh putting this back: six words, "though he condemned the ocean trade"); Liele's mission "a decade before England's famous missionary society"; Wesley's phrase "a scandal to religion and to human nature"; the missionary movement, abolitionism and the black church as the awakening's children, and "a durable temptation to measure God's work by crowd size and feeling"; Newton's "Bible on his desk, human beings in his hold" (left out so nothing describes the hold) and Parliament's abolition of the British slave trade in 1807; "neither cynical nor comfortable" and "The revivalists' God was not fooled by their revivals, and he is not fooled by ours". The lesson's sources (Noll, Marsden, Gonzalez, MacCulloch) aren't named.

**Views named without choosing.**
- *Whitefield's side and Wesley's side.* The lesson names the split (Calvinist and Arminian) but gives neither view's content, so both lines come from one LiveWell page, the doctrine study of election, near word for word: its "Unconditional individual election (Augustinian and Reformed)" and "Conditional election (Arminian and Wesleyan)". The film calls them "Calvinists" and "Arminians" because the lesson uses those names for the two men. The lines are 22 and 21 words. In the picture the two men are the same dot, and the two views are the same lamp, the same size, lit the same way while each line is spoken, with a short phrase from each line under it, and both stay lit. The page's third view (corporate election) is left out because the lesson's split is between these two.
- *What the people of the time called it.* "An outpouring of the Spirit" and "enthusiasm" are given side by side at the same size, as the lesson gives them.
- *Catholic, Orthodox and other readings.* This lesson gives no Catholic or Orthodox reading of the awakening, and I found none for this era in the other history lessons, so the film says nothing for them rather than invent a line. If you want the film to say how the churches read the new birth the awakeners preached, LiveWell's regeneration study (`client/public/bible/doctrines/regeneration.json`) has fair lines for four views, baptismal regeneration among them (Catholic, Orthodox, Lutheran and many Anglicans). Four equal lines would add about 80 words and push the film past 600.
- *Failures named alike.* The only church the film calls cold is the lesson's "much of Protestant Europe". Whitefield's and Edwards's slaveholding are named plainly in the lesson's own words, and the film keeps the lesson's answer from inside the movement: Liele, Equiano, Wesley and Newton. Nothing is tied to any modern nation, party or movement.

**Check these.**
- The lesson quotes no verse. John 3:3 is there because the lesson's banner phrase is "the new birth", and LiveWell's regeneration study says the eighteenth-century revivals "made the new birth their banner". The line before it, "But Jesus had demanded exactly that", answers the lesson's "would certainly never demand anything so indecent as a new birth". Cut or swap it if you'd rather quote only what a lesson quotes.
- The story steps back in time: Aldersgate (1738) is followed by Northampton (1734), as in the lesson. "Across the ocean, in the winter of 1734" is meant to carry the listener back. Worth a listen.
- "Indecent" is the lesson's word, used ironically. Listen for whether it lands that way when spoken.
- The map is a drawing, not data. Coastlines are rough. The mission pins sit in the eastern Caribbean and on Greenland's west coast because the lesson names no island or station. Wesley's voyage is drawn from Bristol because the lesson names no port. The thirteen crossings end at five colonial ports chosen for the drawing, and the fires of New England are symbolic. Equiano's book and Wesley's letter fly to southern England, and the lesson names no city.
- The blind spot is drawn as a dark upright oval inside a disc of light. It is not a figure, but check that it doesn't read as one.
- The passengers, the miners, the townspeople and the believers are plain dots. Nothing shows a slave ship's hold, a chain or anyone suffering. Newton's ship is drawn empty.
- 598 words, at the top of the 480 to 600 range.
