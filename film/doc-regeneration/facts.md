## For James

- **Left out on purpose.** Circumcision of the heart (Deuteronomy 10:16 and 30:6). Ezekiel 18:31 and 36:26 show the same command-and-promise pair without having to explain circumcision. Also left out: Jeremiah, Isaiah 44, David's prayer in Psalm 51, Lydia, Ephesians 2, John 1:12-13 and the perfect-tense argument from 1 John. The last three are the proof texts each side uses in the order debate, so the film leaves the texts to the views and quotes none of them. The word list (palingenesia, anagennaō, apokueō) is out. So are Augustine and Pelagius, the councils of Carthage, Ephesus and Orange, Aquinas, Trent, Luther's Small Catechism, Calvin, Schleitheim, the Remonstrants and Dort, Edwards, the Gorham case, Spurgeon's 1864 sermon, Finney, and 1976 (Carter, Newsweek, Colson). Spurgeon and the Gorham case would put one side of the baptism question into the history chapter. The rest came out to stay at 600 words. The narration says "his people" but doesn't say that Ezekiel's promises were first spoken to Israel as a people. The errors section shows up only in the closing line of the differ chapter ("not a fresh start you make yourself") and, gently, in "people come to faith in many ways".
- **Named without choosing.** There are two questions, each with two views.
  - **Order.** Reformed: dead in sin until God makes them alive, so the new birth comes first in logic, not necessarily in time. Wesleyan and Arminian: no one turns to God by their own power, grace goes before, a person can believe or refuse, and God gives the new birth when they believe.
  - **Baptism.** Catholic, Orthodox, Lutheran and many Anglicans: God gives the new birth through baptism, to infants and adults, to be lived in faith and repentance. Baptist and other believers' churches: the Spirit gives new birth through the word when a person believes, and baptism is its public sign.
  - **Fairness in the words.** God or the Spirit is the one who acts in all four lines, so no view sounds like self-help.
  - **Fairness in the picture.** Each pair gets two panels of the same size, with one touch of mustard each (the light, the grace that goes before, the light into the water, the light onto the person). Both baptism panels have the book, the light and the basin. The close of the chapter is four equal cards under one light.
- **The review rule, applied.** Who speaks and acts is taken from the text itself:
  - "the Lord sees" (Genesis 6:5) and "he says so again" (8:21);
  - "God commands... through Ezekiel" (18:30-31, "declares the Lord GOD");
  - "Jesus replied" and "Jesus spoke of";
  - "Paul writes that God saved us" (Titus 3:4-5);
  - "breath enters them" (37:10) and "the breath God sent" (37:5).

  The confession line says only "it shows in a changed life", not "it shows itself in faith", because that would lean toward the Reformed order. The hope line avoids "not by their own strength", the form the file's life section uses, because a strong contrast with the bones' helplessness leans the same way. The history chapter pairs the early church's tie to baptism with the revivals' preaching of the new birth.
- **For you to check.**
  - John 3:8 in the BSB ends with a closing quotation mark (”), because Jesus' words run from verse 5 to verse 8. It's kept character for character in the narration and on screen.
  - On screen the Greek word is written "anothen", without the macron, in case the film's Latin-subset fonts don't carry ō.
  - The narration is 600 words, the top of the range.
  - The end card reads "Born again. Born from above.", the double sense the file gives for John 3:3, which belongs to neither side.
- **Names to listen for.** Nicodemus (nick-oh-DEE-mus), Pharisee, Ezekiel, Titus, Justin Martyr, George Whitefield (WIT-field), John Wesley, Wesleyan, Arminian (ar-MIN-ee-an), Anglicans. On the picture only, not spoken: lev, ruach, anothen.
