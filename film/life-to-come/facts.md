## Claims, by chapter

1. **How it ends.** Christian hope's content is specific: the return of Christ, the resurrection of the dead in their bodies, the judgment of the world in righteousness, the life of the world to come in a renewed creation: HS "definition", first paragraph. The creed of Constantinople in 381 says the church looks for "the resurrection of the dead, and the life of the world to come": HS "definition"; HS "history", second paragraph ("closed with"). The opening line points to the sibling film when-we-die without naming it.
2. **An enemy, named.** The Bible does not pretend death is nothing; Paul calls it an enemy, the last to be destroyed; Jesus wept at his friend's grave: OF frame "real" (older); ledger "life 1".
3. **A seed, not a ghost.** The final hope is not a soul floating away but the body raised, as Jesus' body was; Paul's seed: OF frame "raised" (older); ledger "life 4"; HS "nt", third paragraph. Oscar Cullmann's Ingersoll Lecture at Harvard in 1955 (published 1958 as *Immortality of the Soul or Resurrection of the Dead?*) argued that the New Testament hope is resurrection and not the Greek idea of an immortal soul; critics thought he overdrew the contrast; his lecture changed the conversation: HS "history", third paragraph. The pause paraphrases HS "life" ("If the body is to be raised, then what is done in and to bodies matters") and HS "questions", third item; it is a question only.
4. **Heaven comes down.** The Bible's last pages do not show people leaving the world for the sky: OF frame "new" (older); ledger "life 6". The holy city comes "down out of heaven from God" (Revelation 21:2): NC "nt", fourth paragraph ("The direction of travel is striking"). Irenaeus of Lyons, *Against Heresies* (about 180), book 5: what passes away is the present form of the world, not its substance (5.36.1): NC "history", first paragraph. "I make all things new", not "I make new things": NC "nt", fourth paragraph; NC "differ" ("Renewal of this creation"). Christians differ on renewal or replacement of this creation, and on whether the vision of God or the renewed earth is at the center: NC "differ" (four views: renewal; replacement, Johann Gerhard; the vision of God at the center, Hans Boersma; the renewed earth and its culture, Wright, Mouw, Middleton). No side taken: OF `differ`.
5. **Every wrong named.** "For a modern reader 'judgment' usually means condemnation. In Scripture it is wider. To judge is to set things right, which means both condemning the oppressor and rescuing the oppressed": JS "definition", first paragraph. Miroslav Volf, after the wars in the former Yugoslavia, in *Exclusion and Embrace* (1996): belief in God's final judgment is not the enemy of peace but a condition of it, "because only those who trust God to judge can lay down the sword of revenge": JS "history", third paragraph; HA "Why there must be a judgment". The pause paraphrases HA's opening paragraph ("a world with no final judgment would be worse, not kinder, especially for the people who suffered most in it") and the argument's second premise (HA; HQ "The argument, laid out", step 1); it is put as a question only. Acts 17:31: OF frame "put-right"; ledger "life 5"; HQ "The evidence"; JS "nt".
6. **The hardest question.** Bertrand Russell, a lecture in London in 1927, published as *Why I Am Not a Christian*; the quotation "I do not myself feel that any person who is really profoundly humane can believe in everlasting punishment": HQ "The objection at its strongest"; HA "The objection at full strength"; HQ `objectionSource`. The proportion argument (a just punishment fits the wrong; human wrongs are finite; endless suffering is infinite): HQ same section; HA "The argument about proportion". Marilyn McCord Adams, a Christian philosopher, in "The Problem of Hell: A Problem of Evil for Christians" (1993), asked how a finite creature's sin could deserve an infinite penalty: HQ same section (the page reports her question as the Stanford Encyclopedia gives it; ledger "am", rows at line 2182 and 2183: the essay itself was not reached). The church has sometimes preached hell with relish and used the fear of it to control people: HQ "What is true in it"; HA "The objection at full strength".
7. **Three answers.** First view, held by most of the church through its history, the lost suffer conscious separation from God without end; Augustine; the Reformation confessions (Augsburg, Westminster): HQ "The evidence", second paragraph; HQ "Where Christians differ"; HL "history" and "differ" ("Eternal conscious punishment"). C. S. Lewis, *The Problem of Pain* (1940): "the doors of hell are locked on the inside": HQ "The evidence" (verified in ledger "am", line 2184); HA. Second view, conditional immortality: God alone is immortal (1 Timothy 6:16), the finally impenitent at last perish; Edward Fudge, *The Fire That Consumes* (1982): HQ "The evidence", third paragraph (Fudge verified in ledger "am", line 1994); HL "differ". Third view, the hope that God's love will restore all; Origen; Gregory of Nyssa; Thomas Talbott: HQ "The evidence", fourth paragraph; HL "history" and "differ" ("Universal restoration"). Most confessions reject it: HQ "Where Christians differ" ("a view most confessions reject"); HL "differ"; HQ "The evidence" ("The church's councils and most of its confessions have not received it"). The task names this view "hopeful universalism"; the film keeps the sources' wording ("hope ... restore all"), which covers both the certain form (Hart) and the hope without certainty (von Balthasar) that HL distinguishes.
8. **Each carries a cost.** The first view's difficulty, the proportion argument and an endless suffering that heals no one: HQ "The evidence", second paragraph. The second view's, Matthew 25:46 sets "eternal punishment" beside "eternal life": HQ third paragraph; HA. The third view's, Jesus' warnings, which speak of no way back: HQ fourth paragraph; HA. No side taken: HQ "What remains unresolved" (between the first two) and "Where Christians differ"; the film states the third only as most confessions have judged it, as HQ does. The pause is a question only.
9. **Not God's pleasure.** The strongest reply is Ivan's (Dostoevsky, *The Brothers Karamazov*): "I want to forgive. I want to embrace. I don't want more suffering" (Constance Garnett's translation): HQ "The objection at its strongest" and "The strongest reply, and an answer". The answer begins by refusing the picture that hell is God's pleasure: HQ "The strongest reply, and an answer". Ezekiel 18:23: HQ "The evidence". "Judgment ... is borne by God before it falls on anyone": HQ "The strongest reply, and an answer". No one knows who will be lost: HQ "What remains unresolved"; HL "errors". Judgment belongs to Christ, who wept over Jerusalem: HQ "The strongest reply, and an answer" (John 5:22; Luke 19:41 per HL "life").
10. **God at home.** "The best thing about the new world is not its river or its gates of pearl but that God dwells there": NC "life", second paragraph. "The God who wept at a grave will wipe away every tear himself": OF frame "tears" (older); ledger "life 7". Revelation 21:3: OF frame "new". Closing line ("Not souls leaving the world, but God making his home in it"): NC "definition" ("The Bible's last hope is not that souls will leave the world for somewhere else"); OF frame "new" ("God making his home with people").
