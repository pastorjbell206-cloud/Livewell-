## For James

**What the film leaves out on purpose.** From the definition: the English root "worthiness", the grammar debate over hishtachavah after the Ugaritic texts, halal, barak and yadah, and the three Greek words. From the Old Testament: Eden as the first sanctuary (Wenham), Cain and Abel, Abraham at Moriah, the meal at Sinai, the tabernacle and its pattern, the Levitical sacrifices and feasts, Nadab and Abihu (a death, and the regulative argument leans on it), Deuteronomy 12, Isaiah 6, the exile and the synagogue, and Malachi 1:11. From the New Testament: Hurtado and Bauckham, Antioch, Paul's gatherings and the early hymns, Hebrews' two remaining sacrifices and Hebrews 12, and the heavenly worship of Revelation 4 and 5 (only the city at the end is kept). The Lord's Supper is left, as the file leaves it, to the study on the sacraments. From history: the Didache, the Apostolic Tradition, Constantine, Egeria, Benedict, the whole Reformation list (Luther, Zwingli, Calvin's psalter, Cranmer, Trent, Heidelberg, Westminster), Watts and Wesley, the Quakers, Finney, Azusa Street, Vatican II and the "worship wars". The iconoclast controversy and Nicaea in 787 are left out on purpose: they lead straight into icons and the veneration of saints, where the file itself records disagreement. The whole `errors` section is left out except the golden calf. Each tradition's supporting texts are left out (Deuteronomy 12:32 for the regulative view, the Augsburg Confession and John 10:22 for the normative view, the church year for the liturgical view, 1 Corinthians 14:26 for the free-church view), so all four get the same short weight. Also left out: the psalm-only, no-instruments wing of the regulative tradition.

**Views named without choosing.** The regulative principle (Presbyterian and Reformed), the normative principle (Lutheran, Anglican and many other Protestants), liturgical and sacramental worship (Orthodox, Catholic and many Anglican and Lutheran Christians) and free-church and charismatic worship (Baptist, free-church, Pentecostal and charismatic). Each gets one line in the narration and its holders' own terms from the file. Each also gets an identical panel in the same ink, and each is lit only while it is being described. The line under all four ("how a church worships shapes what it believes") is the file's statement of what every tradition has found. It isn't a verdict. No church is named, compared or criticized. The panels shorten the holders to two names each (for example ORTHODOX · CATHOLIC); the narration gives the full lists. The film says "Orthodox, Catholic" where the file says "Eastern Orthodox, Roman Catholic".

**Who speaks and acts, in the Bible's own words.** "God tells Moses" (Exodus 3:12, "God said"). "The LORD called Israel out" (Exodus 4:22-23). "Aaron proclaims a feast to the LORD" (Exodus 32:5). "Through Amos, the LORD says" (Amos 5:17, 27). "What the LORD requires" (Micah 6:8). "Jesus replied" (John 4:21). "His servants will worship Him" (Revelation 22:3). Romans 12:1 is spoken without naming Paul.

**In the picture.** God and Christ are never drawn: a light, a cross and a throne stand for them, and the Holy Spirit is only a moving line of wind in the free-church panel. People are faceless stick figures and dots. The golden calf is drawn small and plain, then dimmed and ringed. Mustard is used sparingly: the light, the question mark, the calf, the gifts on the scale, the timeline markers, the Sunday thread, the room floors and the city gates.

**For you to check.** The film's "Sunday's gathering" stands for the file's "an hour on Sunday". The regulative line leaves out "whether expressly or by good and necessary consequence". Pliny's "on a fixed day" is left out; the film keeps "before dawn". Justin's "memoirs of the apostles or the writings of the prophets" becomes "the apostles and prophets were read". The narration runs 598 words (3,454 characters), near the top of the 480 to 600 range.

**Names to listen for.** Pharaoh, Aaron, Amos, Micah, Gerizim (GHER-ih-zim, hard G), Pliny (PLIN-ee), Justin Martyr, Presbyterian, Anglican, Eucharist (YOO-kuh-rist), Pentecostal, charismatic, liturgy, "the LORD" (read as "the Lord"). On the picture only, not spoken: HISHTACHAVAH (hish-tah-khah-VAH) and AVAD (ah-VAHD).
