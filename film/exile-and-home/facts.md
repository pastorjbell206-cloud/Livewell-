# Exile and coming home: the facts in the script, and where each was checked

This film is a rebuild of the Bible Explorer film `client/public/explorer/films/exile-and-home.json`, rewritten as a "big idea" film that follows exile and homecoming through the Bible. The title is the old film's, kept. Its sources are the old film (older captions and think question; it has no differ note), its rows in `docs/work/elite/explorer-ledger.md` ("exile 1" to "exile 7", "exile, care", "exile, left out", and "all four, quotations in captions"), the doctrine page `client/public/bible/doctrines/exile.json`, and the Study Bible notes in `client/public/bible/notes/` for Genesis 3, Psalm 137, Jeremiah 29, Isaiah 40, Ezra 1, Ezra 3, Nehemiah 9 and Revelation 21 (with the summaries of the Luke 15 and Ephesians 2 notes). `new-creation.json` was read and not needed: everything the last chapter says is in the exile page and the Revelation 21 note.

## Claims, by chapter

1. **A song far from home.** The exiles by the rivers of Babylon, the harps hung up, the captors' demand: Psalm 137 note, summary and cultural; "Babylon ... laced with irrigation canals, which is why the psalm speaks of the rivers of Babylon": Psalm 137 note, historical. "In Scripture home is the place where God dwells with his people": exile.json definition; old film frame "garden"; ledger "exile 1".
2. **The first exile.** God sent the man out of the garden and set cherubim to guard the way to the tree of life: BSB Genesis 3:23-24; exile.json "ot"; old film frame "garden". "Exile is what sin does: separation from God and from the place of blessing": exile.json definition, word for word; old film frame "garden"; ledger "exile 1".
3. **Out of Egypt.** Abraham lived in the land of promise as a foreigner: old film frame "egypt" ("Abraham himself lived as a foreigner in the land of promise"); exile.json "ot" (Genesis 23:4). His family went down to Egypt and became slaves; God saw their affliction: old film frame "egypt"; ledger "exile 2"; BSB Exodus 3:7. "The exodus became Israel's great homecoming, the pattern for every rescue after it": old film frame "egypt", word for word in substance; exile.json "ot" ("the exodus becomes Israel's defining homecoming").
4. **By the rivers.** The law warned that covenant breaking would end in scattering (Deuteronomy 28:64; Leviticus 26:33), and the prophets and histories say the exile was that cost: exile.json "ot" and "errors" ("The prophets and the histories insist that it was judgment on covenant unfaithfulness"); old film frame "babylon"; ledger "exile 3". Samaria fell in 722 BC to Assyria: exile.json "ot". In 587 or 586 BC Babylon burned the temple and deported much of the remaining population: Psalm 137 note, historical; exile.json "ot"; ledger row on 2 Kings 25:8-11. The fall is named, not described. "Scripture doesn't hurry past that grief": old film frame "babylon" ("the Bible does not hurry past the grief of it"); exile.json "life". God a sanctuary for the exiles: BSB Ezekiel 11:16; exile.json "ot"; old film frame "babylon".
5. **Build houses.** Jeremiah's letter to the exiles, running "against every instinct of a displaced people": Jeremiah 29 note, story. Build, plant, seek the city's prosperity and pray for it: BSB Jeremiah 29:5, 7; old film frame "babylon"; ledger "exile 3". Praying for a foreign city "was startling": Jeremiah 29 note, cultural (the viewer's pause grows from it and from the old film's think question, "What would it look like to seek the good of the place where you live while you wait?"). Return after seventy years; "most adults who first heard the letter would die in Babylon"; hope real but long: Jeremiah 29 note, hard ("would be fulfilled only after seventy years (verse 10), which meant that most adults who first heard the letter would die in Babylon") and doctrines ("The hope God offers is real but long").
6. **Comfort, comfort.** Isaiah 40 opens the second major part of Isaiah with comfort to a people in exile, and pictures the return as a new exodus: Isaiah 40 note, summary and story; exile.json "ot" ("picture the return as a new exodus"). Authorship: the traditional view, held by Jewish and Christian readers for most of history (Motyer, Oswalt), that Isaiah of Jerusalem in the eighth century BC was given these words for a later generation; most critical scholars since the late eighteenth century assign chapters 40 to 55 to an anonymous prophet of the exile; "either way they address the exiles' situation": Isaiah 40 note, historical; exile.json "ot". No side is taken.
7. **The long road back.** Cyrus of Persia took Babylon in 539 BC and let the exiles return to rebuild the house of the LORD: exile.json "ot"; Ezra 1 note, historical (October 539 BC); BSB Ezra 1:1-3. The foundation laid; many shouted for joy; the older people who had seen the first temple wept: BSB Ezra 3:10-13; Ezra 3 note, summary; old film frame "return"; ledger "exile 4". Nehemiah's prayer, years later back in the land (about 445 BC): Nehemiah 9 note, historical; BSB Nehemiah 9:36; exile.json "ot" ("Yet the return was partial").
8. **Were they home?** Two views, no side: the older reading, that the exile ended with the return under Cyrus and the larger promises are fulfilled in Christ: exile.json "differ", first view ("This is the older reading"). N. T. Wright's view that many Jews of Jesus' day believed the exile had not truly ended and that Jesus announced its end: exile.json "differ", second view, and "history" (Wright, 1992 and 1996). "Scholars still weigh both": exile.json "history" (James M. Scott's 2017 volume in which scholars "assessed the thesis") and "differ" (Wright's critics defend the older reading). The differ views on the twelve tribes (Pitre) and on a modern regathering are not used; the second would tie the passage to a modern nation.
9. **The way home opened.** Mark opens with Isaiah's words to the exiles; the gospel begins as the announcement that God is coming back to his people: exile.json "nt", nearly word for word; Isaiah 40 note, christ; old film frame "jesus"; ledger "exile 5". The father who ran to his son: BSB Luke 15:20; Luke 15 note, summary; old film frame "jesus". Jesus would die to gather the scattered children of God: BSB John 11:52; exile.json "nt".
10. **Foreigners on the way.** Peter calls believers foreigners and exiles; Hebrews: no permanent city here: BSB 1 Peter 2:11; Hebrews 13:14; exile.json "nt"; old film frame "strangers"; ledger "exile 6". Not escape from the world, not any nation as the promised home; like Jeremiah's exiles they seek the good of the city where they live: exile.json "nt", "errors" and "life"; old film frame "strangers", nearly word for word.
11. **Home at last.** "The dwelling place of God is with man": BSB Revelation 21:3; exile.json "nt". The tree of life, guarded since Genesis 3, stands by the river: exile.json "nt" ("stands on both sides of the river"); BSB Revelation 22:2. "The exile from Eden ends not with a return to the garden but with a garden city where God lives with his people": exile.json "nt", word for word; old film frame "city"; ledger "exile 7"; Revelation 21 note, story ("The garden has become a city"). Paul to those once far off: Ephesians 2 note, summary ("Gentiles, once far from God's people and promises, have been brought near"); BSB Ephesians 2:19; old film frame "city".
- **Title card.** "Exile is what sin does. Coming home is what grace does.": exile.json definition ("Exile is what sin does ... and restoration is what grace does"), with "restoration" said as "coming home".

## Notes

- The old film's care line ("If you have had to leave a home, or have lost someone or something you loved...") stays on the page, word for word, and is not in the narration.
- No people is blamed: the exile is told as Israel's own law, prophets and prayers tell it, and exile.json "errors" (the tradition's rejection of using Israel's exile as proof of God's rejection of the Jewish people) is honored by saying nothing of the kind. No modern state, people, border or conflict is named (ledger "exile, left out").
- Psalm 137's cry against Babylon (verses 8 and 9) is not quoted or described.
- Left to sibling films: the nations streaming in, Pentecost and Babel answered (all-nations); the kingdom's announcement (kingdom-of-god); the city's light and "no night there" (Revelation 21:23-25; light-and-darkness), which is why the old film's Revelation 21:25 is replaced by 21:3. The glory leaving the temple and Lamentations are left to temple-destroyed and god-with-his-people.
- Cut for length, though sourced: Matthew's genealogy counted "from the exile to the Christ" (exile.json "nt"; BSB Matthew 1:17); Abraham's own words, "I am a foreigner and an outsider among you" (BSB Genesis 23:4); the Al-Yahudu tablets (Jeremiah 29 note, historical; exile.json "ot").
