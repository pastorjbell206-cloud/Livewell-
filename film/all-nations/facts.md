# A blessing for all nations: the facts in the script, and where each was checked

This film is a rebuild of the Bible Explorer film `client/public/explorer/films/all-nations.json`, rewritten as a "big idea" film that follows the promise to Abraham alone. Its sources are the old film (older captions and older think question; it has no differ note and no care line), its rows in `docs/work/elite/explorer-ledger.md` ("nations creation" to "nations, left out", and the section "A blessing for all nations (`all-nations`)" in the run of 2026-11-25), the doctrine page `client/public/bible/doctrines/mission.json`, and the Study Bible notes in `client/public/bible/notes/` for Genesis 11 and 12, Ruth 1 and 4, Psalm 67, Jonah 4, Isaiah 49, Matthew 2 and 28, Acts 2 and 10, and Revelation 7. `client/public/bible/doctrines/israel.json` was read for its guardrail (no teaching that God has cast off the Jewish people) and nothing in the film is drawn from it. Two stops the old film did not have are added from those sources: Psalm 67 and Cornelius (Acts 10). The old film's Magi frame is reduced to one clause, and its Simeon and "healing of the nations" lines are left out for length.

## Scripture (Berean Standard Bible)

Each quotation was checked word for word against `client/public/bible/text/` by `film/tools/write-script.py` on 2026-11-27.

- **Genesis 11:8** (spoken): "So the LORD scattered them from there over the face of all the earth"
- **Genesis 12:3** (spoken): "all the families of the earth will be blessed through you"
- **Ruth 1:16** (spoken): "your people will be my people, and your God will be my God"
- **Psalm 67:2** (spoken): "that Your ways may be known on earth, Your salvation among all nations"
- **Jonah 4:11** (spoken): "So should I not care about the great city of Nineveh" (the narration ends the clause with a question mark; the verse runs on)
- **Isaiah 49:6** (spoken): "I will also make You a light for the nations, to bring My salvation to the ends of the earth."
- **Matthew 28:19** (spoken): "Therefore go and make disciples of all nations"
- **Acts 2:11** (spoken): "we hear them declaring the wonders of God in our own tongues!" (the only exclamation mark, inside the verse)
- **Acts 10:34-35** (spoken): "God does not show favoritism, but welcomes those from every nation who fear Him" (the line also speaks the start of verse 34, "I now truly understand that")
- **Revelation 7:9** (spoken): "a multitude too large to count, from every nation and tribe and people and tongue, standing before the throne and before the Lamb"

Other Bible words in the narration, not in a scripture field:
- "a tower that reaches to the heavens", "that we may make a name for ourselves": BSB Genesis 11:4.
- "I will make your name great": BSB Genesis 12:2.
- "bless us": BSB Psalm 67:1 ("May God be gracious to us and bless us").
- "I knew that You are a gracious and compassionate God, slow to anger": BSB Jonah 4:2.
- "every nation under heaven": BSB Acts 2:5.

## Claims, by chapter

1. **A name for ourselves.** People settle on a plain (Shinar) and build a city and tower reaching to the heavens to make a name for themselves: Genesis 11 note, summary; BSB Genesis 11:2-4. "The tower is so small that the LORD must come down to see it": Genesis 11 note, hard ("the tower is so small that the LORD must come down to see it"); BSB Genesis 11:5. Judgment and mercy: Genesis 11 note, hard ("Most interpreters ... the scattering is both judgment and mercy"; God "acts to limit what sin can accomplish when concentrated in one place and one power"). The note also gives readings of the story's history (origin of languages, a portrait of Babylon, a real early event); the film does not take one. Old film frame "babel" and ledger "nations fall".
2. **One family, every family.** The next chapter answers Babel; "surprisingly small"; one old man whose wife cannot have children: old film frame "abraham", older; ledger "2 One family" ("The Abraham stop, older, nearly word for word"); Genesis 11 note, story ("in the next chapter God will promise to make a name for Abram"); Genesis 12 note, story; mission.json "ot" ("The next chapter answers Babel"). Sarai barren: BSB Genesis 11:30; Genesis 11 note, words (aqar). The builders wanted a name, God promises one: Genesis 11 note, words (shem). The Hebrew word for families (mishpachah) is the one Genesis 10 uses to list the peoples; "The promise answers Babel family by family": Genesis 12 note, words (H4940), word for word on the last sentence. "Old" restates the old film; Abram is seventy-five at the call (Genesis 12 note, summary).
3. **Your God, my God.** Rahab of Jericho and Ruth from Moab joined God's people: old film frame "ruth"; ledger "nations land"; mission.json "ot". Deuteronomy 23:3 excluded Moabites from the assembly of the LORD: Ruth 1 note, historical; Ruth 4 note, hard. In that world a people and its god belonged together; to change peoples was to change gods: Ruth 1 note, cultural. Great-grandmother of David: Ruth 4 note, hard and christ; ledger "3 Rahab and Ruth". Naomi is Ruth's mother-in-law: BSB Ruth 1. The deaths in Ruth 1 are not named, as in the old film (ledger "left out").
4. **Bless us, so that.** Psalm 67 echoes the priestly blessing of Numbers 6:24-26 and turns it into a prayer; God blesses Israel so the nations will know him: Psalm 67 note, summary, story and cultural; mission.json "ot". Johannes Blauw, The Missionary Nature of the Church (1962), described Israel's role as chiefly "centripetal": the nations drawn in to Israel's God rather than Israel sent out: mission.json "ot". (Said as "In 1962 Johannes Blauw called ...", the year being the book's.) "A means rather than an end": Psalm 67 note, words (H1288, "the psalm frames it as a means rather than an end").
5. **The great city.** God sends Jonah to Nineveh; he runs (toward Tarshish): BSB Jonah 1:2-3; ledger "4 The great city". Assyria "the most feared military power of the day": Jonah 4 note, historical. Jonah is furious that God spared Nineveh and finally explains why he ran: Jonah 4 note, summary. He quotes Israel's central confession from Sinai (Exodus 34:6), and "Jonah recites the creed as a complaint": Jonah 4 note, cultural. The book ends with God's question and no answer from Jonah; the question is aimed past Jonah at the reader: Jonah 4 note, story and hard. "Is there anyone you'd find it hard to see God bless?": the old film's older think question, nearly word for word. Assyria's later deeds (Samaria, 722 BC) are not named.
6. **Not enough.** God tells the servant that restoring Israel is not enough and makes him a light for the nations: BSB Isaiah 49:6; Isaiah 49 note, summary. The servant is called Israel in verse 3 yet sent to restore Israel; readers have answered: the nation of Israel or a faithful remnant within it, the prophet himself, an individual of the prophet's time or a future king; Christians, following the New Testament, identify him ultimately as the Messiah, Jesus: Isaiah 49 note, hard (four main ways). The film names all and takes no side.
7. **All nations.** Early in Matthew (chapter 2), Magi from the east who come to worship: BSB Matthew 2:1-2; old film frame "magi"; Matthew 28 note, story ("The Gentile Magi who worshiped the child find their answer in the command to make disciples of all nations"). On a mountain in Galilee the risen Jesus claims all authority and sends the eleven to make disciples of all nations: Matthew 28 note, summary; BSB Matthew 28:16-19. Whether panta ta ethnē means ethnic peoples as groups or all people is debated; Ralph Winter and Donald McGavran stressed people groups: mission.json "nt". "The order is from Israel to the nations, not Israel instead of the nations": mission.json "nt", word for word but for "runs".
8. **In our own tongues.** Pentecost (the Feast of Weeks); Jews from every nation under heaven, including converts; each heard his own language: Acts 2 note, summary and historical; BSB Acts 2:5-11. Called "Jewish pilgrims", following BSB Acts 2:5 and mission.json "nt" ("Pentecost gathers Jews from every nation under heaven"); the old film said "visitors from many lands" (ledger "6 Every language"), and the Acts 2 note, historical, says some had settled in Jerusalem and others came for the feast. Christians have long read Pentecost as the reversal of Babel; the New Testament does not say this in so many words; the many languages are not erased but each carries the same message: Genesis 11 note, christ; Acts 2 note, story. Cyril and Methodius went to Moravia in 863 and created an alphabet for the Slavic language so that the Scriptures and liturgy could be heard in it: mission.json "history".
9. **No favorites.** The chapter's question, "can a person who is not Jewish belong fully to the people of Israel's Messiah without first becoming Jewish?": Acts 10 note, story. Cornelius, a Roman centurion (officer) who worships Israel's God: Acts 10 note, summary and cultural. Luke tells it at length, has Peter retell it in chapter 11, and refers to it again in chapter 15; "Everything that follows ... depends on the answer": Acts 10 note, story. Ledger "nations church" (Acts 10:34-35).
10. **Too large to count.** The multitude from every nation before the throne and the Lamb: BSB Revelation 7:9; old film frame "multitude". "The promise to Abraham's family has become a people from the whole world": Revelation 7 note, christ, word for word. "Every tongue is still there" restates the verse's "tongue" against chapter 8. "Every believer received the gospel because someone crossed a boundary to bring it": mission.json "life" (said as "heard this message"). Title card: "God chose one family for the sake of every family": old film frame "abraham", ledger "nations abraham" (the thread's own line, word for word).

## Notes

- The old film has no care line (ledger "left out": "no care line, since the film tells of no death, war or loss"), and none is added.
- Left out: the 144,000 (Revelation 7 note, hard; the old film also left it out), Rahab's trade and the fall of Jericho, the number of the Magi, Simeon (Luke 2:32), the healing of the nations (Revelation 22:2), Galatians 3:8, the Yom Kippur reading of Jonah, and every later history of missions beyond Cyril and Methodius. Isaiah 9 and John 8 (light), exile and return, and the kingdom are the sibling films' ground.
- No modern state, people, border or politics. No people held in contempt: Babel is the builders' pride, Nineveh is the city God cares about, and the commission is "from Israel to the nations, not Israel instead of the nations".
