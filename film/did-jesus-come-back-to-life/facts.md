This film rebuilds the Bible Explorer film `client/public/explorer/films/did-jesus-come-back-to-life.json` at greater length. Its sources, and nothing else, are: that film's older captions and its rows in `docs/work/elite/explorer-ledger.md` (lines 386 to 389); the question pages `client/public/questions/jesus/the-empty-tomb.json` and `client/public/questions/jesus/the-appearances-and-the-alternatives.json`, with their record files `docs/work/elite/apol-pilot/jesus/the-empty-tomb.md` and `docs/work/elite/apol-pilot/jesus/the-appearances-and-the-alternatives.md` (same text); and the Honest Answer "Did the resurrection actually happen?" in `client/src/data/answers.ts` (slug `did-the-resurrection-actually-happen`). Below, "Answer" means that Honest Answer; "Tomb page" and "Appearances page" mean the two question pages.

## Claims, in script order

1. **A fair question.** God raised Jesus in his body: Answer, argument premises and conclusion; Appearances page, "Where Christians differ" (raised bodily). History can't prove a miracle; the case is stronger than most have been told: Answer, opening paragraph; old film, frame "question".
2. **The case against.** Hume, "Of Miracles", 1748; the dead staying dead "as uniform as experience gets"; the witness always more likely "deceived or deceiving": Answer, "The case against, at its strongest", paragraph 1. Bart Ehrman, an agnostic (at the University of North Carolina, per the same paragraph); the Gospels written decades after the events: Answer, same section, paragraphs 2 and 3. Rome often left the crucified unburied: Tomb page, short answer; Answer, same section, paragraph 3. "Legends do grow. Grief does produce visions. A claim that a dead man rose should face a higher bar": Answer, same section, paragraph 4.
3. **What historians grant.** A short list nearly every historian of the period accepts, skeptics included: Answer, opening paragraph; old film, frame "died". Crucified under Pontius Pilate; Tacitus, a Roman historian who despised Christians, recorded it: Answer, "What nearly every historian grants", paragraph 1; old film, frame "died". Followers convinced they had seen him alive; Paul passes on a summary he received; most scholars date it within a few years: Answer, same section, paragraph 2; old film, frame "seen". E. P. Sanders, "no apologist", judged the resurrection experiences a fact and did not know what gave rise to them: Appearances page, "The evidence", paragraph 1 (his words there: "That Jesus' followers (and later Paul) had resurrection experiences is, in my judgement, a fact. What the reality was that gave rise to the experiences I do not know."). The film paraphrases; it does not quote him.
4. **Two unlikely witnesses.** Paul tried to destroy the church; James, Jesus' brother, did not believe (John 7:5); James led the church in Jerusalem until he was stoned around 62: Answer, "What nearly every historian grants", paragraph 3; Appearances page, "The evidence", paragraph 2; old film, frame "changed". The "Pause here" question is the film's own, and states no fact.
5. **In the same city.** Preaching began in Jerusalem within weeks, where he was buried and the claim was easiest to refute: Answer, same section, paragraph 4; old film, frame "city". The empty tomb is more disputed; historians still divide over it: Answer, same section, paragraph 5; Tomb page, short answer. The "fair question" about who an inventor would choose is the film's own, drawn from the Tomb page's argument step 3.
6. **Details no one would choose.** Mark names Joseph of Arimathea, a council member; an inventor would more likely have chosen a disciple: Tomb page, "The evidence", paragraph 3. All four Gospels put women first at the tomb: Tomb page, "The evidence", paragraph 4; Answer, paragraph 5 of "What nearly every historian grants". Josephus: "let not the testimony of women be admitted" (*Antiquities* 4.219, Whiston's translation): Tomb page, "The evidence", paragraph 4. The caution that Josephus described a court of law and the argument shouldn't be overstated: Tomb page, "What is true in it", paragraph 2. The earliest opponents we know of said the body was stolen, not that it was still in the grave: Tomb page, short answer and "The evidence", paragraph 5; Answer, same paragraph; old film, frame "city". "An argument among Jews of the first century, Jesus' followers among them", over how, not whether: Tomb page, "The evidence", paragraph 5. (The film names no group as the source of the theft story, and blames no people.)
7. **The word they chose.** Their world had words for seeing the dead; Peter freed from prison, "It must be his angel" (Acts 12:15): Appearances page, "The evidence", paragraph 3; Answer, "The other explanations", paragraph 2. N. T. Wright: resurrection meant the bodily raising of God's people at the end of history; no one expected one man raised ahead of everyone while the world went on: same two places.
8. **The other explanations.** Visions fit Peter's grief; Paul wasn't mourning Jesus but hunting his followers: Appearances page, "The evidence", paragraph 5; Answer, "The other explanations", paragraph 2; old film, frame "explanations". Legend: the core is in a list formed within a few years: Appearances page, "The evidence", paragraph 5. Fraud: men who knew where they had put the body suffered for the claim: same paragraph; Answer, "The other explanations", paragraph 1. David Friedrich Strauss, a skeptic, nineteenth century: a half-dead man needing bandages could never convince anyone he had conquered death: same two places (the film drops "nineteenth century").
9. **The strongest reply.** A mixture of explanations (lost grave, vision, spreading conviction, later legend); any combination of ordinary events beats a miracle; the pieces multiply and each is "thin where it most needs to be thick"; it depends on whether there is a God who might raise the dead: Appearances page, "The strongest reply, and an answer". "Had you already decided that God can't act": Answer, opening paragraph and "What this does not prove"; old film, frame "weigh".
10. **What stays open.** What the witnesses saw is beyond the reach of history; historians who grant the experiences disagree about the cause: Appearances page, "What remains unresolved". Christians differ on how much weight historical arguments carry; the church has confessed together that Jesus was raised bodily, the body that was buried: Appearances page, "Where Christians differ"; Tomb page, "Where Christians differ".
11. **Weigh it.** History won't carry anyone to faith but can move a person from dismissing the claim to weighing it; Matthew doesn't hide the doubt (Matthew 28:17); you can weigh it all and still say no: Answer, "What this does not prove"; old film, frame "weigh". "Just don't say no without the weighing" restates the Answer's "Reaching that conclusion without doing the work isn't [a respectable place to stand]". Title card, "Weigh it before you dismiss it.", is the film's own line from the same paragraph.
