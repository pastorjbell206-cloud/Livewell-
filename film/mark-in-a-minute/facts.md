# Mark in a minute: the facts in the script, and where to check each

None of these was checked against a primary source in this session. Each is standard and widely published; check before recording. Scripture lines are the Berean Standard Bible, copied from `client/public/bible/text/`.

- **Shortest of the four Gospels:** 16 chapters.
- **"Immediately":** the Greek word euthys comes about forty times in Mark, far more than in any other Gospel. Check with the site's own page, /study/word.
- **No birth story:** Mark opens with John the Baptist and Jesus as an adult (Mark 1:1-15).
- **"The kingdom of God is near":** from Mark 1:15, given as a paraphrase.
- **The storm, the healings, "your sins are forgiven", "Who is this?":** Mark 4:35-41; 1:29-34; 2:5.
- **Mark 8:29, 10:45, 15:39:** quoted word for word. The narration leaves out "Jesus asked" and "Peter answered"; the full verse is on screen.
- **"The one who finally says it out loud is a Roman soldier":** the centurion is the first human being in Mark to call Jesus the Son of God. Unclean spirits say it earlier (3:11), so the script does not say "the first".

## Checked on 2026-11-26, before recording

- Scripture: Mark 8:29 (shown whole), 10:45 and 15:39 match `client/public/bible/text/mark/`.
- "Shortest": by the site's own Bible files Mark has 13,823 words in 16 chapters; Matthew 22,193, Luke 23,879, John 18,418. The four bars at the start are drawn to those numbers.
- "Immediately": the site's word index (`client/public/bible/words/occ/G/21.json`, G2112) counts the Greek word 42 times in Mark, 18 in Matthew, 7 in Luke, 6 in John. The Berean text itself prints "immediately" 12 times in Mark, because it also translates the word "at once" and "as soon as".
- Picture: Jesus is never a figure; his way is a mustard line. The cross is bare. The soldier is one small square. On screen beyond the script: the four Gospel names and chapter numbers 1 to 16.
