Read the New Testament and you keep running into a small phrase. In Christ.

What does that mean? How can one person be in another?

A Christian isn't only helped by Jesus from a distance. A Christian is joined to him.

What's true of him becomes true of them. They died with him, were raised with him, and will appear with him.

The Holy Spirit makes that bond, and faith receives it.

Paul uses it, and close cousins like in him, all through his letters. In 1892 the scholar Adolf Deissmann counted a hundred and sixty-four of them.

He also reaches for words that mean with. Crucified with. Buried with. Raised with.

The Old Testament never says this outright. It gives the ground the idea grows from.

A God who makes his home in the middle of his people.

A Hebrew word for clinging, used when husband and wife become one flesh. The prophets made that bond a picture of God's love.

And one standing for many, the way Adam's choice reached the whole human race.

Israel had been God's vine. Jesus took the picture for himself.

I am the vine and you are the branches. The one who remains in Me, and I in him, will bear much fruit. For apart from Me you can do nothing.

Belonging to God's people now means being a branch in him.

Later the risen Jesus asked Saul, who was persecuting the church, why Saul was persecuting him.

To strike the church is to strike Christ.

For Paul, Adam and Christ each stand for a whole humanity. Joined to Adam, people share his death. Joined to Christ, they share his life.

Believers were buried with him through baptism, so they could walk in a new life.

Every spiritual blessing is given in him. Chosen in him. Redeemed in him. Sealed with the Spirit in him.

And no one is joined to him alone. Together, believers are his body.

Around the year 180, Irenaeus taught that Jesus went back over the ground where Adam failed, obeying where Adam disobeyed, so those joined to him share his victory.

In his Institutes, John Calvin wrote that as long as Christ stays outside us, all he suffered and did is no use to us.

The Holy Spirit, he said, is the bond that joins us to him.

Christians describe this union in different ways.

Reformed Christians say believers receive two gifts in him, never separated. Declared right with God, and made new.

Lutherans say Christ comes through Word and sacrament. Luther pictured a joyful exchange. Christ takes the believer's sins and gives his righteousness.

Roman Catholics say baptism joins a person to Christ and his body, the church, and the Eucharist feeds that union.

The Orthodox call it theosis, sharing by grace in God's life, while God's essence stays beyond every creature.

Every one of them insists it's by grace. A creature joined to God is still a creature.

This changes where a Christian finds who they are.

For you died, and your life is now hidden with Christ in God.

Failure and success are real. But they don't define someone whose life is found in another.

Suffering isn't proof that God is absent. It can be one of the ways the union is lived.

And the people beside them at the Lord's Supper, however different, belong to the same body.

Even death doesn't break it. Paul calls believers who have died the dead in Christ, still in him, waiting for the resurrection.

No part of a Christian's life, not even its end, is outside of Christ.
