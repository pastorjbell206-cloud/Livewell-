# The Counter-Reformation: the facts in the script, and where each comes from

Every fact comes from one file, the lesson `client/public/history/essays/the-counter-reformation.json` (page /theology/history/the-counter-reformation). Section keys: **TT** = "Two Tellings, Both Flattering" (two-tellings), **TR** = "Trent Rebuilds the Old Church" (trent), **NO** = "Ignatius, Xavier, Teresa" (new-orders), **FN** = "France, the Netherlands, and the Massacres" (france-and-the-netherlands), **TY** = "The Thirty Years War" (thirty-years), **WP** = "What Westphalia Ended" (westphalia), **KF** = keyFigures, **DR** = dateRange. Paragraphs are numbered within a section (TR2 = the second paragraph of TR).

The one exception is the chapter "Where Christians differ". As the review asked, each tradition's reading there uses only wording a LiveWell page already gives: **RC** = `client/public/history/essays/the-reformation.json` (section "The Catholic Response and the Cost", catholic-response), **RW** = `client/public/history/essays/reformation-and-its-wars.json` (sections "Worms and the Word" and "Geneva, and Rome's Own Reformation"), **JD** = `client/public/bible/doctrines/justification.json` (the differ section).

## For James

**Left out on purpose.** Trent's other two lines (seven sacraments; the Mass a true sacrifice), the three popes, the thirty bishops at the opening session, Matteo Ricci, Xavier "baptizing until his arms were weary", Ignatius reading in his sickbed, and "held that shape for four hundred years": all cut for length. Galileo (1633), and Rome's Te Deum and medal for Saint Bartholomew with the Catholic historians' shame: the lesson gives no Protestant failure of the same kind to set beside them, so keeping them would have weighted the grief toward one side; the Inquisition and the Index stay, followed at once by the conviction every side shared. How anyone died: the cannonball, the sack and fire of Magdeburg, "hanged and quartered", the name "Council of Blood". Henry of Navarre, the Edict of Nantes (1598) and its revocation (1685); Gustavus Adolphus at Lutzen; the cardinal; the angels and the dung heap; Augsburg 1555, the right to emigrate, and Innocent the Tenth's condemnation of the peace. Peter H. Wilson's name (the film says "one historian") and the older reckoning of a third. The lesson's lines spoken to Protestants ("A Protestant should say so plainly", "Protestants should be able to say so") became "any tradition should be glad to claim it", so the film does not assume a Protestant viewer.

**Views named without choosing.** In "Where Christians differ": Catholics see a church renewing itself from within, teaching that grace comes first and no one can earn it; Protestants see Trent rejecting what the reformers had recovered, grace received by faith alone and Scripture as the final word. The two lines are 20 words each; on screen they are two panels of the same size, with the same three label lines, rising from one book ("THE SAME RECORD") and marked with the same water. Trent's teaching (chapter 2) is set beside the reformers' in two mirror-image columns. The failures of every side are named in the same plain words: the Inquisition and the Index; one conviction held by Catholics, Lutherans and the Reformed; the hunted Anabaptists; Saint Bartholomew's Day; Spain's Council of Troubles and Protestant England's executions of priests, in one line; "Over the sword they hardly differed at all"; Protestant nobles at Prague; imperial troops at Magdeburg; Catholic France paying Lutheran Sweden. The Orthodox are not in this lesson, so the film does not name them.

**Points to check.**
- Matthew 5:9 ("Blessed are the peacemakers"). The lesson quotes no verse. Review chose this one for the close, beside the lesson's "the long labor of being a different kind of church", in place of the first draft's John 13:35, because the East-West schism film already closes on John 13:35 and the two lessons sit three apart in the course.
- The film keeps the lesson's own name, "Counter-Reformation". Many Catholics prefer "Catholic Reformation", but no LiveWell page uses that phrase, so the film doesn't; the chapter on Trent's reforms is titled "The reform was real", the lesson's words.
- "Protestants see ..." stands without "Many" so it matches "Catholics see ..." word for word. Justification.json notes that the Joint Declaration (1999) holds the Lutheran and Catholic views share a basic consensus, and that some Lutherans now read Trent's condemnations as not applying to them. Add "Many" to both lines if you want that room.
- The reformers' two cards and both readings use RC, RW and JD wording, not this lesson's.
- "The men survived" names two officials; the lesson adds their secretary.
- Word count is 593, near the top of the 480 to 600 range.
