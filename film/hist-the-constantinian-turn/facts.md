## For James

**Left out on purpose.**
- Every tie to a present-day country, party or ruler. The lesson's "some throne or party or public" became "some power", and its line about "a country that has never burned our Scriptures" is not used.
- Fausta's death, Crispus's name, the dates 326 and 337 (they're in the lesson; the film keeps the facts and drops the names and years for length), and Julian's death on a Persian campaign.
- Julian's letter ordering his priests to copy the Christians' kindness to strangers and care for the dead (a strong detail, cut only for length).
- The return of confiscated property, the churches over Peter's tomb and the holy sites, "the empire's conscience", "Doctrine acquired a politics", "Bishops learned the roads to court", "the world's accent", and "the hand holding it was ours".
- The lesson's sources (Gonzalez, Chadwick, MacMullen, Brown, MacCulloch) are not named.

**Views named without choosing.**
- Was Constantine sincere? The case for calculation and the case for conviction are each given in the lesson's facts, then the reading the lesson says most historians now hold. The film doesn't judge his soul. On the scale, the left pan tips with the first case and the right pan levels it with the second.
- Suspicion or tears: the film gives our suspicion looking back and the tears of those who lived it, side by side, as the lesson does.
- Gains and costs are both laid out at length, and the close keeps the lesson's balance: favor needn't corrupt, but it will try.
- The two exiles (Nicene and non-Nicene) are drawn the same and neither side is named.
- The lesson gives no Catholic, Orthodox or Protestant verdict on Constantine himself (some churches honor him as a saint), so the film gives none.

**Check these.**
- The lesson cites no verse. Hebrews 13:14 was chosen because the lesson ends on Augustine's point that no city of man is the church's destiny; the line "Hebrews says the same" makes the link, which the lesson itself doesn't.
- "granted Christians full legal freedom" shortens "agreed to full legal freedom for the Christian faith".
- "Most historians now see a real believer" shortens "a man genuinely persuaded that the Christian God had given him victory".
- In the crowd picture, about half the dots turn hollow for "some for careers". The lesson adds "often in the same person", which the narration dropped for length; you may want that back.
- 599 words, at the top of the 480 to 600 range.
