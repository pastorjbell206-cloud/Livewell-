## For James

**Left out on purpose.**
- Genesis 50:20 as a spoken verse. It is the file's "classic statement", but the BSB text has a dash in it, so the film gives it in reported speech and quotes Matthew 10:29 and Proverbs 16:9 instead.
- The Hebrew, Greek and Latin words (providentia, providere, YHWH yir'eh, ra'ah, pequddah, pronoia, chashav, manah, pherō), and Genesis 22 and "The LORD Will Provide".
- Most of the Old and New Testament witness: the flood promise, Hannah, Micaiah and Ahab, Esther, Cyrus, Isaiah on Assyria and Cyrus, Daniel, Lamentations 3:38, Amos 3:6, Jonah, the Psalms, Proverbs 21:1, Job's opening chapters in detail, Ecclesiastes 9:11 by quotation, the man born blind (John 9:3), Acts 4:27-28, Paul's ship (Acts 27), Acts 14 and 17, Colossians 1:17, Hebrews 1:3, Ephesians 1:11, Romans 8:28-29 and its manuscripts, Philippians 1:12, 2 Corinthians 12:9, 1 Peter 5:7, Romans 11:33, Revelation 5. Romans 8:28 was left out on purpose, to keep the comfort no larger than the file's and to avoid a verse often used to explain a particular loss.
- The long history: Augustine, Boethius, Aquinas, Zwingli, Calvin, the Belgic Confession, Westminster, Molina and Báñez and the papal commission, Arminius, Wesley's sermon, deism, Leibniz, the Lisbon earthquake and Voltaire, the twentieth century's wars and the Holocaust, Barth, and open theism. The Lisbon earthquake and the Holocaust were left out so that no disaster is named as if it were explained.
- Every name attached to the three views (Aquinas, Calvin, Westminster, Frame, Helm; Arminius, Wesley, Oden, Olson; Molina, Craig, Flint, Keathley), and each view's further arguments (the author and story; God grieving and pleading; Keilah and Tyre and Sidon; which account gives the fullest comfort or best guards God's goodness). Each view gets one claim of the same length and no famous names.
- The errors section (fatalism, deism, naturalism, dualism, God as the author of sin), except the pastoral error of reading every tragedy as punishment, which the Siloam line and the struck-through card answer. Nehemiah's guard and the use of slogans to silence grief.

**Views named without choosing.** The Reformed and Thomist account, the Arminian and Wesleyan account and the Molinist account, in the file's order, each in one line of 27 or 28 words, each in its own defenders' terms. In the picture each is a column of the same width and ink with no mustard in any column: for the Reformed and Thomist account an author's pen writing the page while the figures on the page move by their own arrows (the file's own picture, "as an author is not a character in his story"; drawn without strings so it does not look like puppetry); for the Arminian and Wesleyan account a governing ring with figures at open forks; for the Molinist account three possible worlds and the one chosen. Each column is lit while it is spoken and dimmed while the next is spoken, and all three return to full strength together under one bracket: "EACH POINTS TO SCRIPTURE".

**Points to check.**
- "Each one points to Scripture for its answer." and "Take the next step. The world isn't yours to carry." are the film's summaries, not sentences in the file.
- "Christians have long disagreed..." opens the differ chapter with a sentence from the history section; the views themselves come only from `differ`.
- Suffering is named (the brothers' envy, false accusation, prison, the eighteen at Siloam, the cross, wrong done to you, Job's losses) and never pictured. The cross is drawn empty; Siloam is a struck-through formula, not a tower; the sparrows only perch.
- The film calls the cross "the greatest case" where the file says "supreme case".
- The film is 599 words, at the top of the range.

**Names to listen for.** Joseph, Ruth, Boaz, Ecclesiastes, Siloam, Pentecost, Stoics, Epicureans, Thomist, Arminian, Wesleyan, Molinist, Heidelberg Catechism.
