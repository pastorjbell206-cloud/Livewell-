## For James

**Left out on purpose** (for length or for the violence rule): Tacitus and the charge "hatred of the human race" (cut for length; a good line if you want one more); Justin's six companions and his surviving trial record; the letter from Lyons, and the torture Blandina outlasted (named nowhere, shown nowhere); Perpetua's diary as one of the earliest Christian writings by a woman; forged certificates; Valerian's name and his aim at bishops and church property; Galerius pressing Diocletian, and Galerius asking the Christians to pray for him; the Donatist quarrel; Polycarp and Quintus, the man who volunteered and then sacrificed; Julian's complaint about the Galileans feeding everyone's poor; the lesson's lines "including ages recent enough to include us" and "Most of us would have been among the lapsed". No death is pictured: a lit candle beside a name is the only sign, and fire never appears near a person or a book.

**Views named without choosing:** Novatian's rigorist view (those who denied Christ could never be restored by the church) is stated with the lesson's own fairness ("His case was serious", and the reason: the martyrs had died rather than say the word); Cyprian and the larger church's slower way (return through repentance over time, case by case) is reported as what the church chose, which is history the lesson records. The lesson itself does not set Catholic, Orthodox and Protestant readings against each other, so the film adds none. The turn to the sword is laid on "Christians" in general, with no church or tradition singled out and nothing tied to any modern nation, party or person.

**Small choices to check:** "died still confessing Christ" (the lesson says "died still confessing"); "abandoned babies" for "infants Rome left exposed"; "Trajan told a governor named Pliny" (Pliny wrote first and Trajan answered; the picture shows both letters). The closing card reads "Whom do we worship?"

**Names to listen for:** Nero, Trajan, Pliny, Justin, Lyons (the lesson's English spelling), Blandina, Carthage, Perpetua, Felicity, Decius, Cyprian, Diocletian, Galerius, Constantine, Novatian, Tertullian, catacombs. The years are written as numerals (64, 112, 165, 177, 203, 250, 258, 303, 311, 313, 197): check the voice says them as years ("until three thirteen" or "three hundred thirteen", either is fine), and "twenty-two-year-old" as one phrase.
