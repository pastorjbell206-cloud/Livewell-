## For James

**Left out on purpose.** The scars of persecution on the bishops at Nicaea (cut for length; easy to restore as one sentence). The "sixteen centuries" line and the "fence around a mystery" wording (the picture still shows the fence). The Cappadocians' names (Basil, Gregory of Nazianzus, Gregory of Nyssa) are not spoken. The emperors' and bishops' motives are kept to one sentence. No Greek word is named or shown: the film says "same substance" and never says homoousios or Theotokos, because the lesson doesn't use them. Eutyches is not named, and no group is named as the error behind "blended into some third thing".

**Views named without choosing.** Two readings of Chalcedon. Most churches accepted it. The ancient churches of Egypt, Syria and the East did not. The film says only that they "did not accept it" and that "Christians still read that council differently". It gives no reason for their refusal and doesn't place them beside the "blended into some third thing" line. Those churches also reject Eutyches, and the film never implies they hold his view. It also keeps the lesson's own split verdict on Nestorius: "partly for what he taught and partly for the politics".

**Scripture.** The lesson quotes no verse. Both verses come from John 1, tied to the lesson's own title, "Nicaea and the Word Made Equal". John 1:1 stands beside Nicaea's "true God from true God". John 1:14 opens the close beside "fully God and fully man". If you'd rather keep only verses a lesson quotes itself, cut John 1:1. The script is under the 600-word limit either way.

**Names to listen for:** Nicaea (nye-SEE-uh), Chalcedon (KAL-seh-don), Arius (AIR-ee-us or uh-RYE-us), Athanasius (ath-uh-NAY-shus), Cappadocians (kap-uh-DOE-shunz), Theodosius (thee-uh-DOE-shus), Nestorius (neh-STORE-ee-us), Ephesus, Antioch, Alexandria, Constantinople.
