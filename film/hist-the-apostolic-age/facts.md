## For James

**Left out on purpose.** Tacitus and the tortures he recorded after the fire (named nowhere, pictured nowhere). How Stephen, Polycarp, Ignatius, Peter and Paul died, beyond saying that they died; the film says "stoned" once because that is how the lesson names Stephen's death, and nothing is drawn. Ignatius begging the churches not to rescue him and his wish to die for Christ. The Roman church "feeling responsible" for Corinth, since that point is read differently by different churches and the film had no room to set the readings side by side. The lesson's sentence "The Scripture did not create the church. The church recognized the Scripture," for the same reason. The lesson's four sources (Gonzalez, Chadwick, MacCulloch, Shelley) are not named.

**Named without choosing.** One line in the film is not taken from the lesson: the readings of "that line of leaders" in the chapter "Read in different ways". Catholic and Orthodox Christians are said to see in it "the line of bishops that has guarded the faith ever since", and many Protestants to see "faithful witnesses, whose words are weighed by Scripture". The film then says both start from the same letters and that it doesn't choose. This is a plain description of each tradition's reading, added because the brief asks for the readings to be named. Please confirm the wording, or tell us to cut the chapter and close straight from "What held them". The picture gives the two readings two equal lanes from the same stack of letters, in the same ink, with nothing to mark either one as right.

**Picture choices that go past the lesson.** The map puts Jerusalem, Antioch, Ephesus, Smyrna, Corinth and Rome roughly where they sit, drawn as dots and lines with no coastlines. The traveler's route from Jerusalem through Antioch and Ephesus to Corinth and Rome is a picture of "on foot and by ship along Roman roads", not one real person's journey. Ignatius's road drops letters at small unnamed churches, as the lesson says he wrote to "the churches he passed", without naming which. Saul turns mustard and a ring widens when he becomes Paul; nothing is drawn of how it happened. The fire of 64 is a pale haze over the city's roofs, with no flames and no people.

**One phrase to hear aloud.** "The first opposition came from fellow Jews" follows the lesson's "family quarrel". Please check that it sounds as the lesson means it: a quarrel inside one family.

**Names to listen for.** Antioch, Corinth, Ephesus, Pentecost, Messiah, Stephen, Saul of Tarsus, Nero, Caesar, the Apostolic Fathers, Clement of Rome, Ignatius of Antioch (ig-NAY-shus), Polycarp of Smyrna (POL-ee-carp, SMUR-nuh). Numbers: "In 64" should be "sixty-four", "the year 96" should be "ninety-six", and "Around 110" should be "one hundred and ten" (or "one-ten").
