# The Church Since 1900: the facts in the script, and where each comes from

Every fact comes from one file, the lesson `client/public/history/essays/the-global-church.json` (page /theology/history/the-global-church, lesson 14, the last of the course). Section keys: **CN** = "The Century Nobody Predicted" (the-century-nobody-predicted), **FM** = "Fundamentalists and Modernists" (fundamentalists-and-modernists), **BA** = "Barmen and the German Failure" (barmen), **BO** = "Bonhoeffer" (flossenburg), **UC** = "The Churches Under Communism" (under-the-hammer), **AZ** = "Azusa Street and the Rising South" (azusa-and-the-south), **GR** = "The Average Christian Is Not a Westerner" (the-great-reversal), **KF** = keyFigures, **T** = the lesson's title. Paragraphs are numbered within a section (FM2 = the second paragraph of FM).

The one exception is the chapter "Where Christians differ". As the review asked, each reading there uses only wording a LiveWell page already gives: **MI** = `client/public/bible/doctrines/mission.json` (the differ section: "Evangelism as the church's primary task" and "Integral mission"), **RS** = `client/public/history/essays/reading-from-the-south.json` ("Integral Mission").

**Picture only.** Every label restates the lesson. The century time line at the foot runs 1900 to 2000 (the lesson's "AD 1900 to today"); its marker stops only at years the lesson gives: 1900, 1910, "1910 TO 1925" (The Fundamentals 1910 to 1915, the Tennessee trial 1925, FM1 to FM2), 1933, 1934, 1945, 1939, 1943, 1979, "THE TWENTIETH CENTURY" (UC3), 1906, 1907, 1921, "1930s", "1962 TO 1965", 1974, "AROUND 1980" and "THE CENTURY'S END" (GR1). Map dots sit at their real longitude and latitude on a plain grid with no coastlines: Los Angeles, Pyongyang, the Congo, East Africa, Lausanne, and Walls's centers (Jerusalem, the Greek cities, Rome, the northern peoples, the Atlantic world). The dot for the Second Vatican Council sits at Rome, which the lesson does not name for the Council; it carries no place label. The clusters of Christians are shares, not counts: 50 of 60 dots in Europe and North America in 1900 ("better than four of every five", CN1), then 50 of 102 around 1980 ("most ... were not", GR1); northern dots are never removed, so the picture claims no northern numbers the lesson does not give. The share bar shows the same two fractions. The parishes are 300 small marks thinning to 4 ("tens of thousands" to "a few hundred", UC1), and Africa's bars are 10 against 300 ("perhaps ten million", "more than three hundred million", GR1). The two dark pillars are labelled only "FASCISM" and "COMMUNISM" (CN2) and carry no party sign. "MOST GERMAN PROTESTANTS WELCOMED HIM" (BA1), "BARMEN · MAY 1934", "NOTHING DIRECTLY ON THE PERSECUTION OF THE JEWS" (a blank on the page, BA2), "STUTTGART · OCTOBER 1945" and "NOT COURAGEOUS ENOUGH" (BA3), "DISCIPLESHIP" and "LIFE TOGETHER" (BO1), "NEW YORK · SUMMER 1939", "1943 · ARRESTED", "FLOSSENBURG · APRIL 1945", "WEEKS BEFORE THE WAR'S END", "ONE BONHOEFFER, AND A GREAT SILENCE" (BO2, BO3), "CHINA · THE CHURCHES REOPEN AFTER 1979" and "TENS OF MILLIONS · BY EVERY SERIOUS ESTIMATE" (UC2), the twenty centuries with the twentieth ringed, "MORE THAN IN ANY CENTURY BEFORE IT · BY MANY RECKONINGS" (UC3, no heights, so no counts are drawn), "PENTECOSTALISM SPREADS ALONG SHIPPING ROUTES AND MISSION NETWORKS" (AZ1), "THE CHURCH'S HEART MOVES SOUTH" (AZ2), "EACH MARGIN A CENTER" and "ONE MORE PROVINCE" (GR3). The six arcs from Los Angeles stand for "around the world"; they are not routes the lesson names. Bonhoeffer's death is marked by one hollow ring on his time line, the series' sign for a life remembered. Nothing violent, no camp and no person is drawn; the six million are named in words over an empty page. The throne in the close is an outline with light; the Lamb is not drawn. The multitude wears white (the dots turn white as they arrive) and a few carry small palm strokes.

## For James

**Left out on purpose.** The two California oil men and the three million copies of The Fundamentals; Fosdick's 1922 sermon and Machen's 1923 book (the lesson gives Machen's argument and only Fosdick's question, so keeping them would have weighted one side); the 1925 Tennessee trial, its verdict and Bryan's death ("won the verdict and lost the press" judges one side); the evangelicalism of Billy Graham's generation (Graham survives only as a key figure, not named in the film). "It ought to hurt to read" (a feeling line), the German Christians and the Aryan clause, Niemoller's seven years in the camps, Finkenwalde and the Gestapo, the conspiracy, the camp doctor's words, the ninth of April. The dynamiting of the Cathedral of Christ the Saviour, the swimming pool and the rebuilt cathedral; the clergy shot in the purges; the 1937 census, where more than half still declared themselves religious; the missionaries expelled from China by 1952 and "a pressure that has never fully lifted" (it points at a present-day government). Seymour's blind eye, the newspapers' mockery and "washed away in the blood"; Kimbangu's thirty years in prison and his church of millions; Korea as a missionary-sending nation; the Latin American pope (a modern person). Philip Jenkins's woman in a Nigerian village or a Brazilian favela; Lesslie Newbigin's return to England in 1974; the African pastors leading congregations in Europe's capitals; prosperity preachers and syncretism. All cut for length or to keep to the rules on modern governments and on naming persecution without describing it.

**Views named without choosing.** Fundamentalists and modernists, each in the lesson's own words, 18 words each, in two mirror-image halves of one house of the same size, with the same two label lines; the accusation runs both ways. In "Where Christians differ", after the lesson's own Lausanne sentence: integral mission ("two sides of one witness, not tasks to be ranked") and evangelism as primary ("works of mercy flow from the gospel and serve it"), 18 words each, in MI's wording. Because the lesson's Lausanne line already states the integral view, the evangelism-is-primary reading is given the second, closing place. On screen they are two panels of the same size, each with a title, one moving picture made of the same two signs (a book and a bowl) and one footer line, rising from one base, "THE SAME CALL". Catholics, Orthodox and Pentecostals appear in the story (Vatican II, the Orthodox parishes, Azusa Street), but no LiveWell page gives a reading of this century for each, so the film names none. The Pentecostal and cessationist readings of the gifts (in `bible/doctrines/gifts.json`) are not raised; the film tells Azusa Street as history only.

**Points to check.**
- Revelation 7:9. The lesson quotes no verse. This one was chosen for the close, beside the lesson's "a kingdom that keeps its capital elsewhere", because LiveWell's own page on this era (reading-from-the-south.json) ties the century to "the great multitude of Revelation 7". No other film on the site quotes it. Two other films in this course were being made at the same time, so check that neither also closes on it.
- "In Revelation, John writes what he saw." is there so the verse's "I" is heard as John, not the narrator.
- "executed" for "hanged" (Bonhoeffer), as the Counter-Reformation film did.
- "the northern peoples" on screen for the lesson's "the northern barbarians".
- "The South was stirring too" says "Korea" for the lesson's "Pyongyang"; the map label says "PYONGYANG · 1907".
- The word count is 591.

**Names to listen for.** Barmen, Bonhoeffer (BON-hoff-er), Flossenburg, Stuttgart, Azusa (ah-ZOO-sah), William Seymour, Pyongyang, Kimbangu (in the picture only), Lausanne (loh-ZAN), Andrew Walls, Darwin, Vatican.
