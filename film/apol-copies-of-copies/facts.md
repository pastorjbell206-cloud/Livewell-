# Copies of copies: the facts in the script, and where each comes from

Every fact below is taken from LiveWell's own pages, which carry their sources and a ledger (`docs/work/elite/apol-ledger.md`, tenth and eleventh runs, 2026-11-20). Page keys: **C** = /questions/text/copies-of-copies, **V** = /questions/text/more-variants-than-words, **A** = /questions/text/compared-with-other-ancient-books, **T** = the area page /questions/text.

| Line | Fact | Source |
|---|---|---|
| "We don't have the original New Testament. Not one page." | No autograph of any New Testament book survives. | C, T |
| "copied by hand for nearly fifteen hundred years, until it was first printed in Greek in 1516" | "copied by hand for nearly fifteen hundred years before it was printed"; first printed Greek New Testament 1516 (Erasmus). | T, V |
| "differ ... in more places than there are words in the New Testament" | Estimates of variants run from 200,000-400,000 (Ehrman) to about 500,000 (Gurry 2016, NTS 62); the Greek NT has about 138,000 words. | V |
| "no book of the ancient world survives in more copies than this one" | "more of those copies survive than for any other book of the ancient world". | V, A |
| "The first six books of the Annals of Tacitus survive in a single medieval copy." | Same wording on V and A. | V, A |
| "Most are spelling and simple slips ... word order ... one late copy against all the rest ... a small share ... less than one percent" | The four kinds from Wallace et al., *Reinventing Jesus* (2006); Wallace's estimate of under 1% for meaningful and viable variants. The film says "one leading scholar" and gives no other percentages. | V |
| Matthew 24:36, "nor the Son" | The oldest manuscripts have "nor the Son"; the majority, including the later Byzantine ones, leave it out; Metzger judged it original and dropped for doctrinal difficulty. The film says "most likely". | V, C |
| "from Egypt to Gaul, by people who never met" | "copies were being made in Egypt, Syria and Italy by people who never met"; Irenaeus in Lyon in Gaul around 180; "spread across the Roman world, from Egypt to Gaul". | C, V |
| "A copy of Luke and John from about the year two hundred agrees closely with a great Bible made more than a century later." | P75 (usually dated about 200) and Codex Vaticanus (fourth century); one study put agreement at about 85%. The film gives no percentage. P75's date is disputed (Nongbri argues later); the film says "about" and the next chapter says the dates are argued. | C, V |
| "Around the year one eighty, Irenaeus said the oldest copies of Revelation read six hundred sixty-six, not six hundred sixteen." | Irenaeus, about 180, knew copies with 616 and said 666 stood in "the most approved and ancient copies". | C |
| "The earliest pieces we have are small, and their dates are argued over." | P52 (Roberts: first half of the second century; Nongbri: as late as early third). | C |
| "A fragment of Mark was once announced as first century ... late second or early third ... apologized in public" | Daniel Wallace, 2012 debate; published 2018 among the Oxyrhynchus papyri; Wallace apologized. The film does not name him. | C |
| "to hide in those early years, a change would have had to spread everywhere and erase the older wording in every branch" | The site's answer to the strongest reply. | C, V |
| "Homer's Iliad ... about seventeen hundred and fifty manuscripts" | Clay Jones (2012), 1,757. | A |
| "The earliest copies of Julius Caesar's war in Gaul were made about nine hundred years after he wrote it." | Written first century BC; earliest manuscripts ninth century AD, "some nine centuries later". | A |
| "more copies, in more languages, closer to its writing, than any of them" | "On any honest count the New Testament has more witnesses, in more languages, from more places, and its earliest copies stand closer to the writing". | A |
| "Bart Ehrman ... has said the essential Christian beliefs don't rest on a disputed reading" | Ehrman, *Misquoting Jesus*, Q&A appendix: "essential Christian beliefs are not affected by textual variants in the manuscript tradition of the New Testament." Paraphrased, not quoted. | V |
| "knowing what Matthew wrote doesn't tell you whether it happened" | The site's own concession to Ehrman's Plato question. | A |

## For James

- The film takes no side between the critical text, the Majority text and the King James text, and says nothing on inerrancy. The pages it comes from take no side either.
- Pictures that show counts (the comparison bars) are drawn from the figures above; the "kinds of difference" scene shows order of size only, with no numbers except "under 1%".
- Names to listen for: Tacitus, Irenaeus, Iliad, Gaul, Ehrman.
