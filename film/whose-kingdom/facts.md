# Whose kingdom? The facts in the script, and where to check each

None of these was checked against a primary source in this session (no library access here). Each is standard and widely published; check before recording.

| Line | Fact | Where to check |
|---|---|---|
| "His face was on the coins." | Roman coins carried the emperor's portrait; Jesus points to one in Matthew 22:19-21. | Matthew 22:19-21; any catalogue of the denarius of Tiberius. |
| "people called him lord" | The emperor was addressed as "lord" (Greek kyrios). In Acts 25:26 the governor Festus calls the emperor "the lord" in the Greek. | Acts 25:26 in the Greek text (the Study Bible's interlinear shows it). |
| "Jesus is Lord" | The earliest Christian confession. | Romans 10:9; 1 Corinthians 12:3. |
| "Back then it could get you killed." | Christians were executed for refusing to honor the emperor as lord. The clearest early accounts are from the second century: Pliny's letter to Trajan (about AD 112) and the Martyrdom of Polycarp (about AD 155), where Polycarp is asked what harm there is in saying "Caesar is Lord". The kicker "Rome, the first century" sits over the Caesar scene, not this line. | Pliny, Letters 10.96; Martyrdom of Polycarp 8 to 9. |
| "Your kingdom come, Your will be done, on earth as it is in heaven." | Matthew 6:10, Berean Standard Bible, whole verse. | `client/public/bible/text/matthew/6.json` |
| "Nations rise and nations fall. Rome did." | The Roman Empire in the West ended in AD 476. | Any history of late antiquity. |
| On screen: "we are receiving an unshakable kingdom" | Hebrews 12:28, Berean Standard Bible, a clause of the verse. | `client/public/bible/text/hebrews/12.json` |

## For James

- "Every nation is tempted to borrow God for its own story. Ours is too. So am I." puts the writer inside the charge, as your voice standard asks. It names no party and no person.
- "You can love your country. It's a good gift." is there so the film can't be heard as contempt for patriotism.

## Checked on 2026-11-26, before recording

- Scripture: Matthew 6:10 and the clause of Hebrews 12:28 match `client/public/bible/text/` word for word.
- "people called him lord": Acts 25:26 has "to the lord" (Greek) for the emperor Festus has just named in verses 21 and 25; the Martyrdom of Polycarp 8 has officials urge Polycarp to say "Lord Caesar". The on-screen words "Caesar is lord" stand for that title; they are not shown as a quotation.
- "Back then it could get you killed.": confirmed. Pliny to Trajan (governor AD 111 to 113): "those who persisted I ordered executed"; Polycarp refuses and is put to death.
- "His face was on the coins.": the denarius of Tiberius carried his head (Wikipedia, Tribute penny). The film draws a coin with a wreath and the name, never a face.
- "Jesus is Lord": Romans 10:9 and 1 Corinthians 12:3.
- "Rome did.": AD 476 is the usual date for the end of the empire in the West.
- Every flag in the picture is a plain pennant. No real flag, nation, party or person appears.
