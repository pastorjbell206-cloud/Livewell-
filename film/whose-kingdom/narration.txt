There is a moment in the life of nearly every American church.

The sanctuary is decorated with flags, and the worship set includes a patriotic hymn.

And in that moment, something happens that most people in the room do not notice.

The line between worship of God and celebration of nation becomes invisible.

It has a name. Sociologists call it civil religion.

"God Bless America" quietly replaced "Thy Kingdom Come" as the functional prayer of the church.

Our Father in heaven, hallowed be Your name.

Your kingdom come, Your will be done, on earth as it is in heaven.

That is the invitation: not to stop caring about America, but to stop treating the American project as though it were the Kingdom of God.

A church that knows it belongs to a Kingdom that cannot be shaken can face any political outcome with sorrow, yes, with concern, yes, but never with despair.

The church does not need a nation to survive. The church needs a King.

And it already has one. His name is Jesus.

Let "Thy Kingdom Come" be the prayer of the church.
