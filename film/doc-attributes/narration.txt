What is God like? Many have a quick answer.

The God of the Old Testament is angry. The God of Jesus is kind.

But the Old Testament itself says otherwise.

Christians speak of God's attributes, the Bible's ways of saying what God is like.

He is holy, righteous, faithful, merciful, patient, and wise. All-powerful, all-knowing, present everywhere, eternal, and unchanging.

They never pull against each other. His justice is always loving, and His love is always just and holy.

Israel had just worshiped the golden calf when Moses asked to see God's glory.

Then the LORD passed in front of Moses and called out.

The LORD, the LORD God, is compassionate and gracious, slow to anger, abounding in loving devotion and faithfulness, maintaining loving devotion to a thousand generations, forgiving iniquity, transgression, and sin. Yet He will by no means leave the guilty unpunished

Notice who heard it. A people who had just broken the covenant.

Mercy and justice stand together, but not evenly. Loving devotion to a thousand generations, judgment to the third and fourth.

Loving devotion translates the Hebrew word chesed. It's loyal, committed love, most often within a covenant. No single English word catches it.

No description of God is echoed more in the Old Testament. Moses, Nehemiah, the psalmists, and Joel prayed it back to Him.

Jonah even complained that God was exactly this merciful to Nineveh.

Isaiah heard the seraphim calling out, holy, holy, holy. In Hosea, that holiness shows itself as mercy.

John says the Word made flesh was full of grace and truth, and many hear an echo of loving devotion and faithfulness.

In Jesus, God's character is seen. His compassion on the crowds, His anger at the temple, His faithfulness to the cross.

At the cross, Paul says, God is both just and the one who justifies those who have faith in Jesus. Justice and mercy meet there.

John puts it in three words. God is love, a love defined by His sending His Son.

That quick answer is old. In the second century, Marcion divided a just Creator from a loving Father.

Around the year 180, Irenaeus answered that the one God is both good and just.

The words to Moses are full of mercy, and Revelation is full of judgment. It's one character.

Christians differ here. How does God's steady character fit His real response to human choices?

In one chapter, the LORD regrets making Saul king, and Samuel says the Glory of Israel does not change His mind.

Classical theists, like Thomas Aquinas, say God is unchanging in His being, timeless, and not overcome by passions. Regret, they say, is true speech fitted to human understanding.

Others, like Nicholas Wolterstorff, see God's eternity as endless time, because the Bible's God speaks, waits, and hears prayer.

Still others, like Jürgen Moltmann, say God truly suffers, from love freely chosen. They point above all to the cross.

Believe God is powerful but not quite good, and you'll obey out of fear. Believe He's good but not quite able, and you'll pray without hope.

Scripture holds them together. His power is always the power of His goodness.

In grief, His faithfulness is the reason to keep speaking to Him. In guilt, His mercy is the reason to return. In confusion, His wisdom is the reason to wait.

At the Bible's end, the four living creatures take up Isaiah's cry.

Holy, Holy, Holy, is the Lord God Almighty, who was and is and is to come!

The God who is faithful and cannot lie will keep what He has promised.
