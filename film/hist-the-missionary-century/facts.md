# The missionary century: the facts in the script, and where each comes from

Every fact comes from one file, the lesson `client/public/history/essays/the-missionary-century.json` (page /theology/history/the-missionary-century). Section keys: **MP** = "The Map in 1790" (the-map-in-1790), **KE** = "Carey and the Use of Means" (kettering), **WV** = "Societies, Judsons, Taylor, Livingstone" (the-wave), **EN** = "The Gospel and the Gunboat" (the-entanglement), **WF** = "Crowther and the Local Evangelists" (hidden-workforce), **ED** = "Edinburgh 1910 and the Verdict" (edinburgh-1910), **KF** = keyFigures, **DR** = dateRange. Paragraphs are numbered within a section (KE2 = the second paragraph of KE).

The one exception is the chapter "Where Christians differ". This lesson gives no Catholic, Orthodox or Protestant reading of its era, so, as the review asked, each tradition's line there uses only wording a LiveWell page already gives: **MI** = `client/public/bible/doctrines/mission.json` (its definition and its differ section).

## For James

**Left out on purpose.** Cut for length: the older minister's rebuke, "expect great things from God, attempt great things for God", the thirteen pounds, the Greek, Hebrew and Dutch at the workbench, the East India Company and the Danish station, Marshman and Ward, Carey's campaign against sati and its outlawing in 1829, poverty, malaria and his wife's grief and illness (the film says only "He lost a child"); the four societies and their years; women outnumbering men on many fields; Brighton beach, the ten pounds and the twenty-four workers; India licensing missionaries from 1813; Livingstone's death in 1873, with Chuma and Susi carrying his body a thousand miles and Westminster Abbey; the Congo Free State and George Washington Williams's open letter of 1890; Lamin Sanneh by name; John Mott and the student watchword; the Uganda martyrs; the independent churches as "the bill"; the Thomas Christians by name (the picture says "INDIA"). Three of these name people the review asked to have named where the lesson names them: **Chuma and Susi**, **George Washington Williams** and **the Uganda martyrs** (unnamed in the lesson). They were cut only because the film is at 599 words. If you want any back, the easiest trade is the Hudson Taylor line (18 words) or the Ramabai line (13 words, but she is a key figure).

**Views named without choosing.**
- *Where Christians differ.* The lesson gives no reading of this era by tradition. I checked the other history lessons (reading-from-the-south, the-global-church, after-christendom-era, the-awakenings and the rest) and found no fair Catholic, Orthodox and Protestant line about these missions, so the chapter takes the one question LiveWell's doctrine study of mission says Christians differ on, how preaching and works of mercy belong together, and it ties that question to the lesson only through what the missions built: churches, schools and hospitals. The three lines are 16 words each, in that page's own words: Catholic (proclaiming Christ joined to works of charity, with no true evangelization without proclaiming Jesus), Orthodox (the liturgy as the heart of witness, meant to overflow into the world), Protestant (many put preaching first, others call word and deed one witness). The Protestant line holds two views because the doctrine page shows Protestants on both sides. On screen there are three panels of the same size and frame, each emblem present faintly from the start, each lit only while its own line is spoken, all lit together at the end, and each with one mustard mark of like size. Lesson 7's film used other sentences from the same page (missionary by nature, local languages and clergy, preaching Christ first), so the two films don't repeat each other. If you'd rather keep strictly to the history lessons, cut this chapter; the film falls to about 535 words.
- *Failures and courage.* Both are told in the same plain words: the missionaries' racial contempt, customs made into requirements, the faith beside opium, Crowther's humiliation, the near-empty seats for Asia and Africa at Edinburgh; and Carey's staying, Ann Judson and the women's graves, Knibb, translation, the local believers who were "the engine", Ramabai, Crowther's scholarship and Azariah. The Chinese Christians of 1900 and the missionary families are marked with the same rings. National believers the lesson names are named (Crowther, Ramabai, Azariah); local workers are shown walking, visiting and crossing, never as a crowd acted on.
- *The lesson's own verdict.* "The empires shipped a book that condemned them" and "God used the ships without blessing the cargo" are the lesson's judgments, kept as its own. LiveWell's reading-from-the-south lesson grants that critics who stress the colonial entanglement "are describing something real"; the film shows that entanglement at length but doesn't stage it as a debate between traditions.

**Points to check.**
- **Exodus 20:2.** The lesson quotes no verse. It does say "Converts read Exodus and drew conclusions", so the film quotes the first words of the commandments, and the line before it uses the Bible's own words for who speaks ("And God spoke all these words", Exodus 20:1). The BSB text opens a quotation that closes later in the chapter, so the caption shows an opening quote mark alone. Revelation 7:9 was avoided because lesson 14's film closes on it, and none of the course's used verses (John 13:35, Matthew 5:9, Matthew 19:21, Acts 8:20, Zechariah 4:6, John 3:3, Hebrews 13:14, and the others) is repeated. The film doesn't close on a verse. It closes on the lesson's own last lines.
- "his mission had printed" stands for the lesson's "the Serampore presses"; the picture names Serampore and says "whole or in part".
- The close says "at great cost" (the lesson: "staggering personal cost") and "sped by empires it didn't control" (the lesson: "an empire it did not create and could not control"); the plural matches "the empires" in the rest of the film. "the cargo" shortens the lesson's "the cargo manifests".
- "Race-proud younger Englishmen" is the lesson's word. "Missionaries" would also be true to the history if you'd rather not name a nation, but the lesson doesn't say it, so the film keeps the lesson's word.
- The David Livingstone in this film is the nineteenth-century missionary explorer the lesson names, not your partner in India.
- 599 words, at the top of the 480 to 600 range. Characters in narration.txt: 3,686.
