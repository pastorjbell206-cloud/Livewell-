## For James

- **Left out on purpose.** The Hebrew and Greek words (tsadaq, dikaioō, logizomai). The argument from Luke 7:29 that the word means "declare right" rather than "make righteous", because that argument is part of the Catholic and Protestant dispute. Habakkuk 2:4, Zechariah 3 and Isaiah 53:11. Clement and Calvin by name in the narration (Clement appears only as a label). Osiander, Regensburg, Wesley, Küng, Barth, Sanders, Barclay and the Methodist, Anglican and Reformed responses to the 1999 declaration. Pelagius and Bonhoeffer's "cheap grace". The New Perspective on Paul (Dunn, Wright, and its critics Westerholm and Piper): it is a debate among scholars rather than a church's confession, and naming it fairly with its critics would have pushed the film past 600 words. If you want it, one line can replace "The acquitted have every reason to pass that mercy on."
- **Named without choosing.** Lutheran and Reformed (a verdict, Christ's righteousness credited, faith the empty hand, a changed life that follows); Catholic (made righteous, love poured in by the Spirit, grace first, faith the root with hope and love, can grow, be lost and be restored through repentance and penance); Orthodox (salvation wholly God's gift, theosis, the courtroom one picture among many, the West's line between declaring and making righteous). The three panels on screen have the same size, the same cross at the top, and one touch of mustard each. Paul and James get the majority reading and the minority reading. On 1999, the film gives both the declaration's claim and its Protestant critics.
- **Title changed in review** from "Declared right" (closest to the Reformation emphasis) to "Right with God". To balance it, the closing line and the end card avoid "declared" and "verdict": "It's His work, and it comes as a gift" and "Right with God, as a gift."
- **Names to listen for:** Abram (AY-bram), Job (JOBE), Pharisee, Augustine (aw-GUS-tin or AW-gus-teen; pick one and keep it), Martin Luther, Council of Trent, theosis (thee-OH-sis), sacrament of penance, Lutheran World Federation.
