## For James

**Left out on purpose** (to keep the film near four minutes and on the main line):

- The Isaiah 7:14 debate (*almah* and *parthenos*; a first fulfillment in Ahaz's day or a direct prophecy; the Jewish reading). The film names Immanuel and nothing more.
- The Old Testament appearances (Mamre, Jacob, the angel of the LORD, Manoah), Micah 5:2, Daniel 7, Psalm 45, and the Wisdom of Proverbs 8.
- The manuscript notes on John 1:18 and 1 Timothy 3:16.
- Arius and Nicaea (325), Athanasius, Irenaeus, Tertullian, the Ebionites and adoptionists by name, Nestorius, Cyril, *Theotokos*, Ephesus (431), Eutyches, Leo's Tome, the later councils (553, 680-681, 787), Maximus by name, Anselm, and the modern debates (*The Myth of God Incarnate*, Hurtado and Bauckham).
- The Oriental Orthodox churches and the Church of the East. Because the film does not name Chalcedon's opponents, it never risks pinning Eutyches's error on the churches that did not accept Chalcedon's wording (the file warns against that).
- Two of the four questions in the differ section: whether the Son took a fallen or an unfallen human nature (Irving, Barth, Torrance against the majority view and Crisp), and the Lutheran and Reformed difference over how the natures share their properties (the Formula of Concord and the Heidelberg Catechism).
- Christmas and the Annunciation dates.

**Views named without choosing:**

- Would the Son have become incarnate without the fall? Thomas Aquinas ("more fitting" to say no, since Scripture gives the remedy for sin as the reason) and John Duns Scotus (yes, Christ the goal of creation). The scale stays level.
- What did the self-emptying of Philippians 2:7 involve? The classical reading (He veiled His glory and stayed fully God) and the kenotic reading (He laid aside the use of some divine powers for a time). The film says "Christians answer both ways" and names neither camp's theologians.

**Choices to check:**

- "God only wearing a body?" is the film's plain way of saying "merely appeared human". If it sounds flippant to you, "God only wearing a body?" is the file's own picture.
- The film says "In Greek, made His dwelling means pitched His tent, the same word used for the tabernacle." Strictly, the verb is *skēnoō* and the tabernacle word is its root *skēnē*, the word the Septuagint used. "From" covers that.
- "Ignatius wrote" stands for the file's "insisted" in his letters.
- Pronouns for God and Christ are capitalized (He, His), to match the example film and the BSB text on screen.

**Names to listen for** (hard for the voice): Ignatius of Antioch (ig-NAY-shus, AN-tee-ock), Gregory of Nazianzus (nah-zee-AN-zus), Chalcedon (KAL-seh-don), Thomas Aquinas (uh-KWY-nus), John Duns Scotus (DUNZ SKOH-tus), Immanuel, Malachi (MAL-uh-ky), Gethsemane (geth-SEM-uh-nee), Sinai (SY-ny).
