## For James

**How God is shown.** Never drawn. The Trinity appears only as three rings of the same size, stroke and fill (made by one function in the film, so they cannot differ), always evenly spaced, never merging and never pulled apart; one plain circle for the one God; and light. At the baptism the Spirit is a small light coming down a column of light (no bird is drawn), the Father's voice is sound rings from the opened sky, and Jesus is only the ripples on the water with a label. The water and the masks are written on cards that are struck through, never drawn. No shamrock, egg or other picture appears.

**Left out on purpose** (to keep the film under four minutes and on one line, from "one or three?" to the blessing):

- The vocabulary beyond homoousios: ousia, hypostasis, the Latin substantia and personae, trias in Theophilus around 180, trinitas in Tertullian, the argument from the Hebrew 'echad, and the relations of origin (unbegotten, begotten, proceeds) except where the creed's "proceeds" sets up the filioque.
- Most of the Old Testament evidence: Isaiah 44:6, the plurals of Genesis 1:26 (and the two ways of reading them), the angel of the LORD with Hagar, Exodus 23:21, Isaiah 63:9-10, the three visitors at Mamre, Wisdom in Proverbs 8, Psalms 2, 45 and 110, Isaiah 9:6, 11:2 and 61:1, Daniel 7, Ezekiel 36:27 and Joel 2:28.
- One fair line I wrote and then cut for length: "Jewish readers have read every one of these texts without it." The film keeps only "Christians read it back in the light of Christ." It can go back in if you want it.
- Most of the New Testament list: John 1:18 and its manuscripts, monogenēs, John 5:18, 8:58, 10:30, Thomas's "My Lord and my God", Philippians 2, Colossians 1-2, Hebrews 1, Romans 9:5 and Titus 2:13; "the Father is greater than I" and Mark 13:32; the paraklētos and the pronoun argument; Galatians 4:4-6; Revelation 5:13; and the words added to 1 John 5:7 in the King James Version.
- Most of the history: the Didache, Irenaeus's "two hands", Noetus, Praxeas and Sabellius, Tertullian's Against Praxeas, Origen, Athanasius and his five exiles, the Cappadocians, inseparable works, Augustine's analogies of the mind, the Athanasian Creed, Toledo 589, Photius, 1054, Lyon and Florence, John of Damascus, Aquinas, Servetus, the Socinians, the Protestant confessions, Schleiermacher, Barth, Rahner, Moltmann, Zizioulas and Ayres. Arius's city, Alexandria, is not named; Constantinople is on screen but not spoken.
- The names of the errors (modalism, Arianism, adoptionism, the Pneumatomachians, tritheism) and the present-day groups the file names as their heirs (Oneness Pentecostalism, the Jehovah's Witnesses). The film describes the errors without naming living churches, so it does not read as an attack in a three-minute piece. The egg and "one man as father, son and husband" are not mentioned.
- The life section's paragraph on God coming near (the Son not a messenger outside God, the Spirit not a substitute), John 17:21 and the church's unity, and the hope to share in God's life.

**Views named without choosing** (all four entries of the differ section, as three debates; each pair spoken in two sentences of near-equal length and drawn as two panels of the same size, colour, stroke and type on either side of a level beam that never tilts; nothing highlighted; no defender named, so neither side carries a famous name the other lacks):

- **The filioque.** The West: the Spirit eternally proceeds from both the Father and the Son (14 words). The East: from the Father alone, and the Son sends Him into the world (15 words).
- **Classical and social models.** Classical theologians: one mind, one will, one power in God (12 words). Social trinitarians: three persons knowing and loving each other in communion (12 words).
- **Order and authority within God.** Some evangelicals: the Son, though equal in being, eternally and gladly submits to the Father (15 words). Others: the three share one will, and the Son obeyed as the servant He became (16 words).
- Closed with "These are debates within the confession, not against it." The file says both sides of the last two hold the Nicene confession and the Son's full deity.

**Choices to check:**

- The opening question says "Jesus is God" where the confession says "the Son"; it is put in the words a newcomer would use.
- "Every day they confessed the Shema" comes from the file's first study question, not its body text.
- "A church leader named Arius" stands in for the file's "presbyter in Alexandria".
- "The Son obeyed as the servant He became" renders the file's "his mission as the incarnate servant".
- "You are My beloved Son", "the Word was with God, and the Word was God", "one God, the Father" and "one Lord, Jesus Christ" are spoken inside narration lines, in the BSB's words, without a scripture object. Only Deuteronomy 6:4 and 2 Corinthians 13:14 are the film's quoted verses.
- The love arc in "What it changes" joins only the Father and the Son, because John 17:24 says the Father loved the Son. The light that follows is three equal lights, one behind each ring.
- Pronouns for God are capitalized (He, His, Him), to match the BSB on screen and the other films.
- At 597 words the script is at the top of the 480 to 600 range; the estimate puts the film near 3 minutes 50 seconds with the closing card.

**Names to listen for** (hard for the voice): the Shema (shuh-MAH), the LORD in Deuteronomy 6:4 (read "the Lord", twice), Arius (AIR-ee-us), Nicaea (nye-SEE-uh), Constantinople (kon-stan-tih-NO-pul), "begotten" (bee-GOT-en), "Social trinitarians" (trin-ih-TAIR-ee-uns), "proceeds" (the Spirit proceeds, not the money sense).
