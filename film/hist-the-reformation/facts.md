## For James

**Left out on purpose.** The ways people were killed (drowned, burned, beheaded, burned at the stake, the burning of Servetus): the film says "put to death" every time, for every party alike. The "staggering portion" of Germany's people killed in the Thirty Years War. Luther's "born again" and his excommunication (only "declared an outlaw" stays). Calvin's Institutes by name (it shows on no label either), and the Reformed stress on God's sovereignty and a disciplined common life. England's later fear of Catholicism, Henry's own closeness to the old religion, and the lesson's line that God worked through the crown's tangled motives (a providential reading the film leaves to the lesson). The lesson's sentence that the churches now holding the Radicals' convictions "owe a debt to the people they once helped to kill". The Jesuits' work in contested lands. All were cut for length or to keep violence named rather than described.

**Views named without choosing.** Many Protestants read the century as a recovery of what had been buried (free grace, Scripture's authority, God's Word in the people's own tongue). Catholics read Trent as the church renewing itself from within, holding Scripture and tradition together. Free churches trace believers' baptism and uncoerced faith to the Radicals. Luther's teaching on righteousness is given as what he "came to believe", and the scale that levels is labelled "WHAT LUTHER CAME TO BELIEVE". The failures of every party are named in the same plain words: the sale of indulgences and the abuses Trent corrected; Servetus in Geneva; Catholics and Protestants both hunting the Radicals; Munster; Mary's executions; princes and a king acting for power; "No side's story is clean." The Orthodox are not in the lesson, so the film says "Western Christianity", as the lesson does.

**Points to check.**
- Romans 1:17. The lesson ties Luther's discovery to Paul's letter to the Romans but names no verse. The film says "His answer came from Paul's letter to the Romans" and then quotes 1:17, the letter's own statement that righteousness comes by faith; it never says this was the verse he read. Swap or drop it if you prefer.
- "The church needed renewal" stands where the lesson says "needed the recovery", so the closing line does not adopt the Protestant word after the film has just named "recovery" as the Protestant reading.
- The Catholic reading uses the lesson's own wording about Trent ("sacraments and good works in salvation"). Catholic teaching would add that grace comes first; the lesson does not say so, so the film does not either.
- "Protestant in doctrine" for Elizabeth's settlement is the lesson's phrase. Some Anglicans describe their church as both catholic and reformed.
- Word count is 599, at the top of the 480 to 600 range.

**Names to listen for.** Wittenberg, Zwingli, Zurich, Geneva, Servetus, Anabaptists, Munster (the lesson spells it without the umlaut), Trent, Jesuits, Henry the Eighth, Elizabeth the First, theses, indulgences, purgatory, recant.
