## Introduction: The Question Under the Question

For most of my life I asked the wrong question, and I asked it loudly, and I was sure it was the only serious one there was.

I came to faith out of atheism as a grown man, and I want to tell you what an atheist actually wants, because I was one and I remember. People think the unbeliever is asking what is true and waiting to be handed the evidence. That is not what is happening underneath. The question I was really asking, in the years I would have told you I was asking nothing, was not what should I believe. It was why. Why is there anything here at all. Why will this ache not leave me alone. Why does a man raised without a father spend his whole life looking for one and calling it by other names. I had built a life out of what questions, what do I want, what works, what gets me through, and not one of them ever touched the place the real question lived.

I have come to believe this is the oldest mistake in the world and the most modern one at the same time. We ask what before we ask why. We want the instructions before we have the reason. And a faith built that way, on the what before the why, will function for a while and then it will fail you exactly when you need it, because it was never standing on anything.

This is a short book about the order of the questions. It is an argument that almost everything that goes wrong in a Christian life, and a great deal of what goes wrong in a church, goes wrong right here, at the very first move, in which question we let speak first. We have made Christianity into a vast answer to the question what should I do, and we have been baffled when people do all the things and feel nothing, when they keep every rule and lose every reason, when they can recite the what of the faith fluently and could not tell you for their life why any of it is so.

The what is not wrong. I want to say that plainly at the start. The commands are real, the practices matter, the doing is not optional. But the what was never meant to come first, and a what that comes first stops being obedience and becomes something colder and more frightened. It becomes moralism, which is the law cut off from the love that gave it, and moralism has buried more faith than doubt ever has.

So this is not a book of answers, at least not the kind you are used to. It is a book about learning to ask why again, the way a child asks it, before the world trains the question out of us. It is the book I needed when I was an atheist asking the right question and getting handed the wrong answers, and the book I needed years later as a pastor handing people the wrong answers myself. I have five sons. I am trying to teach them to ask why before they ask what, because I think it is the difference between a faith that holds and a faith that only behaves. I am still learning it. Let me show you what I have found.

## Chapter One: The Wrong Question First

Walk into most of what passes for Christian help and you will notice it answers a question almost no one stopped to examine. The question is what. What are the steps. What do I do with my anxiety, my marriage, my Monday. What does the Bible say about money, in five points, by Friday. It is a whole economy of what, and it sells, because the what feels like progress. You can measure it. You can finish it. You can close the book and feel you have been given something.

I do not mock this, because I built a ministry on it for years before I understood what I was doing. People come to a pastor in pain and they ask, almost always, a what question. What do I do now. And the temptation, the kindly meant and deadly temptation, is to answer the question they asked instead of the one underneath it. To hand them a technique when what they needed was a reason. I gave a lot of good people a lot of good steps, and I watched a fair number of them do every step and come back emptier than before, and it took me too long to see why.

The reason is that the what question, asked first, is the question of someone who is afraid. Tell me what to do is what you say when you do not trust that anything holds you, when the ground feels like it could give, when the only safety you can imagine is in getting the procedure right. It is the question of the anxious and the moral, and the church is full of both, because the church has spent a generation answering the what and starving the why.

> A faith built on what before why will function for a while and then fail you exactly when you need it, because it was never standing on anything.

Here is the thing the steps cannot reach. A person can do everything right and still be standing on nothing. You can pray and read and serve and tithe and parent by the book, and if you do not know why, if the whole machine is running on duty and fear and the hope that God grades on completion, then the first real grief will knock it flat, because duty cannot carry grief. Only love can carry grief, and love is a why, not a what.

So I am asking you, early, to notice how trained you are to reach for the what. To notice the relief you feel when someone hands you steps and the vague panic you feel when someone says the real work is further down, in the reason, in the why you have been skipping your whole life. That panic is worth listening to. It is telling you where the building is hollow.
